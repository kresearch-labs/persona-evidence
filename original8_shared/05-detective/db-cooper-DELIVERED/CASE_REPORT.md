## 0. METHOD, SOURCE CLASSES, AND LIMITS

### 0.1 What was actually done

1. The FBI's public case history page, the July 12, 2016 closure press release, the FBI's 2007
   and 2009 evidence stories, and scanned FBI Vault case-file pages (Part 01; summary report
   SE 164-81 of May 30, 1973 via its mirror transcript) were opened and read by the reporting
   agent. Three contemporary New York Times articles (Nov 26, 1971; Nov 27, 1971; Nov 26, 1972),
   a CNN 2011 elimination summary, and a HistoryLink essay were read first-hand.
2. Four research streams (timeline; evidence register; suspects; source-dependence audit) each
   fetched and quoted additional sources; every claim below carries a source tag resolving to a
   URL in SOURCES.md, and the verbatim supporting quote is preserved in the notes files.
3. The FBI Vault release comprises 123 scanned parts covering November 1971–May 1992 [P06].
   Direct PDF downloads are Cloudflare-challenge-walled for scripted access; several parts were
   read through cooper.theunsolved.net, an AI-transcript mirror whose every page links back to
   the vault scan. Mirror-derived quotations are labeled (mirror) and carry OCR risk; the FBI
   summary report's key passages were additionally cross-confirmed because the same FD-263
   report was filed in at least four parts (41, 76, 78, 95, 111) with matching transcript text.

### 0.2 Source classes used

- VERIFIED — supported by a primary record inspected this session (FBI document, FBI public
  statement, contemporary newspaper article), or independently corroborated.
- VERIFIED-TRANSCRIPT — supported by an FBI document read via the AI-transcript mirror; OCR risk
  acknowledged; original scan not independently opened for every page.
- SUPPORTED (SECONDARY) — supported by an inspected secondary source (modern journalism,
  encyclopedia, research-group site) not checked against a primary record.
- CONTESTED — credible sources materially disagree (the disagreement itself is documented).
- INFERRED — this report's own reasoning from established facts (labeled as such).
- UNVERIFIED — searched for, not established.
- DISPROVEN — contradicted by reliable inspected evidence; contradiction identified.

### 0.3 Explicit limitations (stated up front)

- The original UPI wire copy of November 24, 1971 was never inspected; the name-origin account
  rests on 2016 retrospective journalism (Columbia Journalism Review) and the FBI's own 1973
  internal account, which disagree in emphasis (see §7, claim N-01).
- Florence Schaffner's and Tina Mucklow's individual FD-302 interview reports were not opened
  this session; their testimony is used via the FBI's 1973 summary report, contemporaneous crew
  press coverage, and later FBI statements.
- No primary court record (McCoy's 1972 trial, the 1986 Ingram/insurer judgment) was pulled from
  a courthouse or PACER; those events rest on contemporary press and FBI/press statements.
- The physical ransom bills, parachutes, tie, and lift cards were not examined; their locations
  and survivability are documented only as far as inspected sources state them.
- No numerical probabilities are assigned anywhere; the evidence base does not support defended
  priors.

### 3.1 Recorded event vs later recollection

- Recorded (1971–73 documents/press): T-01 through T-28 — radio logs, FD-302-based summary,
  same-week newspaper timelines, FBI search reports.
- Later recollection: crew anecdote color (e.g., Scott's PA-call story [S21] recorded Nov 26 —
  contemporary but retrospective within 48 h); Mucklow's later interviews; Himmelsbach's 1986/1996
  Washougal-watershed revision of the jump zone [S09]; all 2011–2024 suspect claims.

### 3.2 Principal account disagreements (from inspected sources)

1. Touchdown time 5:43/5:45/5:46; takeoff 7:36/7:37/7:40/7:41; jump 8:11/8:13 — all within
   same-week sources; the FBI document values are the most evidence-anchored.
2. Parachute accounting at Reno: 2 chutes (FBI) vs 3 packs (Times 11/25) vs "one missing" (P-I).
   The FBI summary's 1-back-pack + dummy-chest-pack taken, 1 of each left, is the cleanest.
3. Denomination: demand specified none [S30]; assembled in $20s anyway [P08][S16].
4. Pressurization: cabin "lost pressure" on stair opening [S18] and a pressure gauge reacted
   [S30] — against later tellings that the cabin "would not be pressurized" by design.
5. Name: FBI 11/25–27 usage of "D. B. Cooper" [S25][P08] and NYT statement it was "the name he
   used … when he bought a ticket" [P08] vs FBI's 1973 internal finding that "factually, there
   is no basis for this alias" [P10] and the ticket reading "DAN COOPER" [P12][S30 cover title].

### 5.1 Richard Floyd McCoy II (age 29 in 1971; died 1974)
- Proposed: by FBI agents at his April 1972 arrest for the near-identical United 855 hijacking;
  revived by retired FBI agent Russell Calame + probation officer Bernie Rhodes, "D.B. Cooper:
  The Real McCoy" (1991) [S55][S05].
- Support: near-identical MO ($500,000, four chutes, 727 aft-stairs jump, "no funny stuff" phrasing
  [S55]); his family identified the Flight-305 tie and clasp as his [S05-adjacent reporting];
  2024: his children publicly claim him after a parachute find on family property (low-reliability
  pages only — UNVERIFIED beyond that).
- Against: FBI ruled him out — description mismatch ("didn't match the nearly identical physical
  descriptions … provided by two flight attendants") [S05/P01]; all three attendants, ticket agent,
  gate agent, nearest passenger concluded not him (Vault Part 24 lineup results, mirror-cited);
  age 29 vs mid-40s; blue vs brown eyes; non-drinking Mormon vs bourbon-and-chain-smoke profile;
  whereabouts: Las Vegas Nov 24 / Utah Thanksgiving — FBI: "an unlikely scenario unless he had help"
  [P11].
- Official status: publicly ruled out [S05][P11] — BUT a July 29, 2004 NORJAK memo still sought
  McCoy-family DNA samples (Vault Part 52, mirror) [S02]: internally never fully closed.
- Independence: highest among named suspects (proposing authors had direct case access; the 1972
  copycat crime is court/press documented independent of them) — but the identity claim itself is
  author-constructed, and the widow called key book chapters fiction in a 1992 lawsuit (Deseret,
  SNIPPET-ONLY).
- Strongest falsifier: an independently verified Nov 24, 1971 alibi in Utah/Las Vegas with no
  window to reach Portland by 2:00 PM; none has been produced to primary standard.
- Residual doubt: FBI's own 2004 DNA memo shows the Bureau did not consider the elimination final.

### 5.2 Kenneth Christiansen (NWA purser, ex-paratrooper; died 1994)
- Proposed: brother Lyle Christiansen (letters to FBI ~2003-04); PI Skipp Porteous 2007; profiled
  by Geoffrey Gray, New York Magazine, Oct 18, 2007 [S50]; "Into the Blast" (2010).
- Support: NWA employee, WWII paratrooper, smoker, bourbon drinker, age ~45; Schaffner found his
  photos a better fit than others shown, without conclusive ID [S50].
- Against: 5'8"/150 lb vs 5'10"–6'0"/170–180; FBI: "did not match the physical description and was
  a skilled paratrooper" [P11]; FBI publicly rejected him Oct 2007 ("almost certainly not him") [S65];
  the cash-home-purchase claim was retracted by his own proponents in 2018 — mortgage, 19-year payoff
  [S68].
- Official status: press-statement rejection only; no primary FBI elimination document surfaced.
- Independence: LOW — brother + hired PI + book; only quasi-independent element is Schaffner's
  qualified photo preference.
- Strongest falsifier: physical-description mismatch, which the FBI and Himmelsbach both cite.
- Residual doubt: the "skilled paratrooper" exclusion assumes the hijacker was skilled — an
  assumption the FBI itself abandoned in 2007 [P03]. The exclusion is description-based and stands.

### 5.3 Robert Rackstraw (1943–)
- Proposed: FBI interest 1978–79 (post-Iran arrest); modern campaign by Thomas Colbert (2016 book;
  2017 strap/foam finds; 2018 "coded letter" claims) [S56][S59].
- Support: Vietnam/SF paratrooper, demolitions, pilot, aliases; Colbert team's decoding of the 1971
  "5th letter" and 1972 "6th letter" as naming him; resemblance claims.
- Against: FBI investigated and cleared him in 1979 [S56]; he denied it repeatedly ("a lot of
  [expletive]" — People, 2016, SNIPPET-ONLY); FBI lab found nothing in the letters ("It was never
  proven if the actual hijacker wrote the letters" [S56]); independent cryptologists called the
  2018 decoding a "textbook example of the Texas sharpshooter fallacy" (Oregonian, Aug 2018 —
  inspected via audit stream); age 28 in 1971.
- Official status: 1979 clearance documented in 1979 press; FBI never endorsed the 2016–18 claims
  [S59][P02-adjacent].
- Independence: MEDIUM-LOW — all load-bearing evidence is intra-team; the decoder knew Rackstraw's
  writing style (circularity); the FBI's own 1978–79 interest is the only independent element.
- Strongest falsifier: the FBI's 1979 clearance plus the failed letter forensics.
- Residual doubt: the clearance is press-documented, not primary-inspected this session.

### 5.4 Lynn Doyle "L.D." Cooper (d. 1999?)
- Proposed: niece Marla Cooper, mid-2011, after ~1 year of quiet FBI work on the tip [P11][S07].
- Support: childhood memory of uncles plotting "something very mischievous" the day before; L.D.
  home bloodied on Thanksgiving eve; 1995 deathbed remark by her father; resemblance; FBI (Gutt):
  "so far there's not a lot that's inconsistent" [P11].
- Against: not a skydiver/paratrooper; guitar strap yielded no usable prints [S07]; tie DNA
  comparison "not a match" — but FBI said the tie sample had three donors and was "not enough to
  rule Uncle L.D. out" [S49].
- Official status: explicitly NOT eliminated — nothing to contradict, nothing to confirm [S07].
- Independence: LOW — one witness's childhood recollection + family items; the FBI's year of
  non-confirming investigation is independent but inconclusive.
- Strongest falsifier: a definitive DNA comparison against the (never-published) tie profile.
- Residual doubt: this is the only suspect the FBI has never ruled out in public statements.

### 5.5 Duane L. Weber (d. 1995)
- Proposed: widow Jo Weber after his deathbed "I'm Dan Cooper" [S51].
- Support: nightmares about "leaving fingerprints on a plane"; knee injury story; marginalia in a

- Against: FBI eliminated him on fingerprints (July 1998, secondary-reported) and tie DNA [P03][P11];

- Official status: eliminated on prints + DNA — both via FBI statements; underlying lab results

- Independence: LOW (single-spouse narrative); the eliminations are independent of her but rest on
  partial samples.

### 5.6 Walter R. Reca (d. 2014)
- Proposed: friend Carl Laurin; public May 2018 via "D.B. Cooper and Me" (Principia Media);

- Support: notarized publication permission; claimed spending documents; one witness (Osiadacz)

- Against: Cle Elum ~150 mi from the computed landing zone; expert-paratrooper profile contradicts

## 6. COMPETING HYPOTHESES

H1 — A named suspect committed the hijacking.
- Explains: the crime's mechanics (any of the parachute-experienced candidates could physically
  have done it); McCoy additionally explains near-identical MO eleven weeks later.
- Struggles: no named candidate matches the witness description AND the Nov 24/25 whereabouts
  AND the forensic record simultaneously; McCoy fails description + alibi; Christiansen fails
  description; Rackstraw fails the FBI's 1979 clearance + letter forensics; L.D. Cooper fails
  parachuting experience; Reca fails geography + experience; Weber eliminated by prints/DNA.
- Assumptions: that witness description (mid-40s, 5'10"–6'0", 170–180 lb, brown eyes) is reliable;
  that the FBI's eliminations were competently executed.
- Would increase confidence: a confirmed serial-number bill surfacing in a suspect's estate; a
  published DNA/fingerprint match; a primary record placing a named person in the Portland–Seattle
  corridor on Nov 24, 1971.
- Would decrease: primary-record alibis for each candidate; continued FBI silence after 2016
  despite 45 years of tips.
- Testable prediction: for H1(McCoy), McCoy-family DNA/prints would match case standards if the
  FBI's 2004 memo program were completed and published. Distinguishes H1 from H2 only if a match
  (or definitive mismatch) is published.
- Note: H1 is a family of theories, not one; no member currently meets a defensible threshold.

H2 — The hijacker was never among the publicly named suspects (unidentified individual).
- Explains: every elimination failure; the FBI's 45-year, 800+-suspect outcome; the mixture-
  DNA and partial-print dead ends; the case's resistance to every named theory.
- Struggles: explains nothing about motive/profile (but none is required); the apparent
  parachute competence of the McCoy-type copycat (Nov 1972) shows such individuals existed
  without helping identify this one.
- Assumptions: only that FBI elimination work was sound.
- Would increase confidence: continued absence of any confirmed match as records keep being
  released (Parts through 122 by 2026); serials never surfacing in any named suspect's story.
- Would decrease confidence: a definitive match to any named person.
- Testable prediction: completion of the §9 tests (DNA/prints/particles) yields no match to any
  publicly named suspect. Partially distinguishing: it is compatible with H4.

H3 — Central elements of the prevailing reconstruction are wrong or incomplete.
Sub-hypotheses the record itself generates:
- H3a (jump point): the Ariel/Lake Merwin public fixation is a fossil of the first-week search
  grid; the FBI's own later working area shifted (Washougal watershed per Himmelsbach [S09]);
  H3a says the true landing zone was further south/east, which would explain the Tena Bar money
  (~20 mi SW of the computed zone [S62]) better than river-transport theories.
- H3b (money): the Tena Bar bundles were found in delivered packaging order with intact-but-
  brittle bands [S44][S53] — hard to reconcile with ~8.5 years of river tumbling; H3b says the
  money was buried at/near Tena Bar by human agency or short-range transport, not carried by
  the 1974 dredge (Citizen Sleuths themselves "discount" the dredge theory [S45]).
- H3c (name): "Dan Cooper" itself may not have been the ticket name (see N-01) — if the FBI's
  1973 account and the NYT's Nov 27, 1971 reporting are weighted over the 2024 retrospective,
  the entire "Dan Cooper alias + comic-book hero" chain [P04] weakens.
- Explains: the geographic anomaly (H3a), the packaging anomaly (H3b), the name anomaly (H3c).
- Struggles: the FBI's 8:05 radar fix [S30] and drift computation constrain H3a; H3b has no
  mechanism evidence; H3c must explain the FBI's own later "Dan Cooper" usage.
- Testable predictions: H3a — renewed drift computation from the documented 8:05 fix + FAA wind
  record lands SW of the 1971 grid; H3b — sediment/diatom analysis of surviving Tena Bar notes
  distinguishes riverine vs direct burial; H3c — Nov 24–25, 1971 Oregonian/Oregon Journal and
  UPI copy settles the ticket name as printed at the time.
H4 — The surviving evidence is insufficient to discriminate among the principal explanations.
- Explains: everything above simultaneously; the FBI's own 45-year non-identification.
- Struggles: it is not an explanation but a meta-claim; it is falsifiable only by new
  discriminating evidence.
- Assumptions: that the destroyed/lost items (butts, hairs, at least some records) carried the
  discriminating potential, or that remaining items cannot be lawfully accessed.
- Would increase confidence: repeated FOIA/lab-access failures; serials and DNA never matching
  anyone.
- Testable prediction: §9 tests T-01–T-08 all return inconclusive or access-denied results —
  which would itself be evidence for H4.

## 11. FINAL VERDICT

1. **Highest-confidence established facts.** A man bought a one-way ticket at Portland at 2:00 PM
   on Nov 24, 1971, sat in seat 18E, and hijacked Flight 305 with a bomb threat; received $200,000
   in used $20s plus four parachutes (one a non-steerable backpack, one a dummy training chest
   pack) at Seattle ~5:46 PM; ordered the 727 to 10,000 ft/170 kt with the aft stairs open; was
   last heard from at 8:05 PM and is evidenced to have exited at ~8:11 PM over SW Washington
   (pressure-bump event, replicated Jan 6, 1972 on the same airframe); $5,800 of the ransom,
   in delivered packaging order, surfaced at Tena Bar on Feb 10, 1980 with matching serials.
   Sources: FBI summary report SE 164-81 (mirror), contemporary NYT/Seattle papers, FBI pages.

2. **Strongest available identity evidence.** (a) The two attendants' separately-given,
   near-identical description (mid-40s, 5'10"–6'0", 170–180 lb, brown eyes) — FBI-stated but
   FD-302s uninspected; (b) the tie, the only surviving contact item, with its particle
   assemblage and mixed DNA; (c) partial fingerprints from the aircraft; (d) the constraint set:
   not an expert jumper (FBI's own 2007 conclusion), smoked Raleighs, comfortable directing
   a jet's fueling and flight profile. No biological or print match to any person has ever been
   published.

3. **Does any named suspect meet a defensible threshold?** No. McCoy comes closest to a *falsifiable,
   partially independent* theory (FBI-documented copycat MO) but fails the description and the
   Thanksgiving-alibi evidence, and the FBI publicly ruled him out — while a 2004 internal memo
   shows the Bureau itself still sought McCoy-family DNA. Every other theory rests on a proponent's
   own narrative (family recollection, memoir, private decode) with failure-to-exclude as its best
   evidence. The hijacker may well be none of the named men.

4. **Commonly repeated but inadequately supported claims.** "D.B." was the ticket alias (the FBI's
   own file says the alias is baseless); Ariel/Lake Merwin as the jump site; "he demanded twenties";
   experienced-jumper framing (FBI-withdrawn); "800 suspects" as a firm number; tie particles
   prove a specific occupation; a robust "partial DNA profile" (it is a mixed, unpublished,
   exclusion-only sample); "2017 DNA announcement" (2007); "FBI destroyed the parachutes";
   seat 18C; the Reca taped confession; the Rackstraw letter "codes."

5. **Fundamentally unknowable from the inspected evidence.** Whether he survived; where he landed;
   what he did with $194,200 of the money; whether the bomb was real; his true name. Also now
   partially unknowable: full discrimination via the destroyed cigarette butts and hairs.

6. **Highest expected information gain next action.** T-01: FOIA the FBI Laboratory
   communications (2001 DNA lift, 1998/2001 print work, 2011 mixture caveats) — cheap, the
   custodial chain is affirmed by the FBI's own 2016 preservation statement, and the result
   re-weights every DNA-based elimination and every H1 sub-theory at once. T-02 (completing and
   publishing the McCoy-family DNA program the FBI itself queued in 2004) is the single most
   suspect-decisive action.

7. **What would materially change the conclusion.** A published DNA or fingerprint match between
   the case samples and any named (or newly named) individual; recovery of an additional
   serial-number-matched bill outside the Columbia drainage; documentary proof of a Nov 24, 1971
   Portland–Seattle presence (or airtight absence) for any named suspect; or — conversely —
   documented destruction of the remaining lab materials, which would entrench H4.

**Qualitative confidence:** High (document-anchored, multi-source) for the event reconstruction
and evidence register; moderate for the suspect audit (press-statement-level eliminations);
low — appropriately — for identity. The case's central epistemic fact is that 45 years of
official effort produced only exclusions; this report's classifications mirror that structure
rather than manufacturing certainty.
