# D.B. Cooper / NW Orient Flight 305 — Minute-Level Timeline Source Notes
Investigation date of research: Oct 19, 2026. Event date: Nov 24, 1971.

METHOD: Every claim below was taken from a page actually fetched during this session. For newspaper
clippings and FBI documents, the "page" is the document transcript at cooper.theunsolved.net
(a SECONDARY MIRROR: AI-vision transcripts of scans of pages from the FBI Vault D.B. Cooper FOIA
release, each linking back to vault.fbi.gov). Originals behind those scans are labeled
CONTEMPORARY-1971-72 (newspaper clippings mounted in the FBI file, with FD-350 mount sheet dates)
or FBI-DOC (FD-302s, FD-263 summary report). OCR/OCR-uncertainties in the source are marked
[guess: …] in the mirror transcripts; quotes below reproduce the transcript wording, with obvious
line-break hyphenation joined.

SOURCE CLASSES:
- CONTEMPORARY-1971-72: newspaper/wire copy published Nov 25–Dec 1971 (read as FBI-file clipping scans via the mirror)
- FBI-DOC: FBI document (FD-302 interview report, FD-263 NORJAK summary report) via the mirror
- LATER-RECOLLECTION: crew/agent interviews or books published years later
- SECONDARY: modern narrative (Wikipedia, FBI history page, HistoryLink)
- SECONDARY-MIRROR: cooper.theunsolved.net (AI transcripts of FBI Vault scans — verify against scan)

## TIMELINE CLAIMS (all with verbatim quotes)

### A. Portland — ticket purchase and boarding (~2:00–2:50 pm PST)

A1. Ticket purchase time, fare form, seat. FBI-DOC (NORJAK summary report SE 164-81, May 30 1973,
    investigative period 11/24/71–5/25/73).
    URL: https://cooper.theunsolved.net/documents/Cooper-Part-78%3A228-278
    Title: "FBI report SE 164-81, NORJAK (D.B. Cooper) summary: administrative cover pages,
    suspect-processing checklist, unsub profile, hijack narrative..." (FBI report, 30 May 1973)
    QUOTE: "The Unsub, aka [REDACTED], at 2:00 PM, Pacific Standard Time, November 24, 1971,
    purchased a one-way coach ticket at the Portland International Airport, Portland, Oregon, for
    travel from Portland to Seattle, Washington, on Northwest Orient Airlines Flight Number 305,
    which was a Boeing 727 aircraft. Unsub paid for this flight with a twenty dollar bill and had
    no luggage. He occupied seat number 18 E on the aircraft."
    NOTE: the alias in this sentence is redacted in the released copy; other parts of the same
    report title the case "UNKNOWN SUBJECT; a.k.a Dan Cooper".

A2. Drink order and the note. FBI-DOC, same NORJAK summary.
    QUOTE: "Prior to the time the aircraft taxied on the run- way, he purchased a bourbon and
    Seven Up from Stewardess [REDACTED]. As the aircraft taxied toward the runway for takeoff,
    Unsub turned and handed Stewardess [REDACTED] an envelope containing a note written in black
    felt-tip pen on plain white unlined paper which said: \"Miss, I have a bomb here and I would
    like you to sit by me\"."
    NOTE: places the drink BEFORE taxi and the note hand-off AT taxi — earlier than the popular
    "shortly after takeoff" telling.

A3. Fare $20 cash at the counter — SECONDARY (HistoryLink Essay 1997, posted 2011):
    URL: https://historylink.org/file/1997
    Title: "Dan Cooper parachutes from skyjacked jetliner on November 24, 1971."
    QUOTE: "He had bought a $20 ticket at the last minute, after first confirming that the
    aircraft was a Boeing 727, a model equipped with an aft staircase."

A4. Crew complement. CONTEMPORARY-1971-72 (Seattle P-I, 11/25/71, "Persons On The Plane"):
    URL: https://cooper.theunsolved.net/documents/Cooper-Part-53%3A158-158
    QUOTE: "The FBI identified the crew as William Scott, the pilot; Bob Rataczak, first officer;
    H. E. Anderson, flight engineer, and Tina Mucklow."
    (Note: transcript lists the crew identically to the Seattle Times 11/26 identifications:
    "Scott; Bob Rataczak, first officer; H. N. Anderson, flight engineer, and Tina Mucklow,
    stewardess" — see D-file below; the P-I 11/25 main story names "W. 'Bob' Rataczak and
    H. E. Anderson".)
    Companion quote (Seattle Times 11/25/71):
    URL: https://cooper.theunsolved.net/documents/Cooper-Part-53%3A172-173
    QUOTE: "Crew members aboard the plane when it reached Reno were identified as Scott; Bob
    Rataczak, first officer; H. N. Anderson, flight engineer, and Tina Mucklow, stewardess."
    NOTE the OCR variance H.E./H.N. Anderson and the title disagreement with later accounts
    (Wikipedia: "First Officer William 'Bill' J. Rataczak, flight engineer Harold E. Anderson").

### C. The note, the bomb briefcase, demands (~4 pm)

C1. Schaffner's first account week-of. CONTEMPORARY-1971-72 (St. Paul Pioneer-Press, Kathryn
    Boardman, Nov 27, 1971 — crew press conference):
    URL: https://cooper.theunsolved.net/documents/Cooper-Part-77%3A395-396
    Title: "FD-350 clipping, 'Stewardess Mistook Hijacker's Note' re Florence Schaffner (News
    clipping, 27 November 1971)"
    QUOTE: "\"But he stared at me so hard I kept looking at him,\" Miss Schaffner said at a press
    conference Friday. \"Then he told me that I had better read the note right away. I did and
    almost fainted. It was not a mash note. He said he was hijacking the plane."
    QUOTE (demands): "\"He wanted $200,000,\" she said. \"He wanted four parachutes — two front
    packs and two back packs. He wanted his directions followed with no fuss and he wanted no
    police at the Seattle-Tacoma [guess: airport] airport, where we were to land.\""
    NOTE: press-conference recollection (Nov 27) — closest thing to a crew account in hand that
    names Schaffner; the same-week accounts disagree with the later FBI summary on detail (see
    Contradictions).

C2. Demands as dictated (FBI-DOC, NORJAK summary):
    QUOTE: "The hijacker then dictated the following: \"I want $200,000 by 5:00 p.m. in cash\".
    (No specification as to denomination, age or sequence of bills). \"Put it in a knapsack. I
    want two back parachutes and two front parachutes\" (no other specification concerning para-
    chute types). \"When we land I want a fuel truck ready to refuel\" (probably his flying
    experience caused him to add this). \"No funny stuff or I'll do the job\""
    NOTE: the FBI summary explicitly records NO denomination specification — against the legend
    that Cooper specified "$20 bills" in the note.

C3. Holding pattern message 4:50 pm. CONTEMPORARY-1971-72 (Henderson, Seattle Times 11/25/71):
    QUOTE: "Flight 305 went into a holding pattern 25 miles northwest of Seattle. At 4:50 p. m.,
    for the benefit of the hijacker, ground control relayed this message to the jetliner:
    \"F. B. I. and police have advised no action to be taken. Repeat. No action to be taken."
    (Same message quoted in NYT 11/25/71, Earl Caldwell:
    URL: https://cooper.theunsolved.net/documents/Cooper-Part-50%3A339-343
    QUOTE: "At 4:50 P.M., ground control relayed the following message to the plane:
    \"F.B.I. and police have advised no action be taken. Re-peat no action to be taken. We are
    awaiting [guess: his] concurrence that you should land.\"")

C4. Money/parachute logistics timeline. CONTEMPORARY-1971-72 (Henderson):
    QUOTE: "Northwest control (5:07 p. m.): \"The money is at the airport. Now all we lack are two
    parachutes.\"" / "Scott (5:21 p. m.): \"He's getting very impatient for those chutes. We are
    going to have to come up with them pretty quick.\"" / "Northwest control (5:24 p. m.): \"They
    have just advised that the parachutes have arrived. So we'll go down and pick them up and be
    on our way.\"" / "Northwest control (5:39 p. m.): \"Everything is ready for your arrival."
    NOTE: parachute source per Henderson: "The parachutes had been sought, apparently without
    success, from McChord Air Force Base. Now they were en route in a State Patrol car from the
    Seattle Sky Sports airfield near Issaquah."

C5. Military chutes rejected for sport models. CONTEMPORARY-1971-72 (NYT, Earl Caldwell, 11/25/71):
    QUOTE: "Two military models were found at McChord Air Force Base in Tacoma and rushed to the
    Seattle field. When the hijacker learned that military models were being supplied, he demanded
    that sport models be made available instead. These were found at a nearby airfield used by
    sky divers."

C6. Ransom money provenance. FBI-DOC (NORJAK summary):
    QUOTE: "Ransom money in the amount of $200,000 was made up entirely of used, random 20 dollar
    bills. It was obtained from the Seattle First National Bank, Main Office, and was part of a
    ransom package of $250,000, which had been maintained by the bank for such emergencies. The
    package measured approximately one foot by one foot by eight or nine inches and weighed 19
    pounds."
    NOTE: the summary also records (search-poster text) that "all serial numbers are known and can
    be identified" — QUOTE: "3) 10,000 $20 bills wrapped in a parachute canopy, all serial numbers
    are known and can be identified."

D3. Passengers released by 6:10; stewardesses 6:50. CONTEMPORARY-1971-72 (Clever, P-I):

### E. Takeoff toward Reno (~7:30–7:40 pm)

E1. Cleared ~7:30, airborne 7:40. CONTEMPORARY-1971-72 (Clever, P-I):
    QUOTE: "At about 7:30 p.m., Flight 305 is cleared for takeoff." ... "Scott: \"Sorry for all the
    trouble.\"" ... "7:40 p.m. — Flight 305 is airborne and bound for Reno."
    Variants: P-I A1 story 11/25/71: QUOTE: "The plane took off at 7:41 p.m, after the pilot told
    the FAA control tower, \"sorry for all the trouble.\""
    Seattle Times 11/25/71: QUOTE: "It left Seattle - Tacoma Airport at 7:37 p. m. and took 3½
    hours to fly to Reno."
    FBI-DOC (NORJAK summary): QUOTE: "The plane departed Sea-Tac at 7:36 PM."
    (4-minute spread across same-day/next-day sources; 7:36–7:41.)

E2. Mucklow ordered to cockpit; stair sequence. FBI-DOC (NORJAK summary):
    QUOTE: "Some four or five minutes after take-off, [REDACTED] was sent to the cockpit by the
    hijacker. Some ten minutes after she entered the cockpit, one of the officers received a call
    on the interphone from the hijacker, advising he could not get the air stairs down. The pilot
    then leveled off the aircraft and reduced the air speed. Within a very short time, the
    officer's panel showed that the stairs had been lowered and approximately five minutes later,
    (8:05 PM), the officer contacted the hijacker who said everything was okay. That was the last
    communication with the hijacker."
    CONTEMPORARY corroboration (UPI via Seattle Times, 11/26/71):
    URL: https://cooper.theunsolved.net/documents/Cooper-Part-53%3A145-145
    QUOTE: "Miss Mucklow, dicussing the time interval in which the hijacker jumped, said that
    \"right after the takeoff (from Seattle) he said, 'Go to the cabin, pull the curtain, and don't
    come back.'\" She said she obeyed him."

E3. Stair light at 7:42 at 7,000 ft. FBI-DOC (NORJAK summary):
    QUOTE: "At 7:42 PM, the crew advised that the stair light was on, indicating that the hijacker
    was doing something with the air stairs. The plane, at that time, was flying at 7,000 feet."

### F. The jump (~8:05–8:13 pm)

F1. FBI's minute-level reconstruction. FBI-DOC (NORJAK summary):
    QUOTE: "It is apparent from the following excerpts, taken from logs maintained by ground
    personnel on November 24, 1971, that the hijacker exited the airplane at 8:11 p.m. local time.
    When the hijacker jumped, the air stairs were forced closed causing an increase in air pressure
    similar to shutting a car door with the windows up."
    QUOTE (NWA log): "8:11 p.m. \"Reno looks good (weather). 305 at the present cabin is
    fluctuating playing with air stairs fwd cabin lites on.\""
    QUOTE (reconstructed radio log): "\"(Airplane now at 10,000' 170K, gear down, flaps 15°, FF
    4000 #/hr/engine, TAT -7°C)\"."
    QUOTE: "At 8:11 PM, while the plane was at 10,000 feet, the crew experienced a pressure bump or
    oscillation and the Cabin Pressure Rate of Change Gauge also reacted violently and it was
    thought, at that time, that the hijacker might have left the aircraft."

F2. Location at last-known-aboard point. FBI-DOC (NORJAK summary):
    QUOTE: "An analysis on 11/24 and 25/71, of the flight-path of the aircraft, after it left
    Seattle, and the logs provided by NWA, suggested that the hijacker was still in the airplane as
    of 8:05 PM. At that time the plane, according to radar tracking by the U.S. Air Force, was
    located at a point north of the Pidgeon Springs, Washington area and east of Ostrander,
    Washington. This would have been the northernmost point when he was known to be on the
    aircraft."
    QUOTE (crew report at 8:11): "the crew reported that they were \"getting some oscillations in
    the cabin, must be doing something with air stairs.\""

F3. Contemporary 8:13 estimate. CONTEMPORARY-1971-72 (Dick Clever, Seattle P-I, 11/26/71, "Hijacker
    Hunt Near Woodland"):
    URL: https://cooper.theunsolved.net/documents/Cooper-Part-53%3A124-126
    QUOTE: "He said authorities believe the hijacker bailed out of the low-flying 727 at 8:13 p.m.,
    a little over a half an hour after taking off from Sea-Tac."
    NOTE: internally inconsistent with the same article's own takeoff time (7:36–7:41 + "half an
    hour" would be ~8:10–8:15 — close, but the arithmetic is loose).

F4. Flight conditions. CONTEMPORARY-1971-72 (AP wire, 11/25/71, as clipped from The Sun,
    Vancouver BC):
    URL: https://cooper.theunsolved.net/documents/Cooper-Part-53%3A120-121
    QUOTE: "The plane made the run at 10,000 feet with its rear stair-well open so the hijacker
    could bail out if he chose. At that altitude no oxygen was required." / "\"Because the rear
    door was open, the cabin area lost pres-sure and the temperature in the plane dropped to seven
    degrees below zero,\" FAA offi-cials said."
    FBI-DOC weather: QUOTE: "A check of the Federal Aviation Administration flight section at
    Portland International Airport reflected that on November 24, 1971 between 8:00 PM and 9:00 PM,
    there was a ceiling of 5,000 feet, 3,500 feet broken clouds, and at 1,500 feet scattered and
    broken clouds at times with a wind of 12-14 knots. The temperature at the end of the air stairs
    at the time of the jump was approximately 0° C."
    Scott's UPI account (CONTEMPORARY, Seattle Times 11/26/71): QUOTE: "Scott said it flew at about
    180 knots at about 10,000 feet altitude most of the time. Scott said the hijacker did not
    specify the route."

F5. Last contact over Clark County / Lake Merwin. CONTEMPORARY-1971-72 (Seattle Times 11/26/71,
    UPI):
    QUOTE: "(However, the last communication between the hijacker and the crew was while the plane
    was over Clark County. This resulted in an intensive search of the area around Woodland.)"
    NYT 11/25/71 (Scott): QUOTE: "Captain Scott said that the last time he had communicated with
    the man on the intercom was when the plane was over Lake Mer-win on the Lewis River in

### G. Reno landing and discovery (~11:02 pm – after midnight)

G1. Duration and arrival. CONTEMPORARY-1971-72 (Seattle Times 11/25/71):
    QUOTE: "It left Seattle - Tacoma Airport at 7:37 p. m. and took 3½ hours to fly to Reno."
    (⇒ ~11:07 pm arrival.) SECONDARY (Wikipedia, fetched): QUOTE: "At 11:02 pm, with the airstair
    still deployed, Flight 305 landed at Reno–Tahoe International Airport."
    URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._B._Cooper

G2. Discovery and scene. CONTEMPORARY-1971-72 (Seattle Times 11/25/71):
    QUOTE: "The crew was taken from the plane 35 minutes after it landed in Reno. Officials said
    the delay was due to the possibility that a bomb had been placed aboard. No bomb was found."
    AP 11/25/71: QUOTE: "\"There's no way he could have got off in Reno,\" said Harold Campbell
    Jr., special agent in charge of FBI opera-tions in Nevada. \"We had the airport covered.\""
    Scott UPI 11/26/71: QUOTE: "\"When we landed at Reno, we thought he still was aboard,\" Scott
    said. \"We were trying to call him on the P. A. (public address) system. I stepped into the
    cabin and asked if there were any more instructions, but there was no answer. I looked around
    the curtain and determined he was not there.\""

G3. What was missing/found. FBI-DOC (NORJAK summary):
    QUOTE: "When the aircraft landed at Reno, Nevada, a chest pack chute and a back type chute were
    found on the plane. The missing chest pack chute, sometimes referred to as an emergency chute,
    is a training pack \"dummed up\" to look like a good one. The missing back pack chute is
    non-steerable and anyone utilizing this type chute would have no chance of slowing
    down or picking his spot on which to land."
    CONTEMPORARY accounting confusion: Seattle Times 11/25/71: QUOTE: "the hijacker also was given
    two parachute as-semblies, each consisting of a chest pack and back pack. Three packs were
    found aboard the plane in Reno." vs AP 11/25/71: QUOTE: "Two of four parachutes ob-tained by
    the middle-aged hi-jacker from airline officials in Seattle were missing when the plane landed
    here with four crewmen aboard" vs P-I 11/25/71: QUOTE: "One parachute of the four loaded at
    Seattle was missing."

### H. Immediate search (Nov 25 onward)

H1. First-day search concept. CONTEMPORARY-1971-72 (P-I 11/26/71, Clever):
    QUOTE: "Clark County Undersheriff Tom McDowell said the FBI was searching a five-mile corridor
    a few miles southeast of Woodland, where a command post has been established." / "The area
    outlined for a full-scale ground and air search, which begins this morning, is described as
    \"wooded and rugged.\""
    NYT 11/25/71 (Portland FBI agent in charge Julius Mattson): QUOTE: "said that the search for
    the hijacker was being conducted [beginning] around Amboy, Wash., which is just south of Lake
    Merwin. \"It's not a bad spot to land if the guy knows what he's doing,\" the agent said."

H2. FBI search-area geometry and conduct. FBI-DOC (NORJAK summary):
    QUOTE: "On 11/26-25/71, based on information available from NWA, a search area was laid out in
    the southwest portion of the State of Washington, which was bounded by Woodland, Washington, on
    the west, Amboy, Washington, on the east, Kalama, Washington on the north and a point seven
    miles north of the Columbia River, on the south. The prime search area was in an area seven
    miles east and seven miles west of an eight mile long flight line of the hijacked aircraft,
    bounded by an area two miles north of Lake Merwin and the Lewis River on the north, Amboy on
    the east, Woodland, on the west and Battle Ground, Washington, on the south. The search area
    was divided into six sectors. Four heli-copters and seven fixed-wing aircraft were utilized in
    an intensive, gridded air search of the prime area and a well executed search of the secondary
    search area on 11/26, 11/27 and 11/29/71."
    QUOTE: "Ground units of local law enforcement personnel and Bureau Agents, made a
    house-to-house canvas of every dwelling and business establishment on every road in the prime
    search area from 11/25/71 through 12/3/71."
    QUOTE: "On November 26, 1971 the banks of Lake Merwin, partly located in the prime search
    area, were completely patrolled by two boats of the Cowlitz County Sheriff's Office with all
    suspicious sightings personally checked."

### I. The name: 'Dan Cooper' vs 'D.B. Cooper'

I1. Case titled "Dan Cooper" inside FBI file. FBI-DOC (NORJAK summary cover):
    QUOTE: "TITLE OF CASE: UNKNOWN SUBJECT; a.k.a Dan Cooper NORTHWEST AIRLINES FLIGHT #350
    PORTLAND, OREGON, TO SEATTLE, WASHINGTON, 11/24/71"
    (Flight number typo'd as #350 in the report header; body says Flight Number 305.)

I2. "D.B. Cooper" in use by Nov 25 — CONTEMPORARY. Seattle Times 11/25/71:
    QUOTE: "The Federal Bureau of In-vestigation in Reno tenta-tively identified the hijacker as
    D. B. Cooper. His name was not among 36 on a list of passengers issued by the air-line."
    P-I 11/26/71: QUOTE: "The hijacker, known as \"D. B. Cooper\" to the FBI, had demanded the
    money and parachutes..."
    P-I 11/26/71 (Portland agent, early confusion): QUOTE: "An agent in Port-land said \"D. B.
    Cooper\" was the name the man used when he bought his ticket, \"but he's probably no more D. B.
    Cooper" [transcript cut mid-sentence]
    SECONDARY explanation (Wikipedia): QUOTE: "In a rush to meet a deadline, reporter James Long of
    The Oregon Journal recorded the name \"Dan Cooper\" as \"D. B. Cooper\". United Press
    International wire service reporter Clyde Jabin republished Long's error, and as other media
    outlets repeated the error, the name \"D. B. Cooper\" stuck."
    SECONDARY variant (HistoryLink Essay 23059): QUOTE: "But when the United Press International
    wire service was rushing to move a story about the hijacking, the hijacker was mistakenly
    listed as D. B. Cooper. The FBI corrected it the next day in a news release, but by then the
    error had traveled around the world, and the D. B. moniker stuck."
    URL: https://historylink.org/file/23059

## CONTRADICTIONS BETWEEN ACCOUNTS (all documented above)

1. Seattle touchdown time: 5:43 pm (Seattle Times 11/25) vs 5:45 pm (Seattle P-I, both Clever
   stories) vs 5:46 PM PST (FBI NORJAK summary) vs "shortly before 6 P.M." (NYT 11/25).
2. Seattle takeoff time: 7:36 PM (FBI NORJAK summary) vs 7:37 pm (Seattle Times 11/25) vs 7:40 pm
   (P-I Clever timeline) vs 7:41 pm (P-I A1 story). A 5-minute spread in same-week sources.
3. Jump/exit time: 8:11 p.m. (FBI reconstruction from NWA logs, its "best estimate") vs 8:13 p.m.
   (contemporary P-I 11/26, "authorities believe") vs HistoryLink 1997's narrative that the stair
   light came on "five minutes out of Seattle, at 7:42 p.m." with the jump inferred from
   oscillations afterwards vs Wikipedia's ~8:13. The FBI's own doc places last communication at
   8:05 PM and the pressure event at 8:11 PM.
4. Cabin pressure: whether the cabin "lost pressure" (AP, quoting FAA: cabin lost pressure,
   temperature fell to −7°) vs later tellings that the cabin was unpressurized by design at
   10,000 ft (HistoryLink: "an altitude at which the cabin would not be pressurized"). The FBI
   log records TAT −7°C and a pressure Rate-of-Change gauge reaction — i.e., the aircraft was
   pressurized until the door opened, contradicting the "would not be pressurized" framing.
5. Parachute accounting: demand "two back parachutes and two front parachutes" (FBI summary,
   Schaffner press conference) vs "two parachutes" reported in-flight (Henderson: "Now all we
   lack are two parachutes"; also his lede "The hijacker wanted two parachutes") vs Seattle Times
   11/25 "two parachute assemblies, each consisting of a chest pack and back pack" vs Reno finds:
   FBI "a chest pack chute and a back type chute were found" vs Times "Three packs were found
   aboard" vs AP "Two of four parachutes... were missing" vs P-I 11/25 "One parachute of the four
   loaded at Seattle was missing."
6. Denomination demand: FBI summary explicitly "No specification as to denomination, age or
   sequence of bills" — yet the money was assembled in $20 bills; later accounts (and even the FBI
   history page) say he demanded "$200,000 in twenty-dollar bills."
7. Hijacker's name: "Dan Cooper" on the ticket (FBI file case title "a.k.a Dan Cooper"; agent's
   later statements) vs "D. B. Cooper" reported by UPI/NYT on Nov 25 as the FBI's tentative ID; a
   Portland FBI agent told the P-I (11/26) that "D. B. Cooper" was the name used at ticket
   purchase — directly conflicting with the Dan Cooper ticket legend.
8. Note text: "Miss, I have a bomb here and I would like you to sit by me" (FBI summary; Schaffner
   via HistoryLink/Gray) vs HistoryLink 1997's "Miss, I have a bomb here and I would like you to
   sit by me" (same) vs USPA's paraphrase — minor; but the delivery timing differs: at taxi
   (FBI summary) vs before takeoff (USPA) vs shortly after takeoff (Wikipedia/HistoryLink).
9. Drink order: "bourbon and Seven Up" (FBI NORJAK summary) vs "bourbon and soda" (FBI history
   page; HistoryLink 23059) vs "bourbon with, depending on the source, either Seven-Up or water"
   (HistoryLink 1997).
10. Seat number: 18E (FBI NORJAK summary; Wikipedia) vs the folk memory of 18C; HistoryLink 1997
    says only "row 18, the last row". FBI document is the primary record and says 18E.
11. Flight speed/altitude at jump: "10,000' 170K" (FBI log reconstruction) vs "about 180 knots at
    about 10,000 feet" (Scott to UPI, 11/26) vs "near its minimum cruising speed of 200 miles an
    hour" (NYT) vs "196 mph at an altitude of roughly 10,000 feet" (HistoryLink 23059).
12. Crew titles: contemporary P-I/Times list "Bob Rataczak, first officer; H. E. [or H. N.]
    Anderson, flight engineer"; Wikipedia swaps to "First Officer William 'Bill' J. Rataczak,
    flight engineer Harold E. Anderson" and the FBI NORJAK summary header misprints the flight as
    "#350".
13. When the crew knew he was gone: Scott UPI 11/26 "We first knew that he was not aboard when we
    arrived in Reno" vs the FBI log sequence (last contact 8:05 pm, no reply from ~8:11 on) vs
    HistoryLink's claim the co-pilot spoke to him by intercom after the stair light.
14. Hostage/lockup claims: AP "the hi-jacker locked the remaining four crew members in the
    cockpit" (also Keillor, Northwest spokesman) vs Scott/Rataczak "the flight crew never left the
    cockpit" by choice and Mucklow "Go to the cabin, pull the curtain, and don't come back."
15. Landing-light preference: hijacker "wanted the plane... land in a lighted place" (Henderson/
    Scott radio) then "No, you better turn them down" (Clever) vs P-I A1 "at first insisted that
    the plane be stopped in a brightly lit portion of the airport, but he settled for the isolated
    area" vs later accounts ("lights off") — consistent core, differing details.
16. Early FAA theory that he escaped on the ground: P-I 11/25 "The Federal Aviation Administration
    said the plane stopped long enough as it was taxiing toward the terminal to allow the man to
    escape through an emergency exit" — abandoned within a day ("There's no way he could have got
    off in Reno" — Campbell, AP, same day).
17. Jump-location estimate drift: Amboy/south of Lake Merwin (Mattson, NYT 11/25) → five-mile
    corridor SE of Woodland (P-I 11/26) → prime search area between Woodland/Amboy/Kalama and
    Battle Ground (FBI summary) → Himmelsbach's later revision to the Washougal watershed
    (HistoryLink 1997, LATER-RECOLLECTION/SECONDARY).

## PRIMARY-SOURCE GAPS
- Florence Schaffner's and Tina Mucklow's own FD-302s (likely in Vault Parts 19/59/66) were not
  individually opened; their accounts here come from the NORJAK summary, the crew press conference
  (Pioneer-Press 11/27) and UPI/Seattle Times 11/26.
- The 2:50 pm PST Portland departure time was not found verbatim in any fetched primary/contemporary
  source (only in SECONDARY accounts); Henderson's "scheduled to touch down at Seattle-Tacoma
  Airport at 3:21 p.m." is the closest contemporary anchor.
- The precise Reno touchdown minute (11:02 vs 11:07 pm) is inferred (Seattle Times "3½ hours" from
  7:37 pm) — no fetched source states it outright except SECONDARY Wikipedia (11:02 pm).
