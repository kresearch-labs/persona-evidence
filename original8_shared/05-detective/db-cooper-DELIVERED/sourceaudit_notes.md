## Claim 1 — 'D.B. Cooper' vs 'Dan Cooper': origin of 'D.B.'
**Earliest traceable source: the UPI Portland wire story of Nov 24, 1971, filed by reporter Clyde Jabin (built in part on a story by James Long of The Oregon Journal).**
- FBI's own page (fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/db-cooper-hijacking, dated 2016-05-18, fetched 2026-10-19): "Where did “D.B.” come from? It was apparently a myth created by the press. We did question a man with the initials “D.B.” but he wasn’t the hijacker." — i.e., the FBI itself attributes the name to the press and confirms an early "D.B."-initials person was investigated. This FBI sentence is the root of the "early suspect named D.B." branch of the story.
- CJR, William Browning, "A reporter's role in the notorious unsolved mystery of 'D.B. Cooper'", July 18, 2016 (fetched): traces the name to Clyde Jabin, UPI Portland. Verbatim: "It can be traced directly to Clyde Jabin, the UPI reporter hustling inside his bureau’s windowless, fourth-floor office at 1320 S.W. Broadway in Portland." Two versions of how Jabin got the initials: (a) coworkers' version — FBI agent on phone said "D. Cooper," Jabin asked "D as in dog or B as in boy?", agent replied "Yes"/"That's right"; (b) widow Joan Jabin's version — the agent himself said "D.B. Cooper", paralleling Himmelsbach's 1986 NORJAK account that agents were working a Portland man with initials D.B. and someone relayed the initials to UPI before he was dismissed. Verbatim from CJR: "Himmelsbach wrote that on the night of the hijacking, agents began investigating a Portland man whose initials happened to be D.B. Authorities later dismissed the man as a suspect but, before they did, someone relayed the initials to UPI, which ran with it."
- CJR follow-up, July 22, 2016 (fetched): James Long, then of The Oregon Journal (shared building/floor with UPI), emailed CJR "It was my mistake," claiming he phoned Northwest PR (via former colleague Duane Youngbar's office), got a muddled storm-degraded answer, "wound up with 'D.B. Cooper'", and a UPI reporter (Jabin) took his carbon for the wire story. Note tension: Long's story and the Jabin-family/Himmelsbach FBI-suspect story are COMPETING explanations; CJR never adjudicated ("The truth will never be known").
- OregonLive Dec 2023 "Meet the reporter who put the D.B. in D.B. Cooper" (SNIPPET-ONLY, search result): repeats Long's account ("Somehow I came up with D.B. ... It went out on the UPI wire and went worldwide.").
- The 'D.B. Williams' hypothesis (Portland man named D.B. Williams questioned early) appears in Himmelsbach-derived retellings; CJR (2016-07-18) reports Himmelsbach's version only as "a Portland man whose initials happened to be D.B." — no name given in any fetched page. The name "D.B. Williams" is therefore UNVERIFIED in fetched sources (label: needs primary check; possibly from NORJAK book itself, inaccessible).
- Wikipedia (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._B._Cooper, fetched 2026-10-19): "Because of a reporter's error, the aircraft hijacker became known as D. B. Cooper." CJR noted Wikipedia's entry credits "either Jabin or an Associated Press reporter named Joe Frazier" — CJR's sources (Lerten, Ulrich) disagreed with the Frazier attribution; Frazier joined AP Portland only in 1972 per a website about his book.
**Independence verdict:** the name-origin story has TWO competing single-witness lineages (Jabin camp via coworkers vs Long's self-confession), both converging on the same UPI wire story. Nearly all modern retellings ("myth created by the press" — FBI; "reporter's error" — Wikipedia) depend on ONE wire story as original source. Himmelsbach's "Portland man with initials D.B." version is a second, possibly compatible origin. FBI's own phrasing ("myth created by the press") is itself a secondary characterization that CJR disputes.

## Claim 2 — 'Bourbon and soda' drink order
**Earliest traceable source: FBI agent interviews with flight attendants Florence Schaffner and Tina Mucklow, Nov 24-25, 1971 (FBI case file 164-2111).**
- FBI page (fetched): "He ordered a drink—bourbon and soda—while the flight was waiting to take off."
- Wikipedia (fetched, Bourbon section): "Cooper took seat 18-E in the last row and ordered a drink, a bourbon and 7-Up, from a flight attendant." — NOTE the variant "bourbon and 7-Up" and the note that per Wikipedia's Bourbon section some accounts say bourbon and water (dirkdeklein.net blog copy of Wikipedia: "second bourbon and water").
- NORJAK chronology PDF (norjak.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/norjak-chronology.pdf, fetched): the excerpt fetched does not contain the drink order line; drink detail deferred to full-file check (FBI Vault). Schaffner's own later interviews are secondary retellings.
- Retelling variants in circulation: "bourbon and soda" (FBI, most outlets), "bourbon and 7-Up" (Wikipedia citing an account), "bourbon and water" (second-drink variant). The original FBI 302s appear to record bourbon and soda per FBI's own modern page; the 7-Up variant traces to one account of Schaffner's recollection (per Wikipedia's cited sources — not independently verified here).
**Independence verdict:** all public accounts funnel back to the two flight attendants' FBI statements. "Bourbon and soda" is well supported but the drink-mix detail differs across retellings; the Christiansen suspect narrative leans on "fondness for bourbon" — dependent on the same witness recollection plus suspect-specific anecdotes.

## Claim 3 — Seat 18C vs 18E
**Earliest traceable source: FBI interviews.** Gate agent Hal Williams/FBI interrogation per NORJAK chronology: "sits in the last row on the starboard side of the aircraft in seat 18E" (norjak.org chronology PDF, fetched). Wikipedia (fetched): "took seat 18C[4] (18E by one account, 15D by another)". Crime Museum page (SNIPPET-ONLY): "He was assigned an aisle seat, 18C". WaPo Jan 16, 2017 (SNIPPET-ONLY): "a clip-on necktie that Cooper left on his seat — 18E". McCrone case study (fetched SNIPPET): "The FBI recovered few pieces of physical evidence from seat 18-E".
**Contradiction:** 18C (aisle) vs 18E (window/middle-right) persists in modern outlets; the FBI-document-derived chronology and tie evidence both say 18E. 18C likely arose from conflating "third seat from aisle" or from Schaffner's jump-seat orientation retellings. Verdict: evidence-based answer is 18E; 18C is a popular retelling error that survives in otherwise-careless sources (e.g., historicflix.com, crimemuseum.org).

## Claim 4 — Jump altitude ~10,000 ft; drop zone near Ariel/Lake Merwin; later recalculations
**Earliest traceable source: the flight crew's reports of the planned flight profile (10,000 ft, gear down, 15 deg flaps, ~170 kt) recorded in NWA teletypes and FBI interviews on the night of 11/24/1971; FBI wind-drift calculation by FBI technician/agent (Bohan's data per Wikipedia).**
- Wikipedia (fetched): "Based on Bohan's data and subsequent recalculations of the flight path, the FBI determined Cooper's drop zone was probably over the Washougal River watershed. In 1986, FBI Agent Ralph Himmelsbach wrote, 'I have to confess, if I were going to look for Cooper... I would head for the Washougal.'"
- Initial public landing-zone belief (Ariel / Lake Merwin area, southernmost reach of Mt St Helens) per Wikipedia: "Initial extrapolations placed Cooper's landing zone within an area on the southernmost outreach of Mount St. Helens, a few miles southeast of Ariel, Washington, near Lake Merwin."
- Later re-derivation chain: (a) Himmelsbach 1986 (Washougal); (b) FBI 2009 — Kaye/Citizen Sleuths to retrace flight plan "to determine more exact coordinates for Cooper's landing zone" (archives.fbi.gov March 2009, fetched); (c) Citizen Sleuths' later work and Thomas Colbert camp pushing Tena Bar-adjacent theories. The widely repeated "jumped near Ariel" line in modern media is a fossil of the 1971-era initial extrapolation that the FBI itself moved away from (Washougal watershed) — retellings lag the primary record.
**Independence verdict:** 10,000 ft comes from crew/flight records (independent, multiple cockpit witnesses). The Ariel claim's popularity exceeds its support: primary-based recalculations (Bohan → Washougal; Himmelsbach quote) already contradicted it by the 1980s, yet "Ariel" dominates popular accounts and even town identity (Ariel store/museum).

## Claim 6 — 1980 Tena Bar find
**Earliest traceable sources: Feb 1980 contemporaneous press + FBI confirmation; HistoryLink essay #23059 (fetched via search snippet page — actually fetched page content came through search result; label as fetched-via-search text unless re-fetched).**
- HistoryLink (historylink.org/file/23059): "On February 10, 1980, 8-year-old Brian Ingram ... comes across three deteriorating packs of $20 bills still bound by rubber bands." "The FBI confirmed the money was from ransom packets given to Cooper: two packets of 100 $20 bills and a third packet of 90, arranged in the same order as delivered in 1971." Total $5,800. - 'TV Guide found 2017 by Colbert team': NOT yet verified from any fetched source — searches returned noise. Treat as unverified; re-search with different phrasing (Colbert dive crews; 'Ingram returned to Tena Bar 2017'?).
**Independence verdict:** the core find is strongly sourced (FBI serial-number match + multiple 1980 press + participant accounts). The upstream interpretation (how money got to Tena Bar) is contested between FBI-era 'washed downstream' theory (Himmelsbach quote in HistoryLink) and later sleuth theories; the find itself does not validate any suspect narrative.

## Claim 7 (continued) — Physical-evidence destruction / 'parachute items destroyed' story
The destruction story in circulation is really THREE separable evidence-loss events, frequently conflated in retellings:
1. **Cigarette butts destroyed by FBI Las Vegas field office.** Wikipedia (fetched 2026-10-19): "In 1998, the FBI sought to extract DNA from the cigarette butts, but discovered the butts had been destroyed while in the custody of the Las Vegas field office." Geoffrey Gray, Daily Beast (fetched via search text): eight Raleigh filter-tip butts sent to Quantico 1971, "sent off ... Now they appear to be missing"; FBI Seattle rep Ayn Dietrich: "We think they are in Las Vegas." NY Mag Intelligencer (Jul 2011, fetched via search text) — same account, sourced to Carr. ROOT: FBI case files + Daily Beast/NYMag reporting; Reddit/forums embellish ("intentionally destroyed... memo gave permission" — labeled FORUM CLAIM, unverified vs FBI Vault).
2. **Hair samples.** Wikipedia (fetched): "The limb hair was destroyed after the FBI Crime Laboratory determined the sample lacked enough unique microscopic characteristics to be useful; ... During their attempts to build Cooper's DNA profile in 2002, the FBI discovered the hair sample had been lost."
3. **Parachutes NOT destroyed** — this is a common misconception to flag. FBI pages (both 2007 Redux and 2016 case page, fetched): two of the four parachutes were recovered in Reno and photographed; the unused main chute (NB6) is on display at the Washington State Historical Society Museum (Wikipedia). The 1976 Seattle Times feature (cooper.theunsolved.net FD-350 clipping, fetched): "Cossey said his remaining chute, left on the floor of the plane, was returned to him and is now a treasured souvenir." So the accurate chain is: butts destroyed (Las Vegas, pre-1998), limb hair destroyed (FBI lab), head hair lost by 2002, tie survives ("Of this material evidence, Cooper's necktie is the only item that has not been destroyed." — McCrone case study page, fetched). 'Parachute items destroyed in the 1970s' as commonly stated is NOT supported by fetched sources; what the 1970s-era FBI did was return a chute to Cossey and later lose/destroy biological samples. Author Bruce Smith (HBO doc, OregonLive review fetched) says "the disappearance of physical evidence that years later might have identified the skyjacker through DNA" — consistent with the above.
**Independence verdict:** the destruction narrative rests on FBI statements + Gray's file-based reporting; it is well sourced but routinely garbled (parachutes vs butts vs hair) in popular retellings.

## Claim 4 addendum — the later radar / flight-path re-derivations
- **Bohan data → Washougal recalculation (FBI, by 1986):** Wikipedia (fetched): "Based on Bohan's data and subsequent recalculations of the flight path, the FBI determined Cooper's drop zone was probably over the Washougal River watershed. In 1986, FBI Agent Ralph Himmelsbach wrote, 'I have to confess, if I were going to look for Cooper... I would head for the Washougal.'"
- **Seattle Times Nov 17, 1996 (archive.seattletimes.com/archive/19961117/2360262, fetched):** pilot came to Himmelsbach "on his last day" and it "learned for the first time that the pilot ... was traveling farther east than authorities had realized. That meant the actual drop zone was probably south-southeast of the area that was searched." Also: "Authorities estimated he landed near the small community of Ariel, east of Woodland and one ridge line over from the Washougal watershed." And: "hydrologists concluded the tattered and still-bundled money was more likely deposited by a stream flow than human hands."
- **FBI 2007 Redux (fbi.gov/news/stories/2007/december/dbcooper_123107, fetched):** Carr's hydrologist idea: "Maybe a hydrologist can use the latest technology to trace the $5,800 in ransom money found in 1980 to where Cooper landed upstream."
- **Kaye/Citizen Sleuths 2011 Symposium (The Mountain News — WA, themountainnewswa.net, July 31 2020 article by R.M. Blevins, fetched via search text):** Kaye "examined 100 percent coverage of the primary landing zones" using "an amalgam of radar transcripts from McChord Air Base ... and the civilian readings from the FAA's Seattle Center"; declared "There were no 'death woods' in 1971"; "There was no snow"; cloud layers at 3,000/5,000 ft meant nobody saw the jump. Kaye "did not display any of the original data."
- **Blevins (Mountain News 2021, SNIPPET):** claims FBI "had lost confidence in their original LZ-A of Amboy, and determined the jump zone was ten miles further south, in the area of Hockinson, Washington" — SINGLE-SOURCE (Blevins), a Cooper researcher; treat as researcher claim, not established.
**Verdict:** every public 'Ariel' framing is the 1971-era initial extrapolation; the primary-record trajectory (Bohan → Washougal → Hockinson per Blevins) moves the zone away from Ariel, and the physical money at Tena Bar is consistent with any downstream/river theory, not with Ariel specifically. Kaye's radar re-examination is the best-documented third-party re-derivation but its underlying data was never published.

## TOP 10 OVER-CITED WEAK CLAIMS (final)
1. 'The hijacker's name was D.B. Cooper' — actually Dan Cooper; 'D.B.' = one wire-story error (Jabin/Long), fossilized by repetition.
2. 'He jumped near Ariel/Lake Merwin' — 1971 initial extrapolation, superseded within the FBI itself (Washougal/Hockinson); still printed as fact.
3. 'Partial DNA profile' presented as a robust identifier — never published; three samples on tie; FBI says inconclusive; also chronically mis-dated (2001/2007/2011/2017).
4. '800+ suspects, all but two dozen eliminated' — FBI's own single-source stat (and a competing 'almost 10,000' figure circulates from a 1996 Seattle Times piece).
5. Titanium/rare-earth tie particles → 'Cooper was an engineer/manager in a titanium or chemical plant' — one team's (Kaye) inference; co-published with McCrone; zero independent replication; conclusion shifted between 2017 (aerospace/chemical) and later (CRT plant) tellings.
6. 'The money was buried at Tena Bar, proving a Columbia-River landing' (Colbert) vs 'money floated downstream' (Himmelsbach/FBI hydrologists) — both interpretations of ONE find; neither established; media present Colbert's version as discovery.
7. Rackstraw's CIA-protected-superspy frame — sourced wholly to Colbert/Case Breakers FOIA readings; cryptology experts called the supporting 'decoding' arbitrary; no FBI corroboration ever issued.
8. Reca's '3+ hours of taped confession' + 'spent documents' — publisher press-release evidence; unverified; several details diverge from FBI record (OWNER5).
9. Christiansen 'closest ever suspect' — rests on one brother's letter campaign; physical description (height/eyes/weight) contradicts it per FBI and Himmelsbach; 'good suspect' quote is Himmelsbach's hedged speculation.
10. 'Cooper was an experienced skydiver/paratrooper' — FBI formally withdrew this theory in Dec 2007 (Wikipedia: FBI "withdrew its previous theory that Cooper was either an experienced skydiver or paratrooper"), yet suspect narratives (Reca, Christiansen, Rackstraw, McCoy) still build on jumper expertise.

## Verification addendum (final pass)
FBI 'D.B. Cooper Redux' (fbi.gov/news/stories/2007/december/dbcooper_123107, dated 12/31/07, FETCHED IN FULL 2026-10-19) — verbatim:
- "Duane Weber, who claimed to be Cooper on his deathbed, was ruled out by DNA testing (we lifted a DNA sample from Cooper’s tie in 2001). Kenneth Christiansen, named in a recent magazine article, didn’t match the physical description and was a skilled paratrooper. Richard McCoy, who died in 1974, also didn’t match the description and was at home the day after the hijacking having Thanksgiving dinner with his family in Utah, an unlikely scenario unless he had help."
- "Maybe a hydrologist can use the latest technology to trace the $5,800 in ransom money found in 1980 to where Cooper landed upstream." (Agent Larry Carr)
- Carr: "Diving into the wilderness without a plan, without the right equipment, in such terrible conditions, he probably never even got his chute open."
This is now the anchor primary source for: DNA sample lifted 2001; Weber ruled out by DNA; Christiansen/McCoy description mismatches; FBI's hydrology framing of the Tena Bar money.
