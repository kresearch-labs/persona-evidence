# D.B. Cooper — Principal Named Suspects: Source-Inspected Notes
Compiled 2026-10-19. Rules followed: every quoted sentence was copied verbatim from a fetched page (web_extract or direct HTTP fetch); search snippets are labeled SNIPPET-ONLY. Case context verified from FBI's own case page: hijacking of Northwest Orient Flight 305, Nov 24, 1971; $200,000 ransom; four parachutes; jumped from aft stairs; FBI closed active investigation July 2016.

## Case-level official facts (primary FBI pages, fetched)
- FBI famous-cases page (https://www.fbi.gov/history/cases-and-criminals/db-cooper-hijacking, page notes a July 12, 2016 update):
  - "By the five-year anniversary of the hijacking, we'd considered more than 800 suspects and eliminated all but two dozen from consideration."
  - On McCoy: "One person from our list, Richard Floyd McCoy, is still a favorite suspect among many. We tracked down and arrested McCoy for a similar airplane hijacking and escape by parachute less than five months after Cooper's flight. But McCoy was later ruled out because he didn't match the nearly identical physical descriptions of Cooper provided by two flight attendants and for other reasons."
  - "Where did 'D.B.' come from? It was apparently a myth created by the press. We did question a man with the initials 'D.B.' but he wasn't the hijacker."
- FBI Seattle press release (https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/seattle/news/press-releases/update-on-investigation-of-1971-hijacking-by-d.b.-cooper), 2016 case closure: "In order to solve a case, the FBI must prove culpability beyond a reasonable doubt, and, unfortunately, none of the well-meaning tips or applications of new investigative technology have yielded the necessary proof." Also: "should specific physical evidence emerge—related specifically to the parachutes or the money taken by the hijacker—individuals with those materials are asked to contact their local FBI field office."

## 1) Richard Floyd McCoy Jr. (Richard Floyd McCoy II), 1942–1974
- Proposed by / when: FBI agents suspected him at his April 1972 arrest; publicly revived by Bernie Rhodes (chief probation officer) and Russell Calame (former FBI agent who headed the McCoy investigation) in "D.B. Cooper: The Real McCoy" (University of Utah Press, 1991). [The 1989 UPI/LA Times advance coverage quotes the working title "Beyond a Reasonable Doubt (D.B. Cooper No Longer a Mystery)".]
- Basis: near-identical MO copycat hijacking. LA Times (UPI), Dec 3, 1989 (https://latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1989-12-03-mn-444-story.html), verbatim:
  - "On April 7, 1972, McCoy hijacked a United Airlines flight from Denver to Los Angeles. He bailed out near his hometown of Provo with $500,000 cash. But he was caught a few days later and sentenced to 45 years in prison."
  - "Even the notes were similar. Both contained the phrase 'no funny stuff.'"
  - "At the time of his arrest, FBI agents suspected that McCoy might be Cooper, but eventually ruled him out as a suspect."
  - "Most significant of all, they say, is that members of McCoy's family identified an object, never publicly identified, left behind by Cooper on the Northwest Orient plane." [per Wikipedia: the tie and mother-of-pearl tie clip]
- Arrest/conviction/corroboration: United Flight 855 (Denver stopover, LA-bound 727), alias "James Johnson", $500,000, paperweight grenade + unloaded pistol, left fingerprints on a magazine; arrested April 9, 1972; $499,970 found in his house (Wikipedia, Richard McCoy Jr., citing FBI famous-cases page). NYT Apr 10, 1972 p.1 headline "Skydiver Held as Hijacker; $500,000 Is Still Missing" (subscriber archive stub — SNIPPET-ONLY).
- Supporting evidence: identical demand structure (four parachutes, aft-stairs 727 escape, note with bomb claim), family ID of the tie/clip, his refusal to confirm or deny (UPI: "Rhodes, who conducted pre-sentence interviews with McCoy, said the skyjacker refused to confirm or deny that he was Cooper."), and the arresting agent's quote (via Wikipedia/Crime Library): "When I shot Richard McCoy, I shot D. B. Cooper at the same time."
- Contradicting evidence (official elimination): FBI famous-cases page (above) — ruled out on the two flight attendants' "nearly identical physical descriptions" plus "other reasons." Wikipedia (D.B. Cooper, citing Himmelsbach & Worcester 1986 p.82 and FBI Vault Part 52 p.192): "the FBI does not consider him a suspect in the Cooper case because of mismatches in age and description; McCoy was 29 years old, with projecting ears." Also (same section, citing Rhodes & Calame 1991): witnesses put Cooper in his mid-40s; "dark brown, piercing" eyes vs McCoy's light blue; McCoy's protruding ears (nickname "Dumbo"); observant Mormon who didn't smoke or drink vs bourbon-and-chain-smoking Cooper; southern accent and lisp (cleft-palate surgery).
- Verified whereabouts Nov 24, 1971: Wikipedia McCoy section, citing CNN (Aug 1, 2011) and Seattle Times (Jan 1, 2008): "credible evidence that McCoy was in Las Vegas on the day of the Portland hijacking, and at home in Utah the day after, having Thanksgiving dinner with his family." FBI via CNN (https://www.cnn.com/2011/CRIME/08/01/fbi.db.cooper/index.html), verbatim: "A third, Richard McCoy, who died in 1974, also did not match the description and was at home the day after the hijacking having Thanksgiving dinner with his family in Utah -- 'an unlikely scenario unless he had help,' the agency said." (Basis: family alibi + FBI file; not an independently corroborated document trail in the open record.)
- Witness ID attempts: per Wikipedia (citing FBI Vault Part 24, pp. 306, 317): "the three stewardesses who interacted with Cooper were shown photographs of McCoy and all agreed that he was not their hijacker... McCoy's photo was also shown to the ticket agent who sold Cooper his ticket, the gate agent and Bill Mitchell, the passenger seated closest to Cooper, and they too concluded that McCoy and Cooper were not the same." Limitation: photo-lineup identifications made 1972, ~4 months after the fact; no primary scans inspected directly (FBI Vault scans not text-accessible).
- Death: Nov 9, 1974, shot by FBI agents at Virginia Beach after escaping Lewisburg (Aug 1974) — Wikipedia McCoy page: "News reports stated that on November 9, 1974, McCoy walked into his home and was met by FBI agents Nick O'Hara, Kevin McPartland, and Gerald Houlihan; he fired at them, and all agents opened fire, killing McCoy."
- Residual official interest: 2020 FOIA documents — Wikipedia (citing FBI Vault Part 52, p.192, memo of July 29, 2004): "Discreetly locate and obtain DNA samples from the family or effects of Richard Floyd McCoy" — i.e., as late as 2004 the FBI had not fully cleared him internally.
- 2024 development: McCoy's two children publicly claimed their father was Cooper after YouTuber Dan Gryder found a parachute on the family's former property; "Gryder claims to have handed this parachute over to the FBI, though the FBI has not confirmed this" (Wikipedia, citing Guardian Nov 30, 2024 / Independent Nov 28, 2024 — Guardian/Independent pages not fetched; SNIPPET-ONLY).
- Independence of evidence: HIGH among suspect claims — proposed by the former FBI agent who ran the McCoy case (Calame) plus a probation officer with pre-sentence access; FBI's elimination is documented on FBI's own site and (per Vault citations) in case files. But the pro-Cooper book itself is a two-author narrative; McCoy never confessed.
- Strongest falsifier: the flight attendants' and other witnesses' photo exclusions plus the age/eyes/ears/smoking mismatch; the family alibi for Nov 25, 1971.
- Remaining uncertainty: the 2004 memo shows FBI kept him on a short list; DNA from McCoy family was reportedly sought but no result is public.

## 2) Kenneth Christiansen, 1926–1994
- Proposed by / when: his brother Lyle Christiansen (Morris, Minnesota) in 2003, after watching an "Unsolved Mysteries" episode; wrote the FBI (Nov 2003, Jan 2004), tried to reach Nora Ephron, then hired PI Skipp Porteous (Sherlock Investigations, NYC) in 2007. Public profile: Geoffrey Gray, "Unmasking D.B. Cooper," New York Magazine, Oct 18, 2007 (https://nymag.com/news/features/39593/ — fetched). Porteous & Blevins book "Into the Blast – The True Story of D.B. Cooper" (Adventure Books of Seattle, 2010); History channel "Brad Meltzer's Decoded" S1E6 (2011).
- NOTE on the prompt's "proposed by Robert (Bob) Broussard": no fetched source mentions a Broussard proposal; the documented chain is Lyle Christiansen → Skipp Porteous → (2010 book). Treat "Broussard" as unverified.
- NY Mag 2007, verbatim (Lyle's account): "He was watching TV one night, he told them, and flipped on the show Unsolved Mysteries, which had an episode about the Cooper case. 'I sat up in my chair,' he wrote, 'because my brother was a dead ringer to the composite sketch of D.B.'"; and "Yes, I knew the culprit personally," Lyle wrote one day. "He was my brother."
- Support: NWA employee from 1954 (flight attendant then purser, based in Seattle); Army paratrooper training 1944 (WWII ended before combat deployment; training jumps in occupied Japan); smoked and drank bourbon; age 45 in 1971; bought a Bonney Lake, WA home ~7 months post-hijacking; Florence Schaffner told Gray his photos fit her memory best of those shown (but "could not conclusively identify him" — Wikipedia, citing Gray 2011, "Skyjack", pp. 180–190).
- Contradicting evidence: too short and slight (5'8", 150 lbs vs witnesses' 5'10"–6'0", 170–180 lbs). CNN, Aug 1, 2011, verbatim: "Another man, Kenneth Christiansen, did not match the physical description and was a skilled paratrooper." Seattle PI, Oct 26, 2007 (archived copy fetched: https://web.archive.org/web/20200605110108/https://www.seattlepi.com/local/article/FBI-rejects-latest-D-B-Cooper-suspect-1253715.php), verbatim: "A man identified in a recent magazine article as possibly having been the infamous plane hijacker D.B. Cooper was almost certainly not him, the FBI said Friday."
- The famous "paid cash for a house" claim was retracted by the proponents themselves: Adventure Books (Robert M. Blevins), Mar 21, 2018 (https://thedbcooperhijacking.wordpress.com/2018/03/21/... — fetched), verbatim: "The answer is: NO, he did NOT." and "He presented a document showing that Christiansen financed approximately half the purchase price by assuming a mortgage through Seattle First National Bank, and took almost nineteen years to pay it off. This investigator was right, and we were wrong."
- Official status: FBI rejected him as a prime suspect (2007 press statement; reiterated 2011 on CNN). No evidence FBI ran a dedicated file beyond tip review surfaced; per Wikipedia, FBI cites "the poor match to eyewitness physical descriptions and a complete absence of direct incriminating evidence."
- Independence of evidence: LOW-to-MEDIUM — the theory originates with one brother's hunch and was developed by a PI and a small publishing outfit (Adventure Books) for a book; the only semi-independent data point is Schaffner's qualified photo preference. (Correction to the task brief: his purchase of the home was NOT cash.)
- Strongest falsifier: the physical-description mismatch plus the documented mortgage (no unexplained wealth).
- Remaining uncertainty: his whereabouts on Nov 24, 1971 were never established in any fetched source (proponents claim he was with colleague Bernie Geestman that week — unverified).

## 3) Earl Cossey (1941–2013) — not a suspect; the parachute source, later murdered
- Who: Seattle-area skydiving instructor/rigger; the chutes given to Cooper came from an Issaquah skydive center "which had recently purchased them from Cossey" (MyNorthwest, Apr 30, 2013, fetched: https://mynorthwest.com/local/police-want-help-solving-woodinville-murder-linked-to-db-cooper-case/28874). The used chute was "a military-issue NB6, nylon, 28-foot with a conical canopy" (same article).
- Murder: MyNorthwest, verbatim: "Family members found Earl Cossey dead around 5:30pm Friday, April 26 after they were unable to reach him for several days" (found in his Woodinville home; last seen late Monday April 22, 2013; 71 years old).
- Connection claims: none substantiated. Geoffrey Gray (same page), verbatim: "I think that his death is sort of getting dragged in a little bit. I don't see anybody or anyone having a sort of beef with Cossey over the Cooper case." And: "Owner County Sheriff's Sgt. Cindi West said investigators have found no link between the murder and the Cooper case."
- Role in case: Cossey told the FBI the jump was survivable ("he met with the FBI on numerous occasions and basically gave birth to this idea that D.B. Cooper could have survived the jump" — Gray) and vetted later parachute finds ("They keep bringing me garbage," Cossey told the AP in 2008).
- Official status on the murder: no arrest located in searches (Patch: "Cossey Murder: Investigation Continues"); treat as unsolved; no credible claim ties it to NORJAK.

## 4) Robert Rackstraw (1943–2019)
- First entered the case Feb 1978 — FBI's own task force, not Colbert: after arrest in Iran and deportation on explosives/check-kiting charges; faked his own death over Monterey Bay (Wikipedia, citing Mercury News Jul 12, 2016). "Cooper investigators observed his physical resemblance to Cooper composite sketches even though he was only 28 in 1971, military parachute training, and criminal record but eliminated him as a suspect in 1979 after no direct evidence of his involvement could be found" (Wikipedia, citing Eugene Register-Guard, Feb 7, 1979: "Rackstraw Says He's Not Cooper Of Skyjack Fame" — scan not fetched; cited secondhand).
- Modern proposal: filmmaker/author Thomas Colbert (book with Mark Szollosi, 2016; History channel program July 2016; 40-person volunteer team including former federal agents). Code claims: Rick Sherwood (ex-Army Security Agency) "decoded" the 5th letter (Jan 2018) and 6th letter (June 2018) as naming "LT Robert W. Rackstraw."
- History.com (https://www.history.com/articles/db-cooper-case-fbi-letters — fetched), verbatim: "Colbert's team found codes in the fifth and sixth letters, including the numbers '717171684*,' which they deciphered as '[I'm LT Robert W. Rackstraw](...).' Rackstraw, a Vietnam War vet and former U.S. paratrooper who died in 2019, both denied and refused to discount himself as being the infamous skyjacker, according to the Oregonian. The FBI investigated—and cleared—Rackstraw in the late 1970s." And on the 6th letter: "Again, Colbert's team says this letter is coded to say, 'I'm LT Robert W. Rackstraw, D.B. Cooper is not my real name'..."
- FBI response to the letters (History.com, verbatim): FBI spokeswoman Ayn Sandalo Dietrich told the Reno Gazette Journal in 2014 that the letters were sent to the FBI laboratory in Washington, D.C. for analysis, but nothing was found: "It was never proven if the actual hijacker wrote the letters."
- FBI response to the 2016-18 push (Rolling Stone, https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-news/d-b-cooper-investigators-claim-theyve-discovered-skyjackers-identity-693912/ — fetched), verbatim: "A rep for FBI's Seattle field office told Rolling Stone that they have received 'an immense number' of tips over the years, but 'none to date have resulted in a definitive identification of the hijacker.'"
- Rackstraw's own position: told People (July 2016, via Wikipedia): "It's a lot of [expletive], and they know it is." His attorney: "the stupidest thing I've ever heard" (CBS Miami via Wikipedia). In 1979 he told the Register-Guard he was not Cooper. (People.com page not fetched directly — cited via Wikipedia.)
- Evidentiary weakness of the letter theory (Rolling Stone, verbatim): "This letter, however, does not have any fingerprints or watermarks, and the FBI was never able to confirm a genuine connection to the previous Cooper letters, which limits its evidentiary value."
- Witness limitation: one Flight 305 flight attendant "did not find any similarities" between Rackstraw's photos and her memory (People, via Wikipedia). Colbert's accusation of an FBI cover-up: "This has been a cover up, they're stonewalling" (Seattle PI, via Rolling Stone).
- Independence of evidence: MEDIUM — Colbert's team includes former federal agents, but the decoding (Sherwood) came from a man who "served under Sherwood in two classified units" and knew his writing style — a circularity risk; the coded "identification" has no independent validation, and the FBI lab found nothing in the letters.
- Strongest falsifier: the FBI's 1979 clearance for want of any direct evidence, plus the unverifiable authorship of the 6th letter.
- Remaining uncertainty: Rackstraw "denied and refused to discount himself"; his Taunting-letter authorship is unresolved.

## 5) Lynn Doyle "L.D." Cooper (1931–1999)
- Proposed by / when: niece Marla Cooper, made public late July–Aug 3, 2011 (Telegraph, Jul 30, 2011; AJC Aug 3, 2011; ABC News and CNN Aug 3, 2011; NYT Aug 4, 2011). Note: the FBI had already been working the lead for about a year before publicity (CNN, Aug 1, 2011: "For the past year the FBI has been actively working a new lead... a new suspect who has been dead for more than a decade," brought by "a retired law enforcement officer").
- Basis (family recollection): NYT, Aug 4, 2011 (https://nytimes.com/2011/08/04/us/04cooper.html — fetched), verbatim: "She was 8 years old at the time of the skyjacking in 1971, she said, when she overheard L. D. and another uncle speaking at her grandmother's house in Sisters, Ore. 'My two uncles, who I only saw at holiday time, were planning something very mischievous,' she told ABC."; L.D. came home Thankgiving eve "wearing a white T-shirt, and he was bloody and bruised and a mess" — said to be a car accident; in 1995 her dying father said, "Don't you remember he hijacked that airplane?"
- Physical evidence attempts: NYT, verbatim: "Ms. Cooper said she gave the bureau a leather guitar strap belonging to her uncle... Fred Gutt, a special agent in the Seattle office of the F.B.I., said the bureau had tested the strap but could not lift clear fingerprints from it." (Correction to prompt: the guitar strap was a FINGERPRINT attempt in Aug 2011, not a January 2012 DNA test; L.D. Cooper is a leather worker.)
- DNA result: partial DNA from the tie did not match L.D. Cooper's DNA (AP via LA Times, Aug 9, 2011, as reported by NY Mag: https://nymag.com/intelligencer/2011/08/dna_test_negative_db_cooper_su.html — fetched), verbatim from NY Mag: "Special Agent Fred Gutt said the DNA sample found on the tie had come from three different people and was not enough to rule Uncle L.D. out." So: not a match, but not formally excluded either.
- Official status: NYT, verbatim: "So far, officials said, they have not found anything to contradict Ms. Cooper's account, but neither have they found any evidence to confirm it." CNN (Aug 1, 2011), Gutt: "so far there's not a lot that's inconsistent" with the suspect matching D.B. Cooper. No public elimination.
- Contradicting evidence: he was not a skydiver or paratrooper (Wikipedia, citing ABC News Aug 3, 2011); witnesses described an amateur at best.
- Independence of evidence: LOW — a single witness's childhood recollection relayed by family, plus a secondhand deathbed remark from her father. Its semi-independent element: the FBI spent a year on it without finding contradictions — but also without confirmation.
- Strongest falsifier: the DNA non-match on the tie sample (weak sample) and absence of any parachute/skydiving background.
- Remaining uncertainty: the FBI never said "not the guy"; only "cannot confirm."

## 6) Walter R. Reca (1933–2014)
- Proposed by / when: his friend Carl "Charley" Laurin, made public May 2018 via the book "DB Cooper and Me: A Criminal, a Spy, My Best Friend" (Principia Media, 2018). (Corrections to prompt: the proposer is Laurin, not "Skeeter"; the 2018 publisher is Principia Media; Reca's niece Lisa Story received a confession but the 2018 book is Laurin's; Story's own book, "Better Dead Than Poor: Stories and Letters from Uncle D.B. Cooper," came later.) Wikipedia: "He was proposed as a suspect by his friend Carl Laurin in 2018" (citing Washington Post, May 17, 2018); "In 2008, Reca told Laurin via a recorded telephone call that he was the hijacker" (citing WoodTV, May 17, 2018); Reca gave a notarized letter allowing the story after his death.
- Support: (a) Reca's recorded 2008 confession; (b) witness Jeff Osiadacz, a former cop/dump-truck driver who says he met a soaking-wet man in a black suit near Cle Elum, WA the night of the hijacking and phoned a "Don Brennan" with directions for him (located by the Laurin/Jones team only in 2016 — limitation); (c) endorsement by former Michigan State Police forensic investigator Joe Koenig, who published "Getting The Truth: I Am D.B. Cooper" (2018).
- Against (Mountain News / Bruce A. Smith review of the publisher's claims, https://themountainnewswa.net/... — Principia interview fetched via principiamedia.com), verbatim: "Simply, the inconsistencies challenging Reca-as-Cooper are monumental, yet Jones clings steadfastly to his belief"; "Nor can they shed any light on some of the mysterious elements of the skyjacking"; and the Washington Post point: "Isn't it true that Carl told the Washington Post that Walter Reca didn't know he could jump using the aftstairs, and that he tried going out a side door?" Publisher Vern Jones's reply, verbatim: "You have to remember, this is a memoir. This is Carl's story." Cle Elum is ~150 miles from the assumed drop zone and far from Tina Bar; Reca was an expert jumper (contradicting the amateur profile); he did not resemble the composite; Reca's speech (d/th substitution) differs from the hijacker's Minnesota-style vowels described by Mucklow.
- Official status: FBI declined to evaluate publicly — "the FBI said that it would be inappropriate to comment on specific tips provided to them, and that no evidence to date had proved the culpability of any suspect beyond a reasonable doubt" (OWNER5, via Wikipedia).
- Independence of evidence: LOW — everything flows from Laurin's recordings, his book, and his publisher; the one independent witness (Osiadacz) never connected the encounter to Cooper until the team found him in 2016.
- Strongest falsifier: the flight-path impossibility of the Cle Elum claim and the absence of ransom money/parachute.
- Remaining uncertainty: Osiadacz's encounter is real but unexplained; it proves nothing about Reca's guilt.

## 7) Duane L. Weber (1924–1995)
- Proposed by / when: widow Jo Weber, after his deathbed statement (1995); she went public ~1997-2000 (UPI, Aug 1, 2000; CBS, Aug 22, 2000; NPR, Aug 19, 2000).
- Basis: CBS News, Aug 22, 2000 (https://www.cbsnews.com/news/d-b-cooper-found-at-last/ — fetched), verbatim: "'He says, Come here. Come closer.'... 'He says, I have a secret to tell you.' I said, 'What?' He says, 'I'm Dan Cooper.'" Supporting circumstantials (CBS/UPI, verbatim or near): "Jo recalled the sleep-talking nightmare Duane had about 'leaving finger prints on a plane,' an old knee injury he claimed he got from jumping out of a plane, the local library book on D.B. Cooper with Duane Weber's handwriting in the margins"; UPI adds: "She found a Northwest Seattle-Tacoma ticket she found among his papers in 1994 but never saw again... a remark that he could make a box of flares look like a bomb." A former FBI forensic reconstructionist said "the physical similarities between a composite sketch of Cooper and a younger Duane Weber are striking" (CBS). Jo matched him to the sketch after seeing it (per her account, months after the deathbed statement; the "1997" date in the prompt is plausible but not pinned to a fetched page).
- Independent-ish assessment: Himmelsbach (CBS), verbatim: "He does fit the physical description... He does have the criminal background that I have always felt was associated with the case." — but Himmelsbach "did not believe Weber was Cooper" (Wikipedia, citing CBS).
- Official elimination: FBI eliminated him as an ACTIVE suspect in July 1998 when "his fingerprints did not match any of those processed in the hijacked plane" (Crime Library, via Wikipedia); CNN, Aug 1, 2011, verbatim: "Duane Weber, who claimed on his deathbed to be Cooper, was eliminated by DNA testing, the FBI said." Later, "his DNA also failed to match the samples recovered from Cooper's tie."
- FBI's own hedge (UPI, Aug 1, 2000 — fetched), Agent Ray Lauer, verbatim: "We can't prove that Duane wasn't D.B. Coope,r but we can't prove that he was. We have had 1,100 serious suspects. By serious, I mean there was enough evidence to open a file."
- Independence of evidence: LOW — a widow's narrative built from deathbed words and circumstantial items; the fingerprint and DNA eliminations are official but rest on partial samples.
- Strongest falsifier: fingerprint elimination (July 1998) plus tie DNA non-match.
- Remaining uncertainty: the fingerprint comparison's completeness and the sample quality; Jo Weber disputed the sample quality (NY Mag: "despite Jo's shrill frustration about the quality of the sample").

## 8) William "Wolfgang" Gossett (1930–2003)
- Proposed by / when: Spokane attorney Galen Cook, publicly in July 2008 (Standard-Examiner/AP/Deseret News and CBC coverage). Prompt's "2005 claim by son" not found in fetched sources; the sons' claims (Greg and Kirk Gossett) are secondhand recollections that their father told them he was Cooper.
- Basis (Deseret News/AP, Jul 28, 2008, https://www.deseret.com/2008/7/28/20266328/was-d-b-cooper-an-ogden-resident/ — fetched), verbatim: "His son, Greg, still lives in Ogden, where he said his father told him on his 21st birthday that he had hijacked the plane, then revealed to his son two keys to a safety deposit box at a bank in Vancouver, British Columbia, where the money was stored. 'He said that I could never tell anybody until after he died,' Greg Gossett said." Cook: "He had the opportunity, talent and motive to carry out the crime."
- Against: FBI Special Agent Larry Carr (same page), verbatim: "There is not one link to the D.B. Cooper case other than the statements (Gossett) made to someone." Also (National Post via Wikipedia): "The FBI has no direct evidence implicating Gossett and cannot reliably place him in the Pacific north west at the time of the hijacking."
- Official status: no formal public elimination; treated as one of 1,000+ leads. Cook submitted a fingerprint to the FBI Seattle field office (Deseret); no confirmation of any result found.
- Independence of evidence: LOW — one lawyer's decades-long advocacy plus family deathbed-style claims; no physical trace of Gossett in the PNW.
- Strongest falsifier: Carr's statement — nothing ties him to the case beyond his own statements.
- Remaining uncertainty: the claimed Vancouver safe-deposit box was never produced.

## 9) Ted Mayfield (1936–2015)
- Proposed by / when: named to the FBI by "at least six callers the night of the hijacking" (per Bruce A. Smith, Mountain News, Aug 30, 2015 obituary retrospective: "FBI officials have stated that Mayfield was fingered by at least six callers the night of the hijacking, November 24, 1971, but was soon cleared from the suspect list." — blog source, proponent-adjacent: Smith is a Cooper author; treat the six-caller detail as sourced to Smith's reporting of FBI statements). Re-examined in 2006 by Portland investigators Daniel Dvorak and Matt Meyers (same source).
- Basis: Portland-area parachute businessman (Pacific Parachute Center, Sheridan, OR) with criminal record (armed robbery arrest in youth; 1977 stolen-aircraft arrest), parachute expertise, and pre-existing friction with Portland FBI agent Ralph Himmelsbach.
- Support: none independent found; his daughter Gwen's recollection puts him at his desk that night fielding the FBI's arrival: "He told me that the FBI had already called him looking for parachutes... he sounded very casual the whole time. Now, does that sound like someone who had just hijacked an airplane?" (Smith's report, quoted from daughter's account).
- Against: early FBI clearance; Himmelsbach's book called him "most helpful" in the investigation (Smith's article); no ransom artifacts ever tied to him.
- Official status: cleared early (1971-72); never formally re-opened publicly.
- Independence of evidence: LOW — sustained mainly by Cooper-researcher interest, not by any primary record I could fetch.
- Death: Aug 28, 2015, propeller accident at Sheridan, Oregon (Smith report).

## 10) Jack Coffelt (1917–1975)
- Proposed by / when: Coffelt himself, from 1972 (Wikipedia: "In 1972, Coffelt began claiming he was Cooper and attempted through an intermediary – a former cellmate named James Brown – to sell his story to a Hollywood production company").
- Basis: con man/ex-convict who claimed he landed near Mt. Hood, was injured, and lost the money; "He was reportedly in Portland on the day of the hijacking and sustained leg injuries around that time which were consistent with a skydiving mishap" (Wikipedia, citing InsideEdition, Oct 6, 2008 — original page dead, archive link; cited secondhand).
- FBI review (Himmelsbach & Worcester, NORJAK, 1986, pp. 83–84, via Wikipedia): "Coffelt's account was reviewed by the FBI, which concluded that it differed in several details from information that had not been made public and was therefore a fabrication." Also: "Multiple media venues, including the CBS News program 60 Minutes, considered and rejected the story."
- The prompt's "confession in 1971 book 'In the Saddle with Uncle Sam'": UNVERIFIED — no fetched source confirms a 1971 Coffelt book by that title or a written confession in it. Coffelt is documented (Wikipedia) as the purported chauffeur of Robert Todd Lincoln Beckwith; the book-title claim remains SNIPPET-ONLY/unverified. A 1983 Herald Examiner clipping, "Search for 1971 hijacker leads to mysterious ex-con," exists as a scan in the FBI's released file (cooper.theunsolved.net, Part 62 p.214 — scan index only, not text-fetched).
- Independence of evidence: LOW — self-aggrandizing confession relayed after his 1975 death by his cellmate; FBI found it contradicted non-public details.
- Strongest falsifier: the FBI's non-public-detail mismatch finding.
- Note: he was in his mid-50s in 1971, older than the description; photos "bear a resemblance to the composite drawings" (Wikipedia).

## Other named suspects surfaced (brief, from Wikipedia's Suspects section, wikitext fetched)
- Ted Braden (1928–2007): Special Forces/MACVSOG master skydiver; "believed by many within the special forces community... to have been Cooper" (Beeson 2020; Moore 2018); truck driver for Consolidated Freightways (Vancouver, WA) at the time; never a formal public FBI elimination.
- Fred Catalano (1935–2006): TWA purser, name submitted via airlines at FBI request (FBI Vault Part 17 p.69); passenger Robert Gregory picked his photo as strong resemblance; FBI could not place him Nov 22–28; still un-eliminated as of 2004, DNA sought but apparently never obtained (FBI Vault Part 77 p.247).
