VILLISCA WINS ANOTHER GAME
High School Boys Defeat Clarinda
Friday on Latter's Field—Enter­
tained Following Game
About thirty of the high school
yomis people accompanied Coach F.
L. Moore and his high school football
team to Clarinda last Friday after­
noon where the Villisca team conquer­
ed the Page county team in the sec­
ond game by the score of 19 to 7.
Captain Tommie Stallings did not
start the game at Clarinda • Friday,
remaining out of the fray in the first
quarter on account of an injured
knee Max Gourley pjayed. at; his*Jljalf
in that quarter. The points "by' the Vil­
lisca team were made when, Max
Gourley ploughed through for one
touchdown; %when Stallings hurled a
pass to Vernon Selley, who went over
for a touchdown; and when Stallings
ran across the Clarinda goal line for
one touchdown. The latter also kicked
goal following the touchdown. The
Villisca boys report that the game
was a fast and clean one.
Much enthusiasm was generated by
the Clarinda high school young peo­
ple for the game Friday. They had
borrowed a family goat from some of
the citizens in Clarinda and staged a
big parade in which they tried to
make onlookers believe that Clarinda
was about to "get Villisca's goat." The
Villisca team and their supporters
were also in the parade. '
Friday evening after supper the Vil­
lisca young people were guests of the
Clarinda teachers and high school
students at a party given at the high
school building. The locals report
that they were royally entertained at
that time.

morrow to play the team from that
high school. Essex has a strong team
again this( year and a close game is
anticipated. _

Had Made Her Home WRh Her Son,
F. G. Fulton—Was in Her Seven­
ty-Fifth Year
Mrs. Barbara Fulton died Monday
morning of this week at about 7:15
at the home of her son, F. G. Fulton,
near Sciola. She suffered the first
paralytic stroke about eighteen months
ago and Thursday evening became
sick and later was stricken with the
same trouble which resulted in her
"death.
Barbara Morrison was born in
Pennsylvania August 7, 1S43, and at
the time of her death was seventy-
four years, three months and nineteen
days of age. She was married in 1S62
to Mr. Patton in Pennsylvania and re­
moved to Illiniis in 1864. In 1875 they
came to Iowa and settled in Lucas
county. In 1882 they came to Page
county, where they lived for two
years, then went' to Taylor county and
for thirty years resided on the same
farm eight miles southeast of Villis­
ca.
The father and four children have
preceded the mother in death. The
surviving children are two sons: F.
G. Fulton of Sciola and with whom the
deceased had made her home since
the death of Mr. Patton; and H. R.
Fulton of Greenfield, who was here
dUring the last sickness and at the
time.of the death of the mother.
The funeral services will be held
Wednesday at ten o'clock at the F. G.
Fulton home near Sciola. Burial will
be made in the Guss cemetery beside
the husband-and other deceased rela­
tives. Rev. W. E. Overturf of the Bap­
tist church and Rev. E. S. Menoher
of the Methodist church will have
charge of the funeral services.

ENJOYS TRIP TO THE ORIENT
Miss Esme Anderson Arrives in Pe­
king, China—Spent Month at Sea
JAPAN AND HAWAII "GREAT"
Miss Anderson Also Visits American
Legation in Peking and Con­
verses with Americans
The following interesting letter from
Miss Esme Anderson, daughter of Mr.
and Mrs. J. H. Anderson, who sailed
for Peking, August 25th, will be of in­
terest to readers of The Review:
Villisca Review'and Readers:—
You know not how happy I was to
find the "Review" awaiting me in Pe­
king, nor how its weekly visits are
enjoyed.
We experienced a most pleasant and
interesting trip across the Pacific,
reaching Pekin, September 25th—just
one'month from the day we sailed
from San Francisco. One day was
spent in. Honolulu sightseeing while
our boat coaled and took on cargo.
The great "Libby" pineapple fields
were among the places of interest we
visited. The most striking thing about
Honolulu is its cosmopolitan atmos­
phere, Chinese, Japanese, Europeans,
Americans and Hawaiians all doing
business together, riding in the same
street cars, attending the same pic­
ture theatres and public parks and
bathing beaches—a thing I have seen
in no other place to such an extent.
Upon reaching Yokahoma we left
the steamer in order to "do Japan"
by rail and "rickshaw." The mountain
scenery in this small empire is truly
the most beautiful I have seen. How­
ever, the granduer can in no way com­
pare to that of our own "Rockies."
The Japanses people, with their dainty
dress and manners, the houses with
no walls except beautiful screens, the
perfectly kept flower and vegetable
gardens, making "cfazy quilt" patch
work "all-over the mountain sides,
truly make one think of fairy land.
But you are suddenly brought back to
reality by the extremely fishy odors
