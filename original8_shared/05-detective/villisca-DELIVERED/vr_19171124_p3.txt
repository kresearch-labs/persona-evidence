Kelly's Dentist Testified
A new witness Wednesday morning
was Dr. W. W. Ward of Omaha, who
fixed June 12 or 13, 1912, as one of
the dates on which he did some dental
work for Rev. Kelly. Others who tes­
tified during the morning session
Wednesday were W. C. Ratcliff of
Red Oak, Dr. and Mrs. W. J. Ewing
and John Montgomery.
Direct evidence for the defense was
concluded late Wednesday afternoon.
Rev. West, now pastor the Emer­
son Presbyterian church, testified that
Kelly visited at his home in Malvern
when he made the trip to Omaha of
which Dr. Ward had previously testi­
fied, and that he had no luggage at
that time.
Mrs. Kelly, wife of the accused, tes­
tified as she had at the previous trial,
that Rev. Kelly left Macedonia as he
is claimed to have left for Villisca,
that he had no extra clothing; that h e
returned Monday morning about 7
o'clock and came directly to their
home remaining with her there until
that afternoon. She said there were
no blood stains on his clothing, and
that he brought back just the articles
which he had taken with him. Then
she testified about Kelly giving him­
self up when he came to Red Oak last
spring. The cross-examination of
Mrs. Kelly by Attorney General Hav­
ner was long, and while there were
many things she could not remember
she did not vary at all from her orig­
inal statements. She was excused and
later recalled to tell Rev. Kelly's nor­
mal weight, which she gave as 120
pounds.
A. B. Wolfe, Villisca undertaker,
testified regarding the stain on the
Stillinger girl's body, and Dick Weid-
man 1
told of having measured and
weighed Rev. Kelly. His height was
given as five feet, two and one-half
inches, and his weight as 120 pounds.
Judge Sutton Testified
Judge sutton, one of the attorneys
ror the defense, took the stand to tell
of his trip to Chicago to get Kelly at
the time the latter gave himself up af­
ter waiting several hours to be put in
jail. At this point the order of the
United States District Court, at Sioux
Flls, S. D., committing Rev. Kelly to
the federal hospital for insarifc, was
admitted as evidence, but not until af­
ter considerable argument. The tran­
script of the testimony at that hearing
was also offered, but was not admit­
ted. It was about this time that Kelly
told his jailers of his alleged connec­
tion with the crime. (
- The Rebuttal Begun
A s soon as the defense rested the
state began taking testimony in re­
buttal. They offered the minutes of
the coroner's inquest to rebut the evi­
dence of Ed Landers. The record was
not admitted, but the three members
of the coroner's jury testified in per­
son. They said Landers testified at
the inquest that he had seen no one
around the Moore home the night of
the murder. Dr. Lomas, J. Y. Wicker-
sham and W. R. Finlayson composed
the, jury.
H. H. Bryant, Macedonia, was call­

Harding. H e said he was at the tram
