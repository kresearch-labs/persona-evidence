Attorneys Clash
Attorneys engaged in a clash Friday
afternoon of such seriousness that
Judge Wheeler threatened to fine
them if such actions were even ap-
• proximated again. The clash came dur­
ing the cross examination of Samuel
'
• Barnett of Macedonia by Attorney W.
E. Mitchell of Council Bluffs. Barnett
had testified that Kelly had told him
of the Villisca murders in Macedonia
on the morning of June 10, at a time
earlier than their discovery in Villis
ca. During his questioning by Mitchell
Barnett was not sure of his ground
, and at times contradicted former
points in his evidence. He was unable
to reconcile certain events and be­
came so confused that attorneys for
the state broke into the cross exami­
nation in an attempt to help their wit­
ness. Attorney Mitchell resented this
and said so in no uncertain terms.
The talk became heated, Attorney
General Havner, Attorney Shirley Gil-
lilland of Glenwood and Attorney J. J.
Hess of Council Bluffs taking up the
side of the state. Then the attorney
general and the Glenwood lawyer
dropped out and the two Council
Bluffs attorneys hurled defies across
the table until Judge Wheeler made
his gavel heard above the loud words
and threatened a fine.
But one witness other than Barnett
was on the stand during the afternoon
Friday. He was J. A. Fritch of Mace­
donia, implement dealer, who testified
that Kelly related details of the Moore
crime to him early on the morning
following the murder, while in Mace­
donia.
F. M. McGlothen, court reporter
at the first trial, Friday afternoon read
the transcript of the testimony of Mr.
and Mrs. William Simons, who swore
that they heard Kelly tell of the Vil­
lisca crime while on the early morn­
ing train from Sidney to Carson on a
morning early in June. Other evidence
was introduced to show that the
Simons' made this trip on June 10,
the day following the murder.

A HANDSOME PROFIT
Last week J. L. Himiller sold his
splendid < farm of eighty acres south­
east of Villisca to A. F. Davie of this
city for $225 the acre. Mr. Himiller
bought this place thirty-six years ago
for $37.50 an acre and at that time he
states that it was the highest priced
farm in the whole country. However,
each year that he owned the place it
increased in value $5.21 the acre, the
farm making him a yearly profit, in
enhanced value alone, of over $400.
Mr. Himiller is undecided as to his
future location, but states that he and
Mrs. Himiller are going to take some
time off for a good rest from the daily
grind.
