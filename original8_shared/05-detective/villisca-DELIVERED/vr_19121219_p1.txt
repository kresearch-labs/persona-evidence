Rabies is a disease very often mis­
understood, but its importance can
never be overestimated. Dogs af­
flicted do not as a rule froth at the
mouth, unless it be very hot weath­
er. They are not prone to attack per­
sons or animals unless they come di­
rectly in their way They do not ab­
hor water, but on 1
he contrary crave
it above all else in the world, but a
sort of paralysis of the throat makes
it impossible for them to drink, and
the sight of water, for this reason,
often makes them vicious. They will
refuse their food, and in the early
stage of the disease are inclined to
mope by themselves, slinking away
from their best friends. After a few
days, they stray off, snapping at ev­
erything in their path. The disease
is contracted from their bite, the sa­
liva being very poisonous. The dis­
ease usually develops in from nine
days to six months, although cases
have been known where it took two
years for the infection to show up.
If your dog seems inclined to keep to
himself, refuses his food and water,
put a muzzle on him and tie him up..
If he later attempts to snap or bite,
kill him. There is no cure for rabies
