### [SECONDARY-ENH, AUTHOR IS FOREMOST HISTORIAN] "The Odyssey of the Axe" — Dr. Edgar Epperly, villiscahistory.wordpress.com, Apr 3, 2017 (fetched)

AXE CHAIN OF CUSTODY (fully documented, with Epperly's own source notes):
- Found in Stillinger bedroom: "Town Marshal Hank Horton, first on the scene was quite clear in his grand jury testimony that he found the axe in the Stillingers' bedroom. (1917 Grand Jury investigation, pp. 18-19.)"
- Scene contamination of weapon position: "Several people who traipsed through the house before the scene was sealed by the police reported seeing the axe in various locations... explained by ghoulish spectators moving it from room to room."
- Bloodhounds: "when the bloodhounds arrived at the murder scene about 9:00 p.m., they were given the axe to pick up the scent of the killer. (Red Oak Sun newspaper, June 14, 1912.)"

- 1923–1930: Sheriff Arthur Baker gave axe to James E. Risden (ex-BCI head); Des Moines Sunday Register Jan 28, 1945 photo of Risden with axe.
- Oct 1961: Don Brown obtained axe from widow Risden (notarized statement Dec 28, 1961); display in Brown's Leon IA bookshop; to Epperly mid-1970s; returned to Villisca Heritage Days 1987; Villisca City Hall display; reclaimed 2004; film tour 2004–05; Epperly vault; promised gift to Villisca Historical Society, Inc.

# CONSOLIDATED EVIDENCE REGISTER (final)

Each item: description / 1912 source / chain of custody / survives today? / 1912 examination / modern opportunity / supports / does not establish / limits.

## 1. The axe
- DESCRIPTION: Josiah Moore's own axe; left leaning against south wall of downstairs (Stillinger) bedroom, washed but still bloodstained. Blunt end used on 7 victims, blade on Josiah.
- 1912 SOURCES: Horton grand jury testimony (1917 transcript pp.18-19) via Epperly "Odyssey of the Axe"; Red Oak Sun June 14, 1912; Villisca Review Sept 15, 1917; Des Moines Register Jan 28, 1945 & June 7, 1987.
- CUSTODY: murder house → bloodhound handlers (June 10 ~9pm) → Sheriff Oren Jackson (gunny sack, night of June 10) → county/District Court evidence → Kelly trial exhibit 1917 (exhibit mark on handle) → given by Sheriff Arthur Baker to James E. Risden (1923–1930) → Risden widow → Don Brown (Oct 1961, notarized Dec 28, 1961) → Epperly (mid-1970s) → Villisca Heritage Days 1987 / City Hall → Epperly 2004 → film tour → promised to Villisca Historical Society Inc. → (2026, Epperly comment) Montgomery County Historical Center, Red Oak.
- SURVIVES: YES — on exhibit at Montgomery County History Center ("See the actual murder weapon and more", mocohistorycenter.org/exhibits).
- 1912 EXAMINATION: washed (by killer) before discovery; bloodhounds scented it; handled by spectators; no fingerprint lifting documented on it (1912; prints not widely used per murderhouse.com).
- MODERN OPPORTUNITY: DNA/attribution on handle surface after 114 years and multiple handlers is a long shot; microscopy of exhibit mark; comparison to 1912 Fourth Wall Films photo already done by Epperly.
- SUPPORTS: weapon identity; it belonged to Moore (victims knew attacker used household tool); scene departure order.
- DOES NOT ESTABLISH: owner of killer; handedness claims beyond Linquist's spatter inference; any suspect.
- LIMITS: axe moved by spectators before sealing (Epperly); washed; handled by hundreds.

## 2. Lamps and chimney
- DESCRIPTION: oil lamp from dresser, chimney removed and placed aside (under a chair per Dash/coroner reconstruction; "under the dresser" per later secondary retellings), wick bent/turned down.
- 1912 SOURCES: coroner's reconstruction reported in press (Omaha World-Herald June 11-17, 1912 per Dash's sourcing); murderhouse.com "gas lamp with its glass chimney removed, and its wick turned down."
- PARALLEL SERIES: same lamp modification in Ellsworth KS (Oct 1911) and Paola KS (June 1912) murders (Dash).
- SURVIVES: NO lamp/chimney artifact is documented as preserved anywhere today. House restored 1990s with period furnishings — current lamps are props, NOT evidence.
- 1912 EXAMINATION: positional reconstruction by coroner Linquist; no photometric or soot analysis.
- MODERN OPPORTUNITY: none material (artifact not kept); only documentary.
- SUPPORTS: premeditation/darkness discipline; killer's comfort operating in dark; series signature.
- DOES NOT ESTABLISH: identity; whether lamp was taken from bedroom dresser vs carried in.
- LIMITS: weight and placement details vary between press accounts (chimney "under a chair" vs "under the dresser"; bacon 2 lb vs 4 lb) — first-week reports disagree.

## 3. Mirror/glass coverings
- DESCRIPTION: cloths/garments drawn from drawers hung over every mirror, glass door panels, and windows (skirts and aprons across windows); victims' faces covered (gauze undershirt on Herman, dress over Katherine).
- 1912 SOURCES: press (Colorado Springs June 15, 1912 clip: "In Villisca the murderer strung skirts and aprons across the windows to prevent any one from looking into the house"); Dash; murderhouse.com.
- SURVIVES: the specific cloths — no documented survival. The practice is a series signature (sheets at Wayne/Burnham CO homes; clothing over telephone at Ellsworth).
- SUPPORTS: ritual/anti-observation behavior; link hypothesis to Midwest series.
- DOES NOT ESTABLISH: funeral-wake custom motive vs anti-scrutiny motive; identity.
- LIMITS: interpretation (wake custom vs. counter-surveillance) is speculative; no physical item retained.

## 4. Cigarette butts / tobacco
- DESCRIPTION: "Two spent cigarettes found in the attic" (murderhouse.com). RELATED AND CONTESTED: Kansas City Post reporter Hoban's claim of smoking evidence + crushed cotton-batting box in downstairs closet.
- REBUTTAL (Epperly, iowacoldcases comment): undertaker found cotton box atop uncrushed suit boxes; no smoking evidence; "A false rumor in my judgement."
- SURVIVES: NO. Butts, if real, long gone; no brand identification recorded in fetched sources (brand claims are SNIPPET-ONLY folklore).
- 1912 EXAMINATION: none (no disposition studies).
- MODERN OPPORTUNITY: none — items not preserved.
- SUPPORTS: occupation/lurking hypothesis only if butts were in the attic and attributable.
- DOES NOT ESTABLISH: that the killer smoked or waited in the house (Epperly contests the closet version; attic version rests on secondary retellings).
- LIMITS: no first-week source fetched here documents the butts verbatim; ENH-labeled sites are the main carriers.

## 5. Stillinger room / Lena's body
- DESCRIPTION: Lena found crosswise/lengthwise across bed; defensive wound on arm; undergarments removed, nightdress up; Ina found face down. Bodies moved post-mortem.
- SOURCES: murderhouse.com (verbatim above); legis.iowa.gov PDF ("found lying crosswise on the bed, and with a defensive wound on her arm"); Dash ("found wearing no underwear and with her nightdress drawn up past her waist... doctors found no evidence of that sort of assault"); Wikipedia ("Later investigations showed no evidence of this").
- Dr. F.S. Williams: verified as first physician to examine bodies and estimate time of death (Dash; Historic Mysteries). A SPECIFIC genital examination by Williams with findings and stated limits — NOT verified in fetched sources; UNVERIFIED CLAIM.
- WATCH CHAIN: "a piece of a watch chain which was found in the bed where the Stillinger girls were killed... believed to have been torn by the larger of the girls" (June 15, 1912 press via iagenweb) — the physical trace of struggle; fate unknown.
- SURVIVES: no bodily evidence; only house and documentary record.
- 1912 EXAMINATION: external doctors' examination only; no forensic gynecology; autopsies recorded severe head trauma cause of death.
- MODERN OPPORTUNITY: none bodily (buried 1912); exhumation DNA would target offender contact only where staining preserved — speculative.
- SUPPORTS: Lena woke and struggled (only waking victim); possible sexual intent staged or attempted.
- DOES NOT ESTABLISH: completed sexual assault (explicitly negated by 1912 findings).

## 6. Blood, skull fragments, shoe prints
- Skull fragments: "in one case even removing fragments of Joe Moore's skull as a macabre keepsake" (Dash); DMR 2012: Epperly "followed... the trail of a piece of Joe Moore's skull, plucked from the house and passed among residents." Fate of any fragment today: UNKNOWN — not located to a repository in this pass.
- Scene contamination: "as many as 100 curious neighbors and townspeople tramped as they pleased through the house, scattering fingerprints" (Dash); weapon moved by spectators (Epperly); US Ghost Adventures: crowd "handling the murder weapon, and some even taking pieces of Josiah's skull as a souvenir."
- Bloody shoes/prints: only substantiated adjacent claim is suspect Joe Ricks's "bloodstained shoes... obtained in a trade from a tramp" (iagenweb, June 15, 1912) — i.e., explained innocently, not a scene print match. 'Bloody shoe print in closet dust' comes from the Hoban report Epperly deems false. Any bloody-footprint-at-scene claims: UNVERIFIED.
- 1912 EXAM: no serology beyond visual; Linquist's blood-spatter position analysis (handedness) is the sole scene-pattern analysis on record.
- MODERN OPPORTUNITY: nil for biologicals (all gone); archival photo analysis of spatter/scene is possible but photos are restricted (SHSI collection per NRHP biblio).

## 7. Keys/locks
- DESCRIPTION: back door unlocked (killer lifted latch, per Dash); front door locked in morning; Ross Moore had his own key copy and "unlocked the front door with his copy of the house key" (legis.iowa.gov PDF); killer "Taking the house keys... locking the doors behind him" (Dash); keychain fragment "not belonging to the Moore's was found downstairs" (murderhouse.com) = the short watch/key chain near bacon (Dash/iagenweb).
- TENSION: if the Moores' keys were taken, how did Ross's key open the door? Multiple keys existed (Ross had a copy) — killer took the house keyring. Door lock findings are consistent but the custody of keyring after 1912 is undocumented.
- SURVIVES: no.
- SUPPORTS: killer locked up deliberately (knowledge/coolness); keychain fragment = possible killer's property.
- DOES NOT ESTABLISH: whose keychain (never matched to any suspect); whether front door was locked from inside with key present.

## 8. Joe Moore's business dealings / F.F. Jones feud (documentary basis)
- Employment: Moore "worked for Frank Jones in his implement store for nine years before opening his own John Deere dealership in 1902" (NRHP nomination); Dash says left in 1907 "taking the valuable John Deere account with him" (7 years). DATE VARIANCE (1902 vs 1907) between NRHP and press-derived accounts — flag.
- Feud: "relations between Jones and Moore had grown so cold that the they began to cross the street to avoid each other" (Dash); NRHP: Jones "the owner of a prosperous agricultural implement dealership that dominated the south side of the town square for thirty years," board of Villisca National Bank, state legislator.
- Grand jury: Jones "became the subject of a grand jury investigation" (Dash); slander suit by Jones against Wilkerson; Mansfield payroll alibi ("Payroll records showed..." legis PDF; "work slip signed by supervisors" DMR).
- JOE'S CAR PURCHASE: repeatedly alleged in secondary lore (motive = jealousy over prosperity) but NOT verifiable in any fetched source — UNVERIFIED CLAIM.
- DOCUMENTARY SURVIVAL: business records (John Deere agency ledger, bank records) — not located; likely private/scattered. Only secondary echo: NRHP social-structure narrative.

## 9. Tracks/prints out of doors
- Hay barn: "a depression in some bales of hay stored in the family barn, and a knot hole through which the murderer could have watched the house" (Dash, per Linquist); murderhouse: "imprint in the hay... next to a knothole."
- Bloodhounds: "Bloodhounds were tried without success" (Dash); axe scented June 10 ~9pm (Red Oak Sun via Epperly).
- 'Man seen walking east of the house': NOT verified in any fetched source this pass — UNVERIFIED CLAIM (candidate: Xenia Delaney's 2:10 a.m. telephone-exchange footsteps account, Dash — that's a call, not a sighting).
- 1912 EXAM: no photography/plaster casting documented.
- SURVIVES: no; barn itself replaced (outbuildings removed/replaced pre-1994, per murderhouse restoration page).

## 11. The house
- 1994 purchase by Darwin & Martha Linn; restoration to June-1912 condition (porches reopened, plumbing/electric stripped, pantry→bathroom reverted, outbuildings restored); NRHP listed 12/1/1997 (NPGallery) as "Moore, Josiah B. and Sara, House," NRIS 97001471, 508 E. Second St.; IHPA "Preservation at its Best" 1997; now operated (murderhouse.com / US Ghost Adventures) for tours/overnights.
- Physical evidence surviving IN the house: the building fabric itself (interior spatial arrangement "well preserved" per NRHP); ceiling gouge above Josiah's bed is reported by tradition — NOT independently verified as preserved/marked today. Furniture/fixtures are period props, not 1912 evidence. Two brick chimneys removed at some point (NRHP) — the chimney through which smoke claims ran no longer exists.
- MODERN OPPORTUNITY: architectural archaeology (paint stratigraphy, attic/closet surfaces, floorboards) — but 30 years of tourist traffic and 1990s restoration make forensic values marginal; documentary/archaeological value only.

## 13. Evidence custody 1912→today

## 14. Modern forensic opportunities (assessment)
- DNA: only candidate substrate = the axe (surface DNA after 114 yrs, low probability; contaminated by decades of handling); victims' remains exhumable for family reference DNA — ethically sensitive, no unknown-profile to match against exists.
- Photo analysis: 1912 scene photographs (Fourth Wall Films axe photo already used for identification; SHSI restricted collection) — comparison of wound/scene geometry, lamp position, blood pattern vs Linquist's notes.
- Architectural archaeology: limited; house heavily restored 1994-98.
- Documentary mining: Epperly's 3 file cabinets; Iowa AG files; Montgomery County court records; 1917 grand jury transcript.
- The single most valuable surviving artifact remains the axe, whose forensic value is now historical rather than probative.
