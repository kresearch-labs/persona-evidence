## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (calibrated)

**What is established beyond reasonable dispute (documentary record, multiple independent classes of source):**

- Six people were killed by massive head injuries at the isolated farmstead Hinterkaifeck (officially "Gröbern Nr. 27½", Gemeinde Wangen, Landkreis Schrobenhausen), in the night of Friday 31 March to Saturday 1 April 1922: Andreas Gruber (63), his wife Cäzilia (72), his daughter Viktoria Gabriel (35), her children Cäzilia (7) and Josef (2), and the newly arrived maid Maria Baumgartner (44/45) [1][2][6].
- The bodies were found on the afternoon of 4 April 1922 by three neighbours (Lorenz Schlittenbauer, Jakob Sigl, Michael Pöll) after a repair technician found the farm deserted; the Munich police were notified by telephone at 18:15 on 4 April and arrived in the night [2][6][9].
- The murder weapon was a mattock-type tool (Reuthaue) belonging to the farm, found only in February 1923 during demolition of the buildings, hidden in a false floor (Fehlboden); identified by its unusual screw fixing matching a small round wound on Viktoria Gabriel's head [2][3][6].
- The perpetrator(s) evidently remained on or returned to the farm between 1 and 4 April: food used (bread consumed, broth left on the stove, meat freshly cut), animals tended (cattle calm/fed; multiple witness statements), chimney smoke and oven use observed on 1 April [2][6][9].
- Substantial hard currency was left behind (the court commission found 1880 marks in gold and 1700 marks in silver on 7–8 April 1922); paper money was taken in an unknown amount [4][6][9]. The robbery-motive line was therefore progressively abandoned by the investigators themselves [2][8].
- The case was never solved. Over 100 investigative proceedings were opened against suspects by 6 November 1926 [7], more than 100 proceedings by 1930 per the Munich police [1][2]; interrogations continued for decades; official closure is reported for 1955 [8]. No charge was ever brought; nothing in this report should be read as an identification.

**Calibrated conclusion.** No hypothesis reaches even the "more likely than not" band on surviving evidence. The best-supported family of explanations is a **personal-conflict killing by a perpetrator closely familiar with the farm and family**, with the neighbour **Lorenz Schlittenbauer** the most-documented individual candidate — but the case against him consists of relationship history, opportunity, and behavioural anomalies, not of a single item of evidence that links him to the acts. The competing outsider/robbery explanation retains real weight precisely because the strongest insider signals (body coverings, weapon concealment, silent navigation) have innocent readings. The Karl-Gabriel-returned theory is the weakest of the classical theories: his battlefield death is documented by a comrade's statement [2] and the one 1951 eyewitness story (Matthäus Eser) was retracted as a lie during official investigations [2]. I explicitly do **not** rank Schlittenbauer as "the killer"; I rank the *explanation family* higher than the alternatives, and give the reasons both for and against in §4 and the self-audit in §7.

**Confidence statements (qualitative, no numbers):**
- Facts in the first paragraph block: high confidence, multiply corroborated, archival + contemporary press.
- "The killer stayed several days": moderately supported (several independent observations), but the *duration* is an inference; a single brief return visit(s) is not excluded [2][9].
- "Motive was personal conflict, not gain": the assessment of the 2000 operational case analysis (KHK Klaus Wiest, 21 July 2000) based on the surviving file material, and of later reviewers [2][8]; plausible, not provable — the amount of missing paper money was never established [9].
- Any named suspect: speculative band.

## 2. DETAILED CHRONOLOGY (every dated item attributed)

Pre-history (documented via court records quoted in [2]):

- 1877 — Cäzilia Sanhüter (later Gruber) takes over/marries into the Hinterkaifeck property from her first marriage (Josef Asam); after his death she runs it with second husband Andreas Gruber; the farm later passes to daughter Viktoria and her 1914 husband Karl Gabriel (Gütergemeinschaft) [2][6].
- 12 December 1914 — Karl Gabriel, Viktoria's husband, killed in France (Neville; other accounts say near Arras). Later confirmed by comrade Josef Brummer: "…ihn hat eine Mine getötet." (StAM 8091b, S. 568) [2][6][7].
- 1915 — Viktoria Gabriel and Andreas Gruber convicted by the Strafkammer des Landgerichts Neuburg a.d. Donau of incest ("Blutschande", relationship said to span 1907–1910): one year penitentiary (Zuchthaus) for him, one month prison for her [2][6].
- 7 September 1919 — Viktoria gives birth to illegitimate son Josef; she names Lorenz Schlittenbauer as father [2].
- 10 September 1919 — Schlittenbauer denounces Viktoria and Andreas for incest; 13 September: Andreas in pre-trial custody; 25 September: Schlittenbauer withdraws the accusation; 27 September: Andreas released; 30 September: Schlittenbauer acknowledges paternity and pays a 1,800-mark settlement; Viktoria's father-in-law Karl Gabriel (grandfather of the child) becomes... — note: guardianship of Josef went to Andreas Gruber [2].
- 23 October 1919 — under oath, Schlittenbauer states his withdrawal had not corresponded to the truth; claims Viktoria induced him to withdraw it and had paid him the 1,800 M plus 3,000 M in mortgage bonds, which he says he returned (StAM 8091b, S. 1065) [2].
- 31 December 1919 — Andreas Gruber acquitted; Schlittenbauer's statements judged unreliable [2].
- 1921 — Schlittenbauer marries his second wife, Anna Dick [2]. Autumn 1921 — the previous maid (Kreszenz Rieger) leaves the farm; the reason "weil es dort spukt" (haunted) appears in a 1997 newspaper account [SNIPPET-ONLY 11]; her actual 1922 statements concerned a burglary (see §3) [7]. The "haunted" reason is a later press gloss, not a 1922 file statement I could trace.
- Mid-March 1922 — a Munich newspaper/periodical found near the farm that nobody in the area subscribed to; postman confirms no subscriber (file-sourced, quoted in [2]; repeated in [6]).
- 30 March 1922 — Andreas Gruber finds footprints in snow leading from the forest toward the Motorhütte (engine house), none leading away. Same day he tells this to at least three people (Schlittenbauer, Kaspar Stegmeier, a salesman) and at the ironmongery "Vogel" in Schrobenhausen reports nocturnal noises in the attic and the one-way tracks: quoted witness Wenzeslaus [Bley/Stegmeier] statement, StAM 8091b, S. 1034 [2]. (The "two youths tried to break in last night" variant is a 1922 newspaper embellishment [4].)
- 31 March 1922 — Andreas and Viktoria travel to Schrobenhausen (shopping trip per later accounts [SNIPPET-ONLY web.de summary — not used as evidence]); **about 16:30 ("gegen halb fünf Uhr am Abend") the new maid Maria Baumgartner arrives, escorted by her sister Franziska Schäfer, who leaves after about an hour — the last known person to see the residents alive** (StAM 8091b, S. 1126; Schäfer's own statements of 1930 and 1932) [2][9]. Schlittenbauer sent his two sons to check on the farm that day [4 — Munz. Ztg. says the Ortsführer and two men checked because nothing had been heard since Thursday; the more precise two-boys-then-three-men sequence is in [2][6]].
- Night 31 March → 1 April 1922 — the six murders. Official narrowing: clothing state of victims → 20:00–22:00 on 31 March (Augenscheinprotokoll Wiesner) [2]; the GDA page gives "between 19 and 22 hours" [1]; the 7 April 1922 Münchner Zeitung said "between 8 and 9 pm" [4]; Reingruber's report gives "20–23 Uhr" [9]. A minority view (Meiendres 1948; some forum researchers) argues for early morning of 1 April; the file evidence listed in [9] mostly supports late evening of 31 March. Both windows remain live in the literature; the archival evidence base favours 31 March evening [9].

The three undiscovered days (1–4 April), from dated witness statements [2][9]:

- 1 April — coffee dealers (Hans and Eduard Schirovsky per [6]; "Kaffeehändler" generally in [9]) knock, get no answer, leave; Michael Plöckl/Pöckel (spelling varies in the sources — see §5) passes morning and evening, sees the bake-oven door closed in the morning, half open in the evening, chimney smoke in the evening, and a flashing light near the woods like a torch (his 1951 statement [9]; the 6 Nov 1926 Pielmayer summary [9]).
- 2 April — family absent from church; no alarm (school absence of the child Cäzilia begins Saturday 1 April) [2][6].
- 3 April — postman Josef Mayer delivers the paper ~08:30, sees nobody, notes the child's carriage missing from the kitchen; later disputed by Mayer himself in 1952/1954 statements over whether he stuck the paper in the window on 3 April [9]. A hunter reports (1951) passing the farm that Saturday afternoon, no smoke, no animals visible [9].
- 4 April — repair technician Albert Hofer/Hofner (spellings both occur in the records [2][6][7]) arrives to repair the feed-cutting motor, works ~4.5 hours alone, leaves, reports in Gröbern to Schlittenbauer's daughters. Schlittenbauer sends his two sons; they find nothing. He then goes with Jakob Sigl and Michael Pöll (the year annotation for his earlier statement is 30 March 1931) [2][6], breaks in via the machine house/gate, finds four bodies in the barn (Stadel) covered with hay and a wooden door, the maid in her chamber, the child Josef in his carriage in Viktoria's bedroom [2][3][6]. Gendarmerie Hohenwart notified; Munich Polizeidirektion telephone note 18:15 [2].
- 4/5 April night — Kriminaloberinspektor Georg Reingruber and team (with dog handlers) reach Wangen ~01:30; begin scene examination 05:30 with the Schrobenhausen court commission (Oberamtsrichter Wiesner, Gerichtsassistent Schäfer) [2][6]. By then crowds of onlookers have been on the property for hours; the catalog states that traces were presumably already destroyed and the tracker dog took no scent [2].
- 6–8 April — autopsies: Andreas Gruber and Josef on 6 April; the three female victims on 7 April, 10 a.m. Findings: shattered skull caps in all three women; Cäzilia Gruber seven blows plus neck bruise marks (Würgemale); Viktoria Gabriel right facial side smashed plus a small round wound — later matched to the protruding screw of the Reuthaue; heads detached and handed to the Staatsanwaltschaft Neuburg; **the skulls are lost ever since** [2]. First suspicion of a pickaxe (Kreuzhacke/Spitzhacke) found in the feed barrier is ruled out on wound patterns [2][4].
- 8 April — State Interior Ministry posts a 100,000-mark reward (Fahndungsplakat reproduced in [6]; press confirmation SNIPPET-ONLY [10]); 9 April — Reingruber circulates wanted notices for Adolf Gump, Wilhelm Dreßel, Wilhelm Musweiler alias Weiland, and ex-officer Friedrich N. alias Fischer (Freikorps/Oberschlesia farm-murder group), instructing gendarmerie to check Gump's alibi for 30 March–1 April 1922 [6][7].
- 8 April — burial of all six victims at Waidhofen (Pfarrer Michael Haas); ~3,000 mourners reported by Schrobenhausener Wochenblatt 11 April 1922 [2].
- 29 August 1922 — mill owner Johann Scheller names Schlittenbauer as perpetrator (hearsay from Matthias Ramelmeier, who denies it) — file protocol in [2]; a chain of such denunciations runs through the whole record [7].
- 3 August 1922 — brothers Karl and Andreas Schreyer (Sattelberg) arrested; released for alibi [6][7].
- 5 July 1923 — former farmhand Georg Siegl identifies the Reuthaue as made by Andreas Gruber four years earlier, with the characteristic two iron bands and protruding screw (StAM 8091b, S. 678–679) [2]. During February 1923 demolition (farm bought by Karl Gabriel's father and brother, Josef Gabriel) the Reuthaue and a 21 cm knife are found; the knife yields no blood traces or fingerprints [2][3].
- 6 November 1926 — Staatsanwalt Pielmayer's consolidated suspect review (the "100 proceedings" marker) [7].
- 10 September 1930 — Oberstaatsanwalt Kestel requests a second opinion on the Schlittenbauer suspicion; 1930/1931 — Schlittenbauer re-interviewed (a 30 March statement — the wiki dates it both 1930 and 1931 [7]; second interrogation 30 March 1931) [2][7].
- 1925 — teacher Hans Yblagger reports finding Schlittenbauer at the demolished farmstead ruins; Schlittenbauer explains the perpetrator tried to bury the bodies but the frozen ground hindered it (statement of Yblagger 1931, per [6][7]).
- 28 February 1930 — Reingruber retires; September 1930 — Kriminalinspektor Martin Riedmayer takes over the case [6].
- 1951 — Staatsanwalt Andreas Popp investigates the Gump brothers on the deathbed accusation of their sister Kreszentia Mayer; Anton Gump briefly in custody; case dropped 1954 [6][7]. Matthäus Eser's Donaukurier story (Soviet officer = the Hinterkaifeck murderer = Karl Gabriel) collapses; November 1951 proceedings against Eser for feigning a crime, closed when he admitted the story was invented [2][6].
- 1952–1953 — belated wave of witness interrogations (postman Mayer, farmer Sigl, Schwaiger, Plöckl, etc., 1951–1953) [9]; the maid's sister Schäfer finally heard 1930 (and 1932) — the catalog asks pointedly why key witnesses were heard years or decades late [2].
- 1955 — official closure reported [8]. (An en-Wikipedia claim of "last interrogations 1986 under KHD Konrad Müller-Thumann" could not be verified against the sources I accessed; Müller-Thumann is documented as the Ingolstadt criminal investigator who assembled the file collection now at the Bayerisches Armeemuseum from the 1970s onward [2].) [unverified]
- 21 July 2000 — Kriminalhauptkommissar Klaus Wiest (Polizeipräsidium München, Dezernat 11) produces an operational case analysis (OFA-style) on the Müller-Thumann material; concludes gain (Bereicherung) is excluded, personal/emotional conflict with the family is central, body coverings suggest perpetrator-victim relationship, meticulous concealment and feeding/watering/milking of animals show intent to delay discovery [2].
- June 2007 — 15 police-academy students (FH für öffentliche Verwaltung und Rechtspflege, Fürstenfeldbruck), project led by Polizeihauptmeisterin Michaela Forderberg-/Vorderberg-Zankl, re-examine the case; report concluded a specific person was most likely, name not published; BR quotes the group's own conclusion verbatim [8]. In 2022 the same project leader publicly distanced herself from her group's conclusion, stating she believed the named suspect innocent [6]. (Spelling of the name differs between sources — audit point.) The report itself was not accessible to me (see §6). [unverified re: exact whereabouts]
- 2023–2028 — the Bavarian State Archives digitise related holdings (incl. CAHJP cooperation); the case file StAM Pol.Dir. 8091b remains the core record; a 2026 archival exhibition re-opens the documentary record [1][2][5].

## 4. SUSPECT COMPARISON AND HYPOTHESIS ASSESSMENT

### 4.1 Lorenz Schlittenbauer (neighbour, Ortsführer of Gröbern, 1874–1941)

Why investigated: closest documented relationship to a victim (affair with Viktoria from late 1918, marriage blocked by Andreas Gruber, who chased him off with a scythe); contested paternity of Josef with sworn reversals (see chronology); physically closest habitation (a few hundred metres); most anomalous behaviour at discovery (entered the house alone ahead of companions, later explanations shifted; moved bodies; stayed alone with the scene until officials arrived; took charge of the livestock) [2][3][6][9].

Genuine connecting evidence: everything above is documented in the file; plus the paternity/maintenance tangle of 1919 (his sworn reversal, the 1,800 M settlement and 3,000 M bonds story), the 1925 "frozen ground" remark, an "Ich-Form" gossip item about him speaking of the killer in the first person, and private slander suits he won against neighbours who called him the murderer (Sigl among them) [2][6][7].

What remains unproven: any physical trace, any witness placing him at the farm on 31 March evening, any document showing a decisive financial threat. Police themselves concluded (1930, quoted in [2]) that the guardianship dispute had produced "keine unangenehmen, finanziellen oder namentlich belastenden Folgen" for him, that he was well-regarded and propertied, and that his scene behaviour was explicable by concern for the child he believed (at times) to be his son [2]. He was interrogated repeatedly (5.4.1922; 1930/31) and never charged [7].

Assessment: the strongest *documented* motive-and-opportunity profile; zero linking evidence. Opportunity: high (350 m away, farm visible, knew layout). Direct link to the acts: none.

### 4.2 Karl Gabriel (Viktoria's husband, "killed" December 1914) / the returnee theory

Why investigated: early file line — could a man reported dead have returned? (Ersuchen of 29.4.1922 and Verbleib file of 2.5.1922 [7]); folklore of identity swaps; post-WWII POW-return stories of a German-speaking Soviet officer who called himself the Hinterkaifeck murderer [6].

Connecting evidence: none beyond the motive story (jealousy over Viktoria's children). The counter-evidence is comparatively strong: comrade Josef Brummer's statement "…ihn hat eine Mine getötet." (StAM 8091b, S. 568) [2]; the 1951 Eser story was retracted under investigation for feigning a crime [2][6]. Verdict: weakest classical theory; near-falsified for Gabriel personally, though "someone else entirely" remains formally open.

### 4.3 Itinerant/ex-convict robbery offenders — including Josef Bärtl ("Eiserner Heindl", escaped 1921 from the Günzburg psychiatric hospital, 1926 warrant on "dringender Tatverdacht"), the Freikorps group (Adolf Gump, Dreßel, Musweiler, Friedrich/Fischer, wanted from 9.4.1922 for the Oberschlesien farm murders), the Bichler/Thaler brothers and Georg Siegl (local thieves known from the farm), Kaltenecker/Schweiger/Schnepf lines (1932), Schreyer brothers (1922), Peter Weber (Betz's statement that Weber proposed robbing the "gold" at Kaifeck) [2][6][7].

Why investigated: initial robbery theory; the surveillance-and-concealment pattern (attic, roof tiles, snow traces) fits a staking-out outsider; many had regional opportunity.

Genuine connecting evidence: mostly circumstance; Weber's proposal predates the crime (Betz statement 7.4.1922 [7]); Bichler/Siegl were proven burglars at the farm itself (Nov 1920 break-in, Rieger/Siegl statements [7]); Bärtl was never even caught, so his whereabouts on the night were never established.

Unproven: everything. No loot was ever traced (one 1926 hint — Kloiber allegedly selling "Raubmord" loot — dissolved [7]); no arrest ever produced a confession; several specific denunciations (Seidl's accusations) ended in convictions of the *denouncer* for false accusation [7].

### 4.4 Insider-family and other local theories (Thaler/Bichler brothers, Gabriels of the wider family, Hofer the technician — suspected in 1925 by a retired gendarme, Negendank, Pfleger, Lackermeier via anonymous letters, etc.) [2][7]

These populate the file but each rests on a single denunciation or rumour; the wiki's register shows how many were closed for proven innocence or lack of any record [7]. Included here to show the field, not as live hypotheses.

### 4.5 Modern speculative constructs

- "Man from the Train" serial-killer mapping (Bill James/Rachel McCarthy James, 2017) — a framework hypothesis with no file anchorage known to me; encountered only via en-Wikipedia [unverified].
- Tannöd (Schenkel 2006) and similar fiction — explicitly fiction; not evidence.

### 4.6 Structural hypotheses

- Single vs multiple perpetrators: the archival exhibition text explicitly leaves this open ("Unklar ist, ob die Tat von nur einem oder vielleicht sogar von mehreren Tätern begangen wurde") [2]. Two sets of body-imprints in the hay were reported (hinterkaifeck.net Tatort page [3]) — suggestive, weak.
- Robbery vs staged robbery vs personal motive: police trajectory went robbery → doubt → Wiest 2000 excludes gain [2][8]. But the unknown value of the missing paper money keeps a hybrid (theft by an insider or servant-recruited outsider) unfalsified [9].

## 5. SOURCE-DEPENDENCE AND PROVENANCE AUDIT

**Documentary base (what actually survives):**
- StAM (Staatsarchiv München) Polizeidirektion München 8091b — the "Hauptakt" of the Munich police; *survives as copies/typescripts*, since the Neuburg Landgericht/Staatsanwaltschaft files moved to Augsburg in 1932 and the Augsburg justice building burned in the 25 February 1944 RAF raid; the Reuthaue itself and the skulls were destroyed/lost in that context [2].
- Staatsanwaltschaft Augsburg files, reassembled in the 1950s [2].
- Bayerisches Armeemuseum, Ingolstadt: the Konrad Müller-Thumann collection (PolDok), incl. the 2000 Wiest analysis and 1951–1953 witness protocols [2].
- Contemporary press: Münchner Zeitung 7./8./10.4.1922; Bayerischer Kurier 8.4.1922; Schrobenhausener Wochenblatt 11.4.1922; Salzburger Wacht 6.4.1922; Salzburger Volksblatt 22.4.1922; Augsburger papers — most accessible today via transcriptions in the Hinterkaifeck-Wiki [4][7]; digitized originals exist in Bavarian/Austrian newspaper portals but I verified only the wiki transcription text, not OCR scans [limitation].

**Dependence structure of the most consequential claims:**

1. *The events of 30–31 March (footprints, attic noises, newspaper, key)* — original anchor: Andreas Gruber's reports to named hearers, recorded in the file (StAM 8091b S. 1034) [2]. Everything in modern media flows from this single report chain. The Münchner Zeitung of 7.4.1922 already shows corruption ("two youths tried to break in") [4] — the embellishment process started within a week.
2. *The post-murder farm residence* — anchor: dated witness protocols (Pöckl 1926/1951, Mayer 1952, Sigl 1952, Schwaiger 1951, Hofner 1925) [9]. These are independent statements but recorded 25–30 years after the fact; the 1926 Pielmayer summary is the earliest synthesis. One contradiction: Mayer's 1952 statement that he delivered on 3 April vs his later insistence that he handed the paper to Gruber personally on 31 March and did *not* stick it in the window on 3 April [9] — the "newspaper in the window" image, ubiquitous in modern accounts, is disputed inside the file.
3. *Schlittenbauer's behaviour* — anchor: the 5.4.1922 statements of his two companions + the Wiesner protocol; later layers (Yblagger anecdote, Stammtisch Ich-Form) enter the file only 1931 via Yblagger's protocol [6][7]. Modern accounts (en-Wikipedia, books, podcasts) all draw on Leuschner's 1978/1997/2007 book for the colourful layers; the book is not available to me online, so those specific anecdotes are carried here only via [6] with that caveat.
4. *The "haunted, maid quit" story* — appears in Die Welt 22.3.1997 [11] (SNIPPET-ONLY) and is ubiquitous since; I could not find it in any pre-1997 file document in my sources. Likely a Leuschner-origin or later press line. Treat as unproven (E18).
5. *The 2007 "named but unpublished suspect"* — exists only via the BR-broadcast group conclusion [8] plus secondary retellings; the report itself was not retrievable. The related figure "2007 LKA reopened the case" appears in some modern accounts; the only dated, sourced modern analyses I could document are Wiest 2000 [2] and the 2007 student project [8].
6. *English Wikipedia's specifics* (Gump wanted 9.4.1922; Popp 1951; skulls to Munich; case closed 1955; 1986 last interrogations) — the first four check out against de-Wikipedia/file-derived sources [6][8]; the "1986 last interrogations" I could not verify [unverified].
7. *Circularity risk, worst cases:* (a) "the killer lived in the house for three days" — every modern source repeats one inference chain; no observer ever saw a person; (b) "screams could not be heard" — a single 1922 experiment by Wiesner's commission, repeated everywhere [2][9]; (c) "Cäzilia tore her hair out in tufts" — anchored in the autopsy description but the "survived for hours" framing grew in the literature; (d) the 1927 midnight confession — one anecdotal line in de-Wikipedia [6]; (e) "contamination — onlookers cooked meals in the kitchen" (en-Wikipedia) — I found "Schaulustige destroyed traces" in the archival catalog [2] but no primary statement of cooking; treat the cooking detail as unproven [unverified].

**Circularity verdict:** the case has effectively *one* modern scholarly-literate channel (Leuschner's books, which had file access), one archival exhibition channel (2026, quoting StAM 8091b directly) [2], one community-wiki channel with verbatim file transcriptions [7][9], and a century of press retellings. Cross-source agreement therefore usually means agreement with Leuschner, not independence.

## 6. UNRESOLVED QUESTIONS AND PRIORITISED FOLLOW-UP (top five)

1. **How much money was actually taken, and what exactly?** Resolves the robbery/staged-robbery/personal-motive fork. Document: the Wiesner Augenscheinprotokoll and the money-inventory sections of StAM 8091b; the heirs' probate record (AG Schrobenhausen Nachlassregister 1922/41–43, partly exhibited in [2]); the Augsburger bank depot question (press claim [4]). Located: Staatsarchiv München; Staatsarchiv Augsburg. A precise missing-value list (or proof nothing beyond paper money vanished) would materially weaken either the outsider-theft or the staging hypothesis.
2. **Josef Gruber's paternity — Schlittenbauer or Andreas Gruber?** Resolves the emotional core of the Schlittenbauer motive. Documents: the sworn 23.10.1919 statement (StAM 8091b S. 1065) [2]; church records; potentially DNA from the buried victims (Waidhofen cemetery, burial 8.4.1922, six bodies under one stone) vs Schlittenbauer/Gruber-descendant reference samples — the skulls are lost but the bodies were buried intact, which modern accounts rarely register as an option. Any DNA result re-prices the insider-family-conflict hypothesis.
3. **What exactly did the 2007 Fürstenfeldbruck analysis conclude, and on which document basis?** Document: the student project report (BR 2022 says it is "öffentlich einsehbar" [8]; a copy is cited at hinterkaifeck.net, which I could not retrieve) plus the 2000 Wiest OFA (Armeemuseum PolDok 30, P1951.6.15 [2]). Located: Hochschule für den öffentlichen Dienst in Bayern (FH Fürstenfeldbruck), Bayerisches Armeemuseum Ingolstadt, hinterkaifeck.net. If the group's reasoning rests on file errors (e.g., the key contradiction E12), it can be independently audited — or discounted.
4. **Why were core witnesses heard so late, and are the missing protocols really missing (priest's protocols; the 700-mark confessional deposit of 17.3.1922)?** Documents: file pagination of StAM 8091b (exhibition cites specific gaps) [2]; the 1950s reassembled Augsburg files. If the deposit was made *because* Viktoria feared someone specific, a church or parish record could name the feared person — the single most powerful discriminator currently imaginable between insider and outsider hypotheses.
5. **Was there one killer or more than one?** Evidence to test: the two hay imprints [3]; the physical sequence in the barn (one person cannot easily subdue four adults to "found as they collapsed" positions — though they may have been killed separately over time); the MZ 7.4.1922 "Schandbuben" plural slip [4]. Documents: Wiesner protocol's original position sketches, the 1952 reconstruction interviews [9]. A defensible multi-perpetrator reading would demote every single-person suspect theory, including Schlittenbauer.

## 7. ADVERSARIAL SELF-AUDIT (against my preferred explanation: personal-conflict, perpetrator familiar with the farm)

1. **The behavioural inference may be anachronistic.** "Covered bodies = perpetrator knew the victims" is a 2000 OFA-style reading [2]; post-hoc covering could be practical (delay discovery, screen the sight for the perpetrator's own work in the house).
2. **The robbery component is not closed.** Paper money of unknown value vanished [9]; a destitute outsider takes paper, cannot find hoards of coin hidden in a house he does not know — precisely the observed outcome. The insider-motive reading leans on a Wiest-style inference, not on a document.
3. **The "intimate knowledge" signal cuts the other way.** The attic straw-bed, roof tiles, fed animals and silent navigation are equally consistent with an outsider who hid on the farm for days — the file itself contains the attic-hide reconstruction [2]. A resident insider would hardly need to sleep in straw and watch through displaced tiles.
4. **The farm-residence "three days" may be an overstatement.** The dated observations (smoke 1 April, postman 3 April, milked animals) require at most intermittent presence or one later visit; "lived with the corpses for three days" is literary amplification.
5. **The Schlittenbauer file is contaminated by local feud.** Sigl and Schlittenbauer were in mutual slander litigation [2][7]; companions' 1922 statements about his behaviour were partly reconstructed *after* being asked to re-enact positions [3]; his acquitted-standing and successful suits cut both ways.
6. **Multiple perpetrators remain possible** [2]; any single-perpetrator ranking could be simply wrong.
7. **The record is copies of a partially burned file**, reassembled twice [2]; "no evidence against X" may mean "evidence lost."
8. **Fixation risk:** the incest/child-paternity scandal dominates all accounts partly because it is narratively available; an opportunistic outsider with a stolen paper-money haul (unfalsifiable by 1926 currency reform) fits the physical evidence at least as well. I flag this as the live alternative my ranking underweights.

**Strongest opposing explanation revisited before concluding:** the *outsider-robbery* hypothesis (Bärtl/itinerant class as police framed it). Supports: initial police posture; the professional concealment (weapon hidden where only a demolition found it — though that too could be insider knowledge); the missing paper money; the plausible stalk-and-hide pattern in the hay. Challenges: substantial unsearched coin left; the coverings; the multi-day animal care; Wiest's gain-exclusion. A finding that would substantially weaken it: any traced item from the farm in an outsider's possession (the Kloiber 1926 lead was examined and died [7]); conversely, a finding that the "missing" paper money was accounted for in the probate inventory would largely kill it.
