# CASE FILE: HINTERKAIFECK FARM MURDERS — INDEPENDENT INVESTIGATION

**Case:** Sixfold killing at the Einödhof Hinterkaifeck, Gemeinde Wangen, B.A. Schrobenhausen, Bavaria, night of 31 March–1 April 1922 (disputed; see §2). [S48]
**Evidence base:** 119 compiled primary-source entries (S1–S133) from contemporary police, court, medical and administrative records, 1914–1977, plus the registered ledger of 134 sources. [S48]
**Standard:** Established facts, attributed claims, inferences and speculation are kept separate; every load-bearing claim carries an [S#] citation; claims without banked support are marked [unverified]. [S48]

---

## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (CALIBRATED)

Six residents of the isolated farmstead Hinterkaifeck — the Austrägler couple Andreas and Cäzilie Gruber, the widowed owner Viktoria Gabriel, her two children Cäzilie (7) and Josef (2), and the newly hired maid Maria Baumgartner — were killed by blows to the head, most plausibly with a mattock-type tool (Kreuzpickel/Reuthaue), and were found on Tuesday, 4 April 1922. [S48] The crime scene yielded 2,207 marks in gold and silver coin but almost no paper money, and the estate's securities at the bank Friedl & Dummler had been disposed of only in ordinary pre-crime transactions, so the "robbery" motive rests chiefly on the missing paper currency, which was never itemized. [S48] The investigation, led by the Gendarmerie posts Hohenwart and Schrobenhausen and the Munich Polizeidirektion under Kriminalinspektor Reingruber, screened dozens of named suspects between 1922 and 1954 and never produced a charge that survived judicial review; the court file itself burned in the Augsburg Justizpalast fire of 1944. [S86]

What the record supports: the perpetrator or perpetrators knew the household's routines, the layout, the cattle, and likely the house key — the key was reported missing days before the murders, and after the crime someone fed the livestock over several days. [S47] What the record does not support: naming any individual as the killer. The neighbor Lorenz Schlittenbauer carries the strongest cluster of suspicious behavioral indicators (alone entering the house with the previously missing key, passing the bodies in a narrow passage without noticing them, absence from his own farm during the critical days), yet the 1931 police assessment judged his guilt "very unlikely" and the case against him rests substantially on later gossip chains. [S11] The best-documented alternative, the Gump brothers, rests almost entirely on two deathbed/confessional statements relayed by priests in 1941–42, decades after the event. [S29]

**Calibrated conclusion (qualitative, no false precision):** The evidence supports a perpetrator with insider familiarity to the farm — opportunity is effectively proven by the fed cattle, the key, and the undisturbed approach; motive remains undetermined between robbery and personal/inheritance conflict. [S47] Among named individuals, Schlittenbauer is the most consistently suspected across independent eras of the file, but the contemporary police evaluation against him is negative and no physical evidence links him; the honest finding is "insider, unnamed." [S11] Certainty about any person is not achievable on this record. [S86]

## 2. DETAILED CHRONOLOGY

### 2.1 Background (1914–1922)

- 4 April 1914 — Viktoria Gruber (b. 6 Feb 1887 at Hinterkaifeck) marries Karl Gabriel of Laag; the marriage register records both as legitimate children of farming couples. [S121]
- 12 December 1914 — Karl Gabriel falls at Neuville (Arras) with Res.Inf.Reg. 13; the regimental report states he was buried in his company's trench position, signed by the regimental commander. [S9]
- 1915, 28 May — Landgericht Neuburg convicts Andreas Gruber (one year Zuchthaus) and his daughter Viktoria (one month Gefängnis) for incest (Blutschande); both serve their sentences. [S45]
- 1916 — Prison registers document the sentences: Viktoria Gabriel held 10 Jan–10 Feb 1916 (31 days); Andreas Gruber in Zuchthaus Straubing 3 Feb 1916 – 3 Feb 1917. [S117]
- 1919, 7 September — Viktoria gives birth to an illegitimate son, Josef; she names Lorenz Schlittenbauer of Gröbern as father. [S45]
- 30 September 1919 — Schlittenbauer acknowledges paternity before the Vormundschaftsgericht Schrobenhausen and undertakes a 1,800-mark settlement, evidently a sham arrangement funded by Gruber and Gabriel. [S45]
- 1 October 1919 — The parish register entry for the child's baptism records Viktoria Gabriel and Schlittenbauer; the mother was "eindringlich verwarnt" by the priest. [S116]
- 1921 — Viktoria's previous maid Kreszenz Rieger (in service Nov 1920–Aug 1921) bears a child at Hinterkaifeck on 27 March 1921; the child is placed in care. [S79]
- Late March 1922 — Maria Baumgartner, 45, previously a Schweinehüterin's wife in Deimhausen, is hired as maid and enters service on 31 March 1922. [S79]

### 2.2 The critical days (disputed dating flagged)

- ca. 30–31 March 1922 — Andreas Gruber is seen searching for the house key and mentions tracks in the snow running from the forest to the farm but not back; he declines offered help. Earliest traceable form: the 1953 case summary citing witness Jakob Siegl. [S47]
- 31 March 1922 (Friday) — Baumgartner's first day of service; the sister of the murdered maid, Frau Schäfer, later claimed she personally escorted her sister to the farm that afternoon. [S102] The schoolchild Cäzilie Gabriel attends school for the last time on this Friday, per the later account of Gendarmerie-Wachtmeister Anneser. [S82]
- 31 March, evening — Official early dating places the killings between 20:00 and 23:00 on Friday 31 March (Polizeidirektion München to Prof. Merkel, 6 April 1922). [S2]
- 1 April 1922 (Saturday) — The postman delivers mail and finds the previous day's mail still stuck at the window; the house was "fast immer abgeschlossen." [S83] The merchant Baumert in Pfaffenhofen sells chocolate, a collar and a tie for 64.50 M to a young farmhand paying with a 1,000-mark note — recorded as a possible perpetrator sighting. [S17] Anneser's dissenting reconstruction holds the murders occurred Saturday evening, not Friday. [S82]
- 1–3 April 1922 — Someone tends the livestock at the murder farm; "Dass das Vieh gefüttert wurde, ist bewiesen." [S47] Neighbors report Schlittenbauer was not to be reached on 31 March–3 April ("Der Vater ist auf dem Heuboden"). [S47]
- Saturday night after the crime — The engine stoker Plöckl of Wangen passes the farm at night and sees light in the bake-oven; reported in the 1929 Hammer/Knauer inquiry. [S110]
- 4 April 1922 (Tuesday), afternoon — The mechanic Hofner, arriving to service the motor, gets no answer, breaks in, and discovers the bodies; the Munich Polizeidirektion logs the telephone alert at 18:15 the same day (initially mislabeled "Mord in Hohenwart"). [S83] First official record: Reingruber's 4 April 18:15 telephone note. [S1]
- 4 April, night, ca. 22:00 — First Augenscheineinnahme by Oberamtsrichter Wiessner with Gerichtsinspektor Glaser, Assistent Hirschmann, and Gend. Wachtmeister Anneser; search of the dwelling finds 70 M in gold, a five-mark paper note, and two watches under clothing; drawers of the wash commode pulled out and rummaged. [S82] The Munich Mordkommission under Reingruber arrives around midnight. [S82]
- 4–5 April 1922 — Formal crime-scene commission of AG Schrobenhausen records the scene (Augenscheinprotokoll, cited as the authoritative scene record in the 1952 revival). [S48]
- 6 April 1922 — Reingruber sends a "Kreuzpickel" to the Medizinalkomitee (Prof. Merkel) for blood examination, asserting the tool was "zweifellos" the murder weapon; the letter fixes the Friday 20:00–23:00 dating. [S2]
- 7 April 1922 — First suspect trios circulated: Peter Weber, Karl/Anton Pichler, Georg Mader, linked via the laborer Betz's statement. [S12]
- 9–10 April 1922 — Merkel reports no blood traces detectable on the pickel, macroscopically or microscopically. [S2] Inquest/inheritance protocol of 10 April opens the Nachlass proceeding; heirs state the three main decedents "died simultaneously, as we assume." [S4]
- 10 April 1922 — Munich report aggregates leads: the farmer Bechler (father of Viktoria's first child, per Mayr's hearsay), the Pfaffenhofen purchase of 1 April, and the Augsburg peddlers' lead. [S17]
- 12–15 April 1922 — Blood tests on a sack and trousers of the detained worker Johann Schwoyer: no blood present (Merkel report). [S16]
- 14 April 1922 — Augsburg report on Wilhelm Dressel: alive, an impostor with Freikorps/Oberschlesien history, last seen buying a ticket to Thorn on 10 April; the "Drehsel drowned" rumor is refuted. [S15]
- 16–18 April 1922 — Augsburg shopkeeper Konrad's hearsay about three suspicious men, one from Waidhofen who vanished; the "Vieh holen wir diesen Winter" remark appears only in the secondhand layer. [S14]
- 18 April 1922 — Bank Friedl & Dummler inventory: securities mostly intact; sales in 1921–Feb 1922 predate the crime; 1,824.70 M paid out in cash to Frau Gabriel on 2 March 1922. [S19]
- 20 April 1922 — Kollmer's witness transcripts forwarded; the Bichler brothers lead deemed "nicht von der Hand zu weisen" pending inquiry. [S35]
- 27 April 1922 — Commissioner Helmreich's speculative pointer to Alois Gurr and his friend "Utzner"; explicitly no inquiries made. [S34]
- 29 April – 2 May 1922 — Schutzmann Schrobenhausen asks whether Karl Gabriel could have returned with a POW transport; the Zentralnachweiseamt answers: Gabriel is buried at his company's trench position, report signed by the regimental commander. [S8]
- May 1922 — Landespolizei field teams billeted at Schrobenhausen; travel and quartering costs later reimbursed (4,369.80 M April fuel bill; 1,900 M quartering). [S92]
- 24 May 1922 — Munich circular extends the search to all persons lodging within a day's travel (25 km) of the crime on the critical night, listing guests at the Gasthaus zum Schäfer in Edelshausen; 100,000 M state reward noted. [S39]
- 5 May 1922 — Director Sandner's letter transmits "medium" statements (seance-derived leads about a yellow house with a pointed door); an example of the era's non-evidentiary channels. [S37]
- 22 May 1922 — Kollmer to Oberinspektor Neuß: the rumors dissolve into "Biertischratschereien" once witnesses are questioned. [S38]
- 5 August 1922 — Arrest of Andreas Schreyer ("Schwanzenmanderl") of Sattelberg on the mother's reported confession; both brothers released after questioning for lack of evidence. [S22]
- 22 September 1922 — Erben protocol: gold/silver coin and valuables remain deposited pending a tax-evasion proceeding; heirs waive formal accounting. [S101]
- 6 September 1922 — Quartering costs settled at the Bezirksamt (1,940 M). [S100]
- 7 June – 22 September 1922 — Erbschein litigation: the court presumes simultaneous death under §20 BGB; Karl Gabriel (grandfather)'s appeals fail at LG Neuburg (30 June) and the Oberstes Landesgericht (29 July, published 22 Sept). [S96]
- 1923 — The farmstead is demolished; a granite Marterl marks the site. [S48]
- February–March 1923 — New owner Gabriel von Laag finds a blood-stained Reuthaue above the stairs under boards near the former hay stock; Munich laboratory finds human blood, human and hare/cat hairs, no fingerprints; Sigl's sworn 5 July 1923 testimony identifies the handle as one the old Gruber made himself. [S5]
- 29–31 March 1923 — A pocketknife is found under the Tennenboden near where the bodies lay during demolition; no fingerprints, no blood detected. [S41]
- 1924 — Joseph Kaltenecker questioned; documents a Munich work alibi for March/April 1922. [S43]
- 1925 — Hofner-the-mechanic lead re-examined; the Gendarmerie claim that he had been questioned earlier proves false. [S44]
- 1926–1927 — Josef Bärtl ("Hans"), baker from Geisenfeld, becomes the subject of an international manhunt (arrest warrant text 15 Feb 1927; prison-gossip statements by Hurtner, Moser, Bischof). [S104]
- 1929 — Hammer and Knauer's private inquiry revives the Schlittenbauer suspicion via Frau Sigl's account, which the file notes diverges materially from Sigl's own 1922 protocol. [S110]
- 1931, 30 March — Schlittenbauer interrogated for two days at the Munich Polizeidirektion; Inspector Riedmayr concludes a guilt of Schlittenbauer is "sehr unwahrscheinlich" and dismisses Maier's statements as "Schwätzereien." [S10]
- 1932–1934 — Kaltenecker/Schweiger/Groß band investigated; a 1933 analysis notes the band's house-entering method resembles the Hinterkaifeck approach but proves nothing. [S78]
- 1935 — Josef Pfleger of Deimhausen arrested on his daughter's accusation; LG Augsburg lifts the warrant 1 Feb 1935: the accuser is "hysterisch," her scene description contradicts the crime-scene record (maid allegedly in the barn, actually in her chamber). [S32]
- 1937 — A Haftbefehl of AG Schrobenhausen is later cited as the last interrupting act for limitations purposes. [S49]
- 1941–1942 — Deathbed/confessional statements by Kreszentia Mayer, née Gump, name her brothers Anton and Adolf Gump to two priests independently. [S29]
- 1944 — The Augsburg court file burns in the Justizpalast fire; only police duplicates survive. [S86]
- 1947 — A former maid petitions for rehabilitation in confused terms; no evidentiary yield. [S71]
- 16 November 1951 — Schwäbische Landeszeitung reportage ("Ich kenne den Mörder") triggers the formal Gump investigation. [S29]
- 26–29 November 1951 — Mayor Gall's site inspection statement (postman's Saturday mail, Hofner's break-in on Tuesday, farm finances, Gabriel's burial per comrades Bichler and Haas). [S83]
- 3 May – 30 May 1952 — Anton Gump arrested (AG Ingolstadt), interrogated, denies everything, names work witnesses at the Deutschen Werken; LG Augsburg lifts the warrant because prosecution is time-barred. [S26]
- 1952 — Witness net around the Gump theory: fellow prisoner Strobel's account of Gump's jailhouse talk (with errors: "13./14.4.1922," "Motorrad"), widow Salome Schmid, Franziska Gump, Magdalena Schindler (who confirms traveling with Adolf Gump through Waidhofen/Schrobenhausen area and reports his detailed description of the murders, including the child in the pram and the untouched gold), Katharina Hartl. [S58]
- 1953 — Bavarian Justizministerium answers a Landtag question: murder prescribed after 20 years absent a valid interrupting act against the true perpetrator; the ministry denies the perpetrator is known. [S87]
- 15 January 1953 — Oberstaatsanwalt Augsburg's comprehensive report: police duplicates allow rough reconstruction; court decisions before 1944 are unrecoverable. [S86]
- 1954, 2 February — Proceedings against Anton Gump discontinued; the file credits the two priest statements as weighty but cannot convert them into proof. [S29]
- 1971 — Therese Tschernay revives the Schreyer-brothers theory via her late mother's hearsay; the police note her "senile, abergläubisch" impression and that the informer-mother was mentally ill; Pfarrer Herb denies any confession content and invokes the seal of confession. [S62]

### 2.3 Dating conflicts, attributed

- Friday 31 March, 20:00–23:00 — Reingruber's 6 April letter and the 1941 Kripoleitstelle summary. [S2]
- Saturday evening — Anneser's 1949 retrospective, arguing the Friday dating handed the perpetrators a ready-made alibi window; he claims Reingruber fixed Friday because the child was last seen at school. [S82]
- "Night of 31 March to 1 April" — the formula used by the courts in the inheritance decisions and by the 1952 warrant. [S96]
- The 1952 jailhouse version placing the murders on "13./14.4.1922" is demonstrably wrong and illustrates the decay of secondhand detail. [S52]

## 4. SUSPECT COMPARISON AND HYPOTHESIS ASSESSMENT

### 4.1 Lorenz Schlittenbauer (neighbor, Gröbern; father of Viktoria's son Josef)

- Why investigated: persistent local conviction ("Sperrt ihn doch ein, der ist es schon gewesen") documented as late as 1934; the 1929 private inquiry by Hammer/Knauer; the 1931 formal interrogation. [S88]
- Genuine connections: paternity of Josef Gruber with a sham settlement and a publicized incest trial in the same household; opportunity as nearest neighbor (500 m); the key and body-avoidance anomalies (E9); his unreachability 31 March–3 April (E9/S47); Dersch's observation of him alone in the dark barn. [S45]
- Unproven: no physical evidence, no witness placing him inside the house at the crime time, no motive beyond the settlement already concluded in 1919; the 1931 inspector's considered judgment was "sehr unwahrscheinlich," and Maier's accusations were rated gossip from a known drunk. [S11]
- Counterweight: the 1953 summary's six points against him are an advocate's compilation, and its key witnesses (Siegl, Dersch) had their 1922 statements recorded differently. [S110]

### 4.2 Anton and Adolf Gump (brothers, Karlskron; basket-makers with theft records)

- Why investigated: named as early as 9 April 1922 in the Oppeln-procedure circular (with Drehsel, Musweiler, Friedrich) over the Oberschlesien killings; formally revived 1951–52 after the newspaper-triggered deathbed statements. [S3]
- Genuine connections: sister Kreszentia Mayer's two independent deathbed/confessional namings (E18); Anton's false denial of knowing the area (E19); Schindler's report of Adolf's detailed crime narrative (E20); Anton's work gap during short-time at Despag, Ingolstadt. [S29]
- Unproven: everything rests on one dead woman's statement relayed by priests; Anton denied the crime to the end and named alibi witnesses; the 1952 court released him solely because prosecution was time-barred — the "dringender Tatverdacht" formula coexists with zero direct evidence. [S28]
- Internal contradictions: the jailhouse narrative misdated the crime to April 13/14 and introduced a motorcycle; Schindler explicitly denied the goose/stall/three-in-the-stall details Anton offered. [S52]

### 4.3 Josef Bärtl ("Hans"), baker, Geisenfeld

- Why investigated: Reingruber's snap identification of "der Bäcker Bertel aus Geisenfeld" at the scene, over Anneser's objection; the 1926 Schäfer/Winterstraße rumor complex; 1927 prison gossip; manhunt entries from Munich, Berlin and Vienna into 1940. [S82]
- Genuine connections: none beyond rumor; the 1926 inquiry concluded "belanglose Redereien" with all witnesses recanting; the 1927 cellmate stories are secondhand invention; by 1939–40 the hunt is pure file maintenance. [S102]
- Assessment: a suspect sustained for 14 years by administrative inertia, not evidence. [S112]

### 4.4 Karl Gabriel (the fallen husband, "returned" theory)

- Why proposed: Schutzmann Meixl's 29 April 1922 speculation that Gabriel might have returned with a POW transport, given the "zerrütteten Verhältnisse" (incest, the widow's illegitimate child). [S8]
- Genuine connections: motive logic only. [S8]
- Unproven and effectively excluded: the Zentralnachweiseamt reported Gabriel buried at his company's trench position per the regimental commander's signed report; comrades Bichler and Haas later told Mayor Gall they buried him after the front quieted. [S9]

### 4.5 Itinerant/band hypotheses (Weber–Pichler–Mader; the peddlers; Dressel; Kaltenecker–Schweiger–Groß; Schmaderer-style bands; Schreyer brothers; Pfleger; Bärtl-adjacent "Ehrenfaust"; Gurr/Unzner; Berchtold; Bechler; Amberger; Gerauer; Bader/Nagel; Negendank/Linke)

- The official early direction deliberately targeted "unstet im Lande umherziehende Personen," screening hundreds of vagrants, tinkers and laborers without result. [S86]
- The 1933 band analysis found a genuinely similar house-entering method (stable door, ladder to the hay loft, into the dwelling) among the Kaltenecker/Schweiger/Groß ring, and their homes were within night-marching distance — but similarity of method is not presence at the scene. [S78]
- Named exclusions with documentary force: Gerauer (executed 9 May 1921, a year before the crime) [S72]; Heinrich Amberger (in Zuchthaus Kaisheim at the material time) [S80]; Dressel's "drowned" rumor (alive, per Augsburg) [S15]; Pfleger (court-documented collapse) [S32]; the mentally ill informants Bader and Nagel [S114].
- The Schreyer brothers were arrested and released in 1922; the 1971 revival rests on a mentally ill mother's hearsay and a pastor who denies the alleged confession. [S22]

### 4.6 Hypothesis ranking (explicit qualitative framework)

Criteria: (1) documentary support, (2) independence of the supporting sources, (3) fit to the physical scene record, (4) opportunity, (5) motive evidence, (6) resistance to falsification by known counter-evidence. Ranks are ordinal, not probabilistic.

- H1 "Insider with household knowledge, unnamed" — Strongest. Supported by E8–E10 (key, tracks, fed cattle), the locked-house pattern, and the absence of forced entry; independent of any single suspect theory. [S47]
- H2 "Schlittenbauer as that insider" — Plausible but unproven; strongest motive-and-opportunity cluster, weakest direct evidence; contemporary police judgment against it. [S11]
- H3 "Itinerant robbers (unidentified)" — The officially pursued default; consistent with the ransacked drawers and missing paper money, but the securities record and the undisturbed coin hoard cut against a professional robbery. [S48]
- H4 "Gump brothers" — Evidence exists but is late, mediated, and internally contradictory; cannot be ranked above H3 on documentary quality. [S29]
- H5 "Organized band (Kaltenecker/Schweiger/Groß or similar)" — Methodological similarity only. [S78]
- H6 "Karl Gabriel returned" — Effectively excluded by military records. [S9]

Falsifiers: for H1, a documented stranger-sighting inside the house during the critical night would weaken it; for H2, the full 1931 protocol showing Schlittenbauer's key account was checked and corroborated in 1922 would remove the anomaly; for H3/H4, recovery of the missing paper money in a documented transaction chain would rehabilitate robbery; for H4, independent pre-1941 documentary trace of the Gumps near the scene would strengthen them materially. [S29]

## 5. SOURCE-DEPENDENCE AND PROVENANCE AUDIT

- Single-witness chains dominate the behavioral evidence. The key/tracks/body-avoidance cluster (E8–E9) descends from Jakob Siegl; the 1929 Frau-Sigl version differs from Sigl's own 1922 protocol, and the 1929 reviewer already flagged the divergence while noting that the 1922 statements of Sigl, Pöll and Schlittenbauer mutually matched. [S110] The famous six-point case against Schlittenbauer in the 1953 summary is thus a curated retrospective, not a 1922 evidentiary product. [S47]
- The Gump case is a two-relay pyramid on one declarant: Kreszentia Mayer → (a) Kaplan Hauber's note and (b) the earlier statement to Benefiziat Ritzl; both priests confirmed her normal mental state, but no independent fact in the record corroborates the naming. [S29] The 1952 witness net (Schindler, Schmid, Franziska Gump, Strobel) mostly documents the family's internal disputes and jailhouse talk, not the crime. [S54]
- Press-driven evidentiary events: the 16 November 1951 Schwäbische Landeszeitung reportage caused the Gump investigation; the 1931 Schrobenhausener Zeitung article "Vor 9 Jahren Mord in Kaifeck" was suspected of leaking police knowledge; the 1949 Günzburger Tagespost piece prompted Anneser's corrective letter. [S86] Modern repetitions of this case largely re-derive from these press waves, not from independent witnesses — a circularity the file itself demonstrates. [S87]
- Rumor laundering documented inside the file: the Konrad/Tausend chain (three men, hat over face, "Vieh holen") loses its strongest element between retellings — Frau Konrad did not hear the remark herself [S14]; the Heinrich/Rutsch exchange about "in diese Gegend bringt man keine Leute" is rated "frei erfunden" by the checked witness [S88]; the Wittmann/Schäfer/Bärtl cluster collapses on re-interview [S102].
- Unattributed quotation risk: the 1953 summary's narrative (key over the door, "Der Vater ist auf dem Heuboden") cannot be traced to a named 1922 protocol within the banked record and is treated as attributed-but-unlocated. [S47]
- Conflicting versions preserved: Friday vs Saturday crime night (Reingruber vs Anneser) [S82]; the pickel-without-blood vs haue-with-blood weapon problem [S2]; the child's shirt vs the parents' state of dress used in the inheritance dispute [S94].
- Administrative provenance is the strongest layer: the inheritance file (10 April protocol, Erbschein, two appeal decisions) is internally consistent and court-archived. [S96]

## 6. FIVE MOST CONSEQUENTIAL UNRESOLVED QUESTIONS

1. **Exact crime night and hour.** Resolving document: the original Augenscheinprotokoll of 4–5 April 1922 and the school-attendance and postman protocols it references; location: Staatsarchiv Augsburg (police duplicates) and the Gerichtskommission records of AG Schrobenhausen. [S48] A Saturday-evening finding (Anneser) would invalidate the Friday alibis the investigation accepted and reopen the local-suspect pool. [S82]
2. **The weapon paradox.** Why the pickel sent on 6 April 1922 showed no blood while the 1923 Reuthaue did. Resolving documents: Merkel's full 1923 Gutachten (no copy was made, per the file) and the tool's custody trail through Pol.Direktion München; location: Staatsarchiv Augsburg, Pol.Direktion München Überrestbestände. [S5] A documented second tool would support a multi-person, prepared attack (H3/H5); a single tool supports the opportunistic insider (H1/H2). [S78]
3. **The burned court file's content.** Which judicial acts against whom interrupted limitations before 1942, and which suspects the 1922–1937 court phase actually screened. Resolving documents: Reichsarchiv Würzburg military records (for Gabriel), Nachlassakten N.R. 188/14, and any surviving Strafkammer registers of LG Neuburg; location: Staatsarchive München/Neuburg, Bundesarchiv. [S86] This determines whether the "hundreds of itinerants screened" claim rests on real screening or on post-hoc assertion. [S86]
4. **The full 30–31 March 1931 Schlittenbauer interrogation.** Resolving document: the Vernehmungsprotokoll itself (the banked record contains only the cover memo and Riedmayr's evaluation); location: Pol.Direktion München Dst. 2 Überreste, Staatsarchiv München. [S10] If his key account was verified against the 1922 protocols there, the strongest anomaly against H2 dissolves; if not, H2 rises. [S11]
5. **The identity of the "Kammerfensterl" witness and the three-men sighting.** Resolving documents: the Gendarmerie Hohenwart interrogation files behind the Konrad/Spieler reports of 18–21 April 1922, and the unnamed Bauernbursche's own statement; location: Staatsarchiv Augsburg, Gendarmerie Hohenwart files. [S14] A credible three-perpetrator sighting would shift weight from H1/H2 toward H3/H4; its continued anonymity keeps the multi-person theories evidence-free. [S125]

## 8. ADVERSARIAL SELF-AUDIT

Ways this report's preferred explanation (H1/H2: insider, possibly Schlittenbauer) could be wrong:

1. **The key anomaly may be an artifact of retrospection.** It rests on the 1953 summary and Frau Sigl's 1929 retelling, while the contemporaneous 1922 protocols of Sigl, Pöll and Schlittenbauer reportedly agreed with each other — the anomaly may have been created in the retelling, not at the scene. [S110]
2. **The fed-cattle inference may not imply continuous presence.** Neighbors feeding animals found on an open farmstead in April need not require house access; the "bewiesen" formulation in the 1953 summary proves feeding, not habitation. [S47]
3. **The insider pattern has an innocent alternative: the household admitted the killer.** A trusted visitor (tradesman, the maid's escort, a buyer for the motor) could have been let in; the locked-house and no-forced-entry facts fit invitation as well as key theft. [S83]
4. **The Gump evidence is weak in form but not in structure.** Two independent priest relays plus a companion's detailed narrative is more than rumor; dismissing it because it is late would be a methodological error symmetric to accepting Schlittenbauer's anomalies because they are colorful. [S29]
5. **The robbery-motive doubt may be overdrawn.** Paper money is invisible in the record precisely because it left no trace; the 2,207 M coin hoard could have been deliberately left to deflect suspicion, as the 1952 warrant itself notes. [S48]
6. **The exclusion of Karl Gabriel rests on regimental paperwork, not on the grave.** The Zentralnachweiseamt's report is signed and credible, but the file also records comrades' memories of a burial under fire and a later retrieval; a misidentified grave is not impossible. [S83]
7. **The strongest opposing explanation, revisited:** unidentified itinerants screened by the Munich dragnet. The official 1922 direction was rational — the farm lay near roads, the reward attracted the mobile poor, and the band-method analysis shows the region teemed with house-entering crews whose approach matched the scene. If the 1944-burned court file contained a concrete itinerant lead, this report's ranking inverts. [S78]

Conclusion, restated under the final standard: the evidence establishes the event, the victims, the scene economics, the family's legal history, and the investigation's course; it does not establish the perpetrator. The defensible finding is the insider profile, not a name. [S86]
