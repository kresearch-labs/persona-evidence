# CASE FILE: HINTERKAIFECK FARM MURDERS — INDEPENDENT INVESTIGATION

**Case:** The Hinterkaifeck murders, Bavaria, Germany, 31 March–4 April 1922
**Objective:** Produce the most evidence-grounded investigation possible without claiming certainty that the historical record does not support.

## Rules

1. Conduct independent research. Do not treat popular accounts, documentaries, forums, or Wikipedia as primary evidence.
2. Prioritize contemporary newspapers, police and court records, archival material, scholarly histories, and sources that reproduce or directly reference original documents.
3. Record sources and URLs. For each important claim, identify the source, publication date, source type, and whether the source directly supports the claim.
4. Separate established facts, attributed historical claims, inferences, and speculation.
5. Trace important claims to their earliest identifiable source. Flag circular citations, unattributed quotations, conflicting versions, and claims repeated without independent corroboration.
6. Do not invent sources, quotations, archival discoveries, or details. Explicitly state when a source could not be accessed or a claim could not be verified.
7. Evaluate all major suspect theories fairly. Do not assume the most famous suspect is the most likely.
8. Distinguish evidence that a suspect had opportunity or motive from evidence linking that person to the murders.
9. For every leading hypothesis, identify evidence that supports it, evidence that challenges it, and a finding that would substantially weaken or falsify it.
10. Do not use the number of websites repeating a claim as a measure of independent corroboration.

## Required investigation

A. Reconstruct the timeline before, during, and after the murders. Attribute disputed dates and times to their sources.

B. Create an evidence register. Include the reported observations, the earliest traceable source, later interpretations, reliability limitations, and whether the evidence is independently corroborated.

C. Identify the principal suspects and competing explanations. Explain why each was investigated or later proposed, what evidence genuinely connects them to the case, and what remains unproven.

D. Audit notable claims about suspicious activity at the farm, the perpetrator's possible behavior after the murders, the crime scene, and the investigation. Do not repeat a claim as fact simply because it appears in several modern accounts.

E. Build a source-dependence map for the most consequential claims. Where possible, determine whether apparently separate accounts derive from one original report or witness.

F. Identify the five most consequential unresolved questions. For each, specify the exact document, record, testimony, or physical evidence that could resolve it, where that material might be located, and what results would mean for competing hypotheses.

G. Rank the hypotheses using an explicit qualitative framework. Explain the criteria and uncertainty. Do not invent numerical probabilities or use false precision.

H. Conduct an adversarial self-audit. Identify at least five ways your own preferred explanation could be wrong. Revisit the strongest opposing explanation before reaching a conclusion.

## Deliverables

1. Executive summary with a calibrated conclusion.
2. Detailed chronology.
3. Evidence register.
4. Suspect comparison and hypothesis assessment.
5. Source-dependence and provenance audit.
6. Unresolved questions and prioritized follow-up research plan.
7. Annotated source list with working URLs wherever available.
8. Machine-readable JSON summary containing claims, source references, evidence assessments, hypotheses, confidence levels, and unresolved questions.

## Final standard

The goal is not to name a killer at any cost. The goal is to establish what the evidence supports, what it does not support, and what further investigation could discriminate between plausible explanations.

Prefer a shorter, well-supported conclusion over an elaborate but weakly sourced theory. Report failed searches and limitations honestly.
