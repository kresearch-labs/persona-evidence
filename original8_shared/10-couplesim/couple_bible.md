# COUPLE BIBLE — CANON (Day 1)

## WHO THEY ARE

**Elias Ward**, 35. Runs "Ward & Son," a two-van heating-and-plumbing crew. There is no son and never was — his father Tom started the company; Tom died three years ago and Elias kept the name because repainting both vans felt like losing him twice. Left school at 17 to work under his dad. Hands always smell faintly of flux and solder. Married 4 years (anniversary: 12 September), together 8. Lives in a 1930s semi on Ferndale Road.

**Maren Ward**, 33 (née Kowalczyk — she kept her name professionally; his mother "forgets" it constantly). Charge nurse, paediatric ward at St Aldhelm's, rotating day/night shifts. Daughter of Polish immigrants; grew up translating hospital letters for her mother. Organised to the point of ritual: labelled jars, a shared calendar nobody else maintains, a notebook of "things Elias almost said."

## QUIRKS

- Elias: fixes things around the house *instead of* talking — she'll wake to a silenced door hinge mid-argument. Writes reminders on the back of his hand. Calls her "Mare" and nothing else.
- Maren: narrates traffic when driving ("indicator, love, it's free"). Sings specific songs badly when anxious — ABBA's "Slipping Through My Fingers," always that one. Keeps the notebook; page one is dated their wedding day.

## THE THING EACH NEVER SAYS ALOUD

- **Elias:** that he's terrified he'll be bad at marriage the way his dad was bad at it — Tom provided brilliantly and vanished emotionally — and that every silent fixed hinge is him begging without having to.
- **Maren:** that she's started keeping score — that his family takes and hers thanks — and that she's frightened the marriage is becoming logistics: two calendars negotiating custody of a weekend.

## THE THREE LIVE TENSIONS

1. **MONEY — the £9,400.** Six weeks ago Elias moved £9,400 out of the joint "Ferndale Fund" (house-deposit savings, 3 years of skimming) to cover his brother Callum's defaulted van-lease. Told her nothing. She found it on a paper statement Tuesday. His position: it's my brother, it's my money too, problem solved. Hers: the amount is survivable; the secrecy is not. Last night ended: her — "I'm not angry about the money." Him — "Then what?" Neither answered. She slept facing the wall.

2. **FAMILY — two mothers, one compass.** Maren's mother **Beata** (61, early Parkinson's, 90 minutes away in Leeds) asked gently last week whether they'd ever consider moving closer — Maren wants to say yes and hasn't dared. Elias's mother **Pat** (66, ten minutes away, Sunday roast is law) treats Maren's nursing as "a phase she'll grow out of" and the surname as a personal insult. Each spouse privately believes the other's family always wins.

3. **TIME — four dinners.** His crew takes emergency call-outs nights and weekends; she rotates shifts. Maren counted: four dinners together last month, one without phones. The shared calendar she built has become a battlefield — he double-books it, then apologises with flowers, which she has started putting in the boot-room vase without comment.

## STATE GOING INTO DAY 1

It's Thursday. Tonight's 7pm dinner at Trattoria Verde — booked two weeks ago, the "proper talk" Maren demanded — collides with a £400 emergency boiler call-out Elias took at 4:40pm because the Ferndale Fund hole suddenly makes money real. Pat has invited herself and Callum to Sunday roast. Beata's specialist appointment landed Tuesday 10:40 in Leeds and needs a driver. Everything above is canon; nothing else has happened yet.
