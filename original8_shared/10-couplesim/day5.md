## Scene 1 — 7:05–8:20am, two workplaces: the board comes back to earth

Grey drizzle, the frost a memory, and Van 2's board is mortal again — three callbacks from the weekend surge and a landlord warranty job, nothing heroic [boy/boy_in.md]. Half a frost morning still paid like a full week of radiators, and today the first £150 leaves the account under the only terms the Ferndale Fund has ever had with both names on them [boy/boy_in.md].

Three phones, in sequence. Callum, 8:05, to Elias — friendly as a labrador and not one word about money [boy/boy_in.md]:

Beata, 8:10, to Maren — options language new, bother-guilt in miniature [girl/girl_in.md]:

Grief has a hangover. He knows the drill: say the thing or it says you [boy/boy_in.md].

## Scene 2 — 9:01am, staff room and Van 2: the debit

At 9:01 exactly — she caught herself — Maren opened the banking app. £150 gone, both names on the mandate, no paper discovered after the fact [girl/answer.md]. It felt like being asked. She put the phone face-down and left it there [girl/answer.md].

In Van 2 the confirmation buzzes and Elias doesn't pocket it — he shows her, with a line [boy/answer.md]:

> "Look at that. Boringest miracle we own. Twenty years of me deciding money alone, and now the bank needs two signatures to breathe."

One small line on a statement; the biggest structural change since the hole [boy/boy_in.md]. Partnership or paperwork — she'd set out to notice which. Verdict, logged by 9:02: partnership, audited like a hawk — and she noticed that about herself too [girl/answer.md].

## Scene 3 — 9:20am, the rota rings

Mid-coffee, the flagged collision comes due: Tuesday's evening clinic doubled, can she do it [girl/girl_in.md]. Two years ago she'd have said yes to all of it and resented everyone by Thursday. She lets the ring finish before she answers [girl/answer.md]:

> "I'll take the first half of Tuesday's clinic — four to six, handover done, and then I'm on the A58. The late run isn't mine this week."

Not sorry, not explaining Leeds twice [girl/answer.md]. The day-2 fallback, honored on her terms: the ward gets half a clinic; the eve of her mother's options conversation stays hers. (Whether the rota accepts the counter-offer: NOT IN CONTEXT — no reply recorded.)

## Scene 4 — Mid-morning into afternoon, Van 2: two hard conversations, both doors held open

Between callbacks, the deadline. The lad's just been nice, which makes it harder — old-Elias reads "cheers mate" as a dodge and lets it rot until resentment writes the text for him. New trade: ask like it's a request, because Sunday proved requests land [boy/answer.md]:

> "Lad — good do yesterday, agreed. One thing before it goes stale: the £200. Pay by Christmas or say you can't — both fine, I mean it. But silence isn't an answer anymore, and I'm not chasing. Number or a no, whenever you've got it. Just say which. — E"

Not a warrant. A door, held open [boy/answer.md]. (Callum's reply: NOT IN CONTEXT — nothing recorded on the page.)

## Scene 5 — Evening, Ferndale Road kitchen: toast, tea, and the sentence

Home by six — the ward behaved [girl/girl_in.md]. Over toast Elias mentions Callum's deadline, and Maren pours the tea and says nothing about wording. The old instinct is to draft the text, soften the brother; the new machine says trust the man who set the terms out loud at a table full of witnesses [girl/girl_in.md]. Her mouth opens once [girl/answer.md]:

> "Yours to fire. I'll read whatever you send"

— and shuts. Hands off the shovel. It's the tenderest thing she does all day, and it looks like doing nothing [girl/answer.md].

Later, asked why she's still smiling at a bank notification, she pays the day's other bill [girl/answer.md]:

> "First money we ever moved together, that one. Reviewed whenever one of us says review — including me, apparently, at 9:01, like a hawk. I noticed that about myself. I'm keeping the number, retiring the audit."

And then, because tomorrow she's shared with a Leeds waiting room from 10:40 and unsaid things don't keep, he says the one the whole week has been circling [boy/answer.md]:

> "Last untold thing, tonight version: I'm scared of being Dad. Brilliant with vans, rubbish at rooms. There — said it out loud, so it can't ambush me in a hospital corridor wearing his voice."

The literal sentence — *terrified of being Tom*, in his own dialect — said out loud at a kitchen table, four days after ASK FIRST was inked on his hand [bible §Never-Says; day1.md]. Not at Tom's table with witnesses; at theirs, with the kettle. Saying things, it turns out, kills their interest rates [boy/answer.md].

## Scene 6 — How the day ended

Blue coat on the door for Leeds [girl/answer.md]. The longer sentence — *trying looks like asking now* — rides in her throat, saved for tomorrow's car, where nobody reports back [girl/answer.md]. In the notebook, the newest entry is still his actual words; she wrote slower, because they cost more, and tonight's would cost more still [girl/girl_in.md]. Lights out on Ferndale Road with the A58 ahead, Dr Okafor at 10:40, and a man who said the thing so it can't wear his father's voice in a corridor.
