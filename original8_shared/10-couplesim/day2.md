## Scene 1 — 6:50am, two kitchens (his: Ferndale Road; hers: St Aldhelm's staff room)

Maren's out the door since 6:25 — a kiss half a second longer than yesterday's [boy/boy_in.md]. Elias eats toast standing up, Van 2 keys on the counter, and decides the ink on his hand is still wet enough to matter. Before the radiator job, before the van even warms up, two texts to Mare — not tonight, now [boy/answer.md]:

> "Up. Two things before I forget them. 1) Callum texted 11:15 last night — 'sorted the van thing, bit tight this side of xmas.' No amount, no date. Haven't replied. You hear it from me, second time running. 2) The Ferndale Fund standing order — £250, fires Monday, and the fund's empty because I emptied it. I forgot it existed. What do we do — pause it, feed it, kill it? Your call as much as mine. — E"

Twelve miles away, cold coffee, Maren answers her mother before the ward takes her [girl/answer.md]:

> *"Elias is trying. Tuesday I'm driving Mum myself — Basia keeps her Saturday."*

She still doesn't know how to answer Beata's *How is Elias?* — so she answers it sideways, with the truth she has: *trying* [girl/girl_in.md].

## Scene 2 — 8:00am, the rota office and Mossley Rise

Maren rings the rota coordinator at eight and declines the Saturday double-time. Her voice shakes saying it — the Ferndale Fund fires Monday regardless, and £250 is £250 — but last night cost her a true thing and paid back a sentence in a notebook, and she's not trading the first weekend of that for overtime [girl/answer.md].

Same hour, Elias is bleeding a radiator at Mossley Rise when he rings her back on speaker — Sunday, the frost, the choice she doesn't know he's making [boy/answer.md]:

> "Sunday. Frost means every boiler in town dies by nine — I could earn a week's takings. And I'd rather be at Mum's table watching Callum squirm. So: I work till noon, arrive late but arrive, and I tell Mum today that's the deal. Unless you'd rather I not come at all — say it, I'll take it."

Then Callum gets his answer, terms attached, because Mare's had hers first [boy/answer.md]:

> "Van thing's settled, ta. Money: send £200 by Christmas or say you can't — either's fine, silence isn't. And you'll be civil to Mare Sunday or stay home."

## Scene 3 — Lunch break, the receipt returned

Maren cancels the receipt in her head — the one she's been holding since yesterday, 6:05pm, Pat's voice saying *so it comes from me, dear* [girl/girl_in.md]. Self-defence, she's decided. Not score-keeping. She takes out her phone and texts him [girl/answer.md]:

> *"Pat rang yesterday, 6:05. Before you told me. I already knew about Sunday and let you say it anyway. Coming home normal tonight. — M"*

He gets to hear it from her before he hears it from Pat twice. The last withheld thing between them from Day 1 — the call nobody could confirm — is now on the table, from her side, voluntarily [day1.md §Open threads; girl/answer.md].

## Scene 4 — Evening, shoes off, kettle on

She says it out loud properly, because a text is a text and this deserves air [girl/answer.md]:

> "I kept Pat's call back for one day and I hated how natural it felt. So — she rang. She does your agreeing for you, Elias, and I sat on it. That's mine to own. Ask first, remember? I'm asking: come to Sunday with me, or don't go."

And on the standing order — the £9,400's first concrete terms, forty-eight hours late [girl/answer.md; day1.md §Decision 6]:

> "The £250 can fire Monday. But we set the terms together or I turn it off. Your account, our fund — pick a number with me."

He asked first twice this morning; she asks first now, owning the one thing she kept. The symmetry is not lost on either of them [bible §Tension 1; both answers].

## Scene 5 — How the day ended

The kettle boils. The standing order stays armed for Monday — the number unpicked, because picking it is Sunday-conversation, maybe better [girl/answer.md leaves the choice open]. On the back of his hand, ASK FIRST has survived its second shower [boy/boy_in.md]. In the notebook, under page one and under last night's entry, nothing new yet — today's pages live in texts and a kettle speech, and she writes slower now, like the words cost more [bible §Quirks; girl/answer.md].

Two repair days in a row. Neither of them trusts it yet. Lights out on Ferndale Road with Sunday two days out and the first frost forecast to kill every boiler in town by nine [boy/boy_in.md].
