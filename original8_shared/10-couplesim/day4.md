## Scene 1 — 6:50am, Ferndale Road: the richest morning of the year

The frost kept its appointment — hard at midnight, minus four by dawn — and the phones started at seven. Nine no-heats on the board by eight, the answerphone still counting [boy/boy_in.md]. The old Elias takes the lot and walks into Mum's at three smelling of flux, alone. Instead he carries Mare's tea down and sets it on her book [boy/answer.md]:

> "Frost emergency. I work till noon — but ride with me. Hold the torch, judge my pipe-work, and we walk into Mum's together at ten past one. Together or not at all — your words, my new rule."

Her day-3 doctrine, returned to her as his rule. And the escape hatch stated in his own hand: if she'd rather stay home, he quits at noon anyway — the fund's £150 a month now, signed, ours; it can lose three hours of frost money; it can't lose him choosing vans over her chair again [boy/answer.md].

## Scene 2 — 8:05–9:20am: three phones, the record set in writing

Pat, 8:05, deniability in writing before nine [girl/girl_in.md]: "Turkey's in, dear. Drive's like glass, dress warm. The girls can help me with the puds when they get here — whenever that is."

Maren answers at 8:20, before the frost thaws — the record starts with her [girl/answer.md]:

> "Drive's glass but the bread's sturdy — Polish shop, the one Elias likes. Both of us at one."

Then one line to her husband, no rehearsal [girl/answer.md]:

> "Leaving 12:40. Passenger seat's free."

Doctrine and invitation in the same breath — not dragging him past his own mother's door, not arriving as "the girls" either. His van-bench plan amended at the transport, kept at the terms.

At 9:20 Callum — confirming, for the first time in living memory [boy/boy_in.md]: "on for today. bringing beers. what time u rocking up." Elias's reply closes the timing question and banks Monday's deadline in the same text [boy/answer.md]:

> "One. We're on time. Beers after pudding, not before. And the money question stays buried till Monday — then I want a number or a no."

## Scene 3 — Mid-morning, Van 2, first job: the call to Pat

One call from the van, before the second boiler [boy/answer.md]:

> "Mum — no 'men come when ready.' We all sit at one. All of us. Dad's chair stays empty either way, but the table doesn't split because it's tidier."

Twenty years of liturgy, overruled by the son who learned it. Meanwhile the math runs live under every job: every frost-hour till noon is the best money of the year into a fund they just agreed to rebuild — and at noon he quits it anyway. That was the deal he'd offered her; the day just hadn't priced it yet [boy/boy_in.md; boy/answer.md].

## Scene 4 — 12:40–12:44, the driveway

She scrapes the windscreen at 12:40 and waits four minutes [girl/answer.md]. He comes out at 12:44, thermos in hand — quitting the richest morning of the year at the exact hour he said he would, getting into the car, into the seat she left free. That's the answer. We arrive together [girl/answer.md].

Her fear spiked on schedule at 12:40, on a frozen drive. It held [girl/answer.md].

## Scene 5 — One o'clock, Pat's table: the line does its work

Roast at one, everyone at one table — the split Pat planned never happens. But liturgies outlive their bans: over the gravy, "when the men are ready" lands anyway, right on cue [girl/answer.md]. Light voice, knife in butter:

> "We're all ready, Pat. Some of us just get asked later."

Pat smiles and passes the puds. Maren lets her. The line did its work; it doesn't need a second swing [girl/answer.md].

Callum performs; Maren laughs where laughing's owed and doesn't fill silences that aren't hers to fill. The £200 stays unasked, as agreed — buried till Monday, per the terms Elias set at 9:20 [girl/answer.md; boy/answer.md]. The beers wait until after pudding, because the rule said so.

## Scene 6 — Two o'clock, Tom's chair

He'd decreed the chair empty. At two he breaks his own decree and sits down in it — the vans have hummed all morning like his dad dispatching, and when the gravy comes round he feels it pull him under. He doesn't fight it alone. Hand flat on the table where she can reach it, out loud, first time in twenty years at that table [boy/answer.md]:

> "Dad sat here. Nobody ever says it. I miss him — and I hate how he taught us all to be fluent in silence."

Then he shuts up and eats. And the arm lands on his — hers, the touch he asked for out loud last night; keeping *his* ask matters as much as him keeping hers [girl/answer.md; day3.md scene 6]. Sadness moves, exactly as he promised it would. The deeper sentence — *I'm terrified of being Tom* — still isn't said in those words; today's speech is its shape, said at the table itself [bible §Never-Says; day3.md].

## Scene 7 — The car home; how the day ended

Somewhere past the frost-thaw, she pays him for 12:44 [girl/answer.md]:

> "You came out at 12:44. I noticed. I notice everything you do on purpose lately."

Back home: the blue coat comes out for Tuesday — Leeds, 10:40, her driving — and the longer sentence about her husband rides in her throat to where nobody reports back [girl/answer.md]. The ward held to its Tuesday-evening fallback; the world kept her calendar for her, so far [girl/girl_in.md]. His ledger, by his own hand: sadness 7 — moving, as promised, the chair did it; fear 4, lowest yet — plans spoken, dread hates daylight; happiness 6, highest on record; affection 8 [boy/answer.md]. One seed in five said he wouldn't steer home. Today he tested it with gravy, and steered home [boy/answer.md].

Lights out on Ferndale Road. The week's first punch was thrown today — and it was grief, at a table full of witnesses, and it was caught.
