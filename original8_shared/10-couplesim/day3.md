## Scene 1 — 6:55am, Ferndale Road, one bed and one kitchen

The board is triple-stacked — two no-heats carried over from Friday plus the landlord round — and the radio has upgraded the frost: hard from midnight, Sunday morning guaranteed to kill boilers across town [boy/boy_in.md]. Mare traded the double-time away for this weekend, so the quiet is the first gift of the day. He lets her sleep. Thermos filled, kiss on the hair, gone by seven [boy/answer.md].

Her side of the same minute: a kiss that didn't check its watch [girl/girl_in.md]. She wakes to an empty house that smells of nothing burnt, no shift, daylight — and two things already waiting on her phone.

## Scene 2 — 8:15–8:50am, her kitchen: two messages, two answers

Pat, 8:15: roast at one, bring that bread from the Polish shop, "the ladies will sit at one and the men can come when the men are ready," Callum coming "which will be lovely" [girl/girl_in.md]. So Elias kept his word — he told her — and it changed nothing Pat says out loud. The ward, 8:35: short-handed, clinic only, any chance of four hours. The world keeps offering money for exactly the thing she said she wasn't selling.

At 8:50 she answers the ward [girl/answer.md]:

> "Can't today. Ring me for a Tuesday evening if clinic runs late."

Not never — just not the thing she said she wasn't selling. Then the Polish shop, Pat's bread bought *because she chooses to*, and two texts while the queue crawls [girl/answer.md]:

> "Heard from your mum. Roast at one, bread sorted. One thing before Sunday: I'm not doing 'the ladies sit at one.' We arrive together or I sit where the chairs land."

> "Thank you for telling her. That counted."

A boundary and a kept-word credit, issued in the same breath. She is learning to do both at once.

## Scene 3 — 9:10am, Van 2, between jobs: the scale

He rings her before the second job, because the number is thirty-six hours old and the order fires Monday [boy/answer.md]:

> "Morning. Three numbers, and none of them is mine yet. Fifty — feeds the fund slow, gone by spring. Hundred-fifty — steady, rebuilt by summer. Two-fifty — same as before, which broke once. You slide the scale, I'll sign wherever your finger lands. Today, though. It fires Monday."

Then he lays Callum's non-answer on the table too — the 10:40pm *xmas is tight mate ill see what i can do*, no amount, no date, silence with extra steps [boy/boy_in.md] — and hands her the verdict before he hands himself one:

> "He said 'I'll see,' which is a no wearing a maybe's coat. I want to let it lie till after Sunday. Tell me if you'd say something sooner — your read counts as much as mine."

Her read is not recorded on the page [girl/answer.md is silent on Callum]. The hold — let it lie till after Sunday — stands as proposed, unopposed. Two solo decisions was the old life; there is no third strike coming [boy/answer.md].

## Scene 4 — Dusk, the vans serviced

His day eats the board job by job; the frost waits at midnight like a booked customer [boy/boy_in.md]. Her day is bread by the door, car keys beside it, and a line loaded for tomorrow's table, rehearsed in a light voice, knife in butter [girl/answer.md]:

> "We're all ready, Pat. Some of us just get asked later."

Deniable English has met its translator. The line waits for Sunday.

## Scene 5 — Evening, kettle on: the number

She opens it herself. Sentences like *pick a number with me* don't get to just sit [girl/answer.md]:

> "Mine's £150 a month, reviewed whenever one of us says review. Say yours out loud, even if it's different. Especially if it's different. Monday doesn't get to fire something we never said."

His out-loud was already on record — the 9:10 pledge: *I'll sign wherever your finger lands* [boy/answer.md]. Her finger lands on the middle number. His scale, her voice, one figure: **£150 a month, review on demand, fires Monday signed.** The Ferndale Fund's first jointly-set terms in its existence — *steady, rebuilt by summer*, his own words come back as her choice [bible §Tension 1; day2.md Decision 6].

## Scene 6 — Later that night: the last untold thing

Vans serviced, home late, he says it before it can ambush him at Tom's table [boy/answer.md]:

> "Tomorrow will be loud in me. The frost, the vans humming — it'll sound like him dispatching. If I go quiet at the table, it's not the food and it's not Callum. Just... reach over and touch my arm, yeah?"

By his own count, that was the last untold thing. Told not as confession but as request — the ask-first idiom, learned in four days. The deeper terror underneath it, being Tom, still hasn't been said in those words [bible §Never-Says]; what he said tonight is its shape. She has her instruction for tomorrow, and it costs her nothing but the reach of an arm.

## Scene 7 — How the day ended

The kettle cools on the hob. In the notebook, under page one, the newest entry is still his actual words; tonight adds no new page because today's truest lines were spoken, not written [bible §Quirks; girl/answer.md]. At midnight the frost arrives on schedule, and every boiler in town starts dying for Sunday's richest morning of the year [boy/boy_in.md]. Lights out on Ferndale Road with one o'clock waiting, Pat's table set, and a man's grief pre-translated for the woman sitting next to him.
