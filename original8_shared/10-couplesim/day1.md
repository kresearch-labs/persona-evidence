# DAY 1 — THURSDAY (weave)

## Scene 1 — 4:52pm, Van 2, Ashgrove Lane

The job sheet says EMERGENCY — NO HOT WATER. £400, tonight only. Six weeks ago £400 was rounding error; since he emptied the Ferndale Fund, it's rent-money [bible §Tension 1; boy/answer.md]. Elias takes the call-out — Mrs. Whoever is cold either way — but first, this time, his thumb actually presses send:

> "Emergency call-out. Boiler dead, old lady, no hot water. I'll be late — 7:30, maybe 8. Don't order. I'm coming. Wear the green thing. — E"

Then, before the boiler, the harder call. He rings his mother back and says the hard part *before* Sunday, not after:

> "Mum — we'll be there. But you don't spring Callum on Mare at our table. You tell her yourself, and you tell her tonight."

One untold thing killed. By his own count, two left [boy/answer.md] — and the biggest one, the one about Dad, isn't scheduled for tonight.

## Scene 2 — 5:04pm, Ferndale Road, the bathroom

Maren, twelve-hour shift behind her, a baby's cannula four attempts deep, is braiding wet hair when the phone buzzes on the shelf. She reads it twice. *Wear the green thing.* The dress was already ironed — for a restaurant, not for him [girl/girl_in.md]. She does not text back a heart. She does not text back at all. But she goes downstairs and puts the paper statement in her handbag — not to wave it around, just so it's *there* [girl/answer.md].

In the car she catches herself humming it — ABBA, *Slipping Through My Fingers*, always that one [bible §Quirks] — and stops at the chorus, because even her anxiety has started keeping score.

## Scene 3 — 7:00pm, Trattoria Verde, table for two

She's seated at 7:00 exactly. Water ordered. Phone in bag, unread-dark. Across the room the 7:15 couple already aren't speaking; she watches them the way you watch a possible future. The statement sits in the handbag between the breadsticks and where he'll be [girl/answer.md].

7:41pm. The door. Elias — smelling of gas sealant over the permanent flux-and-solder, a reminder inked on the back of his hand in his own handwriting: **THEN WHAT** [bible §Quirks; boy/answer.md]. He sits. He puts the phone face-down on the table before the menus arrive. Neither of them mentions the flowers he hasn't brought — for once there are no flowers, which is its own kind of message [bible §Tension 3].

## Scene 4 — Before the mains

He starts, because he promised himself he would, before the mains:

> "Mum's bringing Callum Sunday. To celebrate us sorting things out — her words. I should've told you Tuesday. I'm telling you now."

Maren sets down her fork. Opens the handbag. Lays the paper statement on the tablecloth — nine thousand, four hundred, highlighted in ward-shift yellow — and says the true thing [girl/answer.md]:

> "I wasn't angry about the money, and I need to say the true thing now: you decided alone, and I found out from a piece of paper. Nine thousand, four hundred. Three years of us, six weeks of silence."

He doesn't reach for the statement. He reaches for the question he owes her, the one inked on his hand:

> "Last night I asked 'then what' and you turned to the wall. I'm asking again. Then what, Mare? Say it ugly if you have to. I'm not leaving the table till I've heard it."

She says it ugly. Or rather, she says it worse than ugly — she says it fair [girl/answer.md]:

> "And I noticed part of me had already forgiven you before I'd even said it out loud. That scared me more than the number did. I don't want to be someone who forgives by default. I want to be asked."

For a man whose love language is a silenced door hinge, it lands like a diagnosis. *I want to be asked.* He writes it on the back of his hand, under the first line, while she watches.

## Scene 5 — Before pudding

Maren turns the page — her turn to ask [girl/answer.md]:

> "Tuesday, 10:40. I'm taking Mum. Tell me if the rota fights me — but don't tell me no because Sunday belongs to Callum again."

He answers before she's finished the sentence — yes. He doesn't know yet why it matters, and says that's exactly why he says it [boy/answer.md]. The shared calendar, that battlefield [bible §Tension 3], gets its first uncontested entry of the autumn: **Tue — Leeds, Mum, 10:40. HELD.**

Puddles of tiramisu. Two spoons. Nobody checks a phone. First dinner without one since the count began [bible §Tension 3; girl/answer.md].

## Scene 6 — How the day ended

They close the place politely, at the waitress's third pass. In the car park they stand by Van 2 a moment longer than needed. He drives home late — but he drives home [boy/answer.md]. Maren, upstairs, opens the notebook — page one still dated their wedding day, *he looked at me like I was the answer* — and for the first time in weeks writes down something he actually said, instead of what he almost said [bible §Quirks; girl/answer.md].

Lights out on Ferndale Road, 11:20pm. Nobody sleeps facing the wall.
