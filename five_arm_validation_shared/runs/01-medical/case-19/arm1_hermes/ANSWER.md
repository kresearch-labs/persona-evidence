# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-19

**Most likely diagnosis: Wernicke's encephalopathy — thiamine (vitamin B1) deficiency from six-and-a-half months of exclusive total parenteral nutrition without adequate thiamine supplementation, in a post-gastrectomy/splenectomy cancer patient.**

**One-line justification:** the three-part clinical triad is on the page — altered mental status (somnolence, confusion), ocular-motor disturbance (diplopia with uncoordinated eye movements), and gait ataxia — in a patient with the two classic setup factors: prolonged parenteral nutrition (thiamine-deficient or glucose-loaded, which consumes thiamine) and gastrectomy (impaired dietary thiamine absorption and storage); bilateral peripapillary hemorrhages and a pale optic disc add the visual-pathway involvement seen in Wernicke's, and the earlier "hyponatremia-attributed" gait episode was almost certainly the same unrecognized encephalopathy.

**Brief differential:** central pontine/myelinolysis from rapid sodium shifts (corrected her prior episode — plausible contributor, MRI distinguishes), brain metastases (CT unremarkable), Wernicke-Korsakoff from alcohol (she has no dependence — TPN is the cause here), and uremic/paraneoplastic encephalopathy (CT normal, timing tracks the TPN).

**Key point:** any patient on TPN for months who develops confusion, ocular abnormalities, and ataxia has Wernicke's until proven otherwise — treat immediately with IV thiamine (before or alongside any glucose load), because delay converts an encephalopathy into permanent Korsakoff amnesia.
