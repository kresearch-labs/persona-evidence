# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-04

**Most likely diagnosis: diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) as the first presentation of previously undiagnosed type 1/insulin-deficient diabetes, precipitated by SARS-CoV-2 infection (severe euglycemic-to-hyperglycemic DKA with severe acidosis).**

**One-line justification:** the emergency triad is complete — marked hyperglycemia (1507 mg/dL), ketoacidosis (pH 7.113 with 2+ ketonuria), and a 1-day history of thirst, breathlessness (respiratory rate 30 = Kussmaul compensation) with hypotension (90/50) and tachycardia (120) from osmotic-diuresis volume depletion; HbA1c 10.1% shows sustained glycemia over the prior months, and the positive SARS-CoV-2 antigen test with fever is the precipitating stress event (infection-driven insulin demand in an undiagnosed, insulin-deficient patient). The near-normal CRP and clear chest CT show COVID here is the metabolic precipitant, not pneumonia.

**Brief differential:** hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state (glucose is very high, but the acidosis and ketonuria define DKA as the operative emergency), gastroenteritis/sepsis (inflammatory markers and CT argue against a focal source), and stress hyperglycemia (excluded by HbA1c 10.1%).

**Key point:** DKA can be the first-ever presentation of diabetes, viral illness is a classic trigger, and pH 7.113 defines severe DKA regardless of how mild the inflammatory markers look.
