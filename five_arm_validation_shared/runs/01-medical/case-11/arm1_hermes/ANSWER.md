# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-11

**Most likely diagnosis: neurosyphilis — general paresis of the insane (syphilitic meningoencephalitis), presenting as a progressive dementia with personality change, psychosis, autonomic failure with neurogenic (trophic) ulceration, parkinsonism, and incontinence.**

**One-line justification:** the combination over three years of psychiatric-onset dementia (psychosis, hostility, hallucinations) with bradykinesia/parkinsonism, dysarthria, incontinence, global atrophy, a mildly lymphocytic CSF with positive Pandy and normal glucose, and — the telling somatic clue — **necrotic, unhealed, deeply seated trophic wounds on the knee and big toe** (perforating ulceration from loss of deep pain sensation, the tabes-dorsalis/neurosyphilis signature) is the textbook arc of general paresis; antipsychotic-resistant psychiatric decline plus atrophy plus trophic ulcers demands syphilis serology (RPR/VDRL + confirmatory FTA-ABS and CSF-VDRL) as the next step.

**Brief differential:** early-onset Alzheimer/frontotemporal dementia (wouldn't cause perforating trophic ulcers), prion disease (CSF too bland, course too long), neurosarcoidosis/vasculitis (markers negative, atrophy pattern), and diabetic/syringomyelic trophic ulcers with a separate dementia (glucose normal, no cord signs).

**Key point:** "psychiatric" onset plus incontinence plus parkinsonism plus painless perforating ulcers is neurosyphilis — the great masquerader whose test is cheap and whose treatment works; the pupil inequality and sluggish light reflexes fit late syphilis (Argyll-Robertson-adjacent findings).
