Most likely diagnosis: Neurosyphilis, presenting as general paresis (dementia paralytica / paretic neurosyphilis).

Justification: The subacute progressive neuropsychiatric decline (personality change, hallucinations, amnesia, aggression), dysarthria, incontinence, anisocoria with bilaterally impaired light reflexes (Argyll Robertson–type pupils), global cerebral atrophy, and mild CSF inflammatory changes (positive Pandy, slight pleocytosis) with otherwise unremarkable labs are the classic picture of general paresis due to Treponema pallidum infection of the CNS.
