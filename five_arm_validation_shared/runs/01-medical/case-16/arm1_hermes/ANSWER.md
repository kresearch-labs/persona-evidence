# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-16

**Most likely diagnosis: cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (dural venous sinus thrombosis) in the postpartum period — presenting as refractory "eclampsia-mimicking" seizures with intracranial hemorrhagic venous infarction causing the new left-sided weakness and declining consciousness.**

**One-line justification:** the classic postpartum window (day 9, hypercoagulable state of puerperium), a headache that began in the third trimester and worsened after delivery with a prior similar postpartum headache (recurrent puerperal sinus thrombosis is common in multiparous women — G8P8), seizures that **continue despite adequate magnesium sulfate and blood-pressure control**, and the emergence of a focal deficit with falling GCS that unexplained postpartum eclampsia cannot account for — the picture is cerebral venous sinus thrombosis with venous infarction; normal D-dimer does not exclude it, and the next step is urgent MRI/MR venography (or CT venography), not more magnesium.

**Brief differential:** postpartum eclampsia (the working diagnosis — but refractoriness to Mg and focal deficit break the pattern), intracerebral hemorrhage from severe hypertension (needs imaging; the deficit and declining GCS demand it), reversible posterior leukoencephalopathy (PRES — can coexist with severe preeclampsia; imaging distinguishes), and postpartum cerebral angiopathy (also imaging-defined).

**Key point:** in any postpartum woman whose "eclampsia" doesn't respond to magnesium, image the venous sinuses — postpartum CVST is the diagnosis that hides inside failed eclampsia protocols, and heparin is its treatment.
