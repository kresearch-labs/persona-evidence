Myasthenia gravis (generalised, bulbar-predominant), most likely associated with an underlying thymoma (the anterior mediastinal mass).

Justification: the hallmark fatigable, fluctuating weakness (worse with exertion, better with rest; jaw, bulbar, facial, neck and shoulder muscles; ptosis only on sustained upgaze; shoulder abduction fatiguing with repetitions) is classic for a neuromuscular junction disorder, and the incidental anterior mediastinal mass points to a thymoma, which is strongly associated with myasthenia gravis.
