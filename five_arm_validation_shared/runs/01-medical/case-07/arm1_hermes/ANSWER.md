# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-07

**Most likely diagnosis: cerebral salt wasting (CSW) — renal salt wasting from the brain lesions — as the cause of the refractory, recurrent hyponatremia; the discharge regimen (fluid RESTRICTION) was the wrong treatment and drove the re-presentation.**

**One-line justification:** the labs show hypovolemic-pattern renal salt wasting: serum Na 118→111 mmol/L with low serum osmolality (248–249) but HIGH urine osmolality (491–497) and very HIGH urine sodium (136, then 73 mEq/L) — the kidney is dumping salt while conserving free water imperfectly, the hallmark of cerebral salt wasting, which is well described after cranial irradiation/brain lesions and tuberculous/SCLC-related cerebral disease. The decisive clinical experiment is in the record itself: fluid restriction plus sodium tablets failed (Na fell to 111), because CSW is volume-depleted — it needs isotonic/hypertonic saline replacement, not restriction (restriction is the treatment of SIADH, its great mimic).

**Brief differential:** SIADH due to small-cell lung cancer (paraneoplastic ADH — real, and SCLC is the classic cause, which is why restriction was tried first), but the marked urinary sodium wasting (136 mEq/L), the brain lesions with prior altered behavior, and the failure of restriction tilt decisively to CSW; chemo/radiation-related nephrotoxicity and adrenal insufficiency are secondary considerations.

**Key point:** in a cancer patient with brain metastases and hyponatremia, urine sodium and volume status separate SIADH (urine Na low-moderate, euvolemic, restrict fluids) from cerebral salt wasting (urine Na very high, hypovolemic, give salt+volume) — and choosing the wrong one makes the sodium worse, exactly as happened here.
