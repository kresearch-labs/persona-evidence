# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-23

**Most likely diagnosis: parathyroid carcinoma — recurrent hyperparathyroidism with markedly rising PTH (160s → 253) and severe new hypercalcemia years after "parathyroidectomy", causing hypercalcemic crisis with renal impairment.**

**One-line justification:** an 82-year-old with surgically treated "chronic primary hyperparathyroidism" now presents with a calcium jump (10.5 → 14.9 mg/dL) accompanied by a disproportionate PTH rise (160s → 253 pg/mL) — the pattern of a recurrent, more aggressive parathyroid process rather than benign adenoma regrowth: benign primary hyperparathyroidism typically drifts; a several-fold calcium climb with vomiting, constipation, dehydration, and acute-on-chronic kidney injury (1.4 → 1.97 mg/dL) after years of stable biochemistry is the behavior of parathyroid carcinoma (or an aggressive atypical parathyroid adenoma — the operative distinction made at surgery). The decoy workup (normal vitamin D, mildly high calcitriol, normal PTHrP, negative SPEP/UPEP) excludes the common secondary causes, leaving the parathyroid tissue itself as the driver.

**Brief differential:** tertiary hyperparathyroidism from CKD (plausible, but CKD III with a 4-point calcium jump and years-stable PTH is atypical), humoral hypercalcemia of malignancy (PTHrP normal, electrophoreses negative), vitamin D/calcitriol excess (vitamin D normal), and thiazide/lithium effect (none recorded).

**Key point:** when "stable primary hyperparathyroidism" suddenly destabilizes with a steep calcium and PTH rise, think parathyroid carcinoma until the neck imaging and operative findings prove otherwise — and treat the hypercalcemia crisis aggressively while localizing.
