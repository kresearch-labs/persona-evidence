Trousseau syndrome — cancer-associated hypercoagulability (malignancy-associated thrombosis) causing multiple cerebral infarctions.

Justification: multiple acute infarcts in both cerebral hemispheres and the cerebellum with no cardioembolic source (normal ECG/Holter/echo), no arterial occlusion on MRA, markedly elevated D-dimer (28.2 µg/mL) and tumor markers (CA19-9 5594 U/mL, CA125 277.3 U/mL) after esophageal cancer surgery indicate a cancer-driven hypercoagulable state rather than DIC (criteria not met) or atherosclerotic/cardiac embolism.
