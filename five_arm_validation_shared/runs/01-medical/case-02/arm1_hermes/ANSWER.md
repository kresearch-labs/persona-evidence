# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-02

**Most likely diagnosis: acute aortic syndrome — aortic dissection (Type A involving the ascending aorta) causing aortic-regurgitation-related hypotension and dynamic left main/ostal coronary compromise, presenting as a non-ST-elevation acute coronary syndrome.**

**One-line justification:** a 58-year-old with atrial fibrillation presents with chest tightness and refractory hypotension (80 mmHg systolic unresponsive to fluids), a NEW ejection systolic murmur radiating to the carotids (dissection flap causing aortic-root distortion and flow murmur, or ostal narrowing creating a functional aortic-stenosis-like murmur), ST depression V4–V6 with normal troponin (early ischemia from dissection compromising coronary ostia), and — decisively — the team's own escalation to a **CT aortogram** after the echo excludes gross LV failure; the absence of radial–radial/radial–femoral delay or inter-arm difference doesn't exclude proximal Type A dissection, which classically lacks those peripheral signs.

**Brief differential:** genuine primary NSTEMI with papillary-muscle/chordal dysfunction causing the new murmur, acute severe aortic regurgitation, and massive pulmonary embolism (prior DVT, AF — but clear lungs, 100% sats, and the murmur point away).

**Key point:** hypotension + new carotid-radiating systolic murmur + ECG ischemia + normal initial enzymes in a patient whose fluids don't work is dissection-until-CT-aortogram-proves-otherwise; treating it as a plain MI risks fatal anticoagulation/thrombolysis of a dissection.
