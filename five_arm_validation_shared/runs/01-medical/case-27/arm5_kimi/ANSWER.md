# Answer

**Most likely diagnosis: Osteomalacia due to vitamin D deficiency (with associated proximal myopathy).**

Justification: The triad of diffuse bone pain, proximal muscle weakness with a waddling gait, and the characteristic biochemistry — low calcium (7.6 mg/dL), low phosphate (1.9 mg/dL), markedly elevated alkaline phosphatase (866 U/L), and low 25-OH vitamin D (10 ng/mL) — together with severely reduced bone mineral density and normal inflammatory markers/CK, is diagnostic of vitamin D–deficiency osteomalacia rather than a primary myopathy or other metabolic bone disease.
