# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-17

**Most likely diagnosis: primary exercise headache (primary exertional headache) — a benign primary headache disorder triggered by strenuous exercise, with cervical-muscular radiation.**

**One-line justification:** a previously headache-free 44-year-old develops an acute band-like headache in the final minutes of vigorous cycling, radiating to the cervical spine and lower back, persisting for a week — and the entire structural workup is normal (head CT, CT angiogram, brain and whole-spine MRI, MRA of head and neck, bloods), with a normal neurologic exam and no meningeal signs; with exertion as the reproducible trigger and every structural cause excluded, the diagnosis is primary exercise headache by exclusion. The declined lumbar puncture leaves a small residual question of subarachnoid hemorrhage, but a normal CT performed within a week of onset plus normal CTA carries high negative predictive value for aneurysmal SAH.

**Brief differential:** subarachnoid hemorrhage (excluded to near-certainty by CT within the window + CTA; LP would close it fully), reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome (thunderclap with recurrent stabs; MRA normal and course not thunderclap-recurrent), cervical/posterior fossa structural causes (whole-spine MRI clear), and cervicogenic headache from exercise-related muscular strain (possible contributor to the band + cervical radiation, but primary exertional headache is the unifying label).

**Key point:** exertion-triggered headache with a normal early CT + CTA and normal spine/vascular imaging is a primary disorder — treat with indomethacin (the classic response) and relaxation of the exercise trigger, and the LP offer stands as the completeness step.
