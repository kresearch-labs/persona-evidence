# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-01

**Most likely diagnosis: new-onset type 2 diabetes mellitus presenting as euglycemic/hyperglycemic diabetic ketosis — and, given what the 12-lead ECG was ordered to exclude, an acute coronary syndrome (non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction) presenting as severe vomiting.**

**One-line justification:** a 54-year-old smoker with previously undiagnosed diabetes (random glucose 391 mg/dL, 4+ glucosuria and ketonuria) presents with what looks like diabetic ketosis without acidosis (pH 7.40, bicarbonate 25), but persistent severe vomiting with epigastric burning in a smoker with a neutrophilic leukocytosis (22.7 ×10³/L) and disproportionate transaminitis (AST 262) is a recognized atypical presentation of an acute coronary syndrome — which is exactly why the team ordered the 12-lead ECG; the ECG/hypoperfusion-driven stress hyperglycemia unmasked the diabetes.

**Brief differential:** gastritis/PUD (epigastric burning), diabetic ketosis without acidosis (ketonuria with normal pH — supported by labs), viral hepatitis (transaminitis, but AST>ALT pattern and context favor ischemic hepatocyte injury), and the ECG-guided consideration of inferior/posterior MI masquerading as gastritis.

**Key point:** the "diabetic ketosis without acidosis" picture plus epigastric symptoms in a vascular-risk patient is a trap; the ECG is the decisive step, and severe vomiting as an anginal equivalent should not be dismissed once glucose and ketones are explained.
