Most likely diagnosis: Acute myocardial infarction (an inferior-wall ST-elevation MI) presenting atypically as intractable vomiting and epigastric burning, rather than a primary gastrointestinal or metabolic disorder.

Justification: The epigastric pain with vomiting in a smoker, neutrophilic leukocytosis, stress hyperglycemia with ketosis but no acidosis, and an AST-predominant transaminitis (AST 262 vs ALT 49, of cardiac rather than hepatic origin) with normal lipase all point to myocardial injury — which is why the team orders a 12-lead ECG.
