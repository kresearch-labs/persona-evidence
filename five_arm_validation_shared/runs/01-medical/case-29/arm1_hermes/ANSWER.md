# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-29

**Most likely diagnosis: anti-GBM (Goodpasture's) disease — rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis with pulmonary-renal syndrome (diffuse alveolar hemorrhage causing hemoptysis and hypoxemia, plus RPGN with RBC casts), in a woman who mislabeled it as "new-onset hypertension with swelling".**

**One-line justification:** the pulmonary-renal pairing is the diagnosis engine: hemoptysis with severe hypoxemia (74% room air) and bibasilar crepitations/infiltrates on one side, and on the other a nephritic urine — many RBCs/HPF with **RBC casts** and 3+ proteinuria — driving acute kidney injury (creatinine 6.7 mg/dL within days) with anemia and high-gap metabolic acidosis; a 40-year-old with subacute pulmonary hemorrhage plus rapidly progressive nephritis has anti-GBM disease (or ANCA-associated vasculitis) until anti-GBM antibodies and ANCA panels return, and the preceding "hypertension/swelling" episode 20 days ago was the renal onset being missed.

**Brief differential:** ANCA-associated granulomatosis/polyangiitis (the closest mimic and frequent co-driver — both panels are mandatory; lung hemorrhage + RPGN fits either), lupus nephritis with hemorrhage (no other lupus stigmata, obesity/puffiness confounds), severe uremic pulmonary edema (crepitations explained, but RBC casts and hemoptysis outstrip it), and post-infectious GN (antibiotic course without improvement).

**Key point:** hemoptysis + RBC casts + rising creatinine is pulmonary-renal syndrome — a same-day emergency: anti-GBM and ANCA serology, and pulsed steroids ± plasmapheresis without waiting for the biopsy, because both lungs and kidneys are being lost concurrently.
