# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-30

**Most likely diagnosis: euglycemic diabetic ketoacidosis (euDKA) induced by the newly added SGLT2 inhibitor (empagliflozin) — severe ketoacidosis (pH 7.03, bicarbonate 5.9, ketones >10) with near-normal glucose (142 mg/dL) and profound hypokalemia (2.0 mEq/L).**

**One-line justification:** the mechanism is on the timeline: empagliflozin added five days before presentation to a diabetic already on metformin/vildagliptin/glimepiride, then lethargy, drowsiness, dehydration, Kussmaul-pattern hyperventilation (pCO2 19 compensating pH 7.03), a high anion gap (36) fully explained by ketones (>10 mmol/L, 3+ urinary ketones), yet random glucose only 142 mg/dL — the defining euglycemia of SGLT2-inhibitor-associated DKA (glucosuria lowers glucose while the insulin deficit from the drug's off-target insulin suppression plus a recent COVID-19 infection as the precipitant drives lipolysis and ketogenesis); the severe hypokalemia (2.0) with dehydration completes the picture, and stopping empagliflozin plus IV fluids/insulin/potassium is the treatment.

**Brief differential:** classic DKA (glucose far below typical range — the euglycemia is the point), lactic acidosis from metformin (gap here is ketone-explained; metformin contributes little), starvation ketosis (pH 7.03 severity uncharacteristic), and toxic alcohol ingestion (denied, and ketones account for the gap).

**Key point:** any SGLT2-inhibitor patient with malaise and a high anion gap has euDKA regardless of a "normal" sugar — check ketones, stop the drug, and treat aggressively; euglycemia delays recognition and that delay is what kills.
