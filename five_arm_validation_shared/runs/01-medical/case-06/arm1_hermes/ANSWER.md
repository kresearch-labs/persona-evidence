# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-06

**Most likely diagnosis: primary adrenal insufficiency (Addison's disease) presenting with an Addisonian (adrenal) crisis — the persisting hyponatremia, hyperpigmentation, salt craving, and postural hypotension after the pancreatitis episode resolve.**

**One-line justification:** the clues were on general examination, not abdominal: blackish hyperpigmentation of palms, soles, and mucous membranes (a year's duration — chronic ACTH excess from a failing cortex), salt craving, malaise, anorexia with self-limiting abdominal pain and vomiting, and a 20-mmHg postural drop — plus **refractory hyponatremia (103 → 110 mEq/L) with hyperkalemia (5.1–5.3) despite parenteral hydration**, the biochemical signature of mineralocorticoid deficiency. The initial "pancreatitis with AKI" (lipase 318, creatinine 2.2) was likely a partial Addisonian crisis (adrenal insufficiency classically presents as unexplained abdominal pain, vomiting, and prerenal azotemia), and the persisting symptoms after enzymes normalized unmasked the underlying endocrine disease.

**Brief differential:** SIADH (would be hyponatremia without hyperkalemia or postural hypotension), diuretic/parenteral-fluid dilution (excluded by history), hypothyroidism (no bradycardia/delayed reflexes), and persistent pancreatitis (enzymes already normalized; pain pattern is cyclical and chronic).

**Key point:** any patient with unexplained hyponatremia + hyperkalemia + postural hypotension + pigmentation + salt craving has Addison's until a short Synacthen test says otherwise; abdominal pain in adrenal crisis mimics surgical abdomens.
