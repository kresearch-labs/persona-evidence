Primary adrenal insufficiency (Addison's disease) presenting as an acute adrenal crisis mimicking acute pancreatitis.

Justification: The triad of persistent hyponatremia (Na 103–110 mEq/l) with hyperkalemia (K 5.1–5.3 mEq/l) unresponsive to IV fluids, blackish hyperpigmentation of palms/soles/mucous membranes for one year, salt craving, postural hypotension, and chronic abdominal pain with vomiting is classic for Addison's disease, where an acute crisis can mimic pancreatitis (modestly raised lipase with normal amylase) and cause prerenal acute kidney injury.
