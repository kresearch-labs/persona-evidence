# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-15

**Most likely diagnosis: Guillain–Barré syndrome — acute inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (AIDP), post-infectious (gastroenteritis — Campylobacter jejuni the classic antecedent), with a motor-predominant ascending course.**

**One-line justification:** ten days after a self-limited febrile watery diarrhea, a previously healthy physician develops symmetric, ascending weakness (proximal then distal, arms and legs, MRC 3–4 within a week) with preserved mentation and cranial nerves, absent pathologic reflexes, minimal sensory involvement, no sphincter disturbance, a CSF showing elevated protein (0.59 g/L) with a normal cell count (the albuminocytologic dissociation signature), and EMG at three weeks showing polyphasic, reduced-interference neuropathic potentials with normal conduction velocities (axonal-predominant or early demyelinating GBS pattern) — the textbook arc of GBS triggered by a diarrheal illness.

**Brief differential:** myositis/viral polymyositis (normal muscle enzymes, neuropathic not myopathic EMG), tick paralysis (no tick exposure), botulism (cranial-first, pupil involvement), acute myelopathy (no sensory level, no sphincter signs), and West Nile/other viral motor neuropathies (asymmetric usually).

**Key point:** symmetric ascending motor weakness 1–3 weeks after diarrhea with albuminocytologic dissociation in the CSF is GBS — monitor vital capacity hourly, treat with IVIG or plasmapheresis, and don't wait for maximal weakness.
