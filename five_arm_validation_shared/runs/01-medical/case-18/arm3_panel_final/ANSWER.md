Vertebral artery dissection — a left vertebral artery dissection presenting as a transient posterior-circulation ischaemic episode (transient visual loss with headache).

Justification: neck stretching is a recognized minor trauma precipitating cervical artery dissection, and in a young, previously healthy man with no vascular risk factors, a self-resolving 5-minute episode of visual loss accompanied by headache (dissection's most common symptom) with a completely normal examination fits vertebral artery dissection better than migraine aura or amaurosis fugax from carotid atheroma.
