# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-03

**Most likely diagnosis: focal (inferior-left-atrial) atrial tachycardia — a focal atrial tachycardia arising from an inferior left atrial focus, reproducibly triggered by body position (recumbency/forward bending), in a structurally normal heart — i.e., positional focal atrial tachycardia, likely vagal/autonomic-tone mediated.**

**One-line justification:** the salient, consistently reproducible finding is short paroxysms of ~140 bpm atrial tachycardia induced *only* when lying down or bending forward, with an inferior-left-atrial P-wave morphology, abrupt onset without PR prolongation, warm-up phenomenon, and a structurally normal echo — the classic fingerprint of a focal (automatic) atrial tachycardia whose trigger is the autonomic/positional change of recumbency, not re-entry (no PR-dependent initiation) and not AVNRT/AVRT (P morphology and warm-up argue against).

**Brief differential:** AVNRT and orthodromic AVRT (both typically lack this P-wave morphology and warm-up), sinus tachycardia (P morphology differs), and MSH/positional palpitations from a hiatal or pericardial mechanism — but the documented focal AT on continuous 12-lead during the maneuvers settles it.

**Contributory context:** estrogen-containing contraceptive switch and strong paternal history of unprovoked DVT raise thromboembolic vigilance (and the recent OCP switch may contribute adrenergic sensitivity), but with normal saturations, clear exam, and normal labs, PE is not the palpitation driver; the diagnosis is the focal atrial tachycardia itself.

**Key point:** position-triggered palpitations are not anxiety when telemetry reproduces them; the reproducible induction plus inferior-atrial P focus and warm-up defines focal atrial tachycardia.
