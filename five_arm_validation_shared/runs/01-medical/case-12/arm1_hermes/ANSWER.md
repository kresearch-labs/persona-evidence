# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-12

**Most likely diagnosis: acute pulmonary edema from flash pulmonary edema of hypertensive/diastolic (HFpEF) heart failure, superimposed on chronic interstitial lung disease — presenting as severe hypoxemic respiratory failure mistaken for community-acquired pneumonia.**

**One-line justification:** the internals say heart, not infection: proBNP 4056 pg/mL (32x the reference), an echocardiogram showing diastolic dysfunction with preserved EF, diffuse bilateral ground-glass infiltrates on CT with mild cardiomegaly, minor edema, and a rapid decline to BiPAP-dependent hypoxemia — while every microbiological hunt came back negative (Mycoplasma, Legionella, Chlamydia titers, blood cultures) and vasculitis/ANA serologies were negative. The yellow sputum and WBC 12.9 were the red herrings; a diabetic hypertensive woman with HFpEF who presents with orthopnea, crackles, and ground-glass lungs is in acute cardiogenic pulmonary edema until BNP and echo say otherwise — and they did.

**Brief differential:** atypical viral pneumonia (possible contributor but 3 broadened antibiotic regimens failed and proBNP grounds the alternative), acute eosinophilic/organizing pneumonia (would expect different CT pattern and response), interstitial lung disease exacerbation (mediastinal lymphadenopathy and chronic ground-glass hint at an underlying interstitial process worth biopsy after stabilization), and PE (V/Q low probability).

**Key point:** severe hypoxemia + ground-glass lungs + towering proBNP + preserved EF = acute diastolic heart failure with flash edema; escalating antibiotics while withholding decongestion is the classic wrong path.
