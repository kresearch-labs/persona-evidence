# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-08

**Most likely diagnosis: infective endocarditis of the tricuspid valve — right-sided subacute bacterial endocarditis on a congenital perimembranous ventricular septal defect, with the mouth's dental caries as the probable portal of entry — causing chronic constitutional symptoms (fever, weight loss, anemia, hepatosplenomegaly).**

**One-line justification:** 2.5 months of intermittent low-grade fever, anorexia, weight loss, and microcytic anemia in a child with a known perimembranous VSD, plus new tricuspid-valve vegetations on echo (8×6 mm and 6×4 mm with moderate regurgitation), splenomegaly, and raised CRP — the Duke-criteria picture of right-sided endocarditis; the multiple dental caries supply the classic transient bacteremia source seeding the tricuspid valve across the left-to-right shunt jet.

**Brief differential:** the itchy lower-limb rash raises vasculitis/allergic or immune-complex etiologies (and in right-sided endocarditis, septic pulmonary emboli can produce cutaneous findings), juvenile idiopathic arthritis/inflammatory bowel disease (weight loss + anemia + rash, but the vegetations and VSD override), and simple nutritional deficiency/anemia of chronic disease (the anemia is explained by the endocarditis itself).

**Key point:** a child with a VSD, prolonged low-grade fever, and unexplained anemia has endocarditis until an echo proves otherwise — and oral sepsis from carious teeth is the most common preventable seed; blood cultures before antibiotics are the step that names the organism.
