Polycythemia vera (a JAK2-mutated myeloproliferative neoplasm) — the underlying process unifying the simultaneous venous and arterial thromboembolism.

Why: the "unprovoked" DVT/PE at age 44 plus new multi-bed arterial thrombosis (distal aortic, proximal-mid LAD with anterior STEMI, popliteal occlusion) and biventricular apical thrombi is the classic combined venous/arterial thrombotic phenotype of a myeloproliferative neoplasm, and the blood counts clinch it — hemoglobin 19.5 g/dL (erythrocytosis) with leukocytosis (21.6 × 10³/μL) and a low platelet count (114,000/μL, likely consumptive given the extensive thrombosis), a picture far better explained by polycythemia vera than by antiphospholipid syndrome, occult malignancy, or smoking-related secondary erythrocytosis alone.
