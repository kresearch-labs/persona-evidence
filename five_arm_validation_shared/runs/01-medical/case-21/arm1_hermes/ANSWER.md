# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-21

**Most likely diagnosis: chronic lead poisoning — from the ayurvedic (traditional Indian) metallic paste medications — presenting as sideroblastic (microcytic/normochromic) anemia with basophilic stippling, after travel to India.**

**One-line justification:** the anemia workup is the lead fingerprint: normochromic anemia (Hb 78 g/L) with an appropriately elevated reticulocyte count yet NO gastrointestinal blood loss (stool and endoscopy negative), **basophilic stippling** of red cells (the ribosomal-RNA clumping of impaired heme synthesis), marrow erythroid hyperplasia with **siderosis of reticulum cells** (mitochondrial iron loading — the sideroblastic block lead causes by inhibiting ferrochelatase and ALA dehydratase), and mild transaminitis — all in a man taking distinctly metallic-tasting traditional pastes from India, where ayurvedic preparations are a documented and recurrent source of lead (and other heavy-metal) intoxication; the earlier "febrile illness" was the decoy.

**Brief differential:** thalassemia trait/iron studies disorder (basophilic stippling fits both, but the paste + siderotic marrow + no GI loss points to lead), other heavy metals (arsenic, mercury — also ayurvedic contaminants; a blood/metal screen distinguishes), and post-infectious anemia (doesn't produce sideroblastic marrow or stippling).

**Key point:** unexplained sideroblastic anemia with basophilic stippling plus a travel/traditional-medicine history is lead poisoning until a blood lead level proves otherwise — and ayurvedic remedies are among the most common hidden sources worldwide; remove the exposure and chelate.
