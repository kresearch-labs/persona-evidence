# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-22

**Most likely diagnosis: pheochromocytoma (catecholamine-secreting adrenal/paraganglionic tumor) — presenting with long-masked paroxysmal hypertension "attributed to anxiety", ending in a fatal catecholamine/propranolol-precipitated cardiovascular collapse.**

**One-line justification:** fifteen years of "anxiety and depression" with documented episodic hypertension (146/103, 140/80) dismissed as psychiatric, then sudden death preceded by collapse during a phone call, extreme tachycardia (186 bpm), cyanosis, and respiratory arrest — the paroxysmal catecholamine-storm signature of pheochromocytoma; and the **beta-blocker (propranolol) given before alpha-blockade** is the classic trigger of unopposed alpha vasoconstriction/hypertensive crisis and arrhythmia in an unrecognized pheochromocytoma, with the SSRI (sertraline) a lesser serotonergic contributor. The absence of recorded palpitations/headache/sweating episodes doesn't exclude it — paroxysms were attributed to anxiety for over a decade.

**Brief differential:** arrhythmic death from occult cardiomyopathy (catecholamine cardiomyopathy is itself a pheochromocytoma complication), pulmonary embolism (collapse with cyanosis — possible, but the 15-year episodic-hypertension history and propranolol context anchor pheochromocytoma), and primary cardiac channelopathy (no prior palpitations recorded).

**Key point:** "anxiety" with real hypertension in a young adult needs a catecholamine workup (metanephrines) — and never give a beta-blocker first to a patient who might be catecholamine-loaded; alpha comes before beta.
