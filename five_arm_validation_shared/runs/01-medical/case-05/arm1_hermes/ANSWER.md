# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 01-medical case-05

**Most likely diagnosis: diabetic ketoacidosis (severe) occurring in the setting of an undiagnosed diabetes unmasked by untreated thyrotoxicosis — i.e., thyrotoxic crisis/storm precipitating DKA, with a concurrently decompensated Graves' disease.**

**One-line justification:** two emergencies coexist and interact: (a) DKA — glucose 472 mg/dL, HbA1c 13.7% (sustained hyperglycemia for months, matching her 4-month polydipsia/polyuria), 4+ ketonuria, severe metabolic acidosis (pH 7.030, bicarbonate 3.4, base excess −28.3), with influenza A as the precipitant; and (b) thyrotoxic storm features — 200 bpm atrial fibrillation, diffuse goiter with exophthalmos, warm moist skin, drowsiness, post-influenza decompensation in a poorly compliant anti-thyroid patient. Thyrotoxicosis is a recognized DKA precipitant (catecholamine potentiation, increased insulin demand), so the unifying diagnosis is DKA precipitated by influenza in a patient with undiagnosed diabetes and concomitant thyrotoxic storm/heart failure-level tachyarrhythmia.

**Brief differential:** pure thyroid storm (doesn't explain pH 7.03 with 4+ ketones), pure DKA with incidental Graves (doesn't explain HR 200 AF + goiter + exophthalmos as the acute driver), lactic acidosis from sepsis (CRP <0.3, no focus), and salicylate/other toxic acidosis (no exposure given).

**Key point:** the acidosis is ketoacidosis, the diabetes is chronic and previously silent, and untreated Graves' disease with infection is the metabolic amplifier — both must be treated in parallel or neither will resolve.
