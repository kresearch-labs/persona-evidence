# Case 10 — Reference answer (Arm 1)

## Issues the clause presents

1. **Whether the pleaded facts state a "material adverse change in the
business relationship with any Material Customer" under § 3.22.** The
representation is not a bare revenue warranty — it speaks to the
*relationship* with Material Customers. The buyer's alleged facts go
beyond fluctuation: the top three customers reduced or terminated
business post-closing, and a pre-signing internal deck flagged the two
largest accounts as "with decreasing revenues." Together these plead a
deterioration of the relationship itself, not mere revenue noise —
the quintessential MAC-in-relationship fact pattern.

2. **Materiality and "mere fluctuations."** The sellers' framing
("fluctuations of revenue growth and decline") is a dilution argument.
Where the *only* Material Customers that matter — the top three — all
shrink or leave, that is not ordinary-course churn; materiality here is
a fact question ill-suited to resolution on the pleadings.

3. **The survival-period time bar vs the exclusive-remedy carve-out.**
The fifteen-month survival period presumptively time-bars
representation-breach claims. But the remedy clause expressly carves
out "fraud, intentional misrepresentation or intentional breach." The
question is whether the carve-out (i) only preserves *fraud* claims
from the exclusive remedy, leaving § 3.22 breach time-barred, or
(ii) takes intentional breaches outside the survival limitation
entirely. The better reading: the carve-out preserves the buyer's
fraud/intentional-breach claims from the survival cutoff, because
otherwise the "intentional breach" language in the carve-out would be
surplusage — parties do not carve out a remedy that has already expired
by its own terms.

4. **Scienter support via the pre-signing presentation.** The internal
"decreasing revenues" slide predates signing; if known to sellers, it
undercuts the § 3.22 representation as made and supports fraudulent
inducement — it ties knowledge to the representation, defeating a
good-faith-belief defense.

## Construction and enforcement

The § 3.22 representation should be construed as a relationship-level
warranty: a Material Customer's termination or material reduction of
business is direct evidence of a material adverse change in that
relationship, and "material" does the contractual work — judged at the
time of signing and measured against the relationship, not against
abstract revenue variance. The motion to dismiss the § 3.22 count as
time-barred should fail insofar as the claim is framed as fraud/
intentional breach under the carve-out; a pure contract breach count
would be vulnerable to the survival defense, so the buyer's pleading
must carry intent. At minimum, the materiality dispute and the
knowledge question are fact issues that survive a motion to dismiss.
