# Case 05 — Answer

## Issues presented by the delegation clause

1. **Who decides threshold questions of arbitrability?** The clause ("The arbitrator has exclusive authority to resolve any dispute relating to the interpretation, applicability, or enforceability of this binding arbitration agreement") is a delegation clause. The issue is whether it "clearly and unmistakably" delegates gateway questions — including whether the arbitration agreement itself is enforceable — to the arbitrator rather than the court.

2. **Is the delegation clause itself unconscionable?** Because a delegation provision is severable from the rest of the arbitration agreement, a court may refuse to enforce it only if the party opposing arbitration directs a specific challenge at the delegation clause itself. The Employee preserved exactly that challenge, arguing the clause was unconscionable both standing alone and in combination with other provisions (while disclaiming any per se rule against delegation clauses). Both preserved variants must be addressed.

3. **Who decides the Employee's challenge to the agreement as a whole?** If the delegation clause is valid, the Employee's broader claim that the arbitration agreement as a whole is unconscionable is for the arbitrator, not the court.

4. **The class-arbitration bar.** The agreement's prohibition of class arbitration implicates whether the Employee must arbitrate individually notwithstanding her attempt to pursue a class action.

## How the clause should be construed

- The agreement is governed by the Federal Arbitration Act by its own terms. Under the FAA, a delegation clause is an additional, severable agreement to arbitrate threshold issues; it is enforceable under ordinary contract principles unless the delegation clause itself — not the agreement as a whole — is shown to be invalid (Rent-A-Center v. Jackson (2010) 561 U.S. 63; AT&T Mobility LLC v. Concepcion (2011) 563 U.S. 333).
- The language here is clear and unmistakable: it grants the arbitrator "exclusive authority" over disputes concerning the agreement's "interpretation, applicability, or enforceability," and the incorporated provider rules independently empower the arbitrator to rule on his or her own jurisdiction, including validity objections. That satisfies the clear-and-unmistakable standard.
- On the preserved unconscionability challenge to the delegation clause itself: the clause is not substantively unconscionable merely because it commits enforceability questions to the arbitrator, nor does it become so in combination with the other clauses; any procedural unconscionability from the handbook's adhesive character does not, without substantive oppression or surprise specific to the delegation provision, render it unenforceable. The Employee bears the burden on that challenge, and it fails.
- The no-class-arbitration provision is enforceable under the FAA; the Employer may compel an individual proceeding.

## How it should be enforced in this dispute

The trial court's order should be upheld: the delegation clause is valid and enforceable, the petition to compel arbitration was properly granted, and the Employee's contention that the arbitration agreement as a whole is unconscionable must be decided by the arbitrator, not the court. The Employee is relegated to individual arbitration; the writ of mandate should be denied.
