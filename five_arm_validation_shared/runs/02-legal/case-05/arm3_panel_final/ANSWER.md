**Bottom line.** The clause presents five issues — formation, scope, delegation of arbitrability, the class-arbitration ban, and unconscionability — and it should be construed broadly and enforced as written. The delegation clause is valid and severable, so the court decides only the Employee's specific attack on that clause; because that attack fails, the court must compel her to arbitrate all ten claims individually and leave her unconscionability challenge to the agreement as a whole to the arbitrator. The writ should be denied.

**1. Formation and governing law.** A handbook arbitration provision operates as a unilateral offer accepted by continued employment; on the Employee's alleged acceptance there is a written agreement to arbitrate, supported by mutual consideration (the Employer is bound equally). The parties' express choice of the FAA is effective because the employment affects interstate commerce (*Circuit City Stores, Inc. v. Adams* (2001)), so FAA §§ 2–4 supply the enforceability framework, with state unconscionability law applying as a generally available contract defense under § 2.

**2. Scope.** "[A]ny legal controversy or claim arising out of" employment is broad "arising out of" language that reaches every dispute rooted in the employment relationship, including statutory wage-and-hour and unfair-competition claims; statutory claims are arbitrable where the forum preserves their substance (*Mitsubishi Motors v. Soler Chrysler-Plymouth* (1985); *Armendariz v. Foundation Health Psychcare Services* (2000)). The five exceptions — workers' compensation, unemployment insurance, securities-industry claims, criminal referrals, previously resolved claims — are specific carve-outs, and none touches a wires specialist's Labor Code or UCL claims. They should be read as exhaustive; a court should not graft on a public-interest exception the parties did not write.

**3. Delegation — the pivotal issue.** The clause gives the arbitrator "exclusive authority" over "any dispute relating to the interpretation, applicability, or enforceability of this binding arbitration agreement," and the incorporated provider rules independently empower the arbitrator to rule on his or her own jurisdiction, including objections to the agreement's validity. Together that is the "clear and unmistakable" delegation *First Options of Chicago, Inc. v. Kaplan* (1995) requires. Severability then operates in nested layers (*Prima Paint* (1967); *Buckeye Check Cashing v. Cardegna* (2006); *Rent-A-Center, West, Inc. v. Jackson* (2010)): challenges to the employment contract, and challenges to the arbitration agreement as a whole, go to the arbitrator; only a challenge aimed at the delegation clause itself stays with the court.

**4. The class ban.** Whether an agreement permits class arbitration is a question of arbitrability reserved to the court absent clear delegation (*Sandquist v. Lebo Automotive, Inc.* (2016) 1 Cal.5th 233) — but here the delegation is clear, so the arbitrator decides, and the express ban makes the practical answer individual arbitration. The ban is enforceable on the merits: *AT&T Mobility LLC v. Concepcion* (2011) holds the FAA preempts California's *Discover Bank* rule, and its reasoning extends to employment adhesion contracts (*Iskanian v. CLS Transportation* (2014); *Franco v. Arakelian Enterprises, Inc.* (2015); confirmed as to the NLRA in *Epic Systems Corp. v. Lewis* (2018)). No representative PAGA claim is asserted, so *Iskanian*'s carve-out is not in play.

**5. Unconscionability — the live challenge.** The Employee attacked the delegation clause "standing alone and in combination with other clauses" while disclaiming any per se theory, so the court must reach that specific challenge rather than defer (*Rent-A-Center*). It fails. *Standing alone:* beyond mild adhesion, the clause is mutual, strips no claim or remedy, and imposes no cost barrier — *Armendariz* obliges the Employer to bear arbitration-specific costs for non-securities claims, which also answers the objection that an employee must "pay to play" the arbitrability question. *In combination:* California courts refuse to taint a delegation clause by association with other terms (*Serafin v. Balco Properties Ltd.* (2015); *Franco*); unconscionability must inhere in the delegation clause itself, and no identified term — fee-shifting on arbitrability, limits on the arbitrator's powers, employer-favoring carve-outs — supplies the defect. The disclaimed per se argument is the only theory that might have done the work, and it is off the table.

**Construction.** Every word should be given effect: "exclusive" forecloses parallel judicial determination of arbitrability, subject only to post-award review; "interpretation" covers the meaning of the clause and its exceptions; "applicability" covers whether each of the ten claims falls within "arising out of" employment; "enforceability" covers defenses to the arbitration agreement itself — unconscionability, revocation, fraud; and "this binding arbitration agreement" confines the delegation to the arbitration agreement rather than the handbook at large, which is what creates the three-bucket structure above. The provider-rules incorporation is cumulative, not surplusage: it preserves kompetenz-kompetenz even under a narrow reading of the express clause. This reading is stable across the clause's other applications — any challenge, whether to formation, scope, or validity, lands in exactly one bucket, and the class ban disposes of every representative theory the same way — so it does not depend on the particular facts of this dispute.

**Enforcement in this dispute.** The trial court ruled correctly: delegation clause enforceable, petition granted, whole-agreement unconscionability left to the arbitrator. An order compelling arbitration is not directly appealable (Code Civ. Proc., § 1294(a) reaches only orders dismissing or denying a petition), so the writ is the proper vehicle — but writ relief requires clear error or a miscarriage of justice, and none appears. Deny the writ. The arbitrator then takes the unconscionability challenge to the remainder (including *Armendariz* compliance — cost allocation, adequate discovery, a written award, full statutory remedies) and may sever any offending term; the Employee's claims proceed individually, and her class allegations cannot be arbitrated as a class and, because the agreement channels every employment claim into individual arbitration, they yield only individual relief.
