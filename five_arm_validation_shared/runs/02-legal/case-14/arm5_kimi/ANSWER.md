# Answer

**Issues presented.** The clause presents three issues: (1) whether the "time is of the essence" provision makes the 60-day settlement date a strict condition, so that the Buyer's failure to tender the purchase money within that period defeats its right to specific performance; (2) whether the Sellers' statement at signing (that they did not know when their new house would be finished) operated as a waiver of the settlement date or estopped the Sellers from insisting on it, notwithstanding the contract's integration clause barring reliance on any representation "not herein written"; and (3) whether the Sellers owed an independent duty to tender the deed or offer performance on the settlement date before they could declare the contract at an end and retain the deposit.

**Construction and enforcement.** The clause should be construed and enforced against the Buyer, and the chancellor's dismissal of the bill affirmed:

1. **Time of the essence / conditions concurrent.** Where time is expressly made of the essence, the settlement date is a strict term. The Buyer's payment and the Sellers' conveyance are concurrent conditions: neither party can put the other in default without tendering or showing readiness and ability to perform its own part. The Buyer never tendered the balance, never signaled readiness to settle within the sixty days, and offered no excuse for its non-performance; it therefore cannot claim specific performance, an equitable remedy available only to a party who has performed or tendered performance of its own obligations.

2. **No waiver or estoppel.** The Seller's statement at signing could not waive the written settlement date or estop the Sellers. The statement was made at or before signing, concerned only the Sellers' own housing, and said nothing about extending settlement. Moreover, the contract's merger clause expressly provides that neither party is bound by "any terms, conditions or representations not herein written," foreclosing reliance on any oral representation to vary the written terms. Waiver of an essential time term requires a clear, unequivocal act or agreement; nothing of the kind occurred here.

3. **No breach by the Sellers.** Because the conditions were concurrent, the Sellers owed no duty to make a formal tender of the deed where the Buyer gave no indication of readiness to pay; a useless tender is not required. The Sellers were therefore entitled, after the essential date passed with no performance by the Buyer, to treat the contract as at an end. Their prompt return of the $500 deposit (rather than keeping it as damages) and resale to others were consistent with that position and give the Buyer no claim.

**Justification (one line):** Because time was expressly of the essence and payment and conveyance were concurrent conditions, the Buyer's failure to tender or show readiness to settle within the 60 days — with no effective waiver or estoppel under the integration clause — bars its suit for specific performance, so the chancellor's dismissal should be affirmed.
