# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 02-legal case-08

**The issues:** (1) whether a broad-form indemnification clause that never mentions the indemnitee's own negligence shifts the Landlord's 30% share of fault onto the Contractor; and (2) how the comparative-fault allocation (30% plaintiff / 30% Landlord / 40% Contractor) maps onto an indemnity drafted in "arising out of Contractor's performance" language.

**How it should be construed and enforced:**

1. **Indemnity clauses are strictly construed; the indemnitee's own negligence must be express.** The governing rule (restated across jurisdictions in the era's case law) is that indemnification will not be read to include losses caused by the indemnitee's own negligence *unless* the intention is expressed in clear, unequivocal terms — either by naming the indemnitee's negligence or by language "sufficiently broad" that no other reading is possible. This clause is broad as to the *trigger* ("relating to, arising out of … Contractor's performance") but says nothing about the Landlord's fault. "Broad form" drafting of the 1994 era was *understood* to do that work — and the trial court leaned on that understanding — but the modern and better view demands express words ("including any claims caused by the negligence of the Indemnitee"), and courts in many jurisdictions construe ambiguity against the indemnitor only where the clause clearly contemplates the indemnitee's fault.

2. **Apply the two-part structure to this accident.** Subsection (i) covers claims "arising out of … the conditions created thereby" — conditions created by *Contractor's* performance. The cheese-substance spill: if it resulted from the Contractor's janitorial work (failure to clean, improper procedure), the claim arises from Contractor performance and subsection (i) squarely obligates the Contractor — including defense costs — for the 40% (and arguably the whole claim as to "liability … that such Indemnitee may incur relating to" Contractor performance). Subsection (ii) adds breach/inadequate performance. **But the 30% fault the jury assigned to the Landlord arose from the Landlord's own negligence** (premises control, notice, inspection), which the clause never covers. Nothing in the text shifts that share.

3. **Defense obligations track the same limit.** The Contractor must defend claims where indemnity could apply (the "relating to" language is broad enough to trigger defense), but once comparative fault is adjudicated, reimbursement of the Landlord's share (30%) is not owed absent express negligent-indemnitee language.

**Practical ruling:** reverse in part — the Contractor owes indemnity for the portion of liability attributable to its own performance/conditions (the 40%, plus defense costs up to adjudication), but not for the Landlord's 30% own-negligence share, because the clause lacks the express "even if caused by Indemnitee's negligence" language that comparative-fault shifting requires.

**Key issues flagged:** broad-form indemnity vs express own-negligence requirement · scope keyed to "conditions created" by Contractor · construction against the drafter where negligence-shifting is not stated · defense duty broad, reimbursement duty textually bounded.
