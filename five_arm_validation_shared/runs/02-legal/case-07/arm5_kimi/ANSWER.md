# Case 07 — Answer

## Issues Presented

The "Notice of Amendments" clause presents four issues:

1. **Scope of the amendment power.** Does a clause permitting the Credit Union to "change the terms of this Agreement" authorize it to *add* an entirely new arbitration-and-class-action-waiver provision that never existed in the original agreement — or only to modify terms already in the contract?
2. **Assent by silence.** Can the Member's failure to opt out, coupled with continued use of the account, constitute the mutual assent required to form a new arbitration agreement?
3. **Duty of good faith and fair dealing.** Even if the clause grants broad amendment power, is that power limited by the implied covenant — particularly where the amendment was adopted six and a half years into the relationship, while a pending class action over the very same overdraft fees threatened the Credit Union, and was designed to strip members of a forum and a procedural remedy?
4. **Unconscionability / retroactivity.** Is enforcement of the new provision against a member whose claims accrued before the amendment procedurally and substantively unconscionable, or an impermissible retroactive application?

## How the Clause Should Be Construed

- **Plain language.** The clause speaks of *changing* "the terms, rates, or fees" of the Agreement. An arbitration provision with a class-action waiver is not a change to an existing term; it is the addition of a wholly new dispute-resolution mechanism that extinguishes rights (jury trial, class procedure) the Member retained under the original contract. A reasonable member reading the clause would not understand it to reserve power to rewrite the agreement's remedial architecture.
- **Contra proferentem.** The Credit Union drafted the standard-form agreement; any ambiguity in the reach of the amendment power is construed against it.
- **Good faith limits.** A unilateral-modification clause is not a blank check. The implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing confines the power to changes consistent with the parties' reasonable expectations at formation. Using it mid-litigation-environment to immunize the very fee practices already under attack in a pending class action is precisely the kind of opportunistic exercise the covenant forbids.
- **Silence is not assent.** Where the original bargain contained no arbitration term, the Member's inaction — not opting out of a notice she may never have read — cannot supply the affirmative assent arbitration requires. Continued use of a checking account is conduct consistent with the existing contract, not acceptance of a new one.

## How It Should Be Enforced

The Arbitration Amendment should be held **unenforceable against the Member**, and the motion to stay and compel arbitration **denied**. The change-of-terms clause, properly construed, did not empower the Credit Union to add a new arbitration and class-waiver provision unilaterally; the Member never assented to one; and the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing bars the Credit Union from deploying its amendment power to defeat pending or accrued claims over the same overdraft fees. The Member's class action proceeds in court, in the county designated by the governing-law/forum provision.
