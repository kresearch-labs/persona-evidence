# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 02-legal case-06

**The issues:** (1) whether an automatic-renewal clause with a 90-day notice requirement is enforced as written against a notice that was 58 days late; (2) whether the Pennsylvania "no prejudice, no time-is-of-the-essence" line excuses untimely termination notice; and (3) whether the Hotel Owner's withheld payments and claimed 20%-discount commitment change the outcome.

**How it should be construed and enforced:**

1. **Enforce the clause as written — the renewal stands.** The notice provision was clear, bargained-for, and mutual ("Either party … by giving the other party 90 days prior written notice"). Commercial parties — a hotel owner running a business, not a consumer — are held to express notice deadlines in automatic-renewal contracts, because the entire purpose of the clause is certainty for the service provider's scheduling and staffing. The Pennsylvania no-prejudice authority the Owner relies on addresses *consumer-protective* auto-renewal contexts or contracts without explicit notice deadlines; where the contract itself fixes a precise notice window and consideration runs both ways, courts enforce the mechanism rather than excuse the miss. Summary judgment for the Owner on an "untimely is still effective" theory is wrong: the clause's language admits no fairness override on these undisputed facts.

2. **The prejudice analysis, if reached, favors the Service Company.** A five-year elevator maintenance term is priced and staffed on the renewal's certainty — the company planned around it; a party who lets the window lapse and withholds payments for the final months shows exactly the prejudice (lost notice-period opportunity to re-market the contract) the rule exists to prevent.

3. **The payment issue is separate and the Service Company wins it.** Withholding October–December 1990 payments for work complaints is a damages counterclaim, not a defense to contract price — service-level disputes don't excuse paying the agreed monthly price absent a valid setoff or rescission.

4. **The 20% discount claim survives only as a counterclaim.** An oral discount "commitment" tied to renewal, if provable, is a collateral promise or part of the renewal bargain — it may offset damages but does not void the renewal clause itself.

**Practical ruling:** deny the Owner's summary judgment; the automatic renewal for five years is effective, the Service Company recovers the October–December 1990 contract price, and the Owner's discount claim proceeds (if at all) as a counterclaim.

**Key issues flagged:** mutual 90-day notice clauses in commercial auto-renewals enforced strictly · late notice ≠ effective notice absent contract language inviting leniency · no-prejudice doctrine is context-limited (consumer-protective, not hotel-owner-protective) · service complaints are setoff claims, not payment defenses.
