# Case 06 — Automatic Renewal Clause: Issues, Construction, and Enforcement

## Issues Presented

1. **Effectiveness of the late notice.** Whether the Hotel Owner's November 30, 1990 termination letter — sent 31 days before the term ended and 58 days after the 90-day notice window closed — was effective to prevent the automatic five-year renewal.
2. **Construction of the renewal clause.** Whether the clause should be enforced as written (timely notice is a condition to termination) or whether, absent a "time is of the essence" provision, untimely notice suffices unless the Service Company shows prejudice from the delay (the Hotel Owner's proposed rule).
3. **The withheld payments.** Whether the Hotel Owner could withhold the October–December 1990 contract payments because of alleged deficiencies in the Service Company's work.
4. **Damages for the renewed term.** If the renewal stands, what damages the Service Company may recover for the Hotel Owner's refusal to honor the renewed five-year term.
5. **The discount counterclaim.** Whether the Hotel Owner can enforce an alleged oral commitment to a twenty-percent discount said to have been offered in connection with the renewal.

## How the Clause Should Be Construed

- **Enforce the clause as written.** Automatic renewal (evergreen) clauses are valid and are enforced according to their plain, unambiguous terms. This clause is unambiguous: termination is effective only "at the end of" the term and only "by giving the other party 90 days prior written notice." Timely notice is not a mere formality; it is the operative mechanism that lets both parties plan — here, it gave the Service Company a defined window to adjust staffing, equipment, and other contracts after a ten-year relationship. In an automatic-renewal provision, timeliness is of the essence by the very nature of the clause; no separate "time is of the essence" incantation is needed.
- **The Hotel Owner's "no prejudice, no enforcement" authority should be rejected or distinguished.** That line of cases principally concerns options and slight delays where literal enforcement works a forfeiture. Here, (a) the delay was not slight — the notice came nearly two months after the window closed and barely a month before term's end; (b) enforcing the clause causes no forfeiture — the Hotel Owner simply remains bound to the bargain he made for the renewal period he agreed to; and (c) even on its own terms the rule fails, because the Service Company can show prejudice: it reasonably treated the contract as renewed and faced loss of a five-year revenue stream on a long-standing commercial relationship.
- **No equitable relief is warranted.** There is no allegation of waiver, estoppel, fraud, or mutual mistake — only the Hotel Owner's own unexcused failure to mark the deadline. Equity does not relieve a sophisticated party of the consequences of its own neglect of a clear contractual deadline.

## How the Dispute Should Be Resolved

1. **The November 30, 1990 notice was ineffective; the contract automatically renewed for five years (January 1, 1991 – December 31, 1995).** The Hotel Owner's motion for summary judgment on the effectiveness of his late notice should be denied.
2. **The withheld October–December 1990 payments are recoverable.** These months fell within the original term. Alleged deficiencies in the Service Company's work gave the Hotel Owner, at most, a claim or setoff for breach — not a right of self-help to stop paying the contract price while continuing to take the service. The Service Company recovers the three months' contract price, subject to any proven offset for defective performance.
3. **The Hotel Owner's refusal to honor the renewal is a breach.** The Service Company may recover expectation damages for the renewed term measured by its lost profits — the contract price less the costs it saved by not performing — and it is under a duty to mitigate by seeking substitute service work. Damages are not the gross contract price for five years.
4. **The twenty-percent-discount counterclaim should fail.** An oral promise of a discount, made in connection with the renewal, contradicts or adds a material term to the written, integrated contract and is barred by the parol evidence rule; to the extent it modifies a five-year agreement not performable within one year, it also runs afoul of the statute of frauds. Absent a writing or proven detrimental reliance, the counterclaim cannot stand.

## Bottom Line

The clause is enforced as written: the late notice did not stop the automatic renewal, the Hotel Owner owes the three withheld monthly payments plus lost-profit damages for repudiating the renewed term (subject to mitigation and any proven setoff for deficient work), and the oral-discount counterclaim is barred by the parol evidence rule and the statute of frauds.

*Justification: clear automatic-renewal clauses are enforced according to their plain terms, and courts will not excuse an unexcused, substantial failure to give the required 90-day notice absent waiver, estoppel, or mistake — particularly where, as here, literal enforcement works no forfeiture and the non-breaching party relied on the renewal.*
