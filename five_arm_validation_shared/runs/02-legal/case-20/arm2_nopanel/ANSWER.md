This is the dispute decided in *Atlantic Marine Construction Co. v. U.S. District Court for the Western District of Texas*, 571 U.S. 49 (2013). The clause presents four issues, and it should be construed as a valid, mandatory forum-selection clause enforced through a §1404(a) transfer to the Eastern District of Virginia.

**1. The clause does not bear on venue, so the dismissal motions fail.** A forum-selection clause does not render venue "wrong" under 28 U.S.C. §1406(a) or "improper" under Rule 12(b)(3). Venue is "wrong" or "improper" only when the filing district fails the general venue statute, §1391 — an analysis conducted without regard to the clause. Venue in the Western District of Texas was proper (the subcontractor resides there and a substantial part of the events occurred there), so the Contractor's §1406(a)/12(b)(3) motions were correctly denied. Breaching a forum-selection clause is not a venue defect; it is a contract breach remedied by transfer.

**2. §1404(a) is the exclusive enforcement mechanism.** Because the clause points to another federal forum — the Eastern District of Virginia, Norfolk Division, one of the two fora the parties named — §1404(a) is the sole mechanism for enforcing it, and the Contractor's alternative motion was the correct vehicle. (The forum non conveniens doctrine would govern only if the party invoking the clause sought the state forum, the Circuit Court for the City of Norfolk.) Transfer is available because the action "might have been brought" in E.D. Va.: diversity jurisdiction exists and the Virginia contractor is subject to personal jurisdiction there.

**3. Construction.** The clause is mandatory, not precatory ("shall be litigated in"), and it was negotiated between sophisticated commercial parties in an arms-length subcontract. It is presumptively valid and enforceable absent fraud or overreaching in the inducement of the clause itself, or a strong public policy against enforcement.

**4. Enforcement: the modified §1404(a) analysis.** A valid forum-selection clause must be given controlling weight in all but the most exceptional cases, which changes the ordinary §1404(a) calculus in three ways:

- *Plaintiff's choice:* merits no weight; the subcontractor bargained away its right to choose a forum.
- *Private-interest factors:* given no weight; witness and evidence convenience cannot defeat the parties' agreement, and the movant need not show the chosen forum is more convenient.
- *Burden:* the party defying the clause — the subcontractor — bears the burden of showing that public-interest considerations or exceptional circumstances make transfer unwarranted. It is error to place that burden on the movant, as the district court did.

Public-interest factors rarely defeat the clause. The local interest in trying the dispute in Texas is diminished because the parties agreed to litigate in Virginia; federal judges routinely apply another State's law, so Texas judges' familiarity with Texas contract law deserves little weight; and the administrative burden a transfer imposes on the receiving court (docket congestion) gets no weight.

**Conclusion.** The district court committed legal error by treating the clause as merely one factor among many, by weighing the plaintiff's choice and the private-interest factors, and by placing the burden on the Contractor. The case should be transferred to the United States District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia, Norfolk Division, unless the subcontractor shows an exceptional circumstance defeating the parties' bargain — the ground on which the Supreme Court unanimously reversed.
