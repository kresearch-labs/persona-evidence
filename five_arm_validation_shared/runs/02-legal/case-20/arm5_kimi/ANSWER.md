# Case 20 — Answer

**Issues presented.** The clause raises three questions: (1) whether it is valid and mandatory; (2) the proper procedural mechanism for enforcing it when the Subcontractor sues in breach of it; and (3) how the enforcement analysis must be conducted.

**Construction and validity.** The clause is mandatory ("shall be litigated"), not merely permissive, and it designates two fora: the Circuit Court for the City of Norfolk (a state court) and the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia, Norfolk Division (a federal court). Under federal law, forum-selection clauses are prima facie valid and are enforced unless the party resisting enforcement shows they are unreasonable or unjust (M/S Bremen v. Zapata Off-Shore Co.). Nothing in this dispute suggests invalidity, so the clause should be enforced.

**Proper enforcement mechanism.** Because the clause points to another *federal* forum (E.D. Va.), the correct mechanism is a motion to transfer under 28 U.S.C. § 1404(a). (Had the clause pointed only to a state or foreign forum, the common-law doctrine of forum non conveniens would supply the mechanism instead.) Dismissal under § 1406(a) or Rule 12(b)(3) is *not* proper: venue in the Western District of Texas is proper under the general venue statute, 28 U.S.C. § 1391, and a forum-selection clause does not render an otherwise-proper venue "wrong" under § 1406(a) or "improper" under Rule 12(b)(3). The District Court was therefore right that § 1404(a) governs, but wrong in how it applied it.

**How § 1404(a) must be applied when a valid clause exists.** The ordinary § 1404(a) balancing changes in three ways: (1) the plaintiff's choice of forum merits no weight — a plaintiff who files in defiance of a valid clause forfeits the usual deference to its choice, and the clause shifts the burden to the plaintiff to show why transfer should not occur; (2) the court may not consider the parties' private-interest factors, which are deemed to weigh entirely in favor of the preselected forum; and (3) only public-interest factors may be weighed, and those factors will rarely defeat a transfer motion — the practical result is that a valid forum-selection clause should be given controlling weight in all but the most unusual cases. (Additionally, when transfer is granted, the transferee court applies its own choice-of-law rules; the transferor's law does not follow the case.)

**Application to this dispute.** The District Court erred at every step: it treated the clause as merely one factor among many, placed the burden on the Contractor (the party seeking to enforce the clause) rather than on the Subcontractor (the party breaching it), and relied on public-interest reasoning — Texas judges' greater familiarity with Texas contract law — that cannot defeat a valid clause (federal courts routinely apply sister-state law). Because the clause expressly authorizes suit in the Eastern District of Virginia, Norfolk Division, the case should be transferred there under § 1404(a); the clause's additional reference to the state court does not preclude transfer to the named federal forum. The denial of the transfer motion should be reversed and the case remanded for application of the correct standard.

**Justification (one line):** Under *Atlantic Marine Construction Co. v. United States District Court*, 571 U.S. 49 (2013), a valid forum-selection clause pointing to another federal forum is enforced through § 1404(a) transfer — not § 1406(a)/Rule 12(b)(3) dismissal — with the clause given controlling weight, the plaintiff's choice and all private interests disregarded, and only rarely-outweighing public interests considered.
