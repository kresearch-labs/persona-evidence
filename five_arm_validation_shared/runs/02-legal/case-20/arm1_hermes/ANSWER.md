# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 02-legal case-20

**The issues:** (1) whether a party that files suit in a district that does not follow the forum-selection clause should be corrected by §1406(a)/Rule 12(b)(3) (venue "wrong"/"improper") or only by §1404(a) transfer; and (2) how much weight a valid forum-selection clause carries in the §1404(a) balance — one discretionary factor among many, or a presumptively dispositive one.

**How it should be construed and enforced:**

1. **A forum-selection clause pointing to another federal forum is enforced through §1404(a), not §1406(a)/12(b)(3) — but the clause transforms the §1404(a) analysis.** Venue in the Western District of Texas is *proper* under §1391 (a Texas defendant, diversity), so §1406(a) and Rule 12(b)(3) are unavailable; "wrong venue" means venue improper under the venue statutes, and the clause is not itself a venue statute. The mechanism is §1404(a) — as the district court held. But the district court then erred at the step that matters.

2. **A forum-selection clause is not "one factor among many"; it gets controlling weight in all but the most exceptional cases.** The Supreme Court's governing framework (Atlantic Marine) requires: (a) the clause is "given controlling weight in all but the most exceptional cases"; (b) the plaintiff's choice of forum gets *no* weight when it breached the clause (it chose a forum it promised not to use); (c) private-interest factors are deemed to have been accounted for by the clause and drop out entirely — a party's ability to prove that trial in the agreed forum would be so gravely difficult that it is fundamentally unfair is the only way around; and (d) public-interest factors rarely defeat a valid clause, and a district court may not weigh local judges' familiarity with state law as a reason to resist a clause — federal judges routinely apply other states' law, and the choice-of-law question is unaffected by the forum. The district court did the opposite of Atlantic Marine: it treated the clause as one consideration, weighed Texas judges' familiarity with Texas law in the balance, and demanded the Contractor show more than the clause itself — the legal error that mandamus exists to correct, since §1404(a) denials are otherwise unreviewable and a party's bargained-for forum is being lost.

3. **The clause itself is valid and mandatory.** "Shall be litigated in" the Norfolk courts is mandatory language naming specific fora; nothing suggests fraud, overreach, or that trial in Norfolk would be gravely difficult for the Texas Subcontractor. Contract disputes about a Virginia-performed federal construction project with a Virginia prime contractor have substantial connections to the chosen forum.

**Practical ruling:** the District Court's denial should be corrected — under Atlantic Marine's approach to §1404(a), the Contractor's motion to transfer to the Eastern District of Virginia (Norfolk Division) should have been granted absent extraordinary circumstances unrelated to convenience; the clause is controlling, the Texas court's public-interest weighing (judicial familiarity with state law) was impermissible, and mandamus is the proper vehicle because the denial frustrated the bargained-for forum.

**Key issues flagged:** §1406/12(b)(3) unavailable where venue is statutorily proper · §1404(a) is the exclusive federal-forum mechanism · forum-selection clauses are presumptively dispositive under Atlantic Marine · public-interest weighing cannot include judges' familiarity with state law · mandamus lies for clause-defying denials.
