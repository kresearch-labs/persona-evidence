# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 02-legal case-01

**The issues:** (1) whether a limitation-of-liability clause buried in fine print on the reverse of a standardized adhesion contract is enforceable against a consumer-class counterparty who was never made aware of it; (2) whether the clause — capping all recovery at a refund of charges paid — is unconscionable or fails for lack of conspicuousness/assent; (3) whether, even if facially valid, it should be construed against the drafter and limited where the company conceded actual printing errors.

**How it should be construed and enforced:**

1. **Adhesion + notice, not unconscionability, decides it.** A take-it-or-leave-it standard form with a mass advertiser is a contract of adhesion, but adhesion alone does not void a limitation clause. The decisive question is **conspicuousness and assent**: printed on the reverse side of a one-page form, in pertinent part referenced in the front (if the front page referenced "Terms and Conditions on reverse"), the clause is enforceable; if the front page said nothing directing the signer to the reverse terms, a court may find the limitation ineffective for lack of notice.

2. **Construe against the drafter, scope strictly.** The clause limits liability "for errors, deviations or omissions … in connection with any advertisement or listing covered by this agreement." The company's own salesperson accepted change requests over the phone — creating a duty to execute those changes — and the conceded misprints fall within the clause's literal scope. The photographer cannot escape the cap by recharacterizing negligence; the clause covers exactly this.

3. **However, the remedy cap should not bar recovery of out-of-pocket consequential harm the clause does not clearly reach, and a court should scrutinize whether the cap is a fair, negotiated allocation or a surprise.** Where the clause is plainly unreasonable in effect (total failure of the paid-for purpose with only a trivial refund), courts have refused enforcement as unconscionable.

**Practical ruling this dispute supports:** the clause is enforceable against the photographer **if** the reverse-side terms were conspicuously referenced on the face of the contract and the refund remedy is not grossly disproportionate to the harm; otherwise the limitation fails for lack of assent and the company answers for its conceded printing errors in damages. On these undisputed facts, summary judgment should hinge entirely on the conspicuousness/notice element — the unconscionability argument is secondary and weaker.

**Key issues flagged:** inconspicuousness in adhesion forms · assent to reverse-side terms · strict construction against drafter · the cap's proportionality to actual harm.
