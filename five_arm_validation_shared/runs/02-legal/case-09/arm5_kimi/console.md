**Summary of the analysis:**

The case is a COVID-era force majeure dispute over a hotel-event contract. The clause excuses performance only when circumstances beyond a party's control make performance **"illegal or impossible,"** and only if written notice is given **within 10 days** of learning of the basis.

**Issues identified:**
1. Whether the pandemic is a covered event ("acts of God" / "similar circumstances")
2. Whether performance was actually made "illegal or impossible" — the dispositive issue
3. Whether the 10-day notice condition precedent was met
4. The effect of the April 2020 amendment (made with full knowledge of the pandemic)

**Conclusion:** The clause does **not** excuse the organizer. The resort stood ready to host lawfully — masks, distancing, and amenity limits diminish the experience but don't make performance illegal or impossible. Independently, the organizer knew of the pandemic by spring 2020 but gave notice only a week before the July event, blowing the 10-day deadline. The termination was therefore a breach, and the operator should prevail on its counterclaim (deposits/damages, subject to mitigation), while the organizer's $1.2M refund claim fails.
