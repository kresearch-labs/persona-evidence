# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 02-legal case-13

**The issues:** (1) whether the warranty-disclaimer and consequential-damages exclusion was "conspicuous" under UCC § 2-316 when merely underlined and set in the same size, style, and color of type as the surrounding boilerplate, buried in the body of the form; and (2) whether, if not conspicuous, the disclaimer fails entirely and the Buyer's negligence counterclaim for the lost tobacco crop stands.

**How it should be construed and enforced:**

1. **The disclaimer was not conspicuous — the trial court's contrary conclusion cannot stand.** UCC § 2-316(2) demands that disclaimers of implied warranties be "conspicuous," and the Code's definition requires terms that a reasonable person against whom it is to operate *ought to have noticed* — typically by larger or contrasting type, bolding, capitalization, headings, or placement that stands out from the mass of the form. Underlining within identical-size, identical-style, identical-color type, sunk into the middle of the printed provisions, is precisely the *non*-conspicuous pattern the conspicuousness doctrine targets: the term was functionally camouflaged. The stipulated posture (barn inoperative on delivery, crop destroyed by allegedly negligent installation) makes the disclaimer outcome-determinative, so its failure for inconspicuousness revives the Buyer's counterclaim.

2. **The exclusion of consequential damages has the same fate.** While § 2-719 permits contractual limitation of remedies, the limitation rides on the same notice logic; a remedy limitation hidden in non-conspicuous boilerplate is not binding on a party who had no reasonable notice of it. Moreover, an exclusion of consequential damages "whether or not such loss is due to the negligence of the Seller" cannot escape § 2-316's conspicuousness demands or § 2-719's unconscionability scrutiny where the exclusive remedy fails of its essential purpose — here the barn never functioned, so the "manufacturer's standard warranty, if any" exclusive remedy gave the Buyer essentially nothing.

3. **Practical allocation:** the Seller recovers the gas-price claim (that debt was distinct and survived), but the Buyer's counterclaim for the destroyed crop — a classic consequential loss flowing from negligent installation — is restored to judgment, subject to proof of causation and negligence, which the jury already found.

**Practical ruling:** set aside the judgment for the Seller on the counterclaim and reinstate the jury verdict for the Buyer; the disclaimer is ineffective for want of conspicuousness under § 2-316, and the remedy limitation falls with it.

**Key issues flagged:** UCC § 2-316 conspicuousness (underline-only disclaimer in uniform type fails) · exclusive-remedy/exclusion of consequential damages requires the same notice · failure of essential purpose of the exclusive remedy · jury verdict on negligent installation restored.
