# Case 19
## Clause and Dispute
The Contractor entered into a three-year indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity contract with the Government to provide instructor services for a military course, agreeing to support a maximum of fourteen classes per year. The contract incorporated, by reference, Federal Acquisition Regulation 52.212-4, "Contract Terms and Conditions—Commercial Items," which includes a termination for convenience clause stating: "[t]he Government reserves the right to terminate this contract, or any part hereof, for its sole convenience." The contract also incorporated a defense acquisition regulation requiring all services furnished under the contract to be ordered by issuance of delivery or task orders, each subject to the terms and conditions of the underlying contract.

The Government issued three yearlong task orders over the contract's term. Each task order listed one lot of training-instructor services, a price per class, and a total price corresponding to the price of fourteen classes. Each year, the Government used the Contractor's services for fewer than fourteen classes and used its own personnel to teach the remainder. The Government paid the Contractor for each class actually taught and refused to pay the total price listed in the task orders. The Contractor sued for breach of contract, contending it was entitled to the full stated price of the task orders. The government responded that the contract and task orders required payment only for services actually provided, and that, in any event, the termination for convenience clause — constructively invoked once it became clear the Government needed fewer classes than originally anticipated — limited the Contractor's recovery to convenience-termination costs it had not pleaded. The trial court granted the government summary judgment, holding that the contract contained an applicable termination for convenience clause by incorporation of FAR 52.212-4 and that a constructive termination for convenience had occurred.
## Question
What issues does this clause present, and how should it be construed and enforced in this dispute?
