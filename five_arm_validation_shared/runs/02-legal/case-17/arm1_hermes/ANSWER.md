# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 02-legal case-17

**The issues:** (1) whether, under the Federal Arbitration Act, an agreement silent or *ambiguous* on class arbitration can supply the "contractual basis" that Stolt-Nielsen requires for compelling class arbitration; (2) whether state-law contra proferentem/construction-against-drafter rules may fill that gap after First Options and Stolt-Nielsen made class procedure a consent question; and (3) whether adhesion status changes the analysis.

**How it should be construed and enforced:**

1. **The FAA sets the baseline: class arbitration may not be imposed without a contractual basis — silence is not consent.** Stolt-Nielsen holds that a party cannot be compelled to class arbitration absent a contractual basis for it, and that imposing class procedure on an agreement *silent* on the point exceeds the arbitrator's (or court's) power, because class arbitration changes the nature of arbitration — it sacrifices the informality, speed, and confidentiality that made the parties choose arbitration, imposes enormous procedural risks, and binds absent parties who never agreed. Sockets/monster-consumer cases confirm: the "contractual basis" must be found *in the agreement itself*.

2. **Ambiguity analyzed under ordinary state contract principles can supply that basis — the intermediate court's approach was substantively correct, though its contra proferentem reasoning must be handled carefully.** Under Stolt-Nielsen, courts (and arbitrators) construing consent "do not apply the presumption of silence"; but where the contract's language is genuinely *ambiguous* — not silent — ordinary principles of contract construction apply, including construction against the drafter. Here the language cuts both ways: "all claims … in connection with my employment" and "in lieu of any and all lawsuits or other civil legal proceedings" are capacious enough to reach aggregated class claims, and nothing in the agreement *excludes* class procedures (no class waiver, no individual-only language, no limits on claim joinder). An ambiguity resolved through standard state-law construction is a legitimate route to the "contractual basis" Stolt-Nielsen demands — the FAA preempts special state rules *hostile* to arbitration, not neutral contract-construction doctrines.

3. **Contra proferentem's "peculiar force" in adhesion settings is consistent with this result.** The Company drafted a one-sided, take-it-or-leave-it arbitration agreement that it declined to make explicit on class procedure. Construction against the drafter of an ambiguity the drafter could have closed with one sentence is a neutral, generally applicable principle — precisely the kind of state rule the FAA accommodates. The alternative (requiring unmistakable class consent as a matter of federal law) is what courts have required only for *clearing silence*, not for *resolving textual ambiguity*.

4. **Practical consequence:** the agreement, as properly construed, supplies the contractual basis; classwide arbitration may be compelled. The Company had the tools to avoid this (an express class waiver, like the agreement in Case 05) and chose not to use them.

**Practical ruling:** affirm — an ambiguous arbitration agreement, construed under neutral state-law principles against the drafter, supplies the contractual basis for class arbitration under the FAA; the district court's order compelling classwide arbitration stands.

**Key issues flagged:** Stolt-Nielsen's consent requirement met by ambiguity-plus-construction, not silence · neutral state construction doctrines survive FAA preemption · adhesion drafting loses on its own ambiguity · express class waivers are the drafting answer.
