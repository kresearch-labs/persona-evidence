# The Smallest Hoard

Beneath the floorboards of the Wayward Goose tavern, in a nest built inside a stolen thimble, lived the smallest dragon in the world.

His name was Flicker, and he was no bigger than a teacup. His scales were the color of banked coals, his wings were thin as onion skin, and his fire — well, his fire could light a candle. On a very good day, with the wind behind him, he had once set alight a dandelion clock and watched its seeds drift off like tiny burning parachutes. It remained his proudest military victory.

But what Flicker lacked in size, he made up for in treasure.

He had a gold coin.

It was a crown piece, old and heavy, stamped with the face of a king nobody remembered. Flicker had found it in the gutter outside the tavern, where it had fallen from a drunkard's purse, and he had dragged it home over three whole nights, the way his ancestors had once dragged off sheep. It was his hoard. Every morning he polished it with a scrap of velvet until it shone like a little sun. Every night he curled around it and slept the deep, smug sleep of dragons.

Word, unfortunately, got around.

The first thief came on a Tuesday. One-Eye Maud was a magpie with a magpie's religion: all bright things belong to the beak that finds them. She came down the chimney in a sooty rush and snatched the coin in her claws before Flicker had finished his morning polish.

He did not hesitate. He flew up the chimney after her, caught her on the rooftree, and gave her tail feathers a taste of dragonflame. It was a small flame, but a tail is a small target, and Maud went screaming over the rooftops trailing smoke like a comet, and the coin fell spinning back down the chimney, where Flicker caught it on the first bounce.

The second thief was cleverer. Grimnib was an old rat who had survived four cats and two terriers, and he did not believe in grabbing. He believed in negotiating.

"Little lizard," he said, filling the doorway of Flicker's knothole, "that coin is worth more than this tavern. Sell it to me and I'll bring you a wagon of cheese. A wagon."

"A dragon does not sell his hoard," said Flicker.

"Then I'll take it while you sleep."

"A dragon," said Flicker, "sleeps with one eye open, and his fire burns hottest in the dark." He let a lick of blue flame curl out of his nostrils, and Grimnib — who had also survived one fire — remembered it suddenly and left.

But rats talk. And what Grimnib talked about, in the wrong gutters, was gold.

The third thief came at midnight, and he was a man. His name was Hob, and he was a burglar by trade and a fool by nature, and he came with a lantern and a pry-bar and a sack for the hoard. For Grimnib had told him there was a dragon under the Wayward Goose, and where there is a dragon, there is gold, and Hob had not thought to ask about quantities.

The floorboard came up with a shriek of nails. Lantern light flooded Flicker's nest, and Hob's great moon face appeared in the gap, and his eye roved over the thimble, the velvet scrap, the single coin — and the tiny dragon standing on top of it with every scale bristling.

Hob began to laugh. "One coin," he wheezed. "One coin and a lizard in a thimble."

Flicker said nothing. Dragons do not waste breath on thieves. He simply took a deep breath — deeper than any creature his size should be able to take, for a dragon's lungs are as big as his courage, not his body — and blew.

The flame that came out was not candle-sized. It was the size of his whole heart, which is to say it filled the gap in the floorboards, and it took Hob's eyebrows off clean and set his sleeve alight and sent him backwards into the tavern's best table, which caught, and then the lantern broke, and then there was a great deal of fire and shouting, and Mistress Bellows came down with a poker and a temper and chased Hob halfway to the river.

In the morning, Mistress Bellows knelt by the hole in her floor and looked in. Flicker stood on his coin, singed and sooty and unbowed.

"So you're what's been stealing my thimbles," she said.

Flicker said nothing, but he did not deny it.

"Well," she said, and set down a saucer of cream and a fresh thimble, "any dragon that fights like that for one coin can guard my till anytime."

That night Flicker curled around his coin in a nest one thimble richer, and if he slept the deep, smug sleep of dragons, who could blame him? For the world is full of creatures who measure a dragon by the size of his body, and Flicker knew the truth: a dragon is measured by the size of what he defends, and the fierceness with which he defends it.

And his hoard was the most fiercely defended gold coin in all the world.
