**1. The misconception behind the answer**

Two faulty ideas are tangled together:

- **"Dividing always makes a number smaller."** This is only true when the divisor is *greater than 1*. Here the divisor is 1/2, which is *less* than 1, so the answer must come out *bigger* than 3 — a sanity check the student skipped.
- **Dividing by "the 2 on the bottom."** The student reduced the fraction 1/2 to just its denominator and computed 3 ÷ 2. But 1/2 is a quantity in its own right — one half of a whole — and the question is how many of those halves fit into 3, not how many 2s fit into 3.

**2. Explaining the correct concept to the student**

"You already know how to answer questions like 'how many 2s are in 6?' — that's 6 ÷ 2 = 3. Our question is exactly the same kind: 3 ÷ 1/2 asks 'how many halves are in 3?'

Count them: 1 whole chocolate bar breaks into 2 halves. So 3 wholes break into 3 × 2 = 6 halves. **3 ÷ 1/2 = 6.**

Check it backwards: 6 × 1/2 = 3 ✓ — multiplication undoes division, so 6 is right.

And notice what really happens to 'dividing makes smaller': 6 is *bigger* than 3! That's because each piece — a half — is smaller than 1, so *more* pieces fit. Dividing makes a number smaller only when you divide by more than 1; divide by less than 1 and the answer gets bigger.

Rule for any problem like this: **dividing by a fraction = flip it and multiply.**
- 3 ÷ 1/2 = 3 × 2 = 6
- Your turn: 2 ÷ 1/4 — how many quarters are in 2? (2 × 4 = **8**)"
