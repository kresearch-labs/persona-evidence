## 1. The misconception behind the answer

The student holds the belief that **"dividing always makes a number smaller."** That is only true when the divisor is greater than 1 — dividing by a number *less than* 1 makes the result *bigger*, because you are counting how many small pieces fit inside. On top of that, the student ignored the numerator of 1/2 completely and treated ÷ 1/2 as if it were ÷ 2 (dividing by just the bottom number of the fraction). Dividing by a fraction is not the same as dividing by its denominator.

## 2. Explaining the correct concept to the student

Division asks: **"How many of the second number fit into the first?"** So 3 ÷ 1/2 really asks: *how many halves are there in 3 wholes?*

Every whole thing contains 2 halves. So 3 wholes contain 3 × 2 = **6** halves. The answer is 6, not 1½ — and notice the answer got *bigger*, exactly because 1/2 is a small piece.

You can always check a division by multiplying back: 6 × 1/2 = 3 ✓

A rule to remember: **to divide by a fraction, flip it upside down and multiply.**
3 ÷ 1/2 = 3 × 2/1 = 6. Dividing by 1/2 is the same as multiplying by 2 — that's why the answer grows instead of shrinking.

Try one yourself: how many thirds fit into 4 wholes? (4 ÷ 1/3 = 4 × 3 = 12.)
