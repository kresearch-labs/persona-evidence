# Case 08 — Answer

## 1. The misconception

The student believes two things that are both wrong here:
- **"Dividing always makes a number smaller."** This is only true when you divide by a number *greater than 1*. Dividing by a fraction between 0 and 1 makes the number *bigger*, because you're asking how many small pieces fit inside it.
- **"3 ÷ 1/2 means 3 ÷ 2."** The student ignored the numerator and treated the fraction bar as a plain division sign, splitting 3 into 2 parts instead of asking how many halves are in 3.

## 2. Explaining the correct concept

Think of what the question is really asking: **"How many halves fit inside 3?"**

Picture 3 whole pizzas. Cut each one in half. How many half-pieces do you have? Each whole gives you 2 halves, so 3 wholes give you 3 × 2 = **6 halves**.

So 3 ÷ 1/2 = 6 — not 1.5.

The rule to remember: **dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal** (flip the fraction and multiply):

3 ÷ 1/2 = 3 × 2/1 = 6

Why does the answer get *bigger*? Because a half is smaller than a whole, so more of them fit. Dividing by 2 would mean sharing 3 between 2 people (each gets 1.5). Dividing by 1/2 means counting how many half-size pieces are in 3 — a completely different question.

**Try it yourself:** How many thirds are in 4? (Hint: 4 ÷ 1/3 = 4 × 3 = 12. Check it by picturing 4 wholes each cut into 3 pieces.)

*Justification: the student's answer of 1½ comes from computing 3 ÷ 2 and from the false belief that division always shrinks a number; the correct answer is 6, since dividing by 1/2 means multiplying by its reciprocal, 2.*
