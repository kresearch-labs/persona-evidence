# Case 03 — Answer

## 1. The misconception
The student believes a negative exponent makes the *value* of the power negative ("the minus sign in the exponent makes the whole thing negative"). In reality, the sign of the exponent does not control the sign of the result — a negative exponent means **take the reciprocal**, not "make it negative." A positive base raised to any power (positive, negative, or zero) always stays positive.

## 2. The correct concept, explained to the student
A negative exponent is an instruction to **flip the base into a fraction** — it means "divide instead of multiply":

$$a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}$$

Why this makes sense: each time the exponent drops by 1, you divide by the base. Look at the pattern:

- 4³ = 64
- 4² = 16  (÷ 4)
- 4¹ = 4   (÷ 4)
- 4⁰ = 1   (÷ 4)
- 4⁻¹ = 1/4  (÷ 4)
- 4⁻² = 1/16  (÷ 4)

The pattern never goes negative — the values just get smaller as fractions. So:

$$4^{-2} = \frac{1}{4^2} = \frac{1}{16}$$

**How to solve a similar problem:** when you see a negative exponent, (1) write the base with the exponent made positive, (2) put it under a 1 as a fraction, (3) evaluate. Example: 5⁻³ = 1/5³ = 1/125. The answer is small, not negative.

**Justification:** The student's error of −16 comes from treating the exponent's sign as the result's sign; the correct rule a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ gives 4⁻² = 1/16, confirmed by the divide-by-the-base pattern of decreasing exponents.
