# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 05-tutoring case-03 (evaluate 4^-2)

## 1. The misconception

The student reads the negative exponent as a **sign instruction** — "the minus sign travels to the answer" — instead of understanding what a negative exponent *means*: repeated **division** (or "flip into a fraction"). The confusion is linguistic: in arithmetic they've learned a minus sign makes things negative, so a minus in the exponent "should" too. The distinction they're missing: **a negative exponent is not a negative number — it's a direction (count down) in the pattern of dividing by the base.**

## 2. Explaining it to the student

"Exponents are a pattern of multiplying by the same number over and over. Watch what happens as the exponent counts DOWN by one — divide by 4 each time:

4^3 = 64 
4^2 = 16 ← divided 64 by 4 
4^1 = 4 ← divided 16 by 4 
4^0 = 1 ← divided 4 by 4 (that's why anything^0 = 1) 
4^-1 = 1/4 ← divide 1 by 4 again 
**4^-2 = 1/16** ← divide 1/4 by 4 one more time

See it? The negative exponent doesn't make the answer *negative* — it makes the answer *smaller*, because you've hopped to the other side of 1 and you're dividing.

The memory trick: a negative exponent means **'flip it into a fraction.'** 
a^-n = 1/a^n

So: 4^-2 = 1/4² = 1/16. Positive 1/16 — not negative anything.

One-sentence version: **the minus sign in the exponent is an instruction about direction (divide), not about sign (negativity).** The base being negative can make an answer negative; the *exponent* being negative makes it a fraction.

Sanity check you can do anytime: a negative power should give you a number *between* the previous steps in the pattern. Your answer −16 doesn't even fit the sequence (64, 16, 4, 1, ¼...) — 1/16 does."

## Quick check for the student (same idea, new problems)

- 2^-3 = 1/2³ = **1/8** (pattern: 8, 4, 2, 1, ½, ¼, ⅛) 
- 10^-2 = **1/100** (this is exactly why our number system writes small numbers that way) 
- 5^-1 = **1/5** 

**Rule to remember:** negative exponent = flip it over (put it under 1). The base's sign controls positivity; the exponent's sign controls direction.
