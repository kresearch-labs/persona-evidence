## 1. The misconception

The student is treating the negative sign in the exponent as if it were a negative sign on the answer — reading 4^-2 as -(4^2) = -16. In other words, they think the sign of the exponent transfers to the value of the power, instead of understanding that a negative exponent changes the *operation* (dividing instead of multiplying), not the sign of the result.

## 2. The correct concept

A negative exponent means "take the reciprocal" — flip the base and make the exponent positive:

**a^-n = 1 / a^n**

So the minus sign in the exponent is an instruction to divide, not a sign for the answer:

4^-2 = 1 / 4^2 = 1/16

A good way to see why: watch what happens as the exponent counts down, dividing by 4 each step —

4^3 = 64 → 4^2 = 16 → 4^1 = 4 → 4^0 = 1 → 4^-1 = 1/4 → 4^-2 = 1/16

The pattern keeps dividing by 4; it never turns negative. (A negative *answer* could only come from a negative base, like (-4)^2 — the exponent's sign has nothing to do with it.)

**Try it yourself:** evaluate 3^-2. Answer: 3^-2 = 1/3^2 = 1/9.
