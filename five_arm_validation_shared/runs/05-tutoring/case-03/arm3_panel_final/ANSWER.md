# Case 03 — 4^-2 answered as -16

## 1. The misconception

The student treats the minus sign in the exponent as a sign that transfers to the *result*: "negative exponent → the power comes out negative." They compute 4^2 = 16 correctly, then flip the sign of that value to get -16.

In other words, they read the negative as an instruction about the **sign** of the answer, when it is actually an instruction about the **position** of the power — it means "take the reciprocal," i.e. put the power under 1. The exponent's sign never determines whether the answer is positive or negative; it determines whether the power multiplies (numerator) or divides (denominator).

## 2. Explaining the correct concept

**Hook it to what they already got right.** They know 4^2 = 16 — that part is correct, so start there. Exponents are shorthand for repeated multiplication, and there is a pattern when the exponent steps down by 1: each step *divides by 4*.

- 4^3 = 64
- 4^2 = 16
- 4^1 = 4
- 4^0 = 1
- 4^-1 = 1/4
- 4^-2 = 1/16

Following the same "divide by 4 each step" rule past zero, the minus sign doesn't make the value negative — it makes it a *fraction*. The power moves below the bar.

**The rule:** a negative exponent means reciprocal, not negative value:

> x^-n = 1 / x^n   (x ≠ 0)

So 4^-2 = 1 / 4^2 = 1/16 — a small positive number, not -16.

**Fix the sign intuition directly.** The sign of a power comes from the *base*, never from the exponent: 4^2 = 16 and (-4)^2 = 16 are both positive. A negative exponent changes the *size* (it makes the value less than 1), not the sign.

**Try one to check understanding:** evaluate 2^-3. Answer: 2^-3 = 1/2^3 = 1/8. If they can do that — keep the base, make the exponent positive in the denominator — the concept has landed.
