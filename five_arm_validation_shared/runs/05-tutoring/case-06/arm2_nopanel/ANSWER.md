# Case 06 — Answer

## 1. The misconception

The student is overgeneralizing proportional reasoning: "if the triangle is scaled by 2, *everything* in it is multiplied by 2." That rule is true for lengths (and area scales by 2² = 4), but the student extends it to angles. Angles are not lengths — they measure rotation, not size — and scaling a figure does not change its angles at all. So "2 × 180 = 360" feels consistent but applies a length rule to a non-length quantity.

## 2. Explaining the correct concept to the student

Similar triangles have the **same shape**, just different sizes. "Same shape" means two things: corresponding sides are proportional (that's the ×2 you noticed — 3→6, 4→8, 5→10), and corresponding **angles are exactly equal**. The angles don't grow, because an angle measures how two sides *tilt* relative to each other, and stretching a figure uniformly doesn't change any tilt.

Think of enlarging a photo on a copier at 200%: everything gets bigger, but the corners of the picture don't bend — a right angle stays a right angle. Or imagine the 3-4-5 triangle drawn on a rubber sheet and stretched to double size: the corners keep the same opening.

So the larger triangle has the *same three angles* as the smaller one, and since **every** triangle's angles sum to 180° — a fact about all triangles, not about a particular size — the answer is **180°**.

Quick check you can do yourself: the 3-4-5 triangle has angles ≈ 37°, 53°, 90° (it's a right triangle). The 6-8-10 triangle has exactly the same angles: ≈ 37°, 53°, 90°. And 37 + 53 + 90 = 180.

What *does* scale by 2: the side lengths and the perimeter (18 → 36). What scales by 4: the area (6 → 24). The angles are the one thing that never changes under scaling — that's precisely what makes the triangles *similar*.

Try it: a triangle with sides 5, 12, 13 is enlarged so its sides are 15, 36, 39. What is its largest angle? (Answer: 90° — same shape, same angles, and 5-12-13 is a right triangle.)
