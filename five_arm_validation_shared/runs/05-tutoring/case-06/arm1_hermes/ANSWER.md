# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 05-tutoring case-06 (similar triangles, doubled sides)

## 1. The misconception

The student has learned a true rule — **"in similar figures, everything scales by the scale factor"** — and over-applied it to a quantity that doesn't scale: **angles**. When a figure is enlarged, *lengths* multiply by the scale factor (2), *areas* multiply by the scale factor squared (2² = 4), but **angles do not change at all** — similar means "same shape," and "same shape" is precisely the statement that the angles are identical. The student also confused "bigger triangle" with "more angle content" — size and angle measure are independent.

## 2. Explaining it to the student

"Here's the key idea, and it's simpler than it looks: when you blow up a figure — like enlarging a photo — the *lengths* stretch, but the **corners don't bend**.

Think about a photo. If you zoom in on a picture of a slice of pizza, the slice gets bigger, but the pointy tip is still exactly as pointy as before. Zooming doesn't sharpen or soften corners — it just makes everything longer.

That's what 'similar' means in geometry: **same shape, different size** — and 'same shape' is defined by the angles being *identical*. If the larger triangle's angles were twice as big, the shape would actually be different — a triangle with angles 100°, 60°, 20° doesn't look like one with 50°, 30°, 25°.

So:
- Small triangle: 3-4-5 (a right triangle — 90° + the other two angles). 
- Large triangle: 6-8-10 — same shape, same three angles, just longer sides.

Both triangles have interior angles summing to **180°**.

The 180° rule comes from the fact that any triangle, cut from any sheet of paper at any size, has angles that fit together into a straight line. Make the triangle ten times bigger or a hundred times bigger: three angles, one straight line, 180°. Angle sums depend on being a triangle — not on size.

**The scaling rules to keep straight (this is the useful table):**
- lengths: × scale factor (sides 3,4,5 → 6,8,10) ✓ 
- angles: × 1 — unchanged ✓ 
- perimeter: × scale factor (12 → 24) 
- area: × scale factor² (a 3-4-5 right triangle has area 6; the 6-8-10 one has area 24 — that's ×4, not ×2)

So 'everything doubles' isn't quite true — *lengths* double, areas quadruple, and angles stay put."

## Quick check for the student (same idea, new problem)

*A triangle has angles 40°, 60°, 80°. A similar triangle has sides 3× as long. What are its angles?* 
**40°, 60°, 80°** — identical. And its angles sum to **180°**, same as always. 
Bonus: if the small triangle's area is 10, the big one's area is 3² × 10 = **90**.

**Rule to remember:** similar figures stretch lengths, square areas, and never touch angles.
