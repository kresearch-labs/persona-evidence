# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 05-tutoring case-07 (GCF: 24 pencils, 36 erasers)

## 1. The misconception

The student has memorized "GCF = greatest common *something*" and guessed at which something: they picked the **greatest number both values go into** — that's the definition of a common *multiple* (and 72 is indeed the LCM of 24 and 36). The confusion: **factor vs multiple**. A factor of 24 is a number that goes *into* 24 evenly (a piece-size); a multiple of 24 is a number 24 goes *into* (a total). The kit problem asks for the largest number of *kits* — and a kit must use whole pencils and whole erasers — so we need the largest number that divides both quantities: the **greatest common factor**, not the least common multiple.

## 2. Explaining it to the student

"Read the question like a story, and it tells you which one it wants. Mrs. Lee is *splitting* her supplies into equal kits. Splitting means dividing. So the number of kits has to **go into 24 evenly** (each kit gets the same pencils) AND **go into 36 evenly** (each kit gets the same erasers).

A number that goes into 24 evenly is called a **factor** of 24. So we need a common factor — and the question says 'greatest possible number of kits,' so we want the **Greatest Common Factor**.

Find it by listing factors (numbers that divide in evenly):

- Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, **8, 12**, 24 
- Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, **12**, 18, 36

Common factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. The greatest is **12**.

So Mrs. Lee can make **12 kits**, and each kit gets 24 ÷ 12 = 2 pencils and 36 ÷ 12 = 3 erasers. Nothing left over, all kits identical. ✓

Now, about your 72: you found the Least Common *Multiple* — the first number that both 24 and 36 go *into*. It's a real, useful number, but it answers a different question: 'when do two repeating events line up?' (Like: two bells ring every 24 and 36 minutes — when do they ring together? At 72.) Check why 72 can't be the kit count: you can't make 72 kits from 24 pencils — you'd need to cut pencils into thirds. The kits must use **whole** pencils, and 24 ÷ 72 is less than one pencil per kit. Impossible.

**How to decide GCF vs LCM from the words:**
- 'Split into equal groups / no leftovers / greatest number of groups' → **GCF** (piece-size). 
- 'When do they happen together again / first time both events coincide' → **LCM** (line-up point). 

Memory trick: **F**actor = Fewer (or equal) than the numbers — it goes in. **M**ultiple = More than the numbers — they go into it.'"

## Quick check for the student (same idea, new problems)

1. *18 stickers and 27 candies, identical treat bags, none left over, most bags possible?* 
Factors of 18: 1,2,3,6,**9**,18. Factors of 27: 1,3,**9**,27. GCF = **9 bags** (2 stickers + 3 candies each).
2. *Two lights blink every 12 and 18 seconds. When do they blink together?* 
Multiples of 12: 12, 24, **36**… of 18: 18, **36**… LCM = **36 seconds**.

**Rule to remember:** splitting into groups → GCF (factors are pieces). Waiting for events to sync → LCM (multiples are arrivals).
