# Case 07 — Answer

## 1. The misconception
The student has confused the **GCF (greatest common factor)** with the **LCM (least common multiple)**. They think a "common factor" is a number that 24 and 36 *go into* (a common multiple), and they read "greatest" as "the biggest number I can find," so they landed on 72 — which is actually the LCM of 24 and 36. A factor goes *into* a number; a multiple is what a number goes into. The student has the direction backwards.

## 2. Explaining the correct concept
A **factor** of a number is a number that divides it evenly, with nothing left over. Factors of 24 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24 — each one splits 24 into equal groups with no remainder. A number that 24 "goes into," like 48 or 72, is a **multiple**, and that's a different thing.

The **GCF** is the greatest number that is a factor of *both* numbers — the biggest number that divides both evenly:
- Factors of 24: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, **12**, 24
- Factors of 36: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, **12**, 18, 36
- Common factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 → the greatest is **12**.

"Greatest" doesn't mean "pick a huge number" — it means the largest one *on the list of shared factors*. Also notice: a factor of 24 can never be bigger than 24 itself, so 72 could never be a factor of 24 at all.

Why this fits the problem: each kit must be identical with nothing left over, so the number of kits has to divide both 24 pencils and 36 erasers evenly — that's exactly a common factor. The greatest possible number of kits is the GCF: **12 kits** (each with 2 pencils and 3 erasers). Quick check: 12 × 2 = 24 pencils, 12 × 3 = 36 erasers, nothing left over. ✓
