# Case 11 — Photosynthesis reactants and products

## 1. The misconception behind the answer

The student is overgeneralizing the animal breathing model to plants: "leaves are like lungs, and everything alive breathes in oxygen and breathes out carbon dioxide, so a plant's inputs and outputs must work the same way." This makes them conflate two opposite processes. The gas exchange they describe (O₂ in, CO₂ out) is real — but it is **cellular respiration**, not photosynthesis. So they have answered the question by describing respiration and labeling it photosynthesis, which reverses the actual inputs and outputs and leaves out the single most important product: glucose, the food the plant makes. Their equation (6O₂ + light → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O) is essentially the correct equation run backwards, with light stuck in the wrong place.

## 2. Explaining the correct concept to the student

You're actually half right — plants *do* take in oxygen and give off carbon dioxide. That process is called **respiration**, and every living cell does it, plant or animal, day and night. But respiration is the plant *burning* food for energy. The question asked about **photosynthesis**, which is how the plant *makes* that food in the first place. They are two different, opposite processes — and you described the wrong one.

The word itself is a hint: **photo** = light, **synthesis** = making. Photosynthesis means "making with light."

**What a leaf really is:** not a lung, but a solar-powered kitchen.

- **Ingredients in (reactants):**
  - **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)** — from the air, through tiny pores in the leaf
  - **Water (H₂O)** — up from the soil through the roots
  - **Light energy** — captured by the green chlorophyll (light is the *power source*, not an ingredient that turns into matter)
- **What comes out (products):**
  - **Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)** — the sugar. This is the whole point: the plant's food, which it uses to grow
  - **Oxygen (O₂)** — a leftover byproduct, released into the air (lucky for us)

**The overall equation:**

$$6CO_2 + 6H_2O + \text{light energy} \rightarrow C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2$$

Compare it with yours: same numbers, opposite direction. You wrote the process backwards.

**How the two processes fit together** (this is the key idea):

| | Photosynthesis | Respiration |
|---|---|---|
| What it does | **builds** sugar (stores energy) | **breaks down** sugar (releases energy) |
| Inputs | CO₂ + H₂O + light | glucose + O₂ |
| Outputs | glucose + O₂ | CO₂ + H₂O (+ energy) |
| When/where | in the light, in chloroplasts | all the time, in every cell |

Plants do **both**: photosynthesis in the daylight, respiration around the clock — just like you. In sunlight a plant photosynthesizes *more* than it respires, so overall it takes in CO₂ and gives off O₂. That's the gas exchange you were thinking of — it's the *net result* of photosynthesis winning, not the plant "breathing."

**Self-check for the next problem like this:** photosynthesis is food-**making**, so glucose must appear among the **products** and light among the **inputs**. If your products are only CO₂ and water, you've written respiration by mistake — flip it.

*Try it:* "A water plant in a beaker sits in sunlight. Which gas does it release as bubbles?" → CO₂ and water are the inputs, glucose is made, so the leftover gas released is **oxygen**.
