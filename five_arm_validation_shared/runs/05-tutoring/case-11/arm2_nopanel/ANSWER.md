# Case 11 — Photosynthesis

## 1. The misconception behind the answer

The student has **reversed photosynthesis — they described cellular respiration instead**, driven by the analogy "plants breathe like animals." Because they believe leaves work like lungs ("everything alive breathes in oxygen and breathes out carbon dioxide"), they put oxygen on the input side and carbon dioxide on the output side, which is exactly backwards for photosynthesis. Two related slips follow from that one wrong idea:

- **Oxygen and carbon dioxide are swapped.** In photosynthesis, CO₂ is taken in and O₂ is released; the student has it the other way around.
- **The products are wrong.** They list only CO₂ and water as products and miss the main product of photosynthesis entirely: **glucose (sugar)**, the food the plant makes. Their "equation" (6O₂ + light → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O) is respiration written backwards, with the sugar missing from both sides.

(The "plants breathe" idea isn't completely silly — plants *do* respire all the time, taking in O₂ and releasing CO₂ like we do. The misconception is thinking that's what photosynthesis is.)

## 2. Explaining the correct concept to the student

"Your idea that plants breathe like we do is a really good observation — plants *do* respire, and that part you described (oxygen in, carbon dioxide out) is true. But that process is called **respiration**, and it's actually the *opposite* of photosynthesis. Photosynthesis is how a plant makes its own food, and it runs the other way.

Think of a plant as a tiny kitchen, not a pair of lungs:

- **Ingredients it takes in (reactants):** carbon dioxide from the air (through tiny holes in the leaves) and water from the soil (through the roots).
- **Energy source:** sunlight. (Light isn't an ingredient that turns into something — it's the power that drives the reaction, like electricity powering a mixer.)
- **What it makes (products):** glucose (a sugar — the plant's food) and oxygen (which the plant releases, and which we get to breathe).

The overall equation:

**6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂**
(carbon dioxide + water + light → glucose + oxygen)

A quick way to keep them straight: **photosynthesis and respiration are mirror images.**

| | Reactants (in) | Products (out) |
|---|---|---|
| **Photosynthesis** | CO₂ + H₂O (+ light) | glucose + O₂ |
| **Respiration** | glucose + O₂ | CO₂ + H₂O (+ energy) |

So whenever a question says the plant is *making food* or *using sunlight*, you're in photosynthesis: CO₂ and water go in, glucose and oxygen come out. Whenever it says the plant is *burning food for energy* (day or night), that's respiration: glucose and oxygen go in, CO₂ and water come out.

Try this one yourself: *A plant in a sunny window takes in carbon dioxide through its leaves and water through its roots. What does it produce?* If you can answer "glucose and oxygen," you've got it."
