# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 05-tutoring case-11 (photosynthesis: inputs, outputs, and the equation)

## 1. The misconception

**"Plants breathe like animals" — the student has mapped human respiration onto photosynthesis and reversed the whole process.**

The student reasons that because animals take in oxygen and exhale carbon dioxide, a plant must do the same thing. So they conclude the plant's *inputs* are oxygen and sunlight and its *outputs* are carbon dioxide and water — and they even write the photosynthesis equation backwards (6O₂ + light → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O). Two related errors are bundled here:

1. **Conflating photosynthesis with respiration/gas exchange.** The student has described (roughly) what animals do, not what photosynthesis is. In reality, photosynthesis is essentially the *reverse* of respiration: plants take in CO₂ and water and build sugar, releasing oxygen.
2. **Treating light as a substance the plant "breathes in."** Light is the *energy source* for the reaction, usually written above the arrow — the actual matter-based reactants are carbon dioxide and water.

The "leaves are like lungs" analogy feels intuitive, which is exactly why it's such a persistent misconception.

## 2. Explaining it to the student

Your lung analogy was a smart guess, but leaves don't work like lungs — it's almost the opposite! Here's the key difference:

- **You** eat food that someone else made, then use oxygen to *burn* that food for energy, releasing CO₂.
- **A plant** doesn't need anyone to make its food — it makes its own sugar using sunlight. To *build* sugar, it needs raw ingredients: **carbon dioxide** (taken in through tiny pores on the undersides of leaves called stomata) and **water** (absorbed by the roots). The sunlight provides the energy to glue those ingredients together into glucose, and **oxygen** is left over and released.

So the real equation is basically yours flipped around:

$$\text{6CO}_2 + \text{6H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{\text{light}} \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + \text{6O}_2$$

(carbon dioxide + water + light energy → glucose + oxygen)

Here's a concrete way to see it: think about a huge oak tree. Where did all that heavy wood come from? Not from the soil — the soil barely changes. It came mostly from **carbon dioxide pulled out of the air** plus **water from the roots**, assembled by sunlight. If plants breathed in O₂ and exhaled CO₂ like we do, there'd be no way for them to gain all that mass.

One last thing that might clear up your confusion: plants *do* respire too (they break down sugar for energy just like we do, using O₂ and releasing CO₂). But that's a separate process. **Photosynthesis** builds food; **respiration** burns it. During the day, photosynthesis runs so strongly that plants give off far more oxygen than they consume — which is great news for us!

## Quick check for the student (same idea, new problem)

**Problem:** A student places a sprig of Elodea (an aquatic plant) in a sunny aquarium. Over a few hours, tiny bubbles form on the leaves and rise to the surface.

(a) What gas is inside the bubbles?
(b) What two substances did the plant take in to produce that gas?
(c) Write the overall equation for the process.

**Solution:**
(a) **Oxygen** — it's the byproduct of photosynthesis, which is why the bubbles appear when the plant is in sunlight.
(b) **Carbon dioxide** (dissolved in the water, entering through the leaves) and **water** — plus light energy to power the reaction.
(c) 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (with light energy)

Notice the pattern: if you ever find yourself writing oxygen as an *input* and carbon dioxide as an *output* for photosynthesis, flip it — that's respiration talking, not photosynthesis! 🌱
