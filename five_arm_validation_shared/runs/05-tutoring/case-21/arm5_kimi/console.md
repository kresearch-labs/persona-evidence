**Summary of the answer:**

1. **The misconception:** The student mistakes the *rhetorical defense* of secession ("states' rights") for its *cause*. The specific right at stake was the preservation and expansion of slavery — "states' rights" was the legal framing around that one issue, not a neutral principle applied consistently.

2. **The correct concept, explained for the student:** Always ask "rights to do *what*?" — a principle alone doesn't start a war. The primary sources answer it directly: the secession declarations of South Carolina, Mississippi, Georgia, and Texas name slavery as the reason; Stephens' "Cornerstone Speech" calls slavery the Confederacy's foundation; and the South abandoned states' rights whenever it threatened slavery (e.g., demanding the federal Fugitive Slave Act override Northern state laws). The "most Southerners didn't own slaves" point fails because the entire economy and social hierarchy rested on slavery, so non-slaveholders still had a stake in it. The transferable skill: when a conflict is explained by a vague principle, identify the specific interest it protected and check what participants said and did at the time.
