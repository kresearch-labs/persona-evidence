# Case 10 — Mass vs. Weight

## 1. The misconception

The student believes **mass and weight are the same thing** — that "weight" is just another word for mass, measured in kilograms, and that both are properties an object carries with it unchanged everywhere. This is reinforced by everyday language: bathroom scales and doctors report "weight" in kilograms, so the two ideas collapse into one. The real distinction — mass is the amount of matter, weight is the *force* of gravity pulling on that matter — never comes up, so the student concludes nothing can change between Earth and the Moon.

## 2. Explaining the correct concept

**Mass and weight are two different things:**

- **Mass** is the amount of matter in your body. It is measured in **kilograms (kg)** and it does **not** change — whether you're on Earth, the Moon, or floating in deep space, the same atoms are there. Mass is also a measure of how hard it is to accelerate you (inertia).
- **Weight** is the **force** with which gravity pulls on your mass. Because it's a force, it is measured in **newtons (N)**, not kilograms. Weight *does* change when gravity changes, because it is literally the pull of gravity on you.

They are connected by one formula:

**W = m × g**

where *m* is mass in kg and *g* is the gravitational field strength in N/kg. On Earth, g ≈ 9.8 N/kg; on the Moon, gravity is about 1/6 as strong, so g ≈ 9.8 ÷ 6 ≈ 1.6 N/kg.

**Applying it to the problem:**

- Mass on the Moon: **60 kg** — unchanged, because her matter didn't change.
- Weight on the Moon: W = 60 kg × 1.6 N/kg ≈ **96 N** (about 1/6 of her ~590 N Earth weight).

**A quick check you can use on similar problems:** if the question asks "how much matter?" or gives/asks for kilograms, that's mass — it travels with you unchanged. If it asks about gravity pulling, "how heavy," or gives/asks for newtons, that's weight — multiply mass by the local g. So an astronaut on the Moon feels light and could jump high (her *weight* dropped to 1/6), but stopping her once she's moving is just as hard as on Earth (her *mass* is unchanged).
