**1. The misconception:** The student is treating percent changes as fixed amounts that add and subtract (like "+20 and −20 cancel"), when in fact each percent applies to a different starting price — the 20% off is taken of the *raised* price, not the original one.

**2. Explaining the correct concept:** A percent change multiplies the price by a factor: +20% means "times 1.20" and −20% means "times 0.80." Since the second percent is taken of the *new* price, you multiply the factors instead of adding the percents: $80 × 1.20 = $96, then $96 × 0.80 = **$76.80**. A quick check that it can't return to $80: 20% of $96 ($19.20) is bigger than 20% of $80 ($16), so the discount removes more dollars than the raise added — the price ends up lower. To solve any similar problem, multiply by (1 + rate) for an increase and by (1 − rate) for a decrease, in order: e.g. $50 up 10% then down 10% → $50 × 1.10 × 0.90 = $49.50, not $50.
