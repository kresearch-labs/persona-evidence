# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 05-tutoring case-04 (20% up then 20% off)

## 1. The misconception

The student is treating percentages as **absolute amounts that add and subtract** — "+20 and −20 cancel" — when percentages are **relative to whatever they're a percentage OF**, and that "whatever" changes. +20% is 20% of $80; −20% is 20% of the *new, higher* price ($96). Same percent, different bases — so they don't cancel. The underlying misconception: percent changes are not commutative additions; each one multiplies the current value.

## 2. Explaining it to the student

"Percent changes are sneaky because each percent is taken of a *different* starting number. The +20% is 20% of $80. The −20% is 20% of the *new* price — which is bigger, so the 20% you subtract is bigger than the 20% you added.

Do it in two steps and watch:

1. Price goes UP 20%: 20% of $80 = $16. New price = 80 + 16 = **$96**. 
2. Then 20% OFF: 20% of $96 = $19.20. Sale price = 96 − 19.20 = **$76.80**.

So the jacket ends at $76.80 — two dollars *less* than the start, not back at $80. The increase added $16; the decrease removed $19.20. Same percent, different base, different dollars.

**Why that happens (the one idea to keep):** a percent change is multiplication, not addition. 
- +20% means ×1.20 
- −20% means ×0.80 

Do both: 80 × 1.20 × 0.80 = 80 × 0.96 = **$76.80**. 

Multiplying by 1.20 then by 0.80 is the same as multiplying by 0.96 once — you lose 4%, because the 'down 20' multiplies the *after* price.

The general truth: **up a% then down a% never returns to the start — you always end up a little lower** (by a²%, here 4%). Same if you do it in the other order: down 20 first ($64), then up 20 ($64 × 1.20 = $76.80). Same answer — multiplication doesn't care which happens first."

## Quick check for the student (same idea, new problem)

*A $50 game goes up 10%, then down 10%. Final price?* 
50 × 1.10 = 55 → 55 × 0.90 = **$49.50** — a nickel under where you started. 
And: *up 50% then down 50%?* 50 → 75 → **$37.50**. The bigger the percent, the bigger the surprise.

**Rule to remember:** percent changes multiply the current value; same percent up and down always ends *below* the start.
