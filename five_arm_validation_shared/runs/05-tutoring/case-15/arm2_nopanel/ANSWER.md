# Case 15 — Why the ice cube floats

## 1. The misconception

The student believes **weight alone decides floating**: light things float, heavy things sink. This is the "heavy = sinks, light = floats" misconception. It fails immediately in real life — a massive 100,000-tonne ship floats, while a tiny 5-gram coin sinks — so weight cannot be the deciding factor. The student is also treating "9 grams" as if it were small in some absolute sense, without comparing it to anything.

## 2. The correct concept, explained to the student

What actually decides floating is **density** — how tightly packed the stuff inside an object is — not how much it weighs.

Density means mass packed into a certain amount of space. A shoebox full of feathers and the same box full of rocks weigh very differently: the rocks are denser. Water has a density of about 1 gram per cubic centimeter.

Here is the key fact about your ice cube: **when water freezes, it expands** — the same amount of water takes up MORE space as ice. Spread the same mass over more space, and the density goes DOWN. So ice has a density of about 0.92 g/cm³, slightly less than liquid water's 1.00 g/cm³.

Water also pushes UP on anything you put in it (this upward push is called buoyancy). The push equals the weight of the water the object shoves aside. Your 9 g ice cube only needs to shove aside 9 g of water to be fully supported — and because ice is less dense, 9 g of water fits in LESS space than the whole ice cube takes up. So the cube settles with part of its volume underwater and the rest sticking out above the surface. That's why ice floats — and why roughly 90% of an iceberg hides below the surface.

**The rule to remember:** an object floats if it is *less dense than* the liquid it's in; it sinks if it is *more dense than* the liquid. Weight by itself tells you nothing.

**Test yourself with this rule:** a 5 g coin sinks in water, but a 100 g wooden block floats. Which one is less dense than water? (The block — wood's density is around 0.6 g/cm³, less than water's 1.00. The coin's metal is denser than water, so it sinks no matter how light it is.)
