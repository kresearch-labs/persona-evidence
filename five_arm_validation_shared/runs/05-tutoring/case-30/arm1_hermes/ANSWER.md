# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 05-tutoring case-30 (Nominal vs. real income — adjusting for inflation)

## 1. The misconception

The student has confused **nominal income** (the number of dollars on the paycheck) with **real income** (what those dollars can actually buy). This is often called the "money illusion."

Their arithmetic is actually correct: $44,000 ÷ $40,000 = 1.10, so Maria did get a 10% *raise*. The error is concluding that a 10% raise automatically means 10% *more stuff*. That would only be true if prices stayed flat. Since prices rose 10% over the same period, every dollar Maria earns now buys about 9% less ($1.00 ÷ $1.10 ≈ $0.91 of 2020 buying power) — and that exactly cancels out her raise. The statement "more dollars means more buying power" ignores the other half of the story: the price of the goods themselves also changed.

## 2. Explaining it to the student

A salary only matters because of what it can buy — so to compare salaries across years, you have to compare them against prices, not just against each other.

**The key comparison:** 

$$\text{Real change} \approx \%\text{ change in salary} - \%\text{ change in prices (inflation)}$$

(or, more precisely, real growth = (1 + salary growth) ÷ (1 + inflation) − 1.)

**Concrete example — think in baskets:**

Suppose a basket of groceries costs $100 in 2020.

- **2020:** Maria earns $40,000 → she can buy $40,000 ÷ $100 = **400 baskets**.
- **2025:** Prices rose 10%, so the same basket now costs $110. Maria earns $44,000 → she can buy $44,000 ÷ $110 = **400 baskets**.

Same 400 baskets! Her 10% raise gave her 10% more dollars, but each basket costs 10% more dollars, so the two effects cancel out exactly. The answer to the original question is: **she can buy the same amount of goods.**

**Rule of thumb for similar problems:**
- Raise **bigger** than inflation → you can buy **more**
- Raise **equal** to inflation → you can buy the **same**
- Raise **smaller** than inflation → you can buy **less**

So don't stop at "her salary went up X%" — always ask "and by how much did prices go up?" and compare the two percentages.

## Quick check for the student (same idea, new problem)

**Problem:** In 2020, Dev earned $30,000 per year. By 2025, his salary is $31,500. Over the same period, prices rose by 6%. Can Dev buy more, less, or the same amount of goods in 2025 compared to 2020?

**Solution:**
1. Nominal raise: $31,500 ÷ $30,000 = 1.05 → a **5% raise**.
2. Inflation: **6%**.
3. Compare: 5% − 6% = **−1%**, so Dev's buying power fell by about 1% (exactly: 1.05 ÷ 1.06 − 1 ≈ −0.94%).

Check with baskets: a $100 basket in 2020 → $30,000 buys 300 baskets. In 2025 the basket costs $106 → $31,500 ÷ $106 ≈ 297 baskets. Slightly fewer.

**Answer: Dev can buy slightly *less* — his 5% raise was smaller than the 6% rise in prices.**
