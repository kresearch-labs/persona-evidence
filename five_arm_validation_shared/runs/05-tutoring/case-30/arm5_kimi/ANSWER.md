# Case 30 — Answer

**Correct answer: The same amount.** Maria's 2025 salary buys exactly as much as her 2020 salary, so she is neither better nor worse off.

## 1. The misconception

The student is confusing **nominal** income (the number of dollars) with **real** income (what those dollars can actually buy). She compared only the two salary figures and ignored that prices rose over the same period. "More dollars" does not automatically mean "more buying power" — you have to adjust for inflation first.

## 2. The correct concept

To compare buying power across years, convert the 2025 salary into 2020 dollars by dividing out the price increase:

- Nominal salary growth: $44,000 / $40,000 = 1.10 → pay rose 10%
- Price growth: prices rose 10%, so the 2025 price level is 1.10 times the 2020 level
- Real salary in 2020 dollars: $44,000 / 1.10 = **$40,000**

Her raise exactly matched inflation, so her real income is unchanged — she can buy the same amount of goods, not 10% more.

**Rule of thumb for similar problems:** real growth ≈ nominal growth − inflation (here, 10% − 10% = 0%). Only when your pay grows *faster* than prices can you actually buy more.

*Justification: since both her salary and the price level rose by exactly 10%, her purchasing power ($44,000 ÷ 1.10 = $40,000) is identical in both years.*
