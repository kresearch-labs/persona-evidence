# Case 23 — Answer

## 1. The misconception

The student has the two words exactly backwards: they believe **effect** is the action word (verb) and **affect** is the naming word (noun), and on top of that they believe the two spellings are "basically interchangeable." In standard usage it is the other way around — **affect** is the verb (to act on or influence something) and **effect** is the noun (the result or outcome). Because of this flipped rule, the student put "affect" in the noun slot ("a bad affect") and "effected" in the verb slot ("it really effected"), getting both blanks wrong. The correct sentence is: "The cold weather had a bad **effect** on my health, and it really **affected** how I felt all week."

## 2. Explaining the correct concept

The two words are not interchangeable — they do different jobs in a sentence, and you can tell which one you need by asking what the word is *doing*:

- **Affect = Action (verb).** It means "to influence or change something." If the word is something someone or something *does*, use **affect**: "The cold weather **affected** how I felt." Memory trick: **A**ffect = **A**ction.
- **Effect = End result (noun).** It names the thing that results. If you can put "a," "an," or "the" in front of it, use **effect**: "a bad **effect**," "the **effect** of the storm." Memory trick: **E**ffect = **E**nd result.

Quick test for any blank: try putting "the" before the word. If it makes sense ("the ___"), you need the noun — **effect**. If the blank needs an action (something is doing something to something else), you need the verb — **affect**.

Applying it back to the original sentence: "a bad ___" follows "a," so it needs the noun → **effect**. "It really ___ how I felt" needs an action the weather performed → **affected**.

(For completeness: there are rare exceptions — "effect" as a verb meaning "to bring about," as in "effect change," and "affect" as a noun in psychology meaning an observed emotion — but for everyday writing, affect = verb and effect = noun is the rule to rely on.)
