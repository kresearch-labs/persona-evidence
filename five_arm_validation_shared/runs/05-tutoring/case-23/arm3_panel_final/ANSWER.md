**1. The misconception behind the answer**

The student has swapped the two words' grammatical jobs: they believe "effect" is the action (verb) word and "affect" is the naming (noun) word — the reverse of the truth. Their own reasoning shows it: they call "effect" the action word "because an effect is what something does." With the jobs reversed, they naturally put "affect" in the noun slot ("a bad affect") and "effected" in the verb slot. Underneath sits a second belief that makes the error stick: that the two spellings are "basically interchangeable," so choosing between them is cosmetic and doesn't change what the sentence says.

**2. Explaining the correct concept to the student**

"You're actually halfway there — you already worked out that one of these words is a doing word and one is a naming word. You just attached them to the wrong spellings. Here's the fix:

- **Affect = Action.** Both start with **A**. *Affect* is the doing word — it's what something *does* to you: *The cold weather affects my health.*
- **Effect = End result.** Both start with **E**. *Effect* is the thing you're left with — the result: *The cold weather had a bad effect.*

Two quick tests for any blank:
1. Can you put 'a' or 'the' in front of it ('a bad ___')? Then the blank needs a naming word → **effect**.
2. Is the blank where a doing word goes (after 'it really,' 'it can,' 'it will')? Then it needs an action word → **affect**.

So your sentence reads: 'The cold weather had a bad **effect** on my health, and it really **affected** how I felt all week.'

And they're not interchangeable — swapping them changes the meaning. 'The new law effected change' means the law *created* change; 'the new law affected change' would mean it *altered* change that already existed. (Tiny footnote: 'effect' can occasionally act as a verb meaning 'to bring about,' but for school writing the A-for-Action, E-for-End-result rule gets every question right.)

Check yourself: 'The loud noise had an ___ on my concentration, and it ___ my test score.' (Answers: effect, affected.)"
