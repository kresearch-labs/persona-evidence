1. **The misconception:** The student is choosing between "affect" and "effect" by how the word *sounds* and believes the two spellings are basically interchangeable — one word, two spellings, pick whichever "feels" right. Behind that is a reversed picture of the two words: they think "effect" is the action word, when in fact **affect** is normally the verb (the action of changing or influencing something) and **effect** is normally the noun (the result — the thing that happens). The choice isn't decided by sound at all; it's decided by the *job* the word does in the sentence.

2. **Explaining it to the student:**

    These are two different words, not two spellings of one word — and you can always tell them apart by asking what job the word needs to do in the sentence.

    - **Affect** is the *doing* word (verb). It means *to change or influence something*. If you could swap in "changed" or "influenced," you want **affect**: "The cold weather **affected** (= changed) how I felt all week."
    - **Effect** is the *thing* word (noun). It means *the result* — what happens because of something. If a word like "the," "a," "an," or "bad" sits right before the blank, the sentence needs a noun, so you want **effect**: "It had a bad **effect** on my health."

    Memory trick: **A** is for **A**ction → **affect** = verb. **E** is for **E**nd result → **effect** = noun. (Or remember RAVEN: **R**emember — **A**ffect **V**erb, **E**ffect **N**oun.)

    Quick test you can use on any similar problem: try putting "the" or "an" in front of the blank. If that fits ("a bad ___"), the blank is a noun → **effect**. If the blank is something the subject *does* to something else, it's a verb → **affect**.

    So your sentence should read: "The cold weather had a bad **effect** on my health, and it really **affected** how I felt all week."

    (One small footnote for later: writers occasionally use "effect" as a verb meaning "to bring about" — "to effect change" — but that's rare; for everyday sentences, stick with affect = verb, effect = noun.)
