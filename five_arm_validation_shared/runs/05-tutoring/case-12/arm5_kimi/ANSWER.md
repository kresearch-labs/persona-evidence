# Case 12 — Answer

## 1. The misconception

The student believes that Newton's third-law action–reaction forces cancel each other out, as if they were two forces acting on the same object ("equal and opposite forces add to zero"). This confuses a third-law pair — which always acts on **two different bodies** — with two opposing forces acting on a **single** body, which is the only situation where forces can cancel.

## 2. The correct concept

Newton's third law says the truck pushes the car forward and the car pushes the truck backward with an equal, opposite force. But these two forces act on **different objects**, so they never appear in the same free-body diagram and can never cancel:

- **The car** feels the truck's forward push (plus friction from the road). Whether the car accelerates depends only on the **net force on the car**: push minus friction. If the push exceeds friction, the car accelerates forward (Newton's second law, F_net = ma).
- **The truck** feels the car's backward push, but it also gets a forward push from the road (static friction from its driven wheels). If the road's forward force on the truck exceeds the car's backward push, the truck accelerates too.

Forces only cancel when they act on the **same** object. The reaction force the car exerts on the truck belongs to the truck's free-body diagram, not the car's, so it cannot stop the car from moving.

**Quick check for a similar problem:** to find whether any object accelerates, draw only the forces acting **on that object**, sum them, and apply F_net = ma. Third-law partner forces always live on the other object's diagram.
