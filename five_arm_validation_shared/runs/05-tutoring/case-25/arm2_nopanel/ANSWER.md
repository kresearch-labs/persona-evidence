# Case 25 — Active vs. Passive Voice

## 1. The misconception

The student thinks **passive voice means past tense** — that any verb in the past ("blocked," "fixed") makes a sentence passive. That's why they marked (b) active and (c) passive: they sorted the sentences by *when* they happened, not by *who does the action*.

Tense and voice are two different features of a verb:

- **Tense** tells *when* something happens (past, present, future).
- **Voice** tells whether the subject **does** the action (active) or **receives** it (passive).

Every tense has both voices, so "it happened in the past" can never identify passive voice on its own.

## 2. Explaining it to the student

**Active voice: the subject does the action.**
*The goalie blocked the shot.* → The goalie did something.

**Passive voice: the subject receives the action — something is done *to* it.**
*The shot was blocked by the goalie.* → The shot didn't do anything; it got blocked.

### Two quick tests

**Test 1 — the "by ___" test.** A passive sentence usually names the doer after "by": *was blocked **by the goalie***. If the subject is just sitting there having something done to it, it's passive.

**Test 2 — the "be + verb" test.** Passive = a form of **be** (is, am, are, was, were, been, being) + a past-participle verb:
- *was blocked* → passive
- *blocked* (alone, with a doer as subject) → active

### Past tense is NOT passive

Both voices come in every tense:

| | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Past | The plumber **fixed** the pipe. | The pipe **was fixed** by the plumber. |
| Present | The plumber **fixes** the pipe. | The pipe **is fixed** by the plumber. |

Notice: the passive versions add "was/is" + the participle, and the pipe becomes the subject. The past tense alone never changes the voice.

### Try it

- *The dog chased the ball.* → **Active** (dog acts)
- *The ball was chased by the dog.* → **Passive** (ball gets chased)
- *Yesterday, the plumber fixed the leaking pipe.* → **Active** — past tense, yes, but the plumber *does* the fixing, so it's active.

**Corrected answers: (a) active; (b) passive; (c) active.**
