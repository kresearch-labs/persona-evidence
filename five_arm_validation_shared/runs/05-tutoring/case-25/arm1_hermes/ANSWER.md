# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 05-tutoring case-25 (active vs. passive voice)

## 1. The misconception

This student is confusing **tense** with **voice**. Their rule is: *"past-tense verb = passive voice."* That's why they called (b) and (c) passive — "blocked" and "fixed" are past-tense verbs.

But tense and voice are two completely different things:
- **Tense** tells *when* the action happens (past, present, future).
- **Voice** tells *whether the subject does the action or receives it*.

Because the vast majority of sentences are written in the past tense, this rule would label almost every sentence "passive" — including simple active ones like "She kicked the ball." Notice the student's own reasoning breaks down, too: they said both (b) and (c) "happened in the past, so they're passive," yet still labeled (b) active. When your rule contradicts itself, it's a sign the rule is wrong.

## 2. Explaining it to the student

Forget about *when* the action happened — that's tense, and it's a distraction here. Voice only asks one question:

**Is the subject DOING the action, or is the action being done TO the subject?**

- **Active voice:** the subject performs the action. → *The dog buried the bone.* (The dog did the burying.)
- **Passive voice:** the subject sits there and has the action done to it. → *The bone was buried by the dog.* (The bone didn't bury anything!)

Here's the key point: **both of those sentences are in the past tense**, but one is active and one is passive. So past tense can never tell you the voice. You have to look at who's doing what.

A reliable two-step test:
1. **Find the subject and ask: is it doing the action?** If yes → active.
2. **Look for the passive signal: a "be" verb (is, are, was, were) + a past participle**, often followed by "by ___" naming the real doer (like *was blocked by the goalie*). That combination means passive.

Applying the test to your sentences:
- **(a) The goalie blocked the shot.** Subject = goalie. Is the goalie doing the blocking? Yes → **ACTIVE** (even though "blocked" is past tense!).
- **(b) The shot was blocked by the goalie.** Subject = shot. Did the shot block anything? No — it *got* blocked. Plus we see "was blocked" and "by the goalie" → **PASSIVE**.
- **(c) The plumber fixed the leaking pipe.** Subject = plumber. Is the plumber doing the fixing? Yes → **ACTIVE**. "Yesterday" only tells us *when* — it says nothing about voice.

## Quick check for the student (same idea, new problem)

Identify active or passive voice:
- (a) The students finished the project.
- (b) The project was finished by the students.
- (c) Last night, a raccoon tipped over our trash can.

**Solution:**
- (a) **Active** — the students did the finishing. Past tense, but the subject acts.
- (b) **Passive** — "was finished" is a "be" verb + participle, the project received the action, and the real doer (the students) shows up after "by."
- (c) **Active** — the raccoon did the tipping. "Last night" is just telling us when; it doesn't make the sentence passive.
