# Case 25 — Answer

## 1. The misconception

The student thinks **passive voice means past tense** — that "if the verb happened in the past, the sentence is passive." Their own reasoning says it outright: "(b) and (c) both happened in the past, so they're passive... passive voice is when the verb is in the past."

They have mixed up two different features of a verb:

- **Tense** tells *when* the action happens (past, present, future).
- **Voice** tells *whether the subject is doing the action or receiving it* (active, passive).

Because (b) and (c) are the only sentences with past-tense verbs ("blocked," "fixed"), the student sorted by time instead of by who's doing what. That's why they also missed that (b) — the one sentence that really is passive — got labeled active: they were never looking at the doer of the action at all.

## 2. Explaining the correct concept

**Voice is about who does the action — not when it happens.**

- **Active voice:** the subject *does* the action.
  - *The goalie blocked the shot.* → The goalie did the blocking. Active.
- **Passive voice:** the subject *has the action done to it*. The doer gets pushed to the end after "by," or disappears entirely.
  - *The shot was blocked by the goalie.* → The shot didn't do anything; the blocking was done *to* it. Passive.

Here's the key idea your answer missed: **every tense has both active and passive forms.** Past tense doesn't make a sentence passive — these are all past tense, but only one is passive:

| Past tense | Voice |
|---|---|
| The goalie blocked the shot. | active |
| The shot was blocked by the goalie. | passive |
| The plumber fixed the pipe. | active |
| The pipe was fixed by the plumber. | passive |

**Two quick tests you can use on any sentence:**

1. **Ask: is the subject doing the action, or is it being done to the subject?**
   - (c) *Yesterday, the plumber fixed the leaking pipe.* → Who fixed? The plumber — and the plumber is the subject. The subject is doing the action, so it's **active**. "Yesterday" only says *when*; it has nothing to do with voice.
2. **The "by ..." test:** if you can add "by [someone/something]" after the verb and the sentence still makes sense, it's passive.
   - *The shot was blocked **by the goalie*** ✓ → passive.
   - *The goalie blocked the shot by the goalie* ✗ (nonsense) → active.

**Try it yourself:** *The window was broken by the ball.* Is the window doing the breaking, or is the breaking done to it? (It's passive — the window just sat there and got broken.) And *Last night, Maria painted the fence*? Maria did the painting, so it's active — even though it's past tense.
