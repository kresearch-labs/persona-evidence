# Case 25 — Answer

## 1. The misconception

The student confuses **verb tense** with **grammatical voice**. They believe "passive voice" means the verb is in the past tense ("'blocked' and 'fixed' are past-tense verbs, and passive voice is when the verb is in the past"). In reality, tense (when something happened) and voice (who does the action vs. who receives it) are independent: a sentence in any tense can be active or passive. This misconception also led them to misclassify (b) — ironically, they called the one genuinely passive sentence "active," suggesting they may also be pattern-matching on the presence of a named doer ("the goalie" appears in the sentence) rather than on sentence structure.

## 2. The correct concept, explained for the student

Voice is about **who is doing the action and where they sit in the sentence** — not about when it happened.

- **Active voice:** the subject *does* the action. Pattern: **doer → verb → receiver.** "The goalie blocked the shot." The goalie (doer) is the subject.
- **Passive voice:** the subject *receives* the action; the doer is pushed into a "by..." phrase or left out entirely. Pattern: **receiver → form of "to be" + past participle → (by + doer).** "The shot was blocked by the goalie." The shot (receiver) is the subject.

Two quick tests:
1. **Find the verb and ask "who did it?"** If that doer is the subject of the sentence, it's active. If the doer shows up after "by" (or nowhere), it's passive.
2. **Look for a "to be" verb + past participle** (was blocked, is fixed, were eaten). That combo is the fingerprint of the passive. Note "was blocked" contains *was* — past tense — but the past tense is not what makes it passive; "The shot is blocked by the goalie" is present tense and still passive.

Applying this:
- (a) "The goalie blocked the shot." — Goalie (doer) is the subject → **active**.
- (b) "The shot was blocked by the goalie." — Shot (receiver) is the subject; doer is in a "by" phrase; "was blocked" = to be + participle → **passive**.
- (c) "Yesterday, the plumber fixed the leaking pipe." — Plumber (doer) is the subject → **active**. The word "yesterday" and the past-tense "fixed" only tell you *when*, not the voice.

Correct answers: **(a) active; (b) passive; (c) active.**
