# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 05-tutoring case-18 (history — causes of the American Revolution)

## 1. The misconception

The student is falling for the **"crushing taxes" myth**: the belief that the American Revolution happened because British taxes on the colonists were *objectively enormous* — that colonists simply couldn't afford to pay them anymore.

Two things went wrong here:

1. **The student ignored the evidence in the question.** The question hands them the key numbers: colonists paid about **1 shilling per year**, while people in Britain paid about **26 shillings**. That means colonists paid roughly *one-twenty-sixth* of what Britons paid — they were among the *lightest*-taxed people in the entire European world. An answer saying taxes were "extremely high" and "crushing" directly contradicts the data the question provides.
2. **The student assumed revolutions come from economic pain.** Their reasoning ("revolutions happen when taxes get too expensive") replaces the actual historical cause with a generic story. The real grievance wasn't the *size* of the taxes — it was the *right* of Parliament to impose them at all without colonial consent.

## 2. Explaining it to the student

Here's the trick to this kind of question: **when a question gives you numbers, build your answer around them.** If your explanation contradicts the numbers, you need a different explanation — not a louder version of the same one.

So look at the numbers again: 1 shilling versus 26 shillings. Colonists' taxes were tiny. That tells us the rebellion *can't* be about the amount of money. So what was it about?

**It was about who was doing the taxing — and whether colonists had any say.**

- For decades, Britain mostly left the colonies alone. Colonists elected their own assemblies, and *those assemblies* voted on colonial taxes. Colonists were used to taxing themselves.
- After the expensive Seven Years' War (ended 1763), Parliament changed course and began taxing the colonists directly: the Sugar Act, the Stamp Act, the Townshend Acts, and finally the Tea Act.
- Here's the problem: **not a single colonist was elected to Parliament.** A government 3,000 miles away, in which they had zero representatives, was now reaching into their pockets. Hence the famous slogan: **"No taxation without representation."**
- Colonists also worried about the *precedent*: if Parliament could take 1 shilling without asking, what would stop it from taking 26 later? Accepting a small tax meant accepting Parliament's *authority* to tax them at all.

Try this analogy: imagine a stranger starts charging you a $1 fee every time you walk into your own backyard. One dollar is nothing — you wouldn't miss it. But you'd still be furious, because *he has no right to charge you anything*, and if you pay once, next month it might be $20. The colonists' complaint was exactly that: not the price tag, but the *right to set one*.

So a strong answer sounds like: *"Colonists actually paid very low taxes — far lower than people in Britain. They rebelled not because of the amount, but because Parliament imposed these taxes without their consent. Colonists had no representatives in Parliament, so the taxes violated the principle that governments may only tax people with their approval."*

## Quick check for the student (same idea, new problem)

**Problem:** In 1773, Parliament passed the Tea Act. It actually made tea *cheaper* for colonists than ever before — Parliament cut the price of British East India Company tea, leaving only a small 3-penny tax on it. Yet that December, colonists dumped hundreds of chests of tea into Boston Harbor. A student writes: *"The Boston Tea Party happened because tea had become too expensive for colonists to buy."* Use the information given to explain what's wrong with this answer, and give a better explanation.

**Solution:** The information given contradicts the student's claim — the Tea Act made tea *cheaper*, not more expensive. So price can't be the cause of the protest. The real issue was the same principle as before: the cheap tea still carried a 3-penny tax from Parliament, and buying it would mean *accepting Parliament's right to tax the colonists without their consent*. Colonists boycotted and dumped the tea to reject that principle — showing, once again, that the colonists' objection was about **the right to tax them, not the size of the tax**.
