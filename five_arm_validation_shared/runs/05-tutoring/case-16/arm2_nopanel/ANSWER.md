# Case 16 — Answer

## 1. The misconception behind the answer

The student believes **acceleration means only "speeding up"** — that acceleration is the same thing as speed, or a change in speed alone. This is the classic "acceleration = going faster" misconception. It comes from treating velocity as just a number (the speedometer reading) and forgetting that velocity is a **vector**: it has both a size (speed) *and* a direction. Because the student only checks whether the number 60 km/h is changing, they miss that the car's *direction* of motion is changing every instant — and a change in velocity in *any* form is acceleration.

## 2. Explaining the correct concept

**Velocity is not just how fast — it's how fast AND which way.**

- Velocity = speed + direction. Your speedometer shows 60 km/h, but your velocity is really "60 km/h pointing north," then a moment later "60 km/h pointing a bit west of north," and so on.
- **Acceleration means your velocity is changing.** That can happen three ways: speeding up, slowing down, **or changing direction**. If *any* part of your velocity changes, you are accelerating.
- On the circular track, the speed stays at 60 km/h, but the direction you're traveling is turning constantly as you follow the curve. So your velocity *is* changing — the car **is accelerating**, even though the speedometer never moves.

**Which way does this acceleration point?** Toward the **center of the circle**. It's called *centripetal* acceleration ("center-seeking"). On this track it's produced by **friction between the tires and the road**, which is exactly the force the question says holds the car on the circle. (No force pushing outward on the car — the friction pushes inward, and that inward pull is what keeps bending the velocity around the curve.)

**How big is it?** For circular motion at speed *v* on a circle of radius *r*:

a = v² / r

Example: at 60 km/h (≈ 16.7 m/s) on a track of radius 50 m, a = (16.7)² / 50 ≈ **5.6 m/s²** — a real acceleration, pointing at the center, even at constant speed.

**A rule you can use on any similar problem:** don't ask "is the speed changing?" — ask "**is the velocity changing (speed OR direction)?**" If the object is turning, its direction is changing, so it is accelerating. Constant speed around a curve = accelerating. Only motion in a straight line at constant speed has zero acceleration.
