## 1. The misconception

The student has overgeneralized the rule "apostrophe + s = belonging" into "any apostrophe = belonging." They learned one true fact — *Maria's book* shows possession — and concluded that **you're** must be the possessive form, because it's the one with the apostrophe. Underneath that, they don't yet know that **you're** is a *contraction* (a short way to write **you are**), and that the possessive word **your** has no apostrophe at all. So they picked the word by "does it have an apostrophe?" instead of by "what does it mean in the sentence?"

## 2. Explaining it to the student

"Apostrophes actually do **two different jobs**, and you've only met one of them so far.

**Job 1: showing that something belongs to someone.** You already know this one — *Maria's book* means the book belongs to Maria. The word that shows belonging for *you* is **your**, and here's the surprising part: it has **no apostrophe at all**. It's already a whole, complete word — *your book*, *your phone*, *your backpack*. Nothing is missing from it, so nothing needs an apostrophe.

**Job 2: gluing two words together and marking the missing letters.** **You're** is a squeeze of two words: **you are**. The apostrophe is a little placeholder standing where letters got removed — yo**u** **a**re → you're. You already use words like this: *can't* (can not), *it's* (it is), *I'm* (I am).

**Here's your detective test** for every your/you're blank: read the sentence with **you are** in place of the blank. If it sounds right, write **you're**. If it sounds wrong, write **your**.

Try it on your sentence: 'You are going to love you are new phone.' The first one sounds perfect — so **you're** is right there. The second one sounds silly — nobody says 'love you are new phone' — so that blank needs **your**.

Your sentence should read: **'You're going to love your new phone.'**

Now you try one: 'Is that ___ jacket?' Say 'you are jacket'... that sounds wrong, so the answer is **your**. And: '___ my best friend.' Say 'you are my best friend' — that works, so it's **you're**. Once you run the *you are* test, you'll never mix them up again."
