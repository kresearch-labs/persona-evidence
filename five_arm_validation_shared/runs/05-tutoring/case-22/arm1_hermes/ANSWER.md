# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 05-tutoring case-22 (your vs. you're — what apostrophes really do)

## 1. The misconception

The student believes **every apostrophe signals possession**. They learned the rule "'s can show something belongs to someone" (as in *Maria's book*) and overgeneralized it: since *you're* has an apostrophe, they concluded it must be the possessive word that goes before a noun.

The gap here is that the student only knows **one of the apostrophe's two jobs**. Apostrophes are used for:
1. **Possession** — showing something belongs to someone (*Maria's book*, *the dog's leash*)
2. **Contractions** — squashing two words together, where the apostrophe stands in for missing letters (*do not → don't*, *you are → you're*)

Because the student doesn't know about job #2, they picked *you're* for both blanks. In reality, it's the opposite of what they thought: **your** (no apostrophe!) is the possessive, and **you're** is the contraction of *you are*.

## 2. Explaining it to the student

Great thinking trying to use the apostrophe rule — but apostrophes actually have **two** different jobs, and you've seen one of them so far!

**Job 1: Possession.** *Maria's book* means the book belongs to Maria. That's the rule you already know.

**Job 2: Contractions.** An apostrophe can also act like a **shrink ray for two words**. It squeezes two words into one and takes the place of the letters that got removed:
- *do not* → *don't* (the "o" from "not" disappears)
- *I am* → *I'm* (the "a" from "am" disappears)
- *you are* → *you're* (the "a" from "are" disappears!)

So **you're** is just a shortcut for **you are**. And the word that means "belonging to you" is **your** — which has no apostrophe at all, just like *his*, *her*, *my*, and *our*.

Here's a magic trick for solving these every time: **replace the word with "you are."**
- If the sentence still makes sense → use **you're**.
- If it sounds weird → use **your**.

Let's try it on your sentence: *"___ going to love ___ new phone."*

- First blank: "You are going to love..." ✓ Sounds right! Use **you're**.
- Second blank: "...love you are new phone." ✗ Weird! Use **your**.

Answer: "**You're** going to love **your** new phone."

## Quick check for the student (same idea, new problem)

Fill in the blanks with *your* or *you're*: "Is that ___ jacket? I think ___ forgetting it!"

**Solution:** "Is that **your** jacket? I think **you're** forgetting it!"
- First blank: "Is that you are jacket?" — weird, so use **your** (it belongs to you).
- Second blank: "I think you are forgetting it!" — perfect, so use **you're** (short for *you are*).

Remember: apostrophe + "re" = *you are*. No apostrophe = it belongs to you!
