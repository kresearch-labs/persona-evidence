# Case 24 — Answer

## 1. The misconception

The student has collapsed the fewer/less rule into "less = a smaller amount," and is choosing the word by the **size** of the things ("mistakes are small things"), not by the **type** of noun. Their own stated rule — "'less' works in front of any noun" — is the giveaway: they believe any noun can take *less*. The actual rule depends on whether the noun is **countable** (things you count one by one: *mistakes, cookies, chairs* → *fewer*) or an **uncountable amount** (one mass you measure: *water, sugar, time* → *less*). Size is irrelevant — elephants are huge and we still say *fewer elephants*; sand grains are tiny and we still say *less sand*.

## 2. Explaining it to the student

"You told me your rule yourself: 'less just means a smaller amount, so it works in front of any noun.' Nice — now let's test that rule, because it fails on one little question: **can I count it?**

- If you can put a number in front and count — *one mistake, two mistakes, three mistakes* — it's a **countable** noun, and we use **fewer**: *fewer mistakes, fewer cookies, fewer chairs.*
- If it's one big amount you **measure** instead of count — you can't say *one water, two waters* — it's an **uncountable** noun, and we use **less**: *less water, less sugar, less time.*

Your sentence already contains the clue: *mistakes* has a plural — *mistake → mistakes* — and only countable things get plurals like that. So: 'I made **fewer** mistakes.'

And notice size never matters: huge elephants still take *fewer*, tiny grains of sand still take *less*. It's about counting, not size.

Now the second blank — the tricky one, because minutes ARE countable (*one minute, two minutes*…). So why *less than twenty minutes*? Because there you're not counting the minutes one by one; you're treating the whole time as **one single amount** — one stretch, like one puddle. Whenever a number + unit of time, money, or distance acts as one single amount, we use **less than**: *less than twenty minutes, less than ten dollars, less than three miles.*

Your new two-step test:
1. Am I counting separate items? → **fewer** (*fewer mistakes*).
2. Is it one single amount I'm measuring — including '___ than [number]' for time, money, or distance? → **less** (*less than twenty minutes*).

Quick check before you try one alone — which of these can you count one by one: *milk, chairs, homework*? (Chairs — so *fewer chairs*; milk and homework are amounts — *less milk, less homework*.) Now you: 'There are ___ cookies in the jar now' → countable → **fewer**. 'The recipe needs ___ milk than before' → one measured amount → **less**."
