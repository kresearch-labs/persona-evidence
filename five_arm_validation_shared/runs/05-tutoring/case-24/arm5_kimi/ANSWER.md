# Case 24 — Answer

## 1. The misconception

The student believes "less" is an all-purpose word meaning "a smaller amount" that can go in front of any noun. They don't know that English splits this job between two words based on the type of noun: **fewer** for things you can count one by one (countable nouns) and **less** for things measured as a single mass or amount (uncountable nouns). Because "mistakes" are countable, "less mistakes" is wrong — it should be "fewer mistakes."

## 2. Explaining the correct concept

Here's the rule: before you pick "fewer" or "less," ask one question about the noun — **can I count it?**

- If you can put a number in front of it and count the items one by one (one mistake, two mistakes, three mistakes), use **fewer**: fewer mistakes, fewer pencils, fewer friends.
- If it's one undivided amount or mass that you measure rather than count (water, homework, money, time), use **less**: less water, less homework, less noise.

Quick test: try saying "three ___." "Three mistakes" works, so mistakes are countable → **fewer mistakes**. "Three water" doesn't work, so → less water.

Applying it to the original sentence: "I made **fewer** mistakes on my essay this time" (mistakes are countable), "and it took me **less** than twenty minutes to finish" (here the twenty minutes are treated as one single amount of time — a duration — not as twenty separate items, so "less" is correct).

Practice check: "___ people came to the game, so there was ___ noise." People can be counted → **fewer**; noise is a mass → **less**.
