# Case 14 — Answer

## 1. The misconception behind the answer

The student thinks voltage and current are **the same quantity measured in different units** — that "volts" and "amps" are two names for "how much electricity flows." In their words: "they're basically two ways of saying the same thing."

This is the classic **voltage–current conflation**. It shows up twice in the answer:

- "The outlet pushes 230 volts of current" — treating volts as if they were a *flow* that gets pushed.
- "2 amps is how much voltage the lamp uses" — swapping the labels entirely.

The underlying gap: the student has no model of electricity as **charge being pushed (voltage) versus charge flowing (current)** — two different things that happen at the same time in a circuit.

## 2. Explaining the correct concept to the student

**Think of electricity like water in pipes.**

- **Voltage (V) is the push.** It's the electrical "pressure" that makes charge move. A 230 V outlet pushes harder than a 1.5 V battery, just like a tall water tower pushes water harder than a small tank on the roof. Voltage is measured *across* two points (before and after the lamp) — it doesn't flow anywhere itself.
- **Current (A) is the flow.** It's *how much* charge passes a point in the circuit each second. 2 A means 2 coulombs of charge (a huge number of electrons) flow through the lamp every second. Current is measured *through* a point in the wire.

So in the lamp circuit, the two numbers describe two different things happening at once:

- **230 V** = how hard the outlet pushes charge through the lamp (the pressure across it).
- **2 A** = how much charge actually flows through the lamp each second (the flow rate).

The push and the flow are connected but not identical: for the same push, a lamp with low resistance lets a big flow through, and one with high resistance lets only a small flow through (I = V/R). That's why a 230 V outlet is dangerous but a 230 V static spark is not — same push, very different flow.

**Quick self-check you can use on a similar problem:** if a phone charger says "5 V, 2 A," ask yourself — which number is the push, and which is the flow? (5 V is the push from the charger; 2 A is the flow of charge into the phone.) If you can label each number as *push* or *flow*, you've got it — and you'll never again say "volts of current," because volts are the push and amps are the flow.
