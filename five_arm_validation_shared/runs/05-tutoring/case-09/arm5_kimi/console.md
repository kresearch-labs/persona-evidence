**Summary of the answer:**

1. **Misconception:** The student thinks seasons are caused by Earth's *distance* from the Sun (closer = summer). This is wrong — Earth's orbit is nearly circular, Earth is actually near its *farthest* point in early July, and if distance caused seasons, both hemispheres would have summer at the same time (they don't — it's winter in Australia during a U.S. summer).

2. **Correct concept, at a grades 3–6 level:** Seasons are caused by the **tilt of Earth's axis (~23.5°)**. In July the Northern Hemisphere tilts *toward* the Sun, giving (a) more direct, concentrated sunlight — explained with a flashlight-on-a-table analogy — and (b) longer days, both of which mean more heating. The answer closes with a transferable rule: for any seasons question, ask which way the hemisphere is tilted — toward the Sun = summer, away = winter; distance is never the answer.
