# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-24 (Twitter, as of mid-April 2022)

**Recommendation: HOLD (event-driven; do not initiate at these levels; treat the position as an option on the saga's outcome, not an investment).**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The stock is now a probability-weighted merger instrument — trade it as one.** At ~$45 versus Musk's $54.20 all-cash offer, the ~17% spread represents the market's doubt about consummation: Musk's unpredictability, financing questions, a potential hostile route, or a walk-away (with his 9% stake sold down). The binary outcomes are: deal closes at $54.20 (+~20% from $45), negotiations lift the price (a competing bid or board leverage could push higher), or Musk walks and the stock reverts toward its fundamentals-driven level (mid-$30s, where it traded much of the prior year). Expected value at plausible probabilities (say 60/40 close/no-close) sits near $50 — modestly above $45, which is why the stock held up. But the *variance* is enormous.

2. **The fundamentals provide the floor and they are unexciting.** Revenue growth has lagged social peers for years; the stock drifted mid-$30s–$50s on its own merits. A deal break returns the shares to that range: the fundamentals do not support the $45+ price without the deal. This asymmetry — capped downside near ~$36-38, capped upside at $54.20 plus any competitive-bid premium — defines the trade: limited loss if the deal dies, defined gain if it closes.

3. **The market's skeptical 2% drop on the offer is the information.** A $54.20 all-cash premium that *fails* to lift the stock means sophisticated holders discount the buyer, not the price. Musk's "best and final" and funding claims help the probability case; his history (abandoned attempts, impulsive reversals) hurts it. The board's fiduciary duty gives the deal a real path — a board rejecting a 25%+ cash premium over pre-stake price invites proxy fight — but Musk's "Plan B" ambiguity keeps the risk premium wide.

4. **The practical playbook on the date:** holders — sit; the offer (or the fight for it) supplies price support that didn't exist in March. No new money at $45: the risk/reward of buying the spread is decent but the tail risk (Musk sells his stake, walks, and the stock reverts to ~$36) is a real ~20% loss. Discipline rule: reassess on *funding disclosure* (the actual cash commitment, not claims) and on board response — those two events re-price the probabilities more than any product news.

**Bottom line:** hold, as an event-driven position. Do not underwrite Twitter's fundamentals at $45 — underwrite the deal's probability, and accept that a mercurial buyer makes even the arbitrage lively. The floor is the fundamentals (~$36); the ceiling is $54.20 or better; the probability knob is Musk's financing, and it has not been proven on this date.
