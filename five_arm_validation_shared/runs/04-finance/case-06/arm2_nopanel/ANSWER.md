**Recommendation: Buy** — for a patient, long-horizon investor, sized as a contrarian position rather than a core holding.

**Why:**

- **The price already assumes the bear case.** At $97.94, Meta's market cap is roughly $250B. Even in this trough year the company should earn on the order of $23B in net income and generate roughly $15–18B of free cash flow, against ~$40B of cash and marketable securities and ~$26B of debt. That is about 10x earnings on both trailing and forward estimates and a mid-teens free-cash-flow multiple — pricing a structurally dying business, not the world's second-largest digital ad platform in a cyclical ad recession.

- **The core engine is depressed, not destroyed.** Q3 revenue fell ~4% year over year, but ad impressions grew 18% and daily users across the apps reached ~3.71B (+4%). The damage is in price per ad (−18%): weak demand, Apple's ATT, and low-monetizing Reels inventory — cyclical and fixable, not user flight. Ad budgets are high-beta to the economy, and the same leverage cuts the other way in a recovery.

- **The metaverse spend is the real debate — and it is an expense, not a liability.** Reality Labs' ~$12B+/year losses and rising AI capex are the gap between ~$30B+ of pre-spend earnings power and ~$20B post-spend. Management has already signaled 2023 discipline (hiring freeze, roughly flat headcount). Even if Zuckerberg never trims it, the ~35%-margin core Family of Apps business funds it; any trim is pure upside.

- **What a buyer is paid to accept:** TikTok's structural share gains, a possibly weaker 2023 ad market (Q4 guidance of $30–32.5B implies continued contraction), and unaccountable metaverse spending under a controlling CEO. These justify a discount and modest position sizing — not a 60%-off price on a still-growing, net-cash franchise repurchasing roughly $7B of stock per quarter.

**One-line justification:** At ~10x earnings and mid-teens free cash flow with a net-cash balance sheet, the market is charging a permanent-decline price for a temporarily depressed, still-dominant ad franchise whose biggest problem — metaverse spending — is a discretionary expense the core business can comfortably fund: buy.
