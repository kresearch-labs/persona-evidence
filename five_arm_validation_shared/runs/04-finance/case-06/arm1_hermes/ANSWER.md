# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-06 (Meta Platforms, as of October 26, 2022)

**Recommendation: HOLD (with defined conditions; modest adds defensible for long-horizon capital — this is not a value trap, it is an option on cost discipline).**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The core business is wounded but cash-generating and profitable.** Yes: revenue $27.7B, down >4% YoY, second consecutive decline; net income down 52%; Q4 guided below consensus; ad market contraction, TikTok share gains, Apple iOS privacy degradation — all real and all visible in the 24.5% one-day drop to $97.94. But the company still earned $4.4B *in a bad quarter* and generates substantial operating cash flow. Contrast with the distress cases (Enron, WorldCom): there is no solvency question, no audit question, no liquidity wall. This is a profitability-and-allocation question, which is a fundamentally different (and better) risk category.

2. **The real issue is the metaverse spend — and the market has already priced a large part of the bad outcome.** Reality Labs lost $9.4B in nine months; tens of billions more are committed while the funding engine shrinks. That is a legitimate capital-allocation critique. But the down 61% YTD price (one-fifth of peak) now prices in a *lot* of skepticism. The bull math: if Reality Labs spend is disciplined or trimmed, and the core ad business stabilizes at even zero growth, Meta's core earnings alone justify a far higher stock than $97.94. The market is paying ~9-10x earnings for a dominant two-platform (FB+IG) advertising franchise at the bottom of a cyclical ad recession. That is historically cheap for this asset.

3. **The bear case — a value trap — requires Zuckerberg to spend without discipline *indefinitely*.** The single most important variable is not the ad cycle (cyclical, recovers) or TikTok (competitive pressure, manageable with Reels) or Apple's ATT (a real, permanent headwind but already in the numbers). It is whether the controlling shareholder keeps funding Reality Labs at $12-15B/year regardless of returns. There is no governance mechanism to force discipline — but there is a market mechanism: persistent spend with shrinking profits eventually pressures even a controlling founder, and the October selloff itself is that pressure registering.

4. **The asymmetric setup:** long-horizon capital is being paid to wait. Cyclical ad bottoms are recoverable (marketers return when economies stabilize); Apple's targeting changes are being engineered around; TikTok competition imposes discipline; and the AI investments, unlike the metaverse, have direct near-term ad-revenue returns. If *any* of — ad recovery, Reality Labs spend discipline, or Reels monetization — materializes, the stock re-rates hard. The downside is a continuing multiple compression; the upside is a re-rating on ~$40B+ of annual profit power.

**Bottom line:** hold existing positions; for aggressive long-horizon capital, modest adds at these levels are defensible with the explicit understanding that Zuckerberg controls the spending lever and the thesis is "core business funds itself while discipline eventually arrives." Not a value trap — a value trap is a structurally declining business priced as if it weren't; Meta is a cyclically depressed, still-dominant, hugely profitable franchise whose controlling shareholder has a spending hobby. Price the hobby, own the franchise.
