# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-21 (Hertz, as of May 2020 / post-filing)

**Recommendation: SELL / exit equity entirely. The equity is structurally junior, deeply out of the money, and — post-Chapter 11 — a vehicle for speculation, not investment.**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The equity is structurally junior to a distressed debt load while lenders negotiate the company's survival.** In Chapter 11, value flows to creditors first; common equity recovers only if enterprise value exceeds the entire debt stack. With net debt >10x equity, near-zero interest coverage, and lenders actively negotiating, the common shares are an option on an *upside-surprise* restructuring — a lottery instrument, not an ownership stake in a going concern.

2. **The pre-pandemic fundamentals say this business never earned its capital.** In every quarter of the previous five years, capex exceeded operating cash flow; 2.5% return on net operating assets vs. an industry median of 13.3%; GAAP losses or near-breakeven in most years. This is not a good business interrupted by COVID — it's a weak business whose COVID interruption became insolvency. Post-emergence, fleet economics and leverage would have to be rebuilt from scratch, with the old equity diluted toward irrelevance.

3. **The default probability math is explicit.** ~52% 12-month default probability, implied rating CC, ranked last among peers, S&P at B-. When the *quantified* credit measures put a company near a coin-flip on default within a year — and it has already filed — the equity question is answered by the capital structure, not the recovery story.

4. **The pandemic exposed a business-design flaw that survives any recovery.** Revenue concentrated at airports exactly as air travel collapsed; negative FCF of ~$3.96B in one quarter; job postings collapsing to zero. Even in a travel recovery, the old levered-fleet model (capex > operating cash flow, every quarter, five years running) has demonstrated it cannot self-fund. A restructuring that survives will belong to the creditors.

**The honest caveat everyone remembers from this episode:** the equity *did* later spike on retail speculation — a real price event, driven by option-market dynamics and social-media flows, not by recoverable value. Advice written on the date should not predict squeezes; it should describe value. On the date, the answer is: the equity is a call option with no fundamentals behind it. Clients who want to speculate may do so with money they can burn. Clients who own it as an investment should understand there is no investment left to own — the investment thesis died with the filing.

**Bottom line:** sell / exit. Junior equity in a just-filed, highly levered Chapter 11 with weak pre-pandemic economics, ~52% modeled default probability, and creditors in control of the outcome is a speculation ticket. Sizing rule: only burn-money, sized to zero-able.
