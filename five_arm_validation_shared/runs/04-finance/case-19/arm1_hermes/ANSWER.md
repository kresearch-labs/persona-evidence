# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-19 (Intel, as of July 24, 2020)

**Recommendation: HOLD existing positions; for value-oriented long-horizon accounts, selective accumulation on further weakness is defensible — this is an overdone reaction to a profitable, cash-generative franchise with a real but *time-bounded* problem.**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The 7nm delay is a genuine multi-year competitive handicap — the market's direction is right.** A six-month-plus slip pushing products to late 2022/2023 means AMD's TSMC-built 7nm silicon competes effectively for two to three more years, especially in servers where AMD has been taking share for two years. The delay caps the multiple and dims data-center share prospects. The "do we even stay an IDM?" conflict adds strategic uncertainty. The -17% repricing reflects real, knowable, multi-year headwinds.

2. **But the reaction priced Intel as broken, and the numbers say otherwise.** Q2 2020 revenue $19.7B (+20% YoY), a beat driven by genuine notebook/remote-work demand; the core businesses remain hugely profitable and cash-generative; the x86 installed base and enterprise relationships are measured in decades; and the stock, post-drop, trades at a multiple that pays the holder almost nothing for that earnings power. The market moved Intel from "priced for perfection" to "priced for decay" in one day.

3. **The strategic resolution has upside optionality.** The "pay a third-party foundry" contingency — unheard-of for Intel — is a *decision point*, not a doom: if Intel commits to TSMC for leading-edge products, it gets back into process competitiveness on TSMC's timeline (the same advantage AMD enjoys) while retaining its architecture, software ecosystem, and enterprise franchise. The IDM-vs-fabless debate resolves into either a foundry-partnership Intel (fine for shareholders) or a retained-IDM Intel with recovered execution — either way, the *asset* (design + ecosystem + installed base) remains one of the most valuable in semiconductors.

4. **The honest risk framing:** the next 2–3 years cap upside — AMD takes share, margins compress, and the stock may stay cheap ("value trap with a dividend"). The 17% day reflects that. But the bear case requires AMD's execution to be flawless *and* Intel's response (outsourcing, capital reallocation, leadership change) to fail. Intel has survived competitive eras before (AMD's Athlon era) with the same duopoly structure intact.

**Bottom line:** hold existing positions; add on further weakness only in long-horizon accounts sized for a 2–3 year wait. The one-day 17% repricing of a cash-rich dominant franchise over a *delay* (not a cancellation, not a fraud, not a demand break) is historically where value is made in semis — with the explicit understanding that this is a multi-year wait for the 7nm fix, and the checkpoint is Intel's foundry-strategy decision (the next 2–3 quarters' announcements).
