# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-09 (Carvana, as of November 29, 2022)

**Recommendation: HOLD for speculative accounts as a strictly-sized, explicitly-binary position; SELL/avoid for any client who cannot lose the entire position. This is not an investment; it is a distressed-asset wager with asymmetric payoff.**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The liquidity question dominates everything else — and it is genuinely binary.** Cash projected to run out by end-2023 absent new capital; ~$600M annual interest expense; Moody's outlook negative; no announced lifeline; the Garcia family's intentions unknown and their control (CEO + chairman) makes outside-raise outcomes unpredictable. Every other analysis — the business model, the brand, the retail footprint — is conditional on this one fact: does money arrive before it runs out?

2. **The upside case is real and the asymmetry is the honest argument for a small position.** Carvana's problem in late 2022 was *capital structure*, not customer demand for the model: used-car e-commerce with vertical integration and a recognized brand does not vanish because rates rose. If the company raises capital (secured debt against the inventory/vending infrastructure, a family injection, or asset sales) and cuts costs to profitability, the equity at $7.34 — down 96% — can be worth multiples. Distressed-debt investors were already circling this exact dynamic; the equity is the tail of that trade.

3. **The downside is total.** If no financing materializes, unsecured equity holders are behind $600M/year of debt service in a company burning cash — zero is a real outcome, not a rhetorical one. Moreover the raise, when it comes, will likely be dilutive and possibly at terms unfavorable to common shareholders; even "survival" may leave the equity meaningfully reduced.

4. **The high short interest cuts both ways and is *not* a reason to own.** Squeezes create violent bounces (the stock had already shown them) but they are trading phenomena, not investment value, and they amplify volatility in both directions — which for a strictly-sized position is tolerable and for an oversized one is ruinous.

**Position-sizing logic (the actual advice):** the correct client for this is one who (a) can lose 100% of the position without noticing, (b) understands that the payoff is a venture outcome, not a stock-market outcome, and (c) wants exposure to a possible capital-structure reset. For everyone else: sell/avoid — holding through a binary liquidity event with the family's intentions unknown is hope, not analysis.

**Bottom line:** hold strictly-sized speculative positions; the asymmetry (multiple-X upside vs. total loss) justifies a small wager, never a conviction position. The decision that matters will not be made in the market — it will be made in a bank negotiation, and the equity holders will simply learn the result.
