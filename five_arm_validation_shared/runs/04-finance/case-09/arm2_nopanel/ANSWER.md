Recommendation: Buy — but only as a small, strictly sized speculative position (sized so a total loss on the position is acceptable); it is not a core holding, and clients who cannot tolerate a 100% loss on it should hold or sell instead.

Why (as known on November 29, 2022): At $7.34, down 96% year to date and a market cap of only about $1.6 billion against roughly $13 billion in annual revenue, the equity is priced for near-certain distress — and the whole outcome hinges on one variable: whether new financing arrives before the cash runs out at the end of 2023. The odds favor a lifeline. The Garcia family controls the company and has nearly all of its wealth tied up in it, giving it both the motive and likely the means to inject capital or backstop debt; creditors have their own incentive to extend rather than force a liquidation of a going concern; and assets such as the recently acquired ADESA auction business could be sold if needed. If financing lands and management buys time to cut expenses, the stock is worth several times today's price; if nothing materializes, it is worth zero. That asymmetry — many-x upside versus -100% on a small position — is attractive in expectation, and the heavy short interest adds squeeze fuel to the upside scenario. The catch is timing and control: any rescue is unpredictable, will likely come with heavy dilution, and the family's interests may not align with minority holders — so the position must be small, and the thesis should be exited if no financing is announced well before the projected cash-out date.

One-line justification: At $7.34 the stock is a cheap call option on a rescue that the controlling Garcia family has every incentive to provide — buy it small, because the probability-weighted upside of several times the price justifies the real risk of total loss only in a position sized to be losable.
