As known on November 29, 2022, Carvana's equity was a binary bet, not an underwritable investment. Management's own math put cash exhaustion at the end of 2023 without new capital, the company was still burning cash while carrying roughly $600 million of annual interest expense, and Moody's had just revised the outlook to negative. No lifeline was announced, and the Garcia family's control meant any rescue — its size, terms and dilution — would come on the insiders' schedule, not one minority holders could predict or influence. The upside is real but unpriceable: the many-x recovery scenario depends entirely on financing whose probability and terms cannot be estimated from announced facts, while the downside is a total loss of the equity. High short interest amplifies volatility without adding information. A client portfolio should not carry a -100% tail whose catalyst cannot be underwritten; only risk-tolerant speculation, sized to tolerate zero, is defensible.

One-line justification: The stock is a call option on an unannounced, insider-controlled financing event — the recommendation hangs entirely on the assumption that new capital arrives before cash runs out at end-2023, and nothing known at that date lets you underwrite that assumption.
