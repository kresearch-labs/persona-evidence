# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-03 (Lehman Brothers, as of early June 2008)

**Recommendation: SELL (or, for clients with concentrated positions and constraints, reduce aggressively — no new money).**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The rating action is the business model, not a footnote.** A broker-dealer's raw material is cheap, confident funding and willing counterparties. S&P's downgrade to 'A' with a *negative outlook* — explicitly warning that further downgrades follow if losses mount or liquidity weakens — directly raises Lehman's funding costs and puts its counterparty relationships in play. For an investment bank, a ratings trajectory is a leading indicator of the balance sheet's health because it feeds on itself.

2. **The worst quarter isn't even reported yet.** Lehman's fiscal Q1 ended in February; March 2008 — the Bear Stearns failure, the acute funding freeze — hasn't shown up in reported results. S&P *expects* a meaningful deterioration in fiscal Q2: slower business, more write-downs, hedge losses from basis risk and de-levering. Holding through a known, pre-announced bad quarter with a negative-outlook rating is holding a falling object with the second hand already tied.

3. **The whole sector's outlook is negative — there is no diversification in staying.** Morgan Stanley and Merrill were downgraded the same day; S&P said the outlook for large U.S. financials was predominantly negative. This is not a Lehman-specific stumble; it's a systemic repricing of leverage, and the most credit-sensitive names get hit first and hardest.

4. **Cost-cutting is a later story.** S&P's own timeline says margin help arrives in fiscal Q3 — after the Q2 losses land, after the negative outlook does its work on funding. Between now and then: write-downs, higher funding costs, possible further downgrades. The risk/reward to *holding* a levered investment bank through a known-bad quarter with a deteriorating rating is asymmetric to the downside.

5. **The management "capital position" claim cannot be verified by outsiders in time.** Q1's relative strength is stale data from before March; by the time fiscal Q2 results confirm the losses, the stock and the funding market will have already moved.

**Bottom line:** sell / reduce. A levered investment bank's equity is hostage to confidence and ratings; both are moving against Lehman, the bad quarter is telegraphed, and the positive catalyst (Q3 cost savings) lies on the far side of the danger. Prudent advice in early June 2008: exit into strength now rather than defend the position through the write-down quarter — re-examine only after the fiscal Q2 report lands and the liquidity picture is proven.

*Honest caveat on the record: this is a judgment on information as of the date, not hindsight — but the visible stresses (rating action + unreported worst quarter + sector-wide negative outlook) were all knowable, and they all pointed the same direction.*
