Sell — do not average down.

As known on June 24, 2002, the risk/reward on WorldCom's equity is dominated by the $30 billion debt wall: the equity is a residual claim on a balance sheet that must be refinanced on unfavorable terms while the core long-distance business shrinks in price and volume, leaving no visible source of cash to service or repay the debt. Every near-term catalyst points down, not up — the stock at 91 cents risks Nasdaq delisting within 30 trading days below $1, bankers have not restructured the credit facilities, corporate demand shows no sign of recovering, and management credibility is impaired by the accounting scrutiny, which raises the odds of further negative surprises. Averaging down under a dollar is not a margin-of-safety play but a bet on survival; with total loss a realistic outcome and no identifiable turnaround trigger, a client holding the stock should sell rather than add.
