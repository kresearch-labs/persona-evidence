Recommendation: SELL.

As known on June 24, 2002, every factor that determines equity value pointed against WorldCom: roughly $30 billion of debt had to be refinanced on terms the banks would set, while the core long-distance business was in secular decline with falling prices and weak corporate demand — so the cash flows needed to service that debt were shrinking, not growing. The stock's fall to $0.91 (down over 90% since December 2001) put the company at risk of Nasdaq delisting within 30 trading days, which would cut off institutional holders and deepen the liquidity squeeze; management credibility was already impaired by accounting scrutiny, meaning any rescue plan would be discounted by the market; and there was no visible catalyst — no new credit terms, no demand recovery, no asset sales announced. At under a dollar the equity is not "cheap," it is an out-of-the-money option on survival: if the debt wall is not rolled over, shareholders face near-total loss, while the upside even in a successful restructuring is capped because creditors, not shareholders, capture the recovered value. Averaging down adds good money after bad into an asymmetric bet where the realistic downside is zero.

One-line justification: With $30B of debt coming due against a shrinking business, a sub-$1 stock facing delisting, impaired management credibility, and no turnaround catalyst, the risk of total equity loss outweighs any recovery upside — sell.
