Hold — do not initiate new positions at ~$42, and don't add for clients who own it.

As of June 4, 2019 Uber pairs real scale and growth (≈$3.1B quarterly revenue, +67% YoY; category leadership in ride-hailing; fast-growing Uber Eats) with ≈$1B quarterly net losses, no management timeline to profitability, and a ~$70B valuation — roughly 6x 2018 revenue — that already assumes both sustained hyper-growth and fat eventual margins. The two structural facts in the record that most threaten those margins — drivers take the majority of each fare, and Lyft plus regional players compete in every market — look like features of the economics, not temporary investment spend, which is why "hold" rather than "buy": the price embeds profitability the company has never shown. It is not a "sell" either: 67% growth, platform breadth, and a stock already ~7% below its $45 IPO price mean the downside is partly priced and the franchise is not impaired. The call hangs on one assumption: that Uber cannot soon raise its take rate per ride; if rising density lets it, the same facts flip this to buy.
