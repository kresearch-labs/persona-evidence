# Case 11 — Uber Technologies (as of June 4, 2019)

**Recommendation: Hold (do not initiate or add to positions; clients allocated IPO shares should hold only a small, closely monitored position and not add).**

**Why:** At ~$70 billion market value against roughly $12 billion in annualized revenue, Uber trades at a premium multiple of sales while losing about $1 billion per quarter, with management offering no firm timeline to profitability and framing losses as open-ended "investment in growth." The core economics are unproven: drivers take the majority of each fare, competition from Lyft and regional players caps pricing power in every market, and the post-IPO slide (~6.8% below the $45 offer price) plus a mixed first earnings report show the market is not yet rewarding scale alone. The 67% revenue growth and platform breadth (Rides, Eats, Freight) are real and justify keeping the story on the radar rather than selling outright, but until Uber demonstrates improving unit economics and a credible path to positive cash flow, the risk/reward does not justify committing new client capital.

**One-line justification:** Hold — category leadership and rapid growth are genuine, but a ~$70B valuation on billion-dollar quarterly losses with no profitability timeline makes the stock unbuyable here and not yet worth abandoning.
