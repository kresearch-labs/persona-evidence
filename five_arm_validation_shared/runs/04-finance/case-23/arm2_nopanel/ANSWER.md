Recommendation: SELL (or, for a client who must stay invested, a strict hold with a hard re-evaluation trigger) — do not initiate at ~$35.

Why, as known in mid-November 2021:

1. The growth engine has stalled. Q3 2021 revenue was ~$365M, down ~35% sequentially, and crypto revenue — the swing factor of 2021 — collapsed to ~$51M from ~$233M in Q2 as token prices cooled. Funded customers were roughly flat at ~22.4M, and management's Q4 revenue guidance (~$325–350M) implied another sequential decline. The 2020–21 boom (stimulus, meme stocks, dogecoin) increasingly looks like a one-time windfall, and there is no evidence yet that engagement reaccelerates once it passes.

2. The pivot to product investment removes the near-term earnings story. Management explicitly said it would prioritize new products over user growth, which it acknowledged would push adjusted EBITDA negative in the near term — after Q3 adjusted EBITDA had already fallen to ~$31M from ~$179M in Q2 and the company posted a ~$132M net loss in its first public quarter. Falling revenue plus rising spend is the worst combination for a stock priced for growth.

3. The valuation still embeds the boom. At $34.96 (below the $38 IPO price but ~$30B+ market cap), HOOD traded at a double-digit multiple of annualized revenue — versus low-single-digit revenue multiples for profitable online brokers — while its revenue mix is now more crypto-weighted and, by management's own admission, more volatile quarter to quarter. A premium multiple on decelerating, volatile revenue leaves little margin of safety.

4. Regulatory overhang caps the multiple. Pending scrutiny of payments-for-order-flow — the core of the revenue model — is an unquantified tail risk that argues against paying up now.

The stock has already de-rated from the mid-$50s, so some bad news is priced in; but with guidance pointing down, losses widening, no near-term catalyst, and the burden of proof now on management to show engagement is not a one-off, the risk/reward at ~$35 is skewed to the downside. Revisit if Q4 funded-customer trends and the crypto wallet rollout show engagement reaccelerating.

One-line justification: With user growth stalled, revenue and crypto volumes falling sequentially, the product pivot pushing adjusted EBITDA negative, PFOF regulatory risk unresolved, and a still-premium growth multiple, the known facts support selling rather than waiting out an unproven reacceleration.
