**Recommendation: Sell** — exit existing positions; do not initiate at the post-crash price (~$35).

**Why:**

1. **The earnings base is reset, not merely dented.** 2019 EBITDA is guided ~15% below consensus — back to roughly fiscal 2015 levels — after a ~500bp margin rebase over two years, despite more than $300M of stepped-up investment in 2018. Consumption gains came increasingly from promotion rather than brand strength, so the decline is structural (processed, center-store foods ceding share to fresh and private label). The reset numbers, not the old ones, are the right base for valuation.

2. **The stock is not cheap on those reset numbers.** At ~$35 the equity is ~$43B (~1.22B shares); with ~$31B of net debt, enterprise value is ~$73–74B — about 11–12x guided 2019 EBITDA of ~$6.0–6.5B and ~13x guided EPS of $2.55–2.75. That is a premium to better-trending peers (General Mills, Kellogg, Conagra at roughly 9–11x EBITDA) despite worse growth, higher leverage (~4.5–5x net debt to reset EBITDA), and negative momentum. On peer-average multiples the shares are worth roughly $26–31 — below the post-crash price. A 27% one-day fall repriced a full multiple onto lower earnings; it did not make the stock a bargain.

3. **The ownership thesis is broken.** Kraft Heinz was built as a 3G/Berkshire consolidation vehicle: cut costs, deleverage, acquire. With the moat itself written down ($15B impairment on Kraft and Oscar Mayer), the dividend cut 36%, and leverage still elevated, investors rightly doubt that future acquisitions can create value while the core business shrinks — removing the justification for the premium multiple.

4. **Risk without a catalyst.** The SEC subpoena on procurement (plus a $25M charge) keeps an investigation open; any genuine turnaround requires further reinvestment that depresses margins before it helps; and the reduced $1.60/yr dividend (~4.6% yield) no longer pays the client to wait. Nothing disclosed points to stabilization in organic sales or margins during 2019.

One-line justification: Sell — at ~$35 Kraft Heinz still trades at ~11–12x reset 2019 EBITDA (~$73B EV against ~$31B net debt), a premium multiple for a structurally shrinking, ~4.5x-levered business whose brands were just impaired, whose dividend was cut, and which carries an open SEC inquiry, with no catalyst until organic growth and margins stabilize.
