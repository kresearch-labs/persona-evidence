# Recommendation: SELL

Even after the ~27% plunge to roughly $35 on February 22, 2019, Kraft Heinz was not cheap — it was a value trap. At ~$35 (≈$42B equity value, ~1.21B shares) plus ~$28B of net debt, the enterprise value of ~$70B still implied ~11–12x EV/EBITDA on the newly guided ~$6B 2019 EBITDA — a premium to slower-story peers like General Mills, Kellogg, and Conagra at ~9–10x, despite Kraft Heinz having the worst fundamentals in the group. The guidance cut (~15% below consensus, taking EBITDA back to fiscal 2015 levels), the ~500bp two-year margin rebase, and $300M+ of incremental investment that bought only promotion-driven, not brand-driven, volume all pointed to structural decline in center-store packaged food, not a cyclical dip. The $15B write-down of Kraft and Oscar Mayer was management's own admission that the 3G zero-based-cost playbook had hollowed out brand equity, removing the intangible value that justified premium multiples. The 36% dividend cut (~$1.60/year, ~4.6% yield) eliminated the income thesis without fixing the balance sheet: at ~4.3x net leverage, deleveraging now depends on a shrinking EBITDA base, and the build-then-acquire consolidation strategy that was the entire equity story is dead as long as the core contracts — investors correctly doubt that acquisitions can create value from a weakened currency (the stock). The SEC subpoena and $25M charge add governance uncertainty and further M&A/strategic friction. Valuing the business like its peers (~9–10x EBITDA less net debt) implies roughly $22–28 per share, 20–35% below the post-drop price, with asymmetric risk: further estimate cuts, another dividend reduction, or a forced strategic reset to the downside versus limited catalysts (a Berkshire/3G rescue bid or takeover premium) to the upside.

**One-line justification:** Sell — at ~$35 the stock still priced a stable business at a premium to peers (~11–12x EV/EBITDA vs. ~9–10x) while Kraft Heinz offered a shrinking, rebased EBITDA, self-impaired brands, ~4.3x leverage, a broken deleveraging-and-acquire thesis, and no visible catalyst.
