# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-15 (Kraft Heinz, as of February 22, 2019)

**Recommendation: SELL (or hold only as a deeply discounted income position with explicit acceptance of further dividend risk).**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The February 21 disclosure bundle is a business-model indictment, not a bad quarter.** Missing EPS by ~$0.10, guiding 2019 EBITDA ~15% below consensus, cutting the dividend 36%, a $15B write-down of *the two flagship brands*, an SEC subpoena on procurement, and a rebased margin (−500bps over two years) with EBITDA guided back to fiscal-2015 levels — four years of "progress" erased. A quarter is weather; this is climate. The brand write-down is the most damning line: the company is telling you its own brands are worth less than it said, which undermines the entire 3G playbook premise (buy iconic brands, cut costs, harvest).

2. **The 3G model has met its structural limit.** The cost-cutting-and-leverage machine works when the brands it strips have pricing power. Kraft Heinz's own 2018 numbers show the opposite: $300M+ of stepped-up investment produced consumption gains "from promotional spending rather than brand strength" — meaning volume is rented, not owned. When a brand must be promoted to move, it's a commodity with a logo, and private label plus fresh-food shift will keep taking share. The margin re-basing confirms the decline is secular, not cyclical.

3. **The deleveraging thesis — the reason to own — is now in question.** The stock's appeal was 3G+Berkshire shepherding leverage down toward the next consolidation play. But if the core shrinks while leverage stays elevated, and future acquisitions are doubtful-value (the market doubts them, reasonably, given the write-down), there is no second act visible. The dividend cut removes the income thesis; the write-down removes the brand-equity thesis; the subpoena adds an unquantifiable tail; leverage closes the door on the strategic upside.

4. **What's left, honestly, is a yield-degraded, leveraged, declining packaged-food business at a market that hasn't finished repricing it.** After a sharp one-day fall, the initial research wave is only arriving; second-order estimate cuts (2019 EBITDA −15% is *guidance*, and guided numbers this year have a way of worsening) typically take more quarters. The stock mid-$90s → sharp drop is the first leg, not the last.

**Bottom line:** sell. Berkshire's presence is a comfort, not a catalyst — Buffett's team does not manage operations, and a shrinking EBITDA base under real leverage with a subpoenaed procurement office is a balance-sheet story waiting for a worse chapter. Revisit only if EBITDA stabilizes at the new base for two consecutive quarters or if the dividend is credibly re-coverable by free cash flow.
