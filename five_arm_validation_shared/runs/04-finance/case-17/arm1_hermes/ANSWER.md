# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-17 (AMD, as of August 9, 2018)

**Recommendation: HOLD (let winners run with a trailing discipline); trim for clients whose position has become oversized; selective adds only on pullbacks below the post-earnings consolidation range.**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The competitive window is real, structural, and multi-year — the core thesis is intact, not a momentum artifact.** Intel's repeated 10nm delays (now shipping holiday 2019, realistically later) against AMD's 7nm TSMC-built products launching in 2018 is a *process-technology* gap that persists for years, not quarters. Chips designed on smaller geometries are faster and more power-efficient; server buyers — the profitable end of the market — are actively forced to evaluate AMD. This is the first genuine architectural opening in AMD's market position in over a decade.

2. **The share story is early, not exhausted.** ~2% server share in 2018, with credible build to ~5% in 2019 and 9–10% by 2020 — that's a runway measured in years of measurable, reportable progress. Each quarterly server share print is a checkpoint the holder can verify. Revenue +53% YoY with gross margin near 37% (and rising with mix) shows the gains are *economical*, not bought with rebates.

3. **The valuation counter-argument, honestly stated:** after +85.8% YTD, the stock has re-rated from deep value to a growth multiple. Trailing numbers no longer offer a bargain; the buyer today pays for 2019–2020 share gains *happening on schedule*. That's a momentum-exhaustion risk — and the stock's history (dramatic boom-bust cycles) argues against complacency.

4. **Why hold rather than sell:** selling a structural share-gain story because it has run is timing roulette; the correct discipline is position-sizing. Trim whatever size has become oversized for the portfolio; hold the core while quarterly results *confirm* the share-gain model (server share, gross margin trajectory, 7nm Rome/Epyc execution). Sell discipline, not sentiment: any quarter where server share progress stalls or gross margin rolls over is the exit signal.

**Bottom line:** hold the core; trim oversized positions into strength; adds only on consolidation weakness. The Intel process stumble created a multi-year, *reportable* opportunity — the stock's run so far prices the promise, and the next several quarters' server-share data either confirms it (hold/add) or ends it (sell). Key checkpoint: 7nm launch execution and initial EPYC Rome server design wins in H2 2018.
