# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-08 (Peloton, as of May 2022)

**Recommendation: SELL (or, for speculative accounts only, a strictly-sized "asset-value floor" position — the equity is a call option on a turnaround with no margin of safety).**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The demand collapse is structural, not cyclical — that's the decisive fact.** Lockdown pull-forward isn't a dip; it *removed* future demand: households that bought a $2,000 bike in 2020 will not buy another for years. Pandemic favorites that sold durable equipment face a multi-year air pocket, not a dip. Halting production earlier in the year confirms the magnitude. Gym reopening compounds it — Peloton's core "convenience" value proposition is weakest exactly when its competition (gyms, outdoor activity) is strongest.

2. **The turnaround plan is credible in outline and unproven in every particular.** McCarthy is a proven operator, and the plan has real logic: subscription-first economics, sell down excess inventory, restore positive FCF in FY23. But every load-bearing assumption is untested: the June repricing (hardware cut + subscription raise) has an unproven net effect on demand and churn; the inventory sale crystallizes losses; and "100 million members" implies a 14x increase from ~7M — a number that belongs in an aspiration column, not a model. Investors have heard visionary membership targets before; what's missing is evidence.

3. **The cash math is the real risk.** $757M net loss in one quarter; cash burn while working through inventory; deep unprofitability with competition intensifying (Apple Fitness+, Lululemon/Mirror, Tonal, reopened gyms). The turnaround requires *time* and *money* simultaneously — the two things a cash-burning company with a collapsing stock cannot reliably buy. The path to positive FCF in FY23 exists on paper; the probability it arrives before the cash situation forces dilution or asset sales is the whole investment question.

4. **At $12.90, down >90%, the market is pricing near-distress.** The honest framing: this is not "a great company on sale," it's "a company trying to avoid becoming a case study." The asset value (installed base of ~3M subscribers paying recurring fees) provides a partial floor — the subscription base has real value to an acquirer — but there is no controlling-shareholder with deep pockets standing behind the plan (unlike a Carvana-style family), and no proof of liquidity support.

**Bottom line:** sell, or if a client insists on the turnaround, size it as speculation — one small position, explicitly labeled, with FCF-positive FY23 as the kill-or-confirm checkpoint. The 90% drawdown is not the safety net people assume; a collapsing top line can take the last 90% further down. Advice: let the turnaround prove itself with one or two quarters of free cash flow before committing real capital.
