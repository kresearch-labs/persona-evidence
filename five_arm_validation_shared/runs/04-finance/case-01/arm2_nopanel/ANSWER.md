Recommendation: SELL.

Justification: As of November 8, 2001 the one input every valuation depends on — Enron's reported earnings and balance sheet — had just been declared unreliable, with nearly five years of profits restated, a $1 billion write-off and a $1.2 billion equity reduction, so no defensible intrinsic value could be computed; the off-balance-sheet partnerships under congressional scrutiny meant unknown contingent liabilities; the CEO's abrupt August resignation left a credibility gap at the top; and a trading franchise lives on counterparty confidence, which accounting doubt destroys quickly and self-reinforcingly — making the downside (a credit and counterparty spiral wiping out the equity) far more probable and severe than any upside from a franchise whose true financial condition was, at that date, unknowable.
