# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-18 (Micron, as of September 12, 2018)

**Recommendation: SELL / exit the position (for most client accounts); hold only for accounts explicitly sized and committed to trading full memory cycles.**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The cycle's leading indicators have rolled — and memory downturns snowball.** This is the decisive fact, not a sentiment item: KLA-Tencor (whose equipment orders *are* the industry's own forecast) has warned on memory; spot DRAM/NAND prices are softening; customers are delaying procurement to wait for lower prices. That last dynamic is the memory cycle's self-fulfilling engine — buyers defer, inventory builds, producers cut price to move it, buyers defer more. Historically these downturns run *several quarters* and overshoot on the downside, exactly as the early-2018 episode showed.

2. **The low multiple is the cyclical's trap, not its gift.** Micron at 4–5x forward earnings is not "cheap"; in a commodity-memory business, trough earnings are low and peak multiples *should* be low — the multiple expands only when the market believes pricing has bottomed. Buying at 5x peak-cycle EPS into a rolling-over market is paying for earnings that are about to shrink. The classic memory-cycle error is anchoring on peak multiples; the multiple is the cycle's message, not its bargain.

3. **The record results are backwards-looking and at peak.** $7.8B revenue (+40%), $3.15 EPS — records set at cycle-peak pricing. Every cyclical leader shows record numbers at the top; the stock, correctly, prices the *next* pricing period, and the next period's message arrived this week via the equipment maker's warning.

4. **The sell signal is confirmed, not merely suspected.** One week of -12%, another -5% premarket, broad memory-sector weakness — the market is pricing the downturn as begun. In cyclicals, the profitable exits happen when results are still strong and sentiment has begun to crack; the unprofitable ones happen later, into deteriorating estimates. Waiting for confirmed EPS decline means selling into the snowball.

**The honest counter-case (for cycle-traders only):** Micron remains the lowest-cost, best-run of the DRAM oligopoly, and the three-player structure (Samsung/SK Hynix/Micron) rationalizes supply better than old memory cycles — disciplined holders through full cycles have been rewarded historically. But that strategy requires surviving multi-quarter, often >40% drawdowns, and it is a *cycle-timing* commitment, not a portfolio holding.

**Bottom line:** for standard client accounts, exit — the downturn's leading indicators have turned, and peak-earnings multiples don't protect you from trough earnings. For dedicated cycle-traders: sell now and set a re-entry discipline for the next trough (watch for supply rationalization and spot-price stabilization, not the low multiple).
