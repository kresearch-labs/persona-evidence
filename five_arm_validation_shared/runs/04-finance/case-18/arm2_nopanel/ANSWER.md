**Recommendation: Sell.**

Micron's 4–5x forward multiple looks cheap only because it is applied to peak-cycle earnings; memory stocks are effectively priced on mid-cycle earnings, so a single-digit P/E at the top of a cycle is the classic value trap, not a margin of safety. Every leading indicator in the situation points to a genuine turn: spot DRAM and NAND prices are already softening, customers have begun delaying procurement to wait for lower prices — a dynamic that historically makes memory downturns last several quarters and snowball into steeper price declines than investors initially anticipate, exactly as the early-2018 downturn showed — and KLA-Tencor's warning gives an equipment-maker's confirmation that fresh fab supply is arriving just as demand defers. With the stock down 12% in a week and falling another 5% premarket, consensus estimates will be cut repeatedly over the coming quarters, and each cut makes the "cheap" multiple more expensive; the asymmetry favors exiting now and redeploying near the trough, when the multiple will look high but earnings are depressed.

One-line justification: In a cyclical, the lowest P/Es appear at peak earnings — with spot prices rolling over and buyers deferring purchases to wait for lower prices, the downturn will snowball over several quarters and shrink the very earnings that make the stock look cheap at 4–5x forward.
