Micron's record results are backward-looking: the ~$7.8 billion quarter and $3.15 EPS reflect DRAM pricing at cycle highs, and a 4–5x forward multiple is not a margin of safety — for a cyclical, the P/E is always lowest at peak earnings, so the low multiple already discounts the coming fall in E. Every forward indicator in the situation points the same way: KLA-Tencor, an equipment maker with the earliest visibility into fab spending, has already warned on memory; spot prices are softening; and customers have begun delaying procurement to wait for lower prices. That last behavior is the classic self-reinforcing memory-downturn mechanism — buyers defer, inventory builds in the channel, suppliers cut prices to move volume, buyers defer more — which historically stretches memory downturns over several quarters and drives prices down further than investors initially anticipate, exactly as the downturn earlier in 2018 demonstrated. With the stock down 12% in a week and gapping down another 5%, the repricing is underway, not finished. For the client the asymmetry favors stepping aside: into a multi-quarter downturn earnings can fall by half or more while upside is capped until the cycle turns, and the exit is reversible — the stock can be re-bought, likely at an even lower price, once spot prices stabilize and customers resume normal ordering.

One-line justification: at 4–5x peak-cycle earnings with spot prices rolling over and customers deferring purchases — the mechanism that historically snowballs memory downturns into multi-quarter declines — the low multiple is a cyclical trap, not a cushion, so sell and re-enter when procurement delays end.
