# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-16 (Nvidia, as of August 24, 2023)

**Recommendation: HOLD (existing positions; with a disciplined trimming rule for oversized holdings). DO NOT BUY a full position at $1T+ valuation without accepting you are paying today for the 2025–2028 numbers.**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The fundamentals are genuinely extraordinary — this is not a momentum mirage.** Revenue $13.5B (2x YoY); data center alone ~$10B *in one quarter* versus ~$15B for the entire prior year; October guidance ~$16B, far above months-earlier expectations; product broadly sold out with supply constrained. The AI buildout is real enterprise spending backed by cloud capex budgets — demand that customers are pre-committing to, not sampling. Nvidia holds the near-monopoly position in the enabling hardware. When a company beats guidance this dramatically and still can't make enough product, the earnings estimates are more likely to *rise* than fall — and they did.

2. **The valuation counter-case, honestly stated:** the stock more than tripled YTD, sits at $1T+, and at that level prices years of flawless execution and continued pricing power. At ~$41B FY data-center revenue going to ~$60B and ~$100B by FY2028, the projections assume (a) AI workloads become durable enterprise spending, not one-time GPU purchases; (b) Nvidia's competitive moat (CUDA software ecosystem, hardware lead) holds against AMD and internal cloud chips; and (c) supply chains deliver. Buyers today underwrite all three.

3. **The specific risks worth pricing:** customer concentration (a handful of cloud giants drive the demand — their capex cycles are lumpy); competition ramping (AMD's MI300, custom silicon at Google/Amazon/Microsoft designed precisely to reduce Nvidia dependence); and China export restrictions. None of these break the thesis; all of them can dent the *pace*, and at $1T the stock is priced for pace, not for pauses.

4. **Why hold rather than sell:** selling a structural, estimate-rising franchise because it has run is the classic error of this market phase — the durable-AI-spending thesis (shift from one-time purchases to recurring AI infrastructure, like cloud's buildout) is supported by *rising guidance*, not just narrative. The correct discipline is trimming whatever size makes the portfolio over-concentrated in one name, and setting sell-rules on evidence: data-center revenue guidance cuts, gross-margin erosion, or material competitive share loss.

**Bottom line:** hold; trim oversized positions; for new money, stage entries (partial now, partial on any pullback toward the rising guidance line) rather than a full position at the all-time high. The demand curve is real; the $1T price just demands that everything keep going right for several years — which, on this date, it is doing. Checkpoints: quarterly data-center guidance, gross margin, and hyperscaler capex commentary.
