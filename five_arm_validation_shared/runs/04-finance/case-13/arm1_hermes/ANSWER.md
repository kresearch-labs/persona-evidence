# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-13 (Boeing, as of April 8, 2019)

**Recommendation: HOLD for existing holders (with trim rules); selective accumulation on further weakness for long-horizon accounts — this is a temporarily dented dominant franchise, not a broken one, but the unknowns are still large enough that discipline matters.**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **Separate the franchise from the crisis.** The 737 MAX grounding strikes Boeing's highest-volume, highest-margin program — real, costly, and months-long at minimum. But Boeing the company: record $101.1B revenue in FY2018, a backlog exceeding 5,500 orders (multi-year production covered), the duopoly position in single-aisle with Airbus (no third competitor can emerge inside a decade), and defense/space businesses unaffected. The crash aftermath is an *operational* crisis with quantifiable cost buckets — grounding duration, delivery penalties, compensation, retrofit costs — not an *existential* one.

2. **The unknowns are large but bounded and knowable in sequence.** Unlike accounting-fraud scenarios (Enron, WorldCom) where the balance sheet itself is uncertain, here the questions have definable resolution paths: the fix for the MCAS software, regulator recertification, and the compensation math all become visible over the following months. Crisis-of-duration pricing punishes holders in the interim — that's the cost of holding, and the stock's ~9% one-month decline is that discount forming.

3. **The competition structure protects the long-term value.** Airbus cannot absorb 5,500 canceled orders even if airlines wanted to cancel (most won't — they need the jets); the single-aisle duopoly means deferred demand returns to Boeing. The MAX brand damage is real but repairable with a clean safety record post-return — commercial aviation's memory is long but its duopoly is longer.

4. **The risk that argues against aggressive buying today:** the stock is *still up ~21.5% YTD* and richly valued on 2019 earnings, which the MAX crisis makes unforecastable. If the grounding extends past summer, production disruption compounds (already cut to 42/month), compensation accrues, and the 2019 EPS base cracks — the rich multiple gets re-based. So: not a "buy the dip blindly" moment.

**Bottom line:** hold existing positions; trim to a size where a further 10-15% drawdown is emotionally irrelevant, and set the checkpoint at recertification progress + compensation disclosures. For long-horizon accounts *without* exposure: accumulate on weakness in tranches — the duopoly franchise and multi-year backlog make Boeing one of the few industrial companies that can absorb a MAX-year and compound through it. Size tranches by grounding-duration risk; full position only when recertification is visibly on track.
