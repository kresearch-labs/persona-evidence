**Recommendation: HOLD** — keep existing positions, don't add new money until the MAX's return-to-service path is clear.

**Why:**

1. **Not a Sell — the franchise is intact.** Boeing operates a duopoly with Airbus in single-aisle jets, where airlines have effectively nowhere else to go; the record backlog of more than 5,500 jets represents years of production, and historical cancellation rates in such backlogs are low. Fiscal 2018 was a record year ($101.1B revenue, roughly $15B operating cash flow), so the company enters the crisis from a position of financial strength, not stress.

2. **The known costs are mostly timing, not impairment.** Cutting production to 42 jets/month defers roughly ten deliveries a month — a revenue deferral that is largely recoverable once the MAX flies again, not a permanent loss. Airline compensation is a one-off cash cost plausibly in the low single-digit billions, and consensus 2019 earnings (~$20/share) may be cut 5–10%. Painful, but not franchise-threatening for a company of this cash-generating capacity.

3. **Not yet a Buy — the risk is asymmetric until certification is resolved.** The grounding's duration, whether the MCAS fix stays software-only or requires hardware, the lag in approvals from regulators like China's CAAC, and the final size of compensation claims are all unknown. Each month of delay erodes leverage with carriers and risks durable damage to the MAX brand against the A320neo. And after a 21.5% year-to-date run the stock still trades around 19–20x 2019 earnings despite only a ~9% pullback — expectations remain elevated for a company carrying an unquantified liability.

4. **The number hangs on one named assumption:** that the grounding ends within roughly two to three quarters via a software-only MCAS fix, making the costs a deferral rather than a structural loss. If that assumption breaks — hardware changes required, cancellations in size, or a grounding extending past Q3 2019 — the recommendation flips to Sell; if the FAA/EASA timeline is fixed and compensation is quantified, it flips to Buy.

**One-line justification:** Hold — Boeing's duopoly position, record backlog, and cash generation make selling indefensible, but unknown grounding duration, unquantified compensation, and a still-rich ~20x valuation on elevated expectations argue against buying until the MAX's return-to-service path is fixed.
