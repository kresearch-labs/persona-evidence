**Recommendation: HOLD** — keep the stock if the client already owns it, but do not add at ~$10 after a 38% year-to-date rally.

The turnaround thesis is credible: Larry Culp is a proven operator from Danaher, the dividend cut is behind the company, debt is being reduced, and GE Aviation remains a world-class franchise with a massive installed base and high-margin services revenue. But the financials as known on April 8, 2019 do not yet support the rally. GE has lost more than $30 billion over the past two years, management itself calls 2019 a "reset year" in which the industrial businesses could burn as much as $2 billion in cash (one research firm expects a $2.4–2.5 billion first-quarter industrial outflow alone), GE Capital will keep consuming cash following the $15 billion insurance reserve charge, and GE Power — the heart of the problem — is not expected to turn free-cash-flow positive until 2021. In short, the rebound already prices in a recovery that has not yet shown up in the numbers, while significant liabilities and weak Power and Renewables results leave little margin of safety. Hold existing positions and wait for hard evidence — a positive industrial free-cash-flow trajectory and stabilization at Power — before upgrading to buy; the aviation franchise value and new leadership argue against selling outright.
