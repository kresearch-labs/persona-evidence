# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-14 (General Electric, as of April 8, 2019)

**Recommendation: HOLD existing positions only; DO NOT BUY the rebound — the 38% YTD rally has priced a recovery the cash flows haven't shown.**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The market has front-run the turnaround.** GE is up ~38% YTD to ~$10 while management *itself* guides to a potential $2B industrial cash *loss* in 2019 and says GE Power's free cash flow won't turn positive until 2021. The rally prices a successful reset; the numbers so far show only a reset in progress. That gap — price leading proof — is the central risk. One research firm expects a Q1 industrial cash outflow of $2.4–2.5B: when that prints, the "reset is working" narrative meets arithmetic.

2. **The balance-sheet tail is long and still unmeasured.** The $15B insurance reserve charge of late 2018 revealed that GE Capital's liabilities were deeper than decades of disclosure suggested; the reasonable inference is that more surprises remain possible in a financial arm that has consumed cash "for the foreseeable future." Turnarounds in *operational* businesses are trackable; turnarounds that require a financial arm to stop leaking are hostage to items outsiders can't model.

3. **Culp's credibility is real — and it is the entire bull case.** Danaher pedigree, the decisive dividend cut (12 cents → a penny, done), debt reduction, a world-class aviation franchise with high-margin services on a massive installed base: none of that is in dispute. But "the CEO is good" is a reason to *believe*, not a reason to *pay* 38% more than the market paid three months ago for the same uncertain cash flows. The 2019 print — with its guided cash loss — is still ahead.

4. **Power and renewables remain weak**, and the bear case's sharpest point is correct: investors are pricing small positives while underestimating large negatives. When a stock rallies 38% into a "reset year" that management itself calls a loss year, the risk is not that GE fails — it's that the *timing* was wrong and the stock consolidates or gives back the rally while the cash flow catches up.

**Bottom line:** hold existing (trimmed) positions with the dividend-cut-as-floor logic intact; do not initiate or add after a 38% run that precedes the evidence. Define the checkpoint: Q1 industrial cash flow (the $2.4B+ outflow question), 2019 industrial FCF trajectory, and any further GE Capital reserve surprises. If cash burn tracks *better* than guided, the turnaround thesis earns more weight; if worse, the 38% rally unwinds and the stock is a value again. Patience is the position.
