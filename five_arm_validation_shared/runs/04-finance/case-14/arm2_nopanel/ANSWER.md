**Recommendation: Sell** (at ~$10, after the 38% year-to-date rally).

One-line justification: The rally has priced in a recovery that has not yet shown up in the numbers — with industrial free cash flow guided negative for 2019 (up to a $2 billion outflow, and $2.4–2.5 billion expected in Q1 alone), GE Capital set to consume cash for the foreseeable future, GE Power not turning cash-positive until 2021, and heavy legacy liabilities (the $15 billion insurance charge, a near-zero dividend, $30+ billion of two-year losses), the stock trades on hope of Culp's execution rather than cash generation, so the risk/reward favors selling into strength and re-buying only when the cash flow actually confirms the reset.

Supporting reasoning: Culp's operating pedigree, the completed dividend cut, shrinking debt, and the world-class Aviation franchise are real, but they are "small positives" whose value the market is overestimating relative to the severity of the cash burn and balance-sheet risks it is underestimating. At roughly $87 billion of market cap on negative industrial free cash flow, the shares already discount a successful turnaround; until Power stabilizes and the industrial business stops consuming cash — evidence due no earlier than 2021 — there is no valuation support at $10, and any stumble in the reset (Capital charges, weaker aviation demand, slower Power recovery) offers a materially lower entry point.
