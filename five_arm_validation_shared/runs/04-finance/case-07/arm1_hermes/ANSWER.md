# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-07 (Zoom, as of February 28, 2022)

**Recommendation: HOLD (not for growth — for the cash-return option; reduce into strength if the client's thesis was "pandemic growth").**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The quarter was excellent; the guidance was the story.** Revenue $1.07B (beat), GAAP net income $490M, adjusted EPS $1.29 vs ~$1.07 consensus — this is a *profitable, cash-generating* company, which immediately separates it from the distress bucket. But management guided FY23 growth to ~10% (from hyper-growth) and adjusted EPS down to $3.45–3.51 from $5.07 — an explicit admission that the pandemic pull-forward is ending and heavy platform investment begins. The market's 10% after-hours drop is the sound of a growth premium being repriced.

2. **The re-rating is mostly legitimate.** A stock down 66% over 12 months was priced for continuation of hyper-growth; guidance now says "10% grower with EPS declining to invest." The question for the holder: what should a 10%-growth, ~$1.6-1.8B adjusted-earnings software company be worth? Meaningfully more than $30B at the pre-release price — but the crash already removed much of the froth. At ~$115 after hours, the stock trades at a more ordinary multiple for a decelerating software name with best-in-class gross margins and $5B+ revenue run-rate.

3. **The bear case is Microsoft Teams and the free-user conversion question — real, structural, and partially priced.** Teams bundles into an ecosystem enterprises already buy; Zoom's online business (free-user driven) will churn down as pandemic usage normalizes. Against it: the enterprise business is guided to grow substantially faster, the product remains the category default for external meetings, and the contact-center expansion (ContactCenter as-a-Service) is a genuine new TAM, though it's *investment-driven* — hence the guided EPS decline.

4. **The $1B buyback plus ServiceNow's CEO joining the board are the two quiet, telling facts.** A company with $5B+ net cash buying back stock at ~10x adjusted earnings signals management's own view that the repricing overshot. The board addition signals a serious enterprise-software pivot, not desperation.

**Bottom line:** hold, with the honest framing that the *reason to own* has changed. The pandemic-growth thesis is dead and its proponents should exit. The new thesis is a profitable, cash-rich company at a reasonable multiple buying back stock while attempting a platform expansion — an option on the contact-center/enterprise pivot. Size it as a value-with-optionality position, not a growth holding. If Q1 execution (June) confirms the low end of guidance and the enterprise acceleration fails to materialize, trim to zero — the Teams bear case wins by default.
