# Case 10 — Beyond Meat (BYND), late January 2021

**Recommendation: SELL**

**One-line justification:** At ~75x estimated 2021 EV/EBITDA — roughly six times the packaged-food group's 12–13x five-year average — the price already discounts years of flawless execution, while Beyond is unprofitable, margins are pressured, its foodservice recovery is uncertain, and its main catalyst (McDonald's McPlant) is unconfirmed and largely priced in, skewing risk/reward to the downside.

**Why:**

- **Valuation vs. fundamentals.** ~75x 2021E EV/EBITDA against a 12–13x group norm is a ~6x premium that only sustained hyper-growth plus major margin expansion can justify; today the company is loss-making, and relief from the bulk-packaging margin drag is "expected," not demonstrated.
- **Asymmetric expectations.** At this multiple, execution must be flawless: any slip in retail velocity, slower household penetration, or a delayed foodservice recovery compresses the multiple toward group norms and implies severe downside, while the upside case requires everything to go right.
- **Catalyst risk, not catalyst value.** The McPlant link is an industry-check indication, not a disclosed arrangement — volume, terms, and margin contribution are unknown, and even a confirmed partnership would take years to ramp into earnings; paying 75x for a binary, partially priced-in hope is poor risk discipline.
- **What's going right is already in the price.** Retail expansion (≈17,000 → ≈28,000 stores) and an eventual easing of the bulk-pack drag support the business, not the multiple — they are the base case, not incremental upside.
- **Channel mix delays the earnings inflection.** The higher-margin, long-term growth vector (restaurant/foodservice) is the one most suppressed by the pandemic, with major chains delaying plant-based menu launches, so the profit inflection — if it comes — is later and less certain than the multiple assumes.

Bottom line: an attractive growth story at a price that leaves no room for error — at ~$137 the disciplined call for a client is to sell/avoid and revisit if the multiple normalizes toward the group or disclosed McPlant economics de-risk the foodservice ramp.
