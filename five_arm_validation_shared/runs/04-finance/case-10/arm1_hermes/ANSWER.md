# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-10 (Beyond Meat, as of late January 2021)

**Recommendation: SELL (or at minimum, take profits / trim any IPO-cost-basis position to a core holding).**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The price is the problem, and it is quantifiable.** ~75x estimated 2021 EV/EBITDA versus a packaged-food group average of 12–13x: Beyond Meat is priced at nearly *six times* its peer multiple for a company that is — as of this date — unprofitable. That premium is not a margin-of-safety; it is the wholesale discounting of years of flawless execution *and* a successful foodservice recovery *and* sustained consumer adoption of a behavior change (plant-based diets) that has historically never consolidated in food. Food categories do not behave like tech networks; the premium assumes a tech-like franchise in a commodity-adjacent business.

2. **The bull case is real but already in the price.** Retail distribution 17,000 → 28,000 stores is genuine progress; the McPlant association (if it proceeds) is a real foodservice catalyst. But distribution wins are *priced* — the 75x multiple already assumes the shelves convert to velocity, the foodservice channel recovers post-pandemic, margins normalize as bulk packaging unwinds, and McDonald's scale materializes. An investor buying at $137 today must be paid by *incremental* good news the market hasn't imagined.

3. **The margin pressure is small in dollars and large in signal.** Bulk packaging to support pandemic sales pressured margins, expected to ease — fine. But a food company whose profitability is this sensitive to packaging decisions is not yet a pricing-power franchise; the 75x multiple presumes pricing power that hasn't been demonstrated.

4. **The risk/reward is asymmetric to the downside at this valuation.** For the stock to be *cheap*, Beyond Meat must become one of the most successful food launches in history, sustained for a decade. For it to be *expensive*, it merely has to grow like — a food company. The 2020–21 market environment (pandemic stay-at-home favorites, richly priced growth) has also flattered every name in this cohort; a group-wide de-rating would take BYND with it regardless of fundamentals.

**Bottom line:** sell / trim into strength. The growth story is intact, the McPlant catalyst is real — and the valuation already owns all of it plus the execution risk. Advice: realize the gain, keep the company on a watchlist, and re-buy only at a multiple that belongs to the packaged-food group it actually competes in (or on evidence the McPlant scale-up has begun).
