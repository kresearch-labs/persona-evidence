# Netflix (NFLX) — Recommendation as of October 25, 2011

**Recommendation: Buy** — for patient, long-term capital, accumulated in stages and sized for continued volatility.

**One-line justification:** The damage was self-inflicted and concentrated in the declining DVD-by-mail business while the streaming core kept growing with pricing power intact, and at under $100 (roughly a $4–4.5B market cap) the market was valuing Netflix's ~25 million streaming subscribers at under $200 each — a transitory reset, not structural unraveling.

**Why:**

- **The churn is DVD-led, and DVD was already a melting ice cube.** The DVD business still supplies most profits, but management is explicitly managing it into decline; the market is pricing the shrinkage of the legacy business as if it were the future of the company.
- **The streaming franchise is intact.** Revenue was still growing roughly 50% year over year (about $1.6B in Q3) and the company remains solidly profitable; the large majority of members stayed and paid the 60% increase rather than quit — painful proof of pricing power, not evidence the model broke.
- **Qwikster was a communications failure, not an asset impairment.** It destroyed management credibility (a real cost that will take quarters to repair) but was reversed within weeks and changed nothing about the subscriber relationship or the content library.
- **International losses are deliberate investment, not deterioration.** Canada is already trending toward profitability and Latin America is months old; the weak 2012 EPS guide reflects chosen expansion spend, and the originals pipeline and new studio deals defend the moat as the Starz library exits.
- **The valuation reset embeds failure.** After a ~two-thirds decline in four months, the price assumes near-zero value for international expansion and continued US streaming growth; even a scenario of flat US streaming, DVD run-off, and modest ARPU roughly supports the current price, so the asymmetry favors ownership.

**Downgrade to hold/sell if:** churn spreads into streaming itself (Q4 streaming net adds materially negative), or content costs persistently outrun ARPU — those, unlike DVD attrition, would signal genuine structural erosion.
