# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-05 (Netflix, as of October 25, 2011)

**Recommendation: HOLD (do not add; do not panic-sell).**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The wounds are self-inflicted and therefore *diagnosable and reversible* — that's the single most important fact.** The 60% price increase, the Qwikster fiasco, and the reversal: these are management errors of communication and sequencing, not evidence of a broken business. Netflix's core assets — the streaming library, the recommendation engine, the by-mail infrastructure, and crucially the *subscriber relationship* — survived the price shock. When damage is self-inflicted, a credible management team can repair it; when damage is structural (secular decline, technology substitution), it cannot. Everything in this quarter says "self-inflicted."

2. **The subscriber data cuts both ways, and the right read matters.** Yes: DVD (still most of the profits) is shedding faster than expected, Q4 U.S. net adds implied down ~80% year-over-year, growth is the slowest since 2008, and international expansion is burning cash with weak 2012 EPS guided. That's real. But — and this is the judgment call — the *business model itself* is migrating from DVD to streaming, and the subscriber defections are concentrated at the seam of the transition. The customer base is annoyed, not gone. Streaming demand was growing; the company simply priced and messaged the transition catastrophically.

3. **Valuation after a two-thirds drawdown is a different risk than before it.** The stock below $100 has already absorbed the price-increase anger, the Qwikster reversal, and the international loss forecast. A great deal of the bad news is in the price; the marginal seller is now a capitulating momentum holder, not an informed one. Selling here locks in the drawdown at the point of maximum anger and minimum expectation.

4. **The bull case is concrete, not narrative:** streaming was winning cultural adoption globally; content costs, while rising, bought exclusive-value product; international markets (Canada, Latin America) were proven demand pools with execution costs to be worked down. Hastings's own reversal on Qwikster showed responsiveness to customers — humility is rare in founders and it was displayed publicly.

5. **The honest risk:** management credibility takes quarters to rebuild, and the 2012 losses are *guided* — the next two quarters will look bad. A holder needs the temperament to sit through visible ugliness while the subscriber curve turns.

**Bottom line:** hold. Structural-unraveling requires evidence — a streaming model losing subscribers to competitors, churn accelerating *after* the reset, or international losses compounding with no unit-economics path. None of that was in evidence; what was in evidence was a beloved company that enraged its customers and reversed course. Hold through the anger, watch the Q4/Q1 subscriber numbers for stabilization, and let management credibility rebuild at the market's pace. (For new money: wait for one quarter of U.S. net-add improvement — confirmation, not prediction.)
