**Recommendation: Buy** — the subscriber defections were a transitory, self-inflicted reset (a one-time price-shock churn plus a managed DVD decline), not a structural unraveling, and the stock's two-thirds de-rating had priced far worse than the evidence supported.

Why:

1. **The losses are concentrated where decline was already expected.** The DVD-by-mail business — still the majority of profits, but a sunset technology — is shedding customers by design after the price change. That is a managed harvest, not a demand signal about streaming. The churn in combined plans reflects a one-time repricing shock: customers unwilling to pay 60% more have now left, and churn should annualize back down over the next two to three quarters rather than compound.

2. **The actual growth engine — streaming — was still intact.** U.S. streaming subscribers were still growing (slowly), per-member viewing was rising, and domestic streaming carried a positive and expanding contribution margin (roughly 30%). The international losses are discretionary expansion spend, not deterioration of an existing business; management can throttle that spend if needed. Weak 2012 EPS guidance reflects investment, not franchise erosion.

3. **Valuation had de-rated far beyond fundamentals.** From above $300 to below $100 in four months, the market value implied a structural collapse. Yet revenue was still growing ~35% year over year, Netflix held the dominant share of U.S. streaming, and the core business remained profitable at the unit level. The market was pricing unraveling; the evidence supports a reset with a damaged-but-intangible franchise.

4. **The bear case is real but forward-looking and manageable** — rising content costs, the lost Starz output, coming competition from Amazon and Hulu. These argue for position sizing and staged entry, not for selling the market leader at a fraction of its prior valuation.

**What would change the call:** if U.S. streaming net adds turn negative or churn keeps climbing after the price shock annualizes — i.e., if streaming itself starts shrinking — the thesis is wrong and the stock should be sold.
