# Tesla (TSLA, ~$320, April 2018) — Recommendation: HOLD

Do not initiate a new position at ~$320; existing holders should stay through the Q1 print, with hard checkpoints.

**1. The price already assumes flawless execution.** At $50.3B, Tesla is worth more than Ford ($45.4B) and nearly as much as GM ($54.9B) while delivering ~30,000 cars a quarter against their millions per year — and still losing money. The entire premium over legacy automakers is a bet that the Model 3 scales to mass-market volume and margin. That leaves little room for further missteps to be forgiven by the market.

**2. The ramp just missed its own target.** Tesla guided to 2,500 Model 3s/week by the end of Q1 and managed 2,020 in the final week; Q1 deliveries (~29,980) were essentially flat sequentially. Every week of ramp delay defers revenue, deepens cash burn (roughly $3.5B burned in 2017 against ~$3.4B of year-end cash), and raises the odds of a dilutive capital raise — the single biggest risk to the equity.

**3. Safety overhang adds near-term headline risk.** The March Model X fatality and the NTSB investigation (stock −8.2% on March 27) keep Autopilot scrutiny in the news exactly as earnings land in early May.

**4. But demand is intact — so don't sell.** Deliveries grew ~19.7% YoY, the Model 3 backlog (~450,000 reservations) is the largest ever recorded, and if production reaches the promised 5,000/week by end of June, the economics of the story change materially. Selling here means betting against a demand-backed story over an execution stumble that could be corrected within a quarter.

**What would change the call:** Buy on Q1 evidence of a positive trajectory (production ≥3,000/week and trending to 5,000, contained cash burn, no raise needed); Sell on a capital raise, a second consecutive ramp miss, or cash approaching ~$2B.

**One-line justification:** At $50B Tesla is priced for a flawless Model 3 ramp it just failed to deliver, yet a record order backlog means the story is not broken — so hold and let the early-May earnings and weekly production data decide.
