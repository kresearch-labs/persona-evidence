# Arm 1 — Reference answer — 04-finance case-04 (Tesla, as of April 11, 2018)

**Recommendation: HOLD (with reduced position size for concentrated holders; no new money at $320).**

**Rationale (as known on the date):**

1. **The valuation already prices in success — the ramp must be flawless, and it just wasn't.** At $50.3B market cap — larger than Ford, near GM — Tesla's valuation rests entirely on the Model 3 scaling to mass-market volumes. The company *missed its own 2,500/week target* (2,020 in the final week) and Q1 deliveries were flat quarter-over-quarter. When the whole thesis is execution and execution has a visible miss, the margin of safety at $320 is negative. You are paying Ford-of-the-world money for a pre-profit startup's promises.

2. **But selling has its own risks, and the bear case is execution, not solvency.** This is the key distinction from an Enron/WorldCom scenario: nothing in the public record suggests accounting rot or a liquidity wall — the problem is *production*, which is a knowable, observable, fixable variable. Flat deliveries still grew 19.7% year-over-year; demand for the brand was never the question. Manufacturing ramps are exactly the kind of problem that resolves measurably within a quarter or two — and Q1 earnings (due early May) plus weekly production data give the holder near-term checkpoints, not a blind hold.

3. **The safety overhang is real but unquantified.** The NTSB investigation into the March Model X crash knocked 8.2% off the stock; regulatory outcomes are binary-ish and unforecastable, which argues for *not adding* at these prices — you'd be buying an unknowable regulatory tail.

4. **Position-sizing logic:** the prudent move for a client is to hold the position as a venture-style bet sized so that a 50% drawdown is survivable, and to define the checkpoints: weekly Model 3 production prints, cash burn in the Q1 report, and guidance on capex. If the ramp stalls again in Q2, the "hold" becomes "sell" without sentiment — the valuation cannot survive a second miss.

**Bottom line:** hold, don't add. The bull case (EV transition, brand, demand) is real, the bear case is operational and observable — so the holder gets new information every few weeks. Selling into a dip on a fixable problem after the stock already corrected ~12% from its high is poor risk asymmetry; but calling $320 a bargain requires believing a production ramp that just missed target will now execute perfectly. Size for volatility, wait for the Q1 print and the ramp data.

**Bottom-line for the record:** HOLD (trim to a survivable size; no adds below a demonstrated 2,500+/week Model 3 rate).
